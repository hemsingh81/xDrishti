# P3-08b — the leak and invariance properties, and the inventory

Built by [P3-08b](../specs/P3-08-independent-walker-oracle-and-leak-properties.md) (Annex B). This page is read by `PropertyInventoryTests`: it must name every test of the table, every property and the two entries kept elsewhere, so the table cannot drift from the suite. A property here says "an input is moved in a way that the rules cannot see, and the output is the output"; each has a negative control that must fail on at least 10 seeds of the property's own generator while the honest run reports none.

## The inventory

The status is *existing* (before P3-08b) or *added* (P3-08b). The roadmap row P3-08 names truncation and perturbation, independence (instrument order, a fresh frame against `Reset`), determinism, scale invariance and `RNet <= RFilled <= RExact`, each with a negative control, and four named controls: a filler in the signal minute, a stop checker that peeks at the next bar, a scanner that reads the next bar, a universe-dependent fill. ## What each added property claims, and what it does not

**Scale invariance (row 14).** Read from the definitions on 2026-10-19: ORB v1 compares prices with prices only (`close > or_high(or_min)`, the opposite side of the opening range as the stop, `entry ± rr × risk` as the target); `VWAP_PULLBACK` compares `close`, `vwap`, `low` and `vwap + pull_atr × atr(14)`; `BASE_BREAKOUT` compares a range as a percentage of a price (`... * 100 <= base_pct`) and `rel_volume`. **None has a rupee, volume or price-floor threshold**, so the absolute-threshold exclusion list is empty; the test scans the 17 distinct condition texts and fails on any numeric literal outside a call's parentheses or a lag's brackets, with the one named exception of the unit conversion `* 100`. The invariant, run through `Simulator.Run` with prices and volumes multiplied by 2 and by 4 (powers of two, so every double is exact) and the tick by the same factor: the candidates (index, decision instant, trace), every trade (dates, entry, stop, target, exit, reason, R, MAE, MFE, filled R, slippage in R, fill kind, sessions held) and the counters are the base run's, each price being the base run's price times the factor, bit for bit. Not claimed: a scale that is not a power of two (the 8-place decimal rounding and the grid are not homogeneous for 3 or 10 in general), a fixed absolute tick, and the cost chain (charges are in rupees and the quantity is floored). The honest run is exact on all 1,088 scaled runs (994 trades compared); the VWAP_PULLBACK and BASE_BREAKOUT subjects are small (12 series each; 8 and 4 trades) because their generators are long, so the weight of the property is carried by ORB v1 (876 trades in the exact and modelled modes), SWING_1H and SWING_DAILY. The control that keeps the old 0.05 tick for the scaled prices needs modelled fills to be seen: with exact fills a price on a 0.5 grid lands on the same level on a 0.05 and a 0.10 grid, so the control is not measured there.

**Date shift (row 15).** Whole weeks (1, 5 and 52, which crosses a year end) keep the weekdays; the calendar's exceptions (holidays on weekdays, special sessions) are moved with the series. A shift by days that changes the weekday is not claimed (the default week and the weekly bar of `BASE_BREAKOUT` depend on it by design). 15 of the 200 daily series hold a position over a holiday.

**Instrument order (row 16).** Per instrument the replay (record, counters, trades, open positions) is the same in the order given, reversed and in three seeded permutations, and the same as the instrument replayed alone. The assembled totals and the set of trades are equal. **The run key follows the run order by design** (P3-04: one `i=` line per instrument in run order; a basket's order is its position): the key text other than the `i=` lines is the same in every order, the `i=` lines are the same set, the same order twice gives the same key and a permuted order gives another key (asserted in 56 of 56 other-order runs). The brief of this slice expected "the same run key"; the specification of the key does not, and the test asserts what the specification says. The order of the merged trade list is also a function of the order (entry instant, then the place in the universe), so lists are compared as sets.

**State leaks (row 17).** One compiled `Simulator` and one `PreparedStrategy` are run over A (100 series), B (100 others, another generator) and A again in another order; each series of the second A equals its first run, for four loop strategies (ORB, a setup, a per-session feature, SWING_1H) and two replay strategies (ORB, ORB with `ema(10)`). The order is changed on purpose: with the same order a cache of the previous instrument's last value would hide after the first series.

**Daily truncation (row 18).** For a cut after a seeded session, after the session of the first candidate and the session before it, the candidates with a decision at or before the cut are the same (trace included) and so is every leading outcome that is final in the prefix; an outcome is not final when it is `EndOfData`, `OrderPendingAtEnd` or `NoFillBar`, and the comparison stops at the first such outcome because the position gate makes every later outcome depend on it. The walker-level property of the resting order is row 6; this is the same through the scanner, the gate and the walker of `Simulator.Run`.
