# 0047 — The independent oracle and the gaps of the rule tables

- **Status:** Accepted (with spec [P3-08](../specs/P3-08-independent-walker-oracle-and-leak-properties.md), which the owner approved on 2026-10-19; the text of section *Rules that close the gaps* states the behaviour of the engine at `sim-5` as the rule, and every place where that choice is not forced by an earlier sentence is listed in [the disagreement log](../research/p3-08-disagreements.md) for the owner to reverse). Extends [ADR 0037](0037-simulation-conventions-and-the-two-stage-seam.md) to [ADR 0044](0044-the-cost-choice-the-stored-charge-sheet-the-cost-sheet-verbs-and-the-bill-check.md); supersedes no sentence of them (the older texts are not edited).
- **Relation to other ADRs:** the rule tables are ADR 0038 (E0 to E7, X1 to X6, S1 to S4), ADR 0040 (X7 to X9, S5, the gate), ADR 0043 (E8, E9, the pending order) and, for the cost chain, ADR 0042. Numbering fixed centrally: 0045 is the result store (P3-10), 0046 the verification pack (P3-09).
- **Date:** 2026-10-19 (step 1 of P3-08: the dry read and the text of the rules; step 2, the same day: the oracle imported, the adapter and the comparison built, the measurements below; step 3 (P3-08c), the same day: the cost oracle, the cost comparison and the engine label, section *Step 3* below).

## Context
P3-08 writes a second implementation of the walk and the position gate from the written rules alone, and compares it with the engine. The comparison is worth something only if the written rules say everything the comparison needs. The dry read in [`docs/research/p3-08-field-definitions.md`](../research/p3-08-field-definitions.md) went through every compared field (33 of `SimTrade`, 29 counters, the 19 properties of the cost record, the gate) and found fields and questions with **no sentence** that decides them: the arithmetic of `SlippageR` and `RNet`, the midpoint rule and the digit limit of the double-to-decimal conversion, the conversion back, what a well-formed bar is; and several with a sentence that leaves a case open (the target level, MAE in a limit fill minute, the zero-volume count, which minute can be ambiguous, the hold end, the intraday sense of `EntriesAfterMissingMinutes`). Left open, an oracle author would have to guess, and a disagreement would then say nothing about the engine.

## Decision

### The process (D1, D2 of the spec)
1. **Roles.** O writes the oracle and its hand data; E owns the engine, the adapter and the comparison; T (the `reviewer` agent) audits and triages and never edits. O and E are different sessions.
2. **The clean-room workspace.** O works in a directory outside the repository with: ADR 0036 to 0044 and this ADR, the *Definitions* and hand tables of the specs P3-03, P3-03b, P3-03c, P3-03d, P3-03e and P3-04, the design documents 07, 08 and 16, the table of the dry read, and an empty class library that references nothing. No `.git`, no `backend/src`, no `backend/tests`. O is told not to run git, not to open any path under the repository, and to report every file it opened.
3. **What this can and cannot show.** It cannot prevent a read by absolute path; O's list of opened files is a self-report; T audits the transcript of O's tool calls only if the harness lets T read it (UNVERIFIED), and otherwise the audit rests on the self-report and on a reading of the oracle source for engine identifiers, shared helpers and lines copied from a rule table without derivation. The claim any label makes is bounded accordingly: *"written by a second agent from the rule tables, by a process that is checked and not enforced"*. It also shows nothing about whether the tables are right (P3-09's chart comparison does that) and nothing about the scanner, the frame and the compiler, which have no second implementation.
4. **The structure that can be checked** (architecture tests, step 1): `XDrishti.SimOracle` has no project or package reference and its assembly references no `XDrishti.*` assembly; only `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests` (the comparison) and `XDrishti.Architecture.Tests` (which must load it to inspect it) reference it; no `src/` project does; no production comparison passes non-empty `OracleFaults` (IL scan). Sentinels prove each check can fail.
5. **Tolerance and triage** are those of P3-08 D5 and D9: exact equality, then `1e-12` for the five ratio fields only, the measurement written below; classes A to D, the owner decides A and B. The measurement of the tolerance and the runtime added to `scripts/check.sh` are in *Measurements* below.

### Rules that close the gaps (the oracle reads these as the rules)
**R1 - The conversions.** A price, a level, a reward-to-risk and a slippage figure given as a `double` becomes a decimal in two steps: the platform conversion `(decimal)x`, then rounding to 8 places with `Math.Round(value, 8)`, which uses the default midpoint rule, **to even** (banker's). The first step keeps **15 significant digits**: the double `12345678.123456785` becomes `12345678.1234568` (7 places) before the second step, and prices below 10 million are unaffected. Midpoints are decimal midpoints after the first step: `0.123456785` gives `0.12345678`, `0.123456775` gives `0.12345678`, `100.000000005` gives `100.00000000`. (Measured on .NET 10, 2026-10-19.) A value that is not finite or whose magnitude is above `1e12` is refused by the engine (ADR 0038 #7, #12) and is not an outcome of the walk; the oracle's series never contain one. A decimal becomes a stored `double` by the platform cast `(double)x`, once per field, with a decimal zero stored as `+0.0` (ADR 0038 #13); no intermediate value is rounded to 8 places except the inputs and the cases of R5 and R12.

**R2 - `SlippageR`.** `SlippageR` is the difference `RFilled - RExact` of the two **decimals** (before they are converted), converted once and with zero as `+0.0`; it is not the difference of the two stored doubles. (The two can differ in the last digits; D5 tries exact equality first.)

**R3 - Ties.** A price on a level is a touch, never a miss. In the rule tables "beyond" includes equality: a stop is touched when a buy has `low <= stop` (a sell `high >= stop`); a target when a buy has `high >= target` (a sell `low <= target`); an open is beyond the stop when a buy has `open <= stop` (a sell `open >= stop`) and beyond the target when a buy has `open >= target` (a sell `open <= target`); the open is within the limit when a buy has `open <= limit` (a sell `open >= limit`); the limit is touched when a buy has `low <= limit` (a sell `high >= limit`); a fill at or through the stop is `entry <= stop` for a buy (a sell `entry >= stop`). "Strictly beyond" excludes equality and is used only by the gap counters (`StopGapExits`: a buy has `open < stop`; `TargetGapExits`: `open > target`) and by "ambiguous" (the open lies strictly between the stop and the target). These are the inequalities of the P3-03b table; they are restated so that the ADRs carry them.

**R4 - The slippage amount and the grid.** `a = (Ticks + extra) * tick + BarRangeFrac * (high - low)` where `Ticks`, `extra` and `BarRangeFrac` are converted by R1, `high - low` is the range of the **minute the fill happens in** after R1, and `extra` is `StopExitExtraTicks` for a `Stop` exit (including the stop of X1) and 0 for every other fill (a `SquareOff`, `MaxHold` or `EndOfData` exit pays the base amount, ADR 0040 #3). `tickDown(x) = floor(x / tick) * tick`, `tickUp(x) = ceiling(x / tick) * tick`, in decimal. The exit of a **target** has `a = 0` and goes on the grid only, against the trade: `tickDown(target)` for a long, `tickUp(target)` for a short (P3-03b S3). The entry `AtLimit` is the limit, which is already on the grid. With `SlippageConfig.None` no fill price is moved or rounded: the filled price is the exact price.

**R5 - The target level.** `target = entry + k * risk` for a buy (a sell `entry - k * risk`), in decimal, with `entry` the exact entry (the open of the fill minute after R1, or the limit), `risk = abs(entry - stop)` with the stop after R1, and `k` after R1. The product is **not rounded** to 8 places or to the tick (it can have up to 16 decimals); every comparison with the target is exact in decimal. `TargetPrice`, and `ExitPrice` of a `Target` exit, are the platform cast of this decimal. `RExact` of a target exit is therefore `k` exactly. (ADR 0038 #3 says levels are computed "from doubles rounded to 8 decimals", which describes the inputs; #7 says the grid never rounds a level; ADR 0042 "the unit of R" records the ninth decimal. This rule reads them together; see the log, D-01.)

**R6 - MAE and MFE minute by minute.** Let the running extremes start at the exact entry. For an ordinary minute that holds a trade and does not exit, both extremes widen to the minute's low and high. In the fill minute of an `AtLimit` entry only the **adverse** extreme widens (the low of a buy, the high of a sell): the minute's favourable extreme may have come before the fill (X5). On the exit minute: after a `Stop` the adverse extreme becomes the exit price (the stop, or the open when the minute gapped through it), and the favourable side of the minute is not counted, not even its open; after a `Target` that the open had already reached (X1, the exception of X4) the favourable extreme becomes the target and the range of the minute counts nowhere; after any other `Target` the favourable extreme becomes the target and the adverse side of the minute counts in full; after a `SquareOff` the open counts on both sides and the range does not; after a `MaxHold` at the due minute, a `MaxHold` at the last close and an `EndOfData`, the last minute that holds a trade counts in full. **Known exception (amended 2026-10-19 after the independent review of step 2):** when that last minute is also the fill minute of an entry `AtLimit`, X5 wins and the favourable extreme is not counted, so MFE can be 0 while the exit at the close is above the entry: the invariant `-MAE <= RExact <= MFE` is violated in that one corner (both the engine and the oracle do this; the generators never reach it, two planted series do). It is an open owner decision, entry Q3-MFE of the log: the proposal is to widen the favourable extreme to at least the minute's close (engine `sim-6`, golden diff read as in P3-03e); until then `ExcursionInvariantTests` exclude exactly that corner by one predicate. MAE is `(entry - low) / risk` for a buy (a sell `(high - entry) / risk`), MFE `(high - entry) / risk` for a buy (a sell `(entry - low) / risk`), both from the exact entry, in decimal, never below 0, with zero stored as `+0.0`.

**R7 - `EntriesAfterMissingMinutes`.** A trade counts 1 when its fill minute starts **later than the reference instant**: for a decision whose order is placed at once (an intraday walk, and a swing walk whose decision is before the end of its session) the reference is the decision instant; for an order that rests overnight (E8, E9) it is the open of the fill session (`FromMinute` of its session, 09:15 for a regular one). A no-trade counts 0.

**R8 - `AmbiguousMinutes`.** A trade counts 1 when its **exit minute** reached both the stop and the target and its open lies strictly between them (X4); nothing else counts. Because a minute that reaches the stop exits, a minute can be ambiguous only as the exit minute; it is never an X5 fill minute (the target is not checked there), never an X1 minute (the range is not used) and a trade without a target has none.

**R9 - `ZeroVolumeMinutes`.** Count a minute with volume 0 that the walk passes over, in two places. *The search for the fill minute:* the minutes after the candidate's own, in order, up to the first minute that holds a trade; for an intraday order only minutes on the decision date and starting at most 5 minutes after the decision instant are eligible; for an order that rests overnight only minutes on the fill date that start at or after the open of the fill session and at most 5 minutes after it (the minutes of the decision date after the candidate's own are ignored, a minute of the fill date before the open is skipped without a count); the first ineligible minute ends the search and is not counted, so a zero-volume minute beyond the window is not counted. *The position:* the minutes after the fill minute, up to and **before the exit minute**, that the walk visits (on the entry date for an intraday walk; for a swing walk on every date up to and including the due session's due minute, a zero-volume minute on a later date counts). When the walk ends by `EndOfData` (X6, X9) or by `MaxHold` at the last close (X8, second rule), the zero-volume minutes **after the last minute that holds a trade** are not counted. A no-trade counts the minutes of the search only (a `NoFillBar` that ended on a minute of another date, or beyond the window, counts those it passed; `PositionOpen` counts 0).

**R10 - `RiskUnderFourTicks`.** A trade counts 1 when `risk < 4 * tick`, strictly, `risk` being `abs(entry - stop)` in rupees of the exact entry and `tick` the instrument's tick in rupees (`TickSpec.Size`, after the paise conversion of ADR 0038 #6). A no-trade counts 0.

**R11 - Quantity, risk taken and turnover.** The costing reads the stored record: `entry = R1(EntryPrice)`, `risk = abs(entry - R1(StopPrice))`, the quantity from `entry` and `risk` (ADR 0042 #9), each turnover as `quantity * R1(filled price)`. A target filled with no slippage has a ninth decimal in `ExitFilledPrice`; the costing rounds it by R1 while `RExact` and `RFilled` use the unrounded decimal (ADR 0042, "the unit of R"). The entry leg is charged on the schedule in force on `TradeDate`, the exit leg on the India date of `ExitEpochSeconds`.

**R12 - `CostR` and `RNet`.** `CostR = TotalCost / RiskTaken` in decimal, converted once, with zero as `+0.0`; the comparison with `max_cost_R` is in decimal (equal is kept). `RNet` is the **difference of the two stored doubles** `RFilled - CostR`, in `double` arithmetic, with a zero result stored as `+0.0`; it is not computed in decimal.

**R13 - The hold end.** The hold end `E` of an accepted trade is `ExitEpochSeconds`, **the start of the exit minute** (15:29 for an exit at the close of a regular session), except for a trade that left with `MaxHold` at the last close, whose `E` is the instant at which its due session ends: midnight of the due date in India time plus the `ToMinute` of that session in minutes (15:30 for a regular session). A candidate whose decision instant `D` satisfies `D <= E` is blocked, and so is every candidate after an accepted `EndOfData` trade. A candidate decided at 15:30 on the day a position leaves with `MaxHold` at the due minute (`E` = 15:29) is not blocked.

**R14 - The bars the rules are about.** The rule tables assume well-formed minutes: finite, positive prices of at most `1e12` with `low <= open <= high` and `low <= close <= high`, and volume of 0 or more, strictly ascending in time. What the engine does with a minute outside that is not a rule and is not compared; the oracle's series and the adapter's cases are well-formed.

### Measurements (step 2, 2026-10-19)
**The tolerance (D5).** Exact equality is tried first on the five ratio fields `RExact`, `RFilled`, `SlippageR`, `MaeR`, `MfeR`; only a pair that is not bit-equal is held to `|a - b| <= 1e-12`, and a `-0.0` against a `+0.0` is a mismatch whatever the tolerance. **Measured maximum difference: 0.** Every one of the 204,750 ratio pairs of the default run (40,950 trades of 18,500 series, both fill modes, 5 fields each) was equal bit for bit, and so were all 690,000 pairs of the explicit run of 80,000 oracle series (5,000 in each of the 16 categories). The tolerance was therefore never used and **none is needed: the comparison is exact**. The reading of R2 (`SlippageR` is the difference of the two decimals converted once) is the one the engine implements and the oracle, written from the sentence, reproduced without slack. The constant `1e-12` stays in `RatioStats` as the rule of D5, so that a future engine change that moves a last digit is told apart from one that moves a result.

**The comparison.** `docs/research/p3-08-disagreements.md` lists the series compared per generator. In the default check: O's 100 hand rows in two fill modes (the spec numbers, the oracle and the engine agree on all 200 row-mode pairs and on every compared field); the author's gate rows G1, G2, G2b, G3, O9 and the hold-end edge, and the due-session rows K1 to K7, on the engine too; 16,700 series of the author's generator (600 to 2,600 per category, both fill modes) and the engine's 500 seeded days, 500 seeded daily series, 500 swing series and 100 replay series for each of three strategies; **no disagreement of any class**. The explicit run (`LargeRunTests`) of 5,000 series in each of the 16 categories, 80,000 series and 160,000 comparisons: no disagreement, every floor 10 times over.

**Sensitivity.** The triage rehearsal (criterion 11) and the explicit sweep leave each of the 16 faults of D8 on in the oracle in turn and compare 1,920 series with the real engine: every fault is reported (1,740 of 1,920 series for a fill on the signal minute, 1,601 for an off-by-one minute, down to 24 for the 09:21 fill and 2 for the high of an `AtLimit` minute counted into MFE). The hand rows catch each fault too, which is criterion 9 (the fault-to-rows list is in the test output of `FaultTests`: for example `StopCheckedAfterTarget` is caught by F3, H6 and H20, `PendingReadAsNoFillBar` by O8, `Minute0921AllowedAsFill` by O6-0921).

**Runtime added to `scripts/check.sh` (criterion 12).** Measured in Release on the developer machine (Apple silicon, 2026-10-19): the test run of `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests` takes 12.7 s wall clock with the oracle namespace left out (1,080 tests) and 18.2 s with it (1,676 tests, 11 explicit skipped): **+5.5 s wall clock** (the oracle namespace alone: 7.1 s; +27 s of CPU, spread over the cores), plus about 1 s of build for the 33 files of the oracle and the 18 of the comparison, and about 1 s for the architecture tests that load it. The budget of 20 s is met with room. The shared run of the generators is made once (`Corpus`) and read by the walker, gate, replay, coverage and log tests; the author's own two coverage reports (1,200 series per category, a second run each) are explicit in the default check because the coverage tests assert the same floors on the shared run. The 5,000-per-category run is explicit (6 s).

**Limits of the evidence (independent review, 2026-10-19: moderate).** The text of this ADR was derived from the engine, so bit-equal ratios are close to a consequence of the text and not a surprise; the scanner and the calendar are shared inputs, and for the engine-generated groups `OracleAdapter.Calendar` rebuilds holidays and special sessions by asking the engine's `TradingCalendar.SessionOf`; `OracleSeries`, `SeededDays`, `SeededDaily` and `SwingSeries` call `TradeWalker.Walk` and `PositionGate.Apply` directly, and only the 300 replay series (100 for each of three strategies) go through the real `Simulator`; O and the engine are one model family and O's independence is its own report with no transcript audit. The generator parameters are narrow (reward-to-risk 2.0 in the oracle's series, entry limit 0.1 %, square-off 15:15, slippage default or none): widening them is a follow-up.

**What the process could not show.** The independence of O is a process that was checked and not enforced (decision 3). O's report of the files it opened (`FILES-OPENED.md` of the clean room) lists only paths inside the clean-room folder, and no path under the repository; the oracle's source has no `using` of any `XDrishti` namespace and none of the engine's type or helper names (`SimTrade`, `TradeWalker`, `WalkOutcome`, `SessionClock`, `TickGrid`, `TickSpec`, `WalkCounters`, `SwingHold`, `PositionGate`, `FillRules`, `SlippageConfig`, `EntryCandidate`, `TradingCalendar`, `WalkRules`: a search of the 33 files found none). The transcript audit of O's tool calls by T is **not made by the author of this step**: it needs the reviewer's pass, and the harness's access to the transcript is UNVERIFIED. O's questions file lists 17 places where the text allows another reading; the engine gives O's answer on all that a series can reach, and the log lists them for the owner.

**The label (D13).** `RunLabels.EngineVerification` is now *"engine: agrees with a separately written reference implementation of the rule tables (P3-08, sim-5); not checked against charts or the owner's charge sheet"* (the owner's answer, softened after the review: see below) and `RunLabels.EngineVerified` is `true`; the two changed together, and `run.json` writes `engine_verified: true`. The reviewer also found the binding was only a test: a run of another engine version would still have been stamped true. The line and `engine_verified` are now a function of the run's engine version: `RunLabels.VerifiedFor = "sim-5"`; a run of any other version prints "engine: not yet verified for <version>" and `engine_verified: false`; `OracleVerifiedFor`, `RunLabels.VerifiedFor` and `SimulationEngine.Version` must all be equal or `OracleVerifiedForTests` fails. **The binding:** `OracleVerifiedFor = "sim-5"` is a compile-time constant in `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests.Oracle` beside the suite; `OracleVerifiedForTests` fails when `RunLabels.EngineVerified` is true while the constant differs from `SimulationEngine.Version`, when the label text names another version than the engine's, or when any run of the comparison has a disagreement; the next engine version cannot ship the label until someone has rerun the comparison and edited the constant on purpose. It is not a file the suite writes (a failing run would leave a stale file, and a Domain test would have to read a path in another project). **The key is not affected:** `RunAssembler.KeyText` hashes the engine version, strategy, parameters, timeframe, fills, costs, universe, `i=` lines and calendar and not the labels, so the run key and the run-record schema do not change and the engine stays `sim-5`; what changes is the text of `run.json`, `README.txt`, the banner and the cards, and the golden files that render them (regenerated; the diff is the label line and `engine_verified` and nothing else). **The wording search** (`EngineLabelWordingTests`): no current-state text in `backend/src`, `frontend/src`, `docs/prototype`, `docs/specs` or `docs/design` says "not yet verified"; the places that keep the words on purpose are an explicit list with a reason each (the build record of P3-04b1, the spec of this change, and the roadmap, which the coordinator edits); historical ADR text is outside the search.

**Runtime added to `scripts/check.sh` (criterion 12).** About +5 s wall clock in the test run of `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests` (18.2 s to 23.4 s; the cost runs are made once and shared), measured in Release on the developer machine on 2026-10-19; the part adds about 5 s of the 20 s the spec allows (the budget is an increment, not a total).

## Consequences
- **Nothing in the engine changes** by this ADR: every rule above is the behaviour at `sim-5`. Where it picks one reading of two sentences the log says so (D-01 to D-03); if the owner reverses one, the change is an engine decision with a new version and a regenerated golden file (P3-03e's way), and this ADR is amended.
- **O reads these rules** before it starts. They are committed as text before the oracle is written (the commit order is the evidence of acceptance criterion 2).
- **Tests added in step 1:** the architecture checks of the process above, with sentinels. **Step 2** adds the oracle (`tests/XDrishti.SimOracle`, 33 files, no reference of any kind) and the author's tests and 100 hand rows (moved into `tests/XDrishti.Simulation.Tests/Oracle`), the adapter, the comparator, the three-way hand-row check, the gate and calendar rows on the engine, the round-trip tests with their sentinels, the comparison of the generators, the coverage floors, the field-coverage (reflection) test, the log tests, the reproducibility test, the questions test and the triage rehearsal; and the assertion that the fault scan covers types (it is not vacuous). ## Alternatives considered
- **Let O decide each gap.** Rejected: a disagreement would then measure the two authors' guesses, not the engine.
- **Change the engine to a cleaner reading** (for example `SlippageR` from the stored doubles, or the target rounded to 8 places). Rejected here: it would move the golden files and the engine version for no gain; the owner may ask for it through the log.
