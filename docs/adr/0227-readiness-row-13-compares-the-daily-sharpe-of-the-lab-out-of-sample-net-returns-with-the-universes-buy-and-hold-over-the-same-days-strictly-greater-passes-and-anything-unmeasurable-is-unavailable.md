## Context
Row 13 asks that the strategy beats the same-universe equal-weight / buy-and-hold net, on a risk-adjusted basis. P8-07 gives a pure `BuyAndHold` (equal weight, no rebalancing, price return) but one figure per window, not a daily series, and the Lab's out-of-sample trades are not stored after the run, so a later reader cannot rebuild them (the reason rows 10, 11 and 14 are measured at run time).

## Decision
1. **Risk-adjusted is the daily Sharpe:** the mean over the sample standard deviation of the daily returns, not annualised, no risk-free rate, for the strategy and for the buy-and-hold over the identical days. The strategy passes only when its Sharpe is **strictly greater**; equal fails. Pure, in `Domain/Readiness/BenchmarkComparison`.
2. **Which trades and days:** the Lab out-of-sample net trades of the full run (rows 1 to 12 are Lab rows). The strategy's value on a day is the sum of the net R of the trades that exited on it, 0 on a day with no exit (R is a return up to a constant, which a Sharpe ignores). The days are the sessions, from the first to the last exit, on which every member of the run's universe has a close.
3. **The hold:** `Domain/Reporting/BuyAndHoldDaily` takes the P8-07 inclusion rules from `BuyAndHold.Compute` itself (start gap, end gap, bad price, discontinuity, minimum members) and requires that no member is excluded, because a hold with fewer members is not the same-universe hold. Its daily value is the mean of close over the purchase close (the last common session before the first day), so weights drift (no rebalancing). The members are the run's universe as listed; a sampled universe is `unavailable`.
4. **Measured when a full Lab run ends** (`LabBenchmark`, one bounded read of the stored day bars per instrument, after the robustness and nulls stages) and carried on the verdict as `Benchmark` (the two Sharpe ratios, the days, the members, the reason, the basis). Nothing that judges the verdict reads it. The scorecard's `LabEvidenceReader` applies the strict comparison to the stored ratios.
5. **`unavailable` (a fail) for:** no Lab run, a verdict stored before the block, a sampled universe, no out-of-sample trade, a member the hold cannot use or fewer than the minimum, fewer than 60 days (a proposal, `BenchmarkComparison.DefaultMinDays`), a trade that exits on a session a member did not trade, a series with no variance, or a failed read (the message is not repeated). Cancellation propagates.
6. `IReadinessGaps` loses `BenchmarkBeatenAsync` (one method left: the risk share); an architecture pin forbids its return.

## Consequences
Row 13 reads `unavailable` for every verdict stored before this change until the full test is re-run (the trades are not kept). Survivorship (members as of the run, ISS-22) flatters the hold, which makes the test stricter, not looser. Summing R by exit day treats the strategy as one book, ignoring overlap and sizing; the comparison is a screen, not a performance claim. The summed R is a bias, not a neutral choice: trades that exit on one day lump together and inflate the variance, which biases the strategy's Sharpe down (conservative); summing concurrent trades is variable leverage, so when the overlap varies the bias can go either way. A member listed during the out-of-sample window has no purchase close at the start, so the hold refuses it and row 13 reads unavailable. The look-back before the first exit is never less than the hold's `MaxStartGapDays`. The API contract gains `BenchmarkComparisonView` on the Lab verdict (`gen-api` run). Tests: Domain tables with hand-computed Sharpe ratios and the equality boundary, the daily hold by hand, Application fail-closed cases (no run, too few days, an excluded member, no variance, a failed read, a cancel), the reader's row states, the architecture pin.
