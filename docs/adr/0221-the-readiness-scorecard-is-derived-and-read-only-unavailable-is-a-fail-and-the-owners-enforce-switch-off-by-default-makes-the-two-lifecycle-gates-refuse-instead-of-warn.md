## Context
The spec merges two research notes into one object: a 15-row go / no-go checklist of the out-of-sample edge (is the research result believable) and a readiness score of six sub-scores whose minimum is the score (is the forward evidence strong enough to move on). Most producers exist now (CF-01 to CF-10, CF-12) but some inputs have no source at all. The approvals rule of the project says an `unavailable` input never counts as a pass, and that anything that needs manual approval must also be able to run automatically behind a setting that is OFF by default.

## Decision
1. **Pure Domain calculators (`XDrishti.Domain.Readiness`).** `EdgeChecklist` judges the 15 rows (pass, fail, unavailable, Kleene style: a fail anywhere in a row's parts wins, then unavailable, else pass; a boundary equality passes, with a 1e-9 tolerance for binary rounding); `ScoreAnchors` and `ReadinessCalculator` give the six sub-scores (piecewise linear between the spec's anchors, clamped), the minimum as the score, the hard fails and the four labels; `ReadinessGates` gives Gate R (rows 1 to 12 and 14) and Gate S (Gate R, rows 13 and 15 and the label "Ready for the next phase"); `SystemReadinessCalculator` gives `min(integrity, weighted mean)`. No clock, no I/O, no random number (an architecture test). The hand tables of the spec are unit tests (interpolation, power 38.08 %, the research example 20.2 with the power as the weakest link, min and not mean, the seven hard fails, the labels at 39.9, 40, 69.9, 70, 84.9 and 85, the system example 55.2, the 320-trade checklist example at 2, 8 and 64 trials), each boundary equality included.
2. **`unavailable` is a fail everywhere.** A row with an input that does not exist is `unavailable` and blocks the gate; a sub-score that applies and has no input is a hard fail; a hard-fail term whose input cannot be read is a hard fail too (not a pass). Only a sub-score or a term that does not apply (no ML model: no calibration sub-score) is `not_applicable`: it is left out of the minimum and listed. Until its producers exist, a strategy therefore reads **Not ready, score 0**, and the card says what it is waiting for ("row 13 Benchmark: waiting for P8-07 baskets benchmarks") rather than look broken. The score without the hard fails (`rawMinimum`) is shown beside it.
3. **The inputs that have no producer sit behind one port, `IReadinessGaps`, whose only implementation says unavailable with the reason** (`UnavailableReadinessGaps`). They are: row 13 the benchmark (no risk-adjusted net comparison per strategy), row 14 capacity and tier (no per-trade tier or order size against the minute value in the Lab verdict), row 15's P3-09 20-trade check (the owner's signed record has no machine-readable store), row 10's quarter share and instrument share (the verdict has a by-year table only), row 11's Monte Carlo 90th percentile (the verdict stores the 50th and the 95th), the integrity-fault hard fail (the list of integrity checks is unverified), the tier-C-only hard fail, and the planned risk share per strategy (the system level then uses equal weights, labelled). When a producer is built it is added behind the port and the scorecard changes by itself. An architecture test pins that there is exactly one implementation, that every answer is the word unavailable with a reason, and that only the scorecard reader reads the port.
4. **The assembly (`ScorecardReader`) reads the existing producers through the existing queries.** The Lab: the newest finished full run's verdict (`ListLabRunsQuery`, `GetLabRunQuery`), the registration (`GetRegistrationQuery`), the hold-out (`GetHoldOutQuery`) and the cost-sheet confirmation. A producer that judges its own thresholds (CF-05 cost stress and fill delay, CF-07 random-entry, placebo, mirror and plateau, CF-06 hold-out) hands over its own word and is not re-judged. The forward evidence: the edge series through the one shared series rule (`EdgeSeries`, the same that the sequential test and the decay report read, so the scorecard cannot read a different series), the decay report (the point-in-time backtest reference, the P6-09 CUSUM alarm), the sequential failure test (the CF-09 boundary), the kill proposals and the cells they paused (the CF-12 demotion), the P10 phase check and the champion model's calibration (CF-10).
5. **The gate (`IReadinessGate`) is the only route from the lifecycle to the scorecard.** `RecordCellTransitionHandler` asks it for exactly three moves the lifecycle table allows, Candidate to Paper (gate R), **the owner's re-enable of a paused cell, Paused to Paper (gate R, CF-12 D3)** and Paper to Active (gate S), and for no other (a pause, a retirement, a revert lower risk and are never judged); it asks only after the table allowed the move, so an illegal move leaves no gate snapshot. **The owner's own evidence ids are never cut to make room for the gate's**: a gated move may carry at most 18 of its own (the gate adds up to 2, the limit of the row is 20) and is refused with 422 before the gate is asked otherwise. The re-enable's answer carries the gate's own decision as its first warning (passed, did NOT pass with the rows and the enforce switch OFF, or refused 409 when ON). The gate builds the strategy's card (the cell's first part is the strategy id, read at its latest Lab version), stores a `gate` snapshot, and answers. **Enforce OFF (the default): the move goes on and the changelog row's evidence ids carry `readiness-snapshot:N` and `readiness-gate-R:fail:enforce-off:rows-2.3.13`.** **Enforce ON: the move is refused with the error `confidence.gate_failed` (409, kind Conflict) naming the failing rows, and nothing is written but the gate snapshot.** No new changelog column is added (a deviation, below).
6. **Failure reads as the stricter side, per direction (documented trade-off).** A switch that cannot be read reads as **ON** (the move is refused), so a store that is down never lets a move through unchecked; since a move writes to the same database, this costs nothing that would have worked. A scorecard that cannot be built is a gate that does not pass (with no rows named); it refuses only when enforce is ON, like any failed gate. A gate snapshot that cannot be stored is logged and the move carries no snapshot id; the decision does not depend on it. A cancelled read is never swallowed.
7. **The owner's switch `confidence.readiness.enforce` is stored state, not configuration.** `readiness.settings` is append-only, the id is drawn after an advisory lock so the highest id is the last to commit, and the database stamps the time (the pattern of `confidence.kill_settings`); no row means OFF. `PUT /api/confidence/readiness-settings`: **a change either way needs the owner's session, the CSRF token and a fresh password for that act** (a missing one is 403 `auth.step_up_required`, a wrong one the sign-in 401 or 423 with its lockout; the step-up window is neither opened nor used up). **Deliberate tightening of the spec's D6:** turning enforcement OFF removes a refusal and so raises risk, exactly as `confidence.kill.auto` does, so OFF needs the password too (the first build left OFF to the session alone; the review changed it). Owner question below: confirm. The actor is built from the signed-in identity, never from a body; the handler refuses a non-owner actor and a reason that is not 1 to 500 characters. Recorded with a reason, per change.
8. **Settings `Confidence:Readiness`.** The thresholds the scorecard judges itself (row 2: n_eff 300 and 30 per cell; row 3: 0.15 R; row 6: t 2.0; row 10: 70 % of quarters, no year over 60 % and no instrument over 15 %; row 11: 95th percentile under 20 R) and the three label cut-offs (40, 70, 85). The rows another slice judges keep that slice's settings. **Tighten-only is claimed, and is true in both directions of use:** a count, expectancy, t or share bar can only be raised, a ceiling (year share, instrument share, drawdown budget) only lowered, a label cut-off only raised and the three must increase; a stricter row fails more often (the gates refuse more, never fewer) and a higher cut-off gives a lower label for the same score. A looser or non-finite value stops the host at start (tests refuse each). The **anchors of the sub-scores are not settings** (the interpolation points interact, so changing one moves the meaning of the others; a change is a code change with an ADR, and the rules version `cf11-v1` is part of the settings text, so the hash of every snapshot changes with it).
9. **Snapshots (`readiness.scorecard_snapshots`).** Insert-only for the runtime role (no update, delete or truncate), the database stamps the time, a `daily` snapshot is unique per subject, strategy, version and India day, a `gate` snapshot is one per gate use; each row stores the score, the label, the whole card as JSON and the hash of the settings. The service `readiness-snapshot` (daily 18:30, after `decay-monitor`, starts paused, registered last of the application's services) stores one per Lab strategy at its latest version and one for the system, idempotent per day, a failing strategy is counted and repeated the next day. A strategy scorecard read shows the newest 30 daily points (the trend).
10. **The rows and the facts of the Lab are read as follows (interpretations, deviations below).** Rows 2, 3 and 6 are on the development out-of-sample trades and labelled "pre-hold-out" (review fix 1 of the spec); after the unlock they are not recomputed with the hold-out trades, because the hold-out's effective n and spread are not stored. The standard deviation behind row 6 is the verdict's expectancy over the per-trade Sharpe of the deflated-Sharpe block. Row 3 uses the larger of the factor the gates used and the honest factor of the full trial count. The lower bound of the effect sub-score is the mean less 1.645 standard errors (the project's "90 % interval", the 5th percentile). Backtest agreement reads the band's 5th percentile as `mu_ref - 1.645 x sigma x sqrt(1/n_eff + 1/N_ref)` from the decay report's net point-in-time reference.
11. **Enforcement (`ScorecardArchitectureTests`, each scan with a positive control and a sentinel it must catch).** The calculators are pure; nothing in Planning, Selection or Autonomy (Domain and Application) names any type of the slice; the lifecycle recorder is the only caller of the gate (read from the IL) and the only type that builds its request; nobody in the slice builds `RecordCellTransitionCommand` or appends to the changelog, so no path moves a cell without the owner; the switch store has one writer; the snapshots have two writers and one reader; every port has one implementation, in Infrastructure; the switch is not a configuration key; the routes are three reads and the owner's switch; the migration is in a schema of its own, never calls the schema-wide grant helper, and grants no update or delete. The three pinned lists of earlier slices that name readers of the shadow result port and of the shared series rule gain `ForwardEdgeReader`, and the list of Domain namespaces gains `XDrishti.Domain.Readiness`, each with a comment.
12. **Storage (`AddReadiness`).** Schema `readiness` (a schema of its own, so the grants of `confidence` are not touched): `settings` and `scorecard_snapshots`, both insert-only for the runtime role with table privileges granted one by one (never the schema-wide helper, which grants update and delete on every table). Raw SQL, no EF entity, so the model snapshot does not change; Down drops the schema only. Tests: up and down on a scratch database, the grants and every refusal on the real role.

## What the owner sees
`GET /api/confidence/scorecards` (the system card and one line per Lab strategy, weakest first), `GET /api/confidence/scorecards/{strategyId}/versions/{version}` (the 15 rows with value, threshold, producer and state; the six sub-scores with what would move each; the hard fails by name; the label; the two gates and the rows that stop them; what it is waiting for; the settings hash; the trend), `GET` and `PUT /api/confidence/readiness-settings`. Nothing is blocked until the owner turns `enforce` ON; with it OFF every gate use is recorded and shown as a warning. The screen and the verb are part 2.

## Deviations and interpretations (proposals, for the owner)
- **`auto_to_paper` is not built.** The spec's D6 and AC10 add a setting that lets the system make the Candidate to Paper move itself. This part adds no path that moves a cell without the owner (the brief of this part, and the standing rule that a move toward more risk is an owner act); a path that moves a cell on its own needs its own review. The owner's CLI verb `xd learn lifecycle` is, today, the only door of the two moves, and it is gated.
- **No new changelog columns.** The spec says the changelog "gains the gate result columns" (an edit of P6-08). The gate result is carried instead as two evidence ids on the row (`readiness-snapshot:N`, `readiness-gate-R:fail:enforce-off:rows-...`), which cite the stored snapshot that holds everything; no applied migration or schema of the learning log is touched.
- **Names.** The spec names the migration `AddConfidenceSnapshots`, the table `confidence.scorecard_snapshots`, the service `confidence-scorecard` and the route `PUT /api/confidence/settings`; this part uses `AddReadiness`, `readiness.scorecard_snapshots`, `readiness-snapshot` and `/api/confidence/readiness-settings` (a schema of its own keeps the grants of `confidence` untouched; the generic settings route would be ambiguous beside the kill and shadow switches). The settings section is `Confidence:Readiness`, not `confidence.score.*` / `confidence.gate.*`, and carries only the values that can honestly be called tighten-only.
- **Row 11 can never pass yet.** The spec wants the observed drawdown within the Monte Carlo 90th percentile; the Lab stores the 50th and the 95th. The 90th is a gap (unavailable), so row 11 is at best unavailable, which blocks Gate R, until the producer stores it. Using the 95th instead would be looser than the spec, so it is not done.
- **Rows 10, 13, 14 and 15 cannot pass yet** (their sources are gaps, item 3), and the integrity and tier-C hard fails read unavailable, so every system and strategy score is 0 today. This is the spec's own risk ("correct, and the screen must say waiting for CF-xx").
- **Strategy and version.** The scorecard is per strategy id at a Lab version; the forward evidence is per strategy id (a shadow trade key starts with it). A cell's strategy is the first part of its key and is read at the latest Lab version; a cell whose strategy is not a Lab strategy has no Lab result and reads unavailable. The list shows each Lab strategy at its latest version.
- **A strategy that has no forward series** has an effective n of 0 (the sub-scores for evidence and power read 0), and its mean, spread and agreement are unavailable (hard fail).
- **A forward series gross of charges** (the cost sheet is not confirmed) is never offered as net: its mean, spread and months are unavailable; the effect, agreement and paper-confirm rows say so.
- **The effective n uses the placeholder same-day correlation 0.3** until CF-01 is wired into the edge count, as CF-08, CF-09 and CF-12 do.
- **The re-enable is gated (review fix).** The CF-11 spec names two moves (D6) but CF-12 D3 requires the owner's re-enable to meet the gates; it is Paused to Paper and judged by gate R, keeping its own conditions (ADR 0219) as well. `ReEnableCellHandler.GateWarning` is now the start of a sentence that reports the gate's actual decision.
- **Turning enforcement OFF needs a fresh password** (review fix; the spec's D6 and the first build said the session alone). A deliberate tightening: OFF removes a refusal. Open question 8.
- **The phase check reaches the scorecard as a delegate (`PaperPhaseMetReader`), not as a handler contract.** The lifecycle recorder is also the automatic pause's door and reaches the gate; naming the phase check's handler there made the schedule reader, and so every scheduled service (the ML monitor among them), reachable in the closure scan of the automatic kill path, with no run-time path behind it. The delegate is built in the registration; the one word it returns (MET, NOT MET or null) is all the scorecard reads. The sentinels of the ML architecture tests that implement a production contract became top-level types for the same reason (the closure follows a nested type to its declaring type). Log ids of the slice are 7300 to 7307.
- **A strategy whose scorecard cannot be built in the daily run** is counted as failed, repeated the next day, and counts in the system score as 0 with the reason (never dropped, which would raise the mean); a refused insert of one strategy's snapshot does not stop the others, and that strategy keeps its real score in the system figure.
- **The phase check is the system's**, not a strategy's, and is read once per request; row 15 uses it as is.
- **A strategy at the list is built one after the other** (about 6 reads each) and the shadow rows are read once per request; the 200 ms target for tens of strategies is not measured (`// shortcut:` in the readers).

## Consequences
The owner can see, for each strategy and for the system, how believable the edge is, how strong the forward evidence is, which link is weakest and what would move it; every use of a gate leaves a stored snapshot the changelog row cites; and once the owner turns `enforce` ON, a Candidate cannot become Paper nor a Paper cell Active while a required row is not a pass, an unavailable one included. With `enforce` OFF nothing changes for the owner except the warning and the record. Nothing ranks, sizes, plans, approves or orders differently, and the scorecard is invisible to Planning, Selection and Autonomy. Part 2 (the `/readiness` screen, the `xd` verb), the producers behind the gaps, and `auto_to_paper` remain.
