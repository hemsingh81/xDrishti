# 0051 — The trails: `ATR_TRAIL`, `STRUCTURE_TRAIL` and `EMA_TRAIL`

- **Status:** Accepted (spec [P4-03](../specs/P4-03-the-trails-atr-structure-and-ema.md), which the owner approved on 2026-10-19, questions 1 to 7 as recommended; built on 2026-10-21 ahead of the P3 exit check because the owner pulled P4 forward). The text of this ADR has not been read by the owner.
- **Date:** 2026-10-21.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** [ADR 0049](0049-the-exit-tape-trades-with-legs-and-sim-6.md) (the tape, the internal `IExitRule`, the walker over a position state, which this slice uses and extends by one action and one view field), [ADR 0036](0036-the-typed-strategy-model-and-the-compile-of-its-sections.md) Amendment 2 (the tables of the three policies stop being provisional), [ADR 0038](0038-fill-realism-the-entry-limit-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-the-order-inside-a-minute.md) (the rows X1 to X5 and the slippage S1 to S3, unchanged), [ADR 0040](0040-swing-positions-held-across-sessions-the-maximum-hold-the-position-gate-and-simtrade-schema-3.md) (the swing walk, the same rule runs in it), [ADR 0042](0042-costs-and-sizing-the-charge-sheet-the-quantity-net-r-and-simtrade-schema-4.md) (one entry and one exit order), [ADR 0047](0047-the-oracle-and-the-rule-table-gaps.md) (the oracle). The number is 0051: 0050 is the number of the parallel slice P4-02 (partial exits and the time stop).

## Context
P4-01 gave the walker a tape of closed-bar values and a seam for a rule; nothing in production used it. Doc 08 §2 lists three policies that leave in one fill and differ only in how the stop moves or when the position is signalled out: a chandelier (`ATR_TRAIL`), a stop under the N-bar extreme (`STRUCTURE_TRAIL`) and an exit on a close beyond the EMA (`EMA_TRAIL`). P4-03 builds them, lets the simulator run a strategy that has one of them, and ends the provisional status of their parameter tables.

## Decision
1. **Three internal rules, no new walker.** `AtrTrailRule`, `StructureTrailRule` and `EmaTrailRule` (`XDrishti.Simulation.Walking`, internal, sealed) implement `IExitRule`; `Simulator.Create` builds the rule of a side's one exit (`TrailRules.Of`) from the compiled `ExitPlan` and the place of the plan's feature in the scanner's tape (`FeatureNames.Of(plan.Reads[0])`), and passes it to the walker with the tape. A trade of a trail has `Legs = null`, `TargetPrice = null`, `StopPrice` the **initial** stop, and the costs of one entry and one exit order; `FIXED_RR` is still the walk with no rule.
2. **`ATR_TRAIL` (D1 to D3).** The level of a counted bar *k* with a number for the ATR is `H − mult × ATR` (a sell: `H + mult × ATR`), `H` being the highest high (a sell: the lowest low) of the minutes that hold a trade from the **fill minute** through the end of bar *k*. The anchor is the entry, not the activation. The trail is active from the first counted bar whose **close** is at least `activate_at_rr` initial risks in favour (`>=`: a close exactly at the level activates; a touch inside a bar that closes back does not), and for ever after; with `activate_at_rr` 0 it is active from the first counted bar, whatever it closed at. A bar with a NaN ATR gives no level but still counts for the activation. A level takes effect from the first minute at or after the close of its bar, only if it is not looser than the stop in force (a strictly looser one is ignored and counted in `StopLoosenIgnored`; an equal one is taken and not counted); the stop is then the gap-aware stop of the minute table (a level, or the open when the minute opened through it; market-type; reason `Stop`).
3. **How the rule gets `H` and the first counted bar without keeping state.** The rule stays a pure function of what it is handed. `ExitView` gained one trailing member, `Trail` (`TrailInput(Extreme, FirstBar)`): `LegState.Observe(minute)` keeps the running favourable extreme of the minutes the walk has finished with (the fill minute included, with its **whole** high even in an entry at the limit, which the spec's "the minutes from the fill minute" says and the excursion rule X5 does not apply to), and `FirstBar` is the first bar that closes after the fill minute started. Bar *k* is read at the first minute that starts at or after its close, when every minute before it has been observed and no minute at or after the close has, so the extreme at that moment is `H_k`. The activation is found by looking at the closes from `FirstBar` to *k* (O(bars) per bar, no allocation).
4. **`STRUCTURE_TRAIL` (D4, D5).** The level of every counted bar is the value of the tape (`lowest(n)` of the low for a buy, `highest(n)` of the high for a sell, which are the catalog's default sources, the N-bar extreme **including bar *k***); no activation and no offset; the same "tighter than the stop in force" and gap-aware rules. A level the price has already passed fills at the open, not at the level.
5. **`EMA_TRAIL` (D6, D7).** A counted bar whose EMA is a number and whose close is below it (a sell: above; equal is no signal) signals the whole position out. A new action kind, `ExitActionKind.SignalExit`, sets `LegState.SignalExit` for the minute that sees the bar. The position leaves at the **open of that minute** (the first minute that holds a trade at or after the close) as a market fill, reason **`EmaExit`** (new member of `ExitReason`, stored by name), counter `ExitsEmaExit`. Three orders inside that minute are decided here because the spec's wording leaves them open: (a) **the stop comes first only when the open is already at or through it**: then the stop row takes the minute (reason `Stop`, at the open), exactly as `Take` does for a partial exit; a minute that opens above the stop and later falls through it is an `EmaExit` at the open, because the signal order is filled at the open before any later print can touch the resting stop; (b) **the square-off comes before the signal** (row X1 is tested first), so a signal on the bar that closes at the square-off minute is a `SquareOff`; (c) the swing walk takes the signal before the `MaxHold` close of the due minute, since the open is earlier. The excursions of an `EmaExit` count the open only (like the square-off), and its slippage is that of a market fill without the extra stop ticks.
6. **What the simulator accepts (D8).** One candidate exit among `FIXED_RR`, `ATR_TRAIL`, `STRUCTURE_TRAIL` and `EMA_TRAIL`, one value in each list (`rr`, `mult`); more than one candidate, a list of two values and every other policy stay `simulation.unsupported_exit` at `<side>.exits`, the message naming the policy and the parameter. `ExitTables.RunBySimulator` holds the four names, so the warning `exit_not_evaluated` is no longer given for the three; a test asserts that it and the simulator's refusal agree for every policy. Overlays are still refused (`time_stop` is P4-02's, built in parallel; when it lands the accepted set widens to "at most `time_stop`" as the spec says, and the row of the hand tables that follows a level with a `TimeStop` is added then).
7. **Versions and stores.** The engine stays `sim-6`; `FIXED_RR` runs differ from before only by the new counter `exits_ema_exit` (zero) in `run.json` and the regenerated golden files of it, and by nothing in the walk. `research.sim_trades` has a check constraint on the reason names: the migration `AddEmaExitReason` replaces it (a drop and an add; the table is unchanged and its append-only grants are not touched). The export spells the reason `ema_exit`; the check-log reader of the verification pack accepts it as an exit reason; the card strata gain `ema_exit-buy` and `ema_exit-sell`.
8. **The oracle (D10).** `WalkRun` gained `OracleTrail` (kind, the closed bars with the value the rule reads, the multiple and the activation) and a `TrailStep`, written from D1 to D7 and this ADR's decisions 2 and 5 only: `H` is found by scanning the list of minutes from the fill minute to the bar's close (not by the engine's running extreme), the activation is a sticky flag set when a close qualifies, a signal is a flag for the minute. The comparison uses a test tape (`TrailTape`: the mean range, the N-bar extreme and an exponential average of 15-minute or daily bars, NaN until the period is reached), the same input for both, in both fill modes.

## Consequences
- A strategy with one trail runs end to end through `Simulator.Run`, the replay and the export; no verb offers it yet, because a verb runs a built-in strategy by id and the three built-ins carry `FIXED_RR` (see *Honest limits*).
- The walker costs nothing extra for `FIXED_RR`: `LegState` exists only when a rule does, and `Observe` is called through `legs?.`.
- A trail run allocates the trade and nothing per visited minute or per bar read (a test compares a 21-minute and a 330-minute position for each rule).
- `StopLoosenIgnored` is now non-zero in production runs of an `ATR_TRAIL` or `STRUCTURE_TRAIL` strategy; it was documented as zero for every production run in P4-01 and the comment of `WalkCounters` is unchanged in meaning (it counts requests, not defects).
- The chandelier anchor includes the high of an at-limit fill minute that the MFE of the trade does not count. A trade stopped by a chandelier level can therefore show a level a little higher than its MFE explains. This follows the spec literally and is reported rather than patched.

## Honest limits
- The rules, the oracle and the test tape were written by the same model family from the same spec; the comparison finds differences between two readings, not a misreading they share. The three open orders of decision 5 are exactly the kind of reading both could share; they are written in the ADR so that the owner can overturn them.
- The oracle does not recompute the ATR, the extreme or the EMA: it takes the tape's values as given. Whether the scanner's frame computes them right is P3-01's and the indicator tests', and the tape-against-frame test of P4-01.
- No verb runs a trail yet (criterion 11 is met at the simulator, the replay and the exporter, not at `simulate` and `verify-pack`): choosing an exit for a built-in strategy needs a place for it in the verb's options or a stored strategy (ADR 0002), neither of which is part of this slice.
- The hand rows and the properties use 15-minute and daily bars; a 5-minute or 1-hour tape differs only in the bar boundaries the tape builder gives.
- The time stop after a level is not tested, because the overlay is not built in this branch.

## Alternatives considered
- **A stateful rule, one instance per walk.** Rejected: the P4-01 contract says a rule is a pure function of the view and the tape, which is what the leak property needs; two small view fields give the rule what it lacks.
- **Take `H` from the tape** (a `highest(since entry)` feature). Rejected by the spec (D2): it is not a feature of the frame, and the walker already has the minutes.
- **Check the stop before the signal for the whole minute** (a resting order that a later print can touch). Rejected: the signal order is placed after the close and filled at the open, before any later print; the only case the stop can win is an open already through it.
- **A `Trail` exit reason for a moved stop.** Deferred (spec question 4): the exit price against the initial stop already tells it; P4-05's diagnostics can ask for a flag.
