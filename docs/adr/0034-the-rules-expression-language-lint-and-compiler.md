# 0034 — The rules expression language: one grammar, three-valued logic, a look-ahead lint and a compiler to expression trees

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-10-16: "approved" on [P3-02](../specs/P3-02-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md), with the recommendations of its open questions 1–7). **Open question 8 is not answered by this approval and stays open:** the six flagged build deviations of [ADR 0033](0033-weekly-monthly-timeframes-calendar-features-and-the-market-context.md) (the `vix_pct` window, the `regime_vol` fallback, the cursor of the context read, the first partial week, the exact thirds, the reading past the end of the context) still wait for the owner; this slice only uses the names of those features, so it does not depend on the answer, but doc 11 changes if one is reverted. **Seven build deviations from, or narrowings of, the approved text wait for the owner's confirmation** — `x[0]` is refused (Amendment 1), the cold-start budget is missed (2), the nesting limit counts the depth of the tree (3), the calendar and context features are a clock of their own, the date, in the lag rule (4), "all problems are reported" holds within a stage and for 100 syntax problems (7), the negative controls of the look-ahead property are feeders and the property cannot see a read of a forming bar made of past minutes (14), and two claims of the approved text — constant time, type errors naming both sides — are corrected (18); each is marked ⚠ in the *Amendments* below — builds on [ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md), [ADR 0032](0032-frame-history-closed-flags-and-the-remaining-indicators.md) and [ADR 0033](0033-weekly-monthly-timeframes-calendar-features-and-the-market-context.md) (the 56 features, the frame, the context) and on [ADR 0002](0002-config-and-strategies-in-app.md) (strategies live in the database as text)
- **Date:** 2026-10-16 (built the same day: see *Consequences from the build* and *Amendments*)

## Context
The simulator (P3-03) and the live engine must run the **conditions** of a rule — `rsi(14) > 55`, `crosses_above(close, vwap)`, `tf("1h").ema(20) > tf("1h").ema(50)`, `time >= "09:45"`, `regime() == "UP_LOW_VOL"` — over the `FeatureFrame` of [ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md) to [0033](0033-weekly-monthly-timeframes-calendar-features-and-the-market-context.md). The text comes from a person (later from an assistant) and lives in the database; four things are asked of the way it is read. It must be **safe** (never executed as code). It must **not read the future** — the main risk of [doc 19](../design/19-risks-and-compliance.md) — not even by a slip in the wording ("the previous value" of two features on different timeframes). It must be **fast**: a condition is evaluated for every closed bar, instrument and parameter set of a test. And its **errors must be fixable**: where, which rule, and what was probably meant.

## Decisions
The owner's answers of 2026-10-16 to the seven recommendations of the spec:
1. **Split.** This slice is the *expression language* — the text of one condition becomes a checked, compiled, evaluated predicate. The *strategy file* around it (the YAML sections, `params` of at most four, the `sell:` block and the automatic BUY → SELL mirror, the JSON Schema, storage in `lab.strategies`) is **P3-02b**. This slice receives the text, the evaluation timeframe and a `{name → number}` map, and nothing else.
2. **Superpower** (one NuGet package, [doc 03](../design/03-target-architecture.md)), version pinned in `Directory.Packages.props` (3.2.1), with compiled expression trees; the alternative, a hand-written parser without a dependency, was not taken.
3. **Unknown is not true.** A condition whose data is not ready is *unknown*, not *false*: Kleene's three-valued logic, so `not (rsi(14) > 55)` cannot fire before RSI exists. A decision treats unknown as false.
4. **`time` is the close time of the evaluated bar**: the start of the last accepted 1-minute bar plus one minute (the bar 09:44 makes `time` = 09:45), so `time >= "09:45"` first holds on the 15-minute bar that *closes* at 09:45, not on the one before it.
5. **Lagged evaluation needs one timeframe.** `crosses_above`, `crosses_below`, `rising` and `falling` are refused when their operands sit on different timeframes (the "previous value" of the two would be different instants); the message gives the explicit form (`close > tf("1D").ema(20) and close[1] <= tf("1D").ema(20)`).
6. **Strict crossings.** `crosses_above(a, b)` is `a[0] > b[0] and a[1] <= b[1]` (a touch that does not pass is not a cross; equality at the previous bar counts as "not above yet"); `rising(x, n)` is strict at every step.
7. **The prerequisites come first**, inside this slice: `ContextFeature` / `ContextFeatureKind` become `DateFeature` / `DateFeatureKind`, `IndicatorInfo` gets `RequiresContext` and `RequiresExpiryDays`, and `FeatureFrame` gets `LastBarTime` — the follow-ups M18 of the P3-01c review, done before the first user of the names.

Design decisions of the spec, approved with it:

8. **A pure library `XDrishti.Strategies`** that references `XDrishti.Domain` and `XDrishti.Indicators` only (and Superpower), with four namespaces: **Syntax** (lexer, parser, immutable AST with source spans, canonical printer), **Binding** (names, types, catalog arguments; all internal), **Lint** (the look-ahead and timeframe rules and the codes) and **Compilation** (the plan, the delegate, the merge of several conditions). No I/O, no clock, no code made from text, no mutable static state, every class sealed (the three abstract bases of the syntax tree excepted); enforced by architecture tests.
9. **The grammar** is the one of [doc 11 §2](../design/11-strategy-lab.md): `or` < `and` < `not` < comparison (not chained) < `+ -` < `* /` < unary `-` < postfix `[k]` < primary; keywords `and or not true false`; numbers, double-quoted strings, no comments, free whitespace, case-sensitive. A subscript is only read after a reference.
10. **A closed vocabulary.** An identifier means, in this order, a strategy parameter (a number known when the condition is compiled), then a builtin, a constant or `time`, then a catalog feature. The dotted spellings are exactly `prev_day.open|high|low|close|volume`, `today.open|high|low`, `index.trend` and `index.adx` (mapped to the flat catalog names); everything else dotted is an error. Nothing else is ever looked up.
11. **Types:** `number` and `bool` are what a finished condition is made of; `time` (the keyword), `regime` (the function `regime()`) and `text` exist only next to a comparison. A condition must be `bool`.
12. **Errors are data.** A failed compile returns every problem it found — a stable code, a message that names the offending text, a source span with offsets, line and column, an optional suggestion, a severity — in source order, never an exception; at most 100 syntax problems are listed, then one that says there are more (Amendment 7). The library's `Result` carries one error, so `ParseResult` and `CompileResult` carry the list (the spec foresaw this).
13. **The look-ahead and timeframe lint** (table below), with the codes `strategy.lint.*`; the syntax problems have the codes `strategy.syntax.*`.
14. **Compile in two steps.** `ConditionCompiler.Compile` (parse, bind, lint) returns a `CompiledCondition`: exactly the features the text reads, the history depth, whether the frame needs a market context or expiry dates, the warnings. `Bind(frame)` resolves the handles and returns a `BoundCondition` whose `Evaluate` runs a delegate compiled from a `System.Linq.Expressions` tree built from the bound tree. `ConditionSet.Merge` unites the plans of several conditions (one frame for all).
15. **When to evaluate is the caller's business.** A bound condition is a pure function of the frame as it is *now*; the simulator evaluates at the steps where `frame.BarClosed(evaluationTimeframe)`.

### The lint rules
Each rule has a stable code `strategy.lint.<name>`; the messages are English sentences that name the offending text and may be reworded, the codes and spans are the contract. The full reference with examples is [doc 11 §2](../design/11-strategy-lab.md).

| Code | Severity | Rule |
|------|----------|------|
| `unknown_name` | error | an identifier or function that is not a parameter, builtin, constant, `time` or catalog feature; a dotted pair that is not one of the spellings; a regime name that is none of the nine; a builtin or parameter written with `tf(…)`. Suggestion: the closest names within edit distance 2 (case-insensitive; distance 1 for a text of one or two letters), at most five |
| `bad_arguments` | error | a wrong number, kind or range of arguments (the catalog's parameter names, ranges and defaults are quoted); an argument that is not a number known at compile time; a function or a feature with parameters written without parentheses (`rsi`; suggestion `rsi()`); a bad or repeated `source=`; a parameter or constant called like a function |
| `unsupported_timeframe` | error | `tf("…")` is none of `1m 5m 10m 15m 30m 1h 1D 1W 1M` (`"1d"` suggests `"1D"`), or the catalog does not support the feature on it (`vwap` on 1D, `prev_day_*` on 1W, a calendar or context feature on anything but 1m) |
| `negative_or_zero_lag` | error | a subscript or `rising/falling` count that is not a whole number of at least 1 (`close[0]`, `close[-1]`, `rising(close, 1.5)`) |
| `lag_too_deep` | error | the history needed (the largest lag read plus 1; the lags of `crosses_*` and `rising/falling` add to the lags inside) is above 256 |
| `lag_on_non_feature` | error | a subscript on a strategy parameter, a constant or `time` (on a call, a parenthesis, a literal or a second subscript it is the syntax problem `subscript_on_non_reference`) |
| `mixed_timeframes_in_lag` | error | the features under `crosses_*`, `rising` or `falling` are not all on one timeframe; the suggestion is the explicit form where one side is a plain reference on the strategy's timeframe. The calendar and context features are a clock of their own, the date: they are never lagged together with a bar feature, on 1m too (Amendment 4) |
| `time_on_daily` | error | `time` on a 1D, 1W or 1M strategy |
| `bad_time_literal` | error | a text compared with `time` that is not `HH:MM` within 09:15 … 15:30 (`"9:45"` suggests `"09:45"`) |
| `type_mismatch` | error | a value of the wrong type for its place (see *Types*); a parameter that is not a finite number |
| `constant_condition` | warning | a true/false part that reads no data (`1 < 2`, `crosses_above(1, 2)`, a parameter against a number); a bare `true`/`false` only when it is the whole condition; a bounded oscillator above 100 is *not* detected |
| `redundant_timeframe` | warning | `tf("15m").ema(20)` on a 15m strategy; suggestion `ema(20)` |
| `needs_context`, `needs_expiry_days` | information | the plan reports that the frame must be created with a `MarketContext` / the expiry dates; reported at the first feature that needs it, and only for a condition without errors |
| `shadowed_name` | warning | *added by the build* (Amendment 11): a strategy parameter with the name of a feature, builtin, constant or `time` wins, and says so |
| `lag_across_session` | warning | *added by the build* (Amendment 19): a lagged read of a feature that starts again at the session open and has a value on its first bar (`vwap`, `today_*`, `prev_day_*`, `gap_pct`) on an intraday strategy: on the first bars of every session "the previous value" is the previous session's. The message names the features and the check that clears it, `and time >= "09:45"` for one bar back on 15m; such a check in the same chain of `and`s, late enough for the lag asked (`rising(vwap, 3)` on 15m needs `time >= "10:15"`), silences it |

## Why a condition cannot look ahead
The guarantee has three layers. The first is structural and is pinned by an architecture test and an audit of the expression trees, the second is the lint table, and the third is a property test that can fail — for what it can see, which is the feed of the frame, not the compiler (its limits are written below).

1. **The language has no word for the future.** A compiled condition reads the world through exactly three doors of the frame: `frame[handle]` (the value as of the **last closed bar of the feature's timeframe**), `frame.At(handle, lag)` with a lag of 0 or more (the value `lag` closed bars of that timeframe ago) and, for `time`, `frame.Steps` and `frame.LastBarTime` (the minute that was just fed). A negative or zero subscript is refused when the text is read (`negative_or_zero_lag`), and the compiler only ever adds the non-negative offsets of the lagged builtins, so no lag is below the one written. There is no syntax for a forming bar, a later bar or a value computed from them. A feature on a higher timeframe is whatever the frame says it is — the last *closed* bar (ADR 0031), a weekly bar only when its last session has closed (ADR 0033), a context value as of the previous session (ADR 0033). Two tests keep the library to these doors: an architecture test lists the only seven members of `FeatureFrame` the compiled library may reference (`get_Item`, `At`, `get_Steps`, `get_LastBarTime` to read; `HandleOf`, `get_HistoryDepth` and `Create` to bind), never `Step`, `Reset`, `BarClosed`, `TryLastClosed` or any other way to feed or peek at the frame, with a sentinel that proves the rule can fail; and `CompiledTreeTests` audits the expression trees of the written and of about 2,700 generated conditions on all nine timeframes: a tree can call only the public functions of `Kernel` and `Math.Abs/Min/Max`, gives the frame it was given to the three kernel functions that take one (`Value`, `ValueAt`, `CloseMinute`), holds nothing but numbers (no closure over a frame, a bar or a list), uses no member access, construction, invocation or indexing other than the handle array, and builds the lag of `ValueAt` only as a sum of non-negative whole numbers and the counter of a run — never a subtraction, a negation or a negative constant, so a feature is read at the lag written or further back, never nearer. Hand-built trees (a call of `IsReady`, a lag of −1, a lag from a subtraction or a negation, another frame, a member access, a closure) show that each rule can fail.
2. **The lint refuses what would silently be wrong.** A lag that needs more history than the frame keeps (`lag_too_deep`), "the previous value" of features on different timeframes (`mixed_timeframes_in_lag`), a subscript on something that has no past (`lag_on_non_feature`) and a `time` on a bar that has no clock time (`time_on_daily`) are errors with a message and, where there is one, the correct form.
3. **A property test over the feed of the frame and the determinism of the chain** (`LookAheadPropertyTests`). For 240 seeds (40 on each evaluation timeframe 1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 1D and 1W) and 1,200 random conditions (five to a seed, all in one frame, a market context on half of the seeds), the three-valued result and the decision of every condition at every closed bar of the evaluation timeframe is identical (a) when every later 1-minute bar is removed and (b) when every later bar is replaced by arbitrary values — prices of 1e12, 1e300, 1e-9 and 0, zero volumes, huge volumes —, for cut points at the closed bars (all of them when there are few, a sample with the first and the last otherwise) and a few minutes in the middle of a bar. On the seeds with a context the same runs also cut the context at the date of the cut: in (a) the context days dated at or after it are removed, in (b) they are replaced by changed ones (other prices, a VIX of 99.9, a breadth of 3), as the prefix property of the date lane in the Indicators tests does — a step on the date *D* reads the day before *D*, so the record of *D* and the later ones must not matter. The conditions are generated as a model, printed to text in several spacings and bracket styles, and go through the whole chain (parse, bind, lint, compile, bind to a frame, evaluate). **Negative controls** show that the property can fail: run through a feeder that gives the frame the first bar after the step before reading (`NextBarFeeder`), one that runs the frame on to the close of the slowest higher-timeframe bar before reading (`FormingBarFeeder`), or one that lets the date lane read the context record of the date itself (`SameDayContextFeeder`, modelled by moving every record one calendar day back), the same property reports violations on all six timeframes, for hand-written conditions and (for the first two) for the random ones, while the honest feeder reports none on the very same inputs.

**What the property proves, and what it does not.** It proves that, fed an honest series, the answer at a step depends on the bars up to that step and nothing later: it guards the *feed* and the *frame* (a closed value, a calendar or a context lookup that used a bar it had not been given) and the determinism of the chain (no hidden or static state). It cannot fail for a change in the *compiler*: a compiled condition is a pure function of the frame as it is at the step, and the frame holds only the bars up to the step, so whatever the compiled code computes, removing or replacing the later bars cannot change it. A compiler that read the wrong lag or the wrong clock (a `time` one minute late, a crossing whose previous value is the current one) passes the property and is caught by the other layers: the interpreter oracle (864 conditions compared at every step), the hand-calculated examples, the audit of the expression trees and the architecture test on the members of the frame, which exist for that reason (they were added after review showed that two mutations of the compiler passed all of `LookAheadPropertyTests`). The negative controls are three wrong *feeders*, not a wrong compiler: they show that the harness can see a leak in the feed. The property also cannot by itself see a read of the *forming* bar that uses only past minutes, because a partial bar is a function of the minutes before it and truncation does not change it; the negative control `FormingBarFeeder` therefore models the leak as reading the bar in its final state before it closes. The forming bar is guarded where it is made — the frame never shows it (ADR 0031: the `CloseIndex` of every bucket against an independent reference, the frame-semantics tests, the prefix property) — and, in the library, by the two tests above: the compiled code reads the frame through `frame[handle]` and `frame.At` (and `Steps` and `LastBarTime` for `time`) and nothing else. It also does not cover look-ahead in the *data* (a restated close, ADR 0033).

## Security
No string is ever executed. Every identifier is checked against a closed vocabulary (the catalog, nine builtins, seven constants, `time`, and the strategy parameters, which are a map of numbers and so cannot carry text); the dotted spellings are three scopes (`prev_day`, `today`, `index`) with fixed members; `source=` is one of four prices. The only dynamic code is a `System.Linq.Expressions` tree **built from the bound tree** in one class (`ExpressionBuilder`) that calls a fixed list of the library's own functions (`Kernel`, plus `Math.Abs`, `Min` and `Max`), found by name once; the framework's `LambdaExpression.Compile` emits the IL for that tree, which the rule against `Reflection.Emit` in our own code does not cover and is not meant to. Architecture tests keep it that way: no `Reflection.Emit`, Roslyn, dynamic LINQ, CodeDom, `Activator`, `AppDomain`, assembly loading, file, network, database, clock or environment access (scanned in the compiled code, with negative controls proving the scans can fail), and no `System.Linq.Expressions` outside `ExpressionBuilder`. Literals are bounded (`1e400` and `1e-400` are problems), the text is at most 4,000 characters, the nesting at most 64 levels, and every recursion checks that stack is left and ends in a `too_deep` problem rather than a crash. Fuzz tests feed the parser 6,000 random mixes of language and junk, 4,000 valid sources damaged by one character and every prefix of every valid source: nothing throws, hangs or breaks the contract (every problem has a span, a line and a column, in source order). The work of the recovery is bounded by a count, not by a clock: it parses the tokens at most 232 times and lists at most 100 problems (see *Consequences from the build*), and a test counts the parses for 18 shapes of the worst texts of 4,000 characters. What it cannot do is protect the *meaning* of a valid condition — a wrong rule is the Lab's business (doc 11 §5), not the compiler's.

## Consequences
- The simulator and the live engine call one thing: `ConditionCompiler.Compile` → `ConditionSet.Merge` → `FeatureFrame.Create(plan.Features, …)` → `plan.BindAll(frame)` → `Evaluate` at the closed bars. **The frame is created from the plan's features**; if no condition reads a feature on the evaluation timeframe (a plan of `time >= "09:45"` alone has no feature at all) the caller must add one before it can ask `BarClosed` — the plan does not (P3-03).
- A bound condition belongs to the **layout** of its frame (the same features in the same order and the same history depth). `Evaluate` does not check which frame it gets, because that would cost time on every evaluation and would stop one strategy being reused across instruments; a frame of another layout reads other features **without an error**. Build every frame of a strategy from the same plan.
- Parsing and checking never pay for the delegate: it is built once, at the first `Bind`, whatever the number of frames, so an editor that only checks conditions pays 0.9 ms (warm) for ten of them and a test over ten thousand instruments compiles each condition once.
- The cold start of a process is slow (Amendment 2): the first compile of a fresh process costs about 40 ms, mostly the JIT of the parser and the expression compiler. A host that compiles on request will see it once.
- The error wording is English text for now; the codes, spans and suggestions are what a form builder or an editor (Monaco, a later UI story) reuses. Messages may be reworded without a new ADR.
- A condition with a syntax problem is answered with its syntax problems only; the name and lint problems appear once it parses.
- `rsi(14) > 100` (a bounded oscillator that can never be true) is not a `constant_condition`: only literals and parameters are constant. A wrong rule that compiles is for the Lab's checks.
- P3-02b writes the YAML around this: it hands each condition and the parameter map to `Compile`, turns `all:` / `any:` lists into `and` / `or`, and may use `ExpressionPrinter` for the canonical text of a condition (whitespace and redundant brackets removed) when it hashes a strategy.

## Consequences from the build
What was learnt while building [P3-02](../specs/P3-02-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md); the deviations from the spec are listed under *Amendments* below.

**The prerequisites in `XDrishti.Indicators`.** The rename is mechanical (`DateFeature`, `DateFeatureKind`; the catalog names and behaviour are unchanged). `IndicatorInfo.RequiresContext` is true for exactly the ten names `index_trend index_adx index_vol_pct vix vix_pct vix_chg breadth regime_trend regime_vol regime`, `RequiresExpiryDays` for `is_expiry_day` only; `FeatureFrame.Create` reads the two flags instead of switching on the kind (the error fields and messages are unchanged), so a thirteenth date feature needs no edit in `Create`. `FeatureFrame.LastBarTime` is the epoch-second start of the last *accepted* 1-minute bar, 0 before the first step, unchanged by a skipped bar and cleared by `Reset`; reading it allocates nothing. Tests: 226 cases pin the flags for all 56 names (including the exact error when an option is missing, and that giving only the other option does not satisfy the requirement) and 54 cases pin `LastBarTime` (8 frame shapes, hand-calculated epochs, skipped bars, `Reset`, a 20-seed property), plus one allocation test; no existing assertion was weakened.

**The grammar as built** (the reference is [doc 11 §2](../design/11-strategy-lab.md)). The lexer is a Superpower tokenizer that never fails: what it cannot read becomes a token of an error kind with its own text, so every wrong place is reported. Only ASCII is part of the language outside strings; a full-width `＞`, a no-break space or an accented letter is an `unknown_character` problem with the code point, never silently its look-alike — and a look-alike with a clear meaning (`＞`, `&&`, `||`, `!`, `≥`, `×`) is reported once and then read as the token it stands for, so one mistake is one problem. A string in single or typographic quotes is one problem with the double-quoted string as the suggestion. Numbers are `digits [. digits] [e [+-] digits]` with a lower-case `e`; `.5`, `1.`, `1E5` and `55abc` are problems. Identifiers are letters, digits and underscores. A subscript is `[` an optional minus and digits `]` and follows a reference only (`close[1]`, `prev_day.high[1]`, `tf("1h").close[1]`; not `rsi(14)[1]`). `tf` is written `tf("1h").name` or `tf("1h").name(args)`; a bare `tf("1h")` is `bad_timeframe_qualifier`. A parenthesised node's span includes its parentheses.

**The parser is a hybrid.** The tokenizer and the small productions (names, literals, the `tf("…").` qualifier, a named argument, a subscript) are Superpower combinators. The recursive spine — an expression with its operators (precedence climbing), a parenthesised expression and the arguments of a call — is written as plain Superpower parser functions: nine combinator layers, one per precedence level, needed more than 1 MB of stack at 64 nested levels in a Debug build, the spine needs 512 KB in Release and 768 KB in Debug (including the 128 KB the runtime keeps in reserve), and every nesting level checks that stack is left. Superpower is not visible in the public API (an architecture test reads the signatures). **Recovery** wraps the parser in a loop: parse; at a failure explain it from the tokens around it (code, span, message), *repair the token list* (insert a missing value, delete up to the next safe restart, delete a broken subscript, close open brackets, fix the kind of a closing bracket) and parse again. The first problem is always exact; later ones are those of the repaired text — independent faults (a second unknown character, a trailing comma and a chained comparison, a missing operand and an unclosed bracket) are reported truthfully, a single typo gives one problem and not a chain: a follow-on failure right after a value the recovery supplied (`close <> 5`), at a comma it has just reported as having no argument (`rsi(,)`), at a `!` after a value that the lexical pass has already reported as an unknown character (`close ! 5`: the text has no `not` to blame) or at an end that a repair made (`close === 5`) is repaired without a second report. **Cost and bound.** Every attempt parses the whole token list again and builds the one array of tokens that Superpower reads, so k problems cost O(k·n) for n ≤ 4,000 tokens; before the bound a text of nothing but faults took 0.43–0.68 s and 1.5 GB (3,990 commas after `f(`), 0.13 s and 387 MB (3,999 `&`), 0.17 s and 435 MB (a long sum followed by 2,000 closing brackets). The recovery now stops after 100 problems (the lexical ones counted) or 232 parses (twice the problems plus 32), whichever comes first, and the parser lists the first 100 problems in source order followed by one `too_many_problems` for the rest of the text; the worst texts measured take 13–52 ms and 14–27 MB. A test counts the parses for 18 shapes of the worst texts, so no clock is involved. The copy of the token list that the recovery made before its first attempt is gone (it works on the list the lexical pass built).

**Limits.** Text longer than 4,000 characters: `too_long`, the span is the part beyond the limit. Nesting: `too_deep` in two places — before the parse (the brackets open at once plus the prefix operators pending may not exceed 64; the span is the token that goes over) and after it (the depth of the tree may not exceed 64: every call, prefix operator, subscript and each operator of a chain is a level, parentheses are none; the span is the whole condition) — so a chain of 64 `and` operators of single names passes and 65 do not, and 63 conditions of the form `rsi(14) > 55` joined by `and` pass and 64 do not (Amendment 3).

**The binder.** Names resolve in the order of decision 10. The catalog is asked, not copied: the arguments of a feature become a canonical `FeatureSpec` and `FeatureFrame.Create` is run on a one-spec frame to get the catalog's own answers (ranges, whole numbers, `fast` below `slow`, an opening range of whole bars, a timeframe the feature cannot run on), whose field errors become problems at the argument, the timeframe or the feature they are about; messages quote the catalog's parameter names, ranges and defaults. Two spellings of one feature give *equal* specs (`rsi()`, `rsi(14)`, `rsi(14, source=close)`, and `highest(10)` with `highest(10, source=high)`), so the frame shares one instance. A feature argument and the count of `rising/falling` are *constants known at compile time*: a literal, a parameter, `MON … SUN`, or arithmetic, unary minus, `abs`, `min` and `max` of those (`rsi(rsi_len * 2)`); a division by zero is NaN and is refused. The calendar and context features are always read on 1m, whatever the strategy timeframe. `regime()` is compared with `==` and `!=` only, against one of the nine names (resolved to the code when the text is read) or a constant integer 1 to 9. The suggestion for a typo is the closest vocabulary entries by optimal string alignment distance (insert, delete, replace, swap two neighbours), compared case-insensitively, nearest tier only, ties broken by the smaller case-sensitive distance (`2m` suggests `1m` before `1M`), at most five; a property test checks one deleted, inserted or swapped letter in each of the 56 names plus the builtins against an independent edit-distance implementation. After an error the part becomes a `BoundInvalid` that keeps its bound operands, so the lint still finds the other faults of the condition: all binder and lint problems of a condition that parses are reported together, in source order. A `crosses_*`, `rising` or `falling` that cannot be bound (a type error in an operand, a count that is not a whole number) keeps its operands as the lagged ones of the call (`LaggedCall`), and a `time` compared with a bad text or with a number keeps the keyword, so the clock rules (`mixed_timeframes_in_lag`, `lag_too_deep`, `time_on_daily`) are still applied and fixing one fault does not make another appear. What is not kept is the arguments of a feature that cannot be bound (`rsi(time)`): they are the same fault.

**The compiler.** The bound tree has 17 internal sealed record node types behind an interface (so the library keeps its rule that no class is open for inheritance). `ExpressionBuilder` turns it into a `Decision` — a delegate over the frame and an `int[]` of handles (`handles[i]` is the handle of `Features[i]`), returning the numbers of `Truth` (1 true, 0 false, −1 unknown) — with two kinds of node: a *number* is an expression of type `double` (NaN when not ready), built *at an offset* (how many closed bars further back every feature in it is read, which is how `crosses_*` and `rising/falling` evaluate their operands at several lags while constants stay constants), and a *truth* is an `int` that calls the functions of `Kernel`. `Kernel` is the one place where each rule is written in plain code that a test can walk exhaustively: Kleene's tables, the six comparisons (unknown for a NaN), `crosses_above/below` (unknown if any of the four values is NaN), `between` (both ends included), the run state of `rising/falling` (a loop over the lags 1 to n), the division that is NaN instead of infinite, and `CloseMinute` for `time`. `and` and `or` skip the right side when the left one decides (an optimisation; the result is Kleene's). The delegate holds no frame and no handle, so one delegate serves every frame the condition is bound to.

**Semantics the spec left open, decided in the build.** The arithmetic of a NaN is NaN; only an exact zero divisor gives NaN, a quotient that overflows is ±infinity (a number), `inf − inf` is NaN (so unknown), `min` and `max` pass a NaN on. The lagged builtins are *atomic*: `crosses_*`, `rising`, `falling` and `between` are unknown if any of their values is NaN, even when the other values would already make the answer false. "Not ready" is NaN, relying on the `IIndicator` contract that `Value` is NaN exactly when not ready (a test pins `IsReadyAt` against NaN for every catalog feature at lags 0 to 2). `time` is unknown while `frame.Steps == 0` (not when `LastBarTime == 0`, so a real bar at epoch 0 is still a bar), is the India clock minute (the domain's `SessionClock.MinuteOfDay`, shared with the rest of the system, as are the session bounds 09:15 and 15:30 of `bad_time_literal`) and wraps at midnight. A condition that reads no feature binds to any frame.

**Lagged reads are by position, not by session.** `x[k]` is the value `k` closed bars of its own timeframe ago, and the history of the frame runs on across sessions ([ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md), decision 4; P3-01b criterion 2): a rolling feature wants that (`ema(20)` at 09:30 uses the previous day's bars). A feature that starts again at the session open and already has a value on its first bar does not: `vwap`, `today_open/high/low`, `prev_day_*` and `gap_pct` (from the second session on) — `IndicatorInfo.HasValueAtSessionOpen`, which is false for `or_high` / `or_low` of several bars because they are not ready until the range has closed, so a crossing against them is unknown on the first bars. For these, the read one bar back on the first bar of a session is the *previous session's last value*. The headline example `crosses_above(close, vwap)` is therefore true on the first bar of a session whenever that bar closes above its own typical price and the last close of the day before was at or below the last vwap of that day: a cross of two sessions. The final review found it (on a seeded random walk of 500 sessions the crossing was true on the first bar of about a quarter of the sessions, against a few percent at every other bar); the same holds for `close > vwap and close[1] <= vwap[1]`, `rising(vwap, 1)` and `crosses_above(close, today_open)`. Pinned by a hand-calculated two-session example (`SessionBoundaryExamplesTests`: true at the first bar of the second session, `time >= "09:17"` removes it on 1m). The rule built (Amendment 19): the meaning of `x[k]` is unchanged and the lint warns, `lag_across_session`, naming the features, how far back, and the check that clears it. Reads `lag` bars of timeframe `T` back stay inside the session from the bar closing `(lag + 1) · T` minutes after the open (at most 15:30; none when `lag · T` is a whole session or more), and a strategy on timeframe `E` is affected when its first evaluation, `E` minutes after the open, comes before that: `E < (lag + 1) · T`. A strategy on 1D or longer evaluates at the end of a session and is not warned. The check is recognised when it is a lower bound of `time` (`>=` or `>`) in the top-level chain of `and` of the same condition and the first evaluation it lets through is late enough; under an `or` or a `not`, or as an upper bound, it is not. A check in another section of a strategy file is not seen (follow-up for P3-02b). The numbers are verified against the real frame by `LagAcrossSessionTruthTests` (28 combinations of strategy timeframe, feature timeframe and lag: the lint warns exactly when some evaluation of a second session reads the first, and no evaluation at or after the asked-for time does). **Known limits of the warning** (the stronger rule is ISS-13): (a) a *plain subscript* of an `or_high` / `or_low` of several bars still reads the previous session's range on the first bars (`close > or_high(30)[1]` on 15m at 09:30 compares with yesterday's high) and is not warned; (b) a 1D or longer strategy is exempt by timeframe, although a very long lag of an intraday feature (`tf("5m").vwap[80]`) lands in the previous session; (c) the `time` check is a clock check, the lag counts accepted bars: it assumes a complete 09:15 opening (a session whose first minutes are missing from the data, or an evening Muhurat session, is not protected).

## Amendments (2026-10-16)
Deviations of the build from [P3-02](../specs/P3-02-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md); the guarantees of the acceptance criteria hold except where Amendments 2, 7 and 14 say otherwise. **Amendments 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 14 and 18 deviate from, narrow, or settle a contradiction in, text the owner approved (all seven marked ⚠ *needs the owner's confirmation*)**: the owner confirms them or they are reverted. The others add behaviour the spec left open, resolve a contradiction inside the spec in favour of the code, or record how it was built; the spec carries the same list.
1. ⚠ *Needs the owner's confirmation.* **`x[0]` is refused.** The spec's *Meaning* said "`x[0]` is `x`"; its lint table said that a subscript that is not an integer in 1 … 255 is `negative_or_zero_lag`. Built: `close[0]` is `negative_or_zero_lag` with the suggestion `close`, and `close[-1]` the same, so that the language has one spelling for the current value. Reverting means making the binder read `x[0]` as `x` and changing the lint row, doc 11 and the tests that pin the refusal.
2. ⚠ *Needs the owner's confirmation.* **The cold-start budget is missed.** Criterion 12: "parse + bind + lint + compile of a 10-condition strategy ≤ 20 ms cold and ≤ 3 ms warm". Warm is met (median 2.5–2.7 ms) and so is the 0.3 µs per set (255 ns while the frame moves); **cold is 39–41 ms** in a fresh process (see *Performance*). The cold figure is the first compile of a process and is paid once; it is not asserted anywhere. The owner may accept it, or ask for `PublishReadyToRun` / a lighter lexer (ISS-12).
3. ⚠ *Needs the owner's confirmation.* **The nesting limit of 64 counts the depth of the tree.** The spec: "a source nested more than 64 levels … is refused with a problem". Built, two checks with the code `too_deep`: more than 64 brackets plus pending prefix operators open at once (before the parse), and a syntax tree deeper than 64 (after it), where each operator of a chain is a level, so a chain `a and b and …` is as deep as it is long: 63 conditions of the form `rsi(14) > 55` joined by `and` pass, 64 are refused. A hand-written condition does not come near; the limit protects everything that walks the tree (printer, binder, compiler).
4. ⚠ *Needs the owner's confirmation* (the spec was silent). **The calendar and context features are a clock of their own in the lag rule.** They are read on 1m whatever the strategy timeframe, but they change once a *date*, and their previous value is the previous date, never the previous minute. So a lagged call (`crosses_*`, `rising`, `falling`) may read them among themselves and against constants (`crosses_above(vix, 20)`, `rising(vix, 2)`, `rising(vix - index_trend, 2)`; `regime() == 3` has no lag) and never together with a bar feature, on any strategy timeframe, 1m included, and also for `tf("1m")` bar features: `crosses_above(close, vix)` is `mixed_timeframes_in_lag` on 1m, 15m, 1D and the rest, and the message gives `close > vix and close[1] <= vix`, which lags the bar only. The alternative, letting a context feature mix with any timeframe, would make "the previous value" mean a previous date for one side and a previous bar for the other. *History:* the first build counted these features as timeframe 1m, which let the mix through on a 1m strategy (and `tf("1m")` on any); a review showed that `crosses_above(close, vix)` is then `close > vix and close[1] <= vix[1]`, where `vix[1]` stays the previous *date's* value all day: with the VIX at 50 yesterday and 30 today and the close at 25 yesterday and 40 all today, the "crossing" holds at every minute of today, while the explicit form holds at the first minute only (`HandExamplesTests` pins both). The rule was tightened. On a 1D strategy the previous date and the previous bar coincide, but the refusal is kept there too, so that one rule holds on every timeframe; the explicit form is available.
5. **The result types.** As the spec foresaw ("if it cannot carry a list of problems, a `CompileResult` record is used"): `ParseResult(Tree, Problems)` and `CompileResult(Condition, Problems)`; on success `Problems` holds the warnings and the information notes. `ConditionCompiler` is a static class (the spec sketched a sealed class with a static method). The plan of one condition is `CompiledCondition` and the merged plan `CompiledPlan`, as in the contract sketch.
6. **Additions to the contract.** `CompileContext` has a constructor that takes only the evaluation timeframe (no strategy parameters; there is no constructor without arguments); `CompiledCondition.EvaluationTimeframe` and `BoundCondition.Condition`; `CompiledPlan` has `Conditions` and `BindAll(frame)`; `Bind` also throws an `ArgumentException` when the frame keeps less history than the condition needs (otherwise `At` would throw later, inside `Evaluate`); the delegate is built at the **first `Bind`**, not by `Compile`; the delegate is `Decision(FeatureFrame, int[] handles) → int` (not a `Func<FeatureFrame, sbyte>` with the handles baked in), so one delegate serves every frame; `Evaluate` does not check the layout of the frame it is given (see *Consequences*). `CompiledCondition.Tree` is internal.
7. ⚠ *Needs the owner's confirmation.* **The parser is a hybrid, recovery is best effort after the first problem, and at most 100 problems are listed.** See *Consequences from the build*. *Approved (scope item 4): "all problems found are reported, not only the first".* Built: it holds within a stage — the syntax stage stops before binding, so a source with a syntax problem and a name problem reports the syntax problem only — and for 100 syntax problems: a review showed that every repair parses the whole text again, so a text of 4,000 characters made of nothing but faults cost up to 0.7 s and 1.5 GB; the first 100 problems are listed in source order and one `strategy.syntax.too_many_problems` covers the rest of the text. The binder and the lint report all their problems, uncapped (their number is bounded by the size of the tree).
8. **Grammar details the spec left open or contradicted.** Identifiers accept upper-case letters (the spec's `[a-z_]…` contradicts the constants `MON … SUN`; the binder refuses `RSI` as `unknown_name` with the suggestion `rsi`); a subscript parses with an optional minus (`close[0]`, `close[-1]`, `close[300]` parse and the lint decides), `close[1.5]` and `close[1e3]` are `bad_subscript`, a subscript beyond the range of an integer `number_out_of_range`; a subscript on anything but a reference is the syntax problem `subscript_on_non_reference` (the spec's criterion 1 asks for a syntax problem on a subscript on a call), so `lag_on_non_feature` remains for a parameter, a constant and `time`; only a lower-case `e` is an exponent; strings have no escapes; `tf` bare is an ordinary reference that the binder rejects.
9. **Type rules the spec contradicted.** The spec's *Types* said `==`/`!=` take `time` with a time string and ordering takes numbers, while its own main example is `time >= "09:45"`: `time` accepts all six comparisons with a text on either side. `regime()` accepts `==` and `!=` only (against a name or a constant integer 1 to 9; `regime() < 3` is `type_mismatch`). A feature with parameters written bare (`rsi`) and a function written bare (`abs`, `regime`) are `bad_arguments`; a parameterless feature takes both `vwap` and `vwap()`. The type errors of `and`/`or`/`not`, of arithmetic and of the builtins name the operand that is wrong, not both operands (Amendment 18).
10. **The lag-rule boundaries.** The spec's `negative_or_zero_lag` ("not an integer in 1 … 255") overlaps `lag_too_deep` ("the history exceeds 256"): `negative_or_zero_lag` is a count or subscript below 1 or not whole, `lag_too_deep` is 256 and above, and lags add up (`rising(close[200], 100)`). `mixed_timeframes_in_lag` applies to `crosses_*`, `rising` and `falling`; the spec's "`x[k]` of an expression" does not exist because a subscript is only read after a reference.
11. **Added and extended lint.** `strategy.lint.shadowed_name` (warning) is new: the spec's test plan asks for "a warning" when a parameter shadows a feature and the table had no code. `constant_condition` also flags a data-free *part* of a condition (the largest one), and counts parameters as constants.
12. **Arithmetic and constants** as in *Consequences from the build* (NaN, ±infinity, `inf − inf`, atomic lagged builtins, a non-finite parameter is `type_mismatch` where it is used).
13. **`time`** is unknown before the first accepted bar, is read with the domain's `SessionClock` and wraps at midnight (see above).
14. ⚠ *Needs the owner's confirmation.* **Test shape; the negative control of criterion 10 is not the approved one.** *Approved (criterion 10): a negative control through "a deliberately wrong frame wrapper (reads the first bar after the step; reads the forming bar of a higher timeframe)" that is reported as a violation.* The oracle is model-based and also covers the binder; it compares after every step on all nine timeframes (the spec: every closed bar, 1m to 1W); the negative controls of criterion 10 are three feeders (`NextBarFeeder`, `FormingBarFeeder`, and `SameDayContextFeeder` for the context, which the property also cuts), not a wrapper of the frame, and the approved control "reads the forming bar of a higher timeframe" is modelled as running the frame on to the bar's close, because a truncation property cannot see a read of the forming bar that uses only past minutes (that is guarded where the bar is made, by the frame's own tests); the limits of the property are written under *Why a condition cannot look ahead*: it guards the feed and the frame, not the compiler, which is guarded by the oracle, the hand examples, the audit of the expression trees (`CompiledTreeTests`) and the architecture test on the members of the frame (both added after review).
15. **The prerequisites** (scope item 0) differ from the sketch in four details, none of which changes behaviour: the internal `IndicatorInfo.DateFeature` property is gone (its only user was `FeatureFrame.Create`, and the name clashes with the type inside `IndicatorInfo`); the two requirement checks in `Create` are independent `if`s instead of `if … else if` (the result is the same for the catalog, no feature has both flags); the programmer-error messages of two `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`s read "Not a known date feature." instead of "…context feature."; `DateFeature.ReadsContext(kind)` stays as the internal helper the catalog uses to set the flag (the tests pin the flags independently of it). The existing tests changed only by the rename (`FrameContextFeatureTests` → `FrameDateFeatureTests`, one line of `FrameContextCreateTests`, the benchmark label and one line of `AllocationTests`).
16. **Architecture tests:** 21 new (111 → 132) instead of "the new project is covered": the anonymous types the compiler makes for LINQ query syntax are excluded from the forbidden-namespace check (they carry `System.Diagnostics.DebuggerDisplay`), the Superpower-leak rule inspects public signatures, not method bodies, and exactly three unsealed classes are allowed (`SyntaxNode`, `Expression`, `Argument`); two of the 21 pin the members of `FeatureFrame` the library may reference, with a sentinel that feeds a frame to show the rule can fail.
17. **The older ADRs name the old types.** [ADR 0033](0033-weekly-monthly-timeframes-calendar-features-and-the-market-context.md) and the spec P3-01c say `ContextFeature` / `ContextFeatureKind`; they are history and keep the names, with a note pointing here.
18. ⚠ *Needs the owner's confirmation.* **Two claims of the approved text are corrected, because the code does not make them true.** (a) *Approved (Goal): "decides in constant time".* Built: a condition takes the same time on every bar and whatever the history, except `rising`/`falling(x, n)`, which compile to a loop of n steps (n up to 255). A scratch probe on a Release build (Apple-silicon Mac, a series that rises all the way so that the run never breaks early, 2 million evaluations each) gave 14.5 ns for `rising(close, 3)`, 117 ns for `rising(close, 50)`, 452 ns for `rising(close, 255)` and 950 ns for `rising(rsi(14) + ema(20), 255)` per `Evaluate`, against 2.8 ns for `close > ema(20)`: one long run costs more than the whole ten-condition set of the 0.3 µs target, whose longest runs are `rising(close, 3)` and `falling(roc(5), 2)`; no benchmark measures a long run. (b) *Approved (Types): "Anything else is a type error naming both sides".* Built: a comparison names both sides; `and`/`or`/`not`, `+ - * /`, the unary minus and the arguments of a builtin name the operand that is wrong, the type the place needs and the type it has (`close + true > 1`: "The right side of `+` needs a number, but `true` is a true/false value"); the code, the span and the suggestion are unchanged.
19. **Lagged reads are by position, not by session; `lag_across_session` (warning) is new** (the approved text was silent). Found by the final review: `crosses_above(close, vwap)` compares the first bar of a session with the last bar of the one before (see *Lagged reads are by position, not by session* above). Built: `x[k]` keeps its approved meaning (k closed bars of its own timeframe ago, in whatever session that bar is), `IndicatorInfo.HasValueAtSessionOpen(spec)` is new in the catalog (true for `vwap`, `today_open/high/low`, `prev_day_*`, `gap_pct` and an opening range of a single bar), and the lint warns, once for each call or lone subscript, when a strategy on an intraday timeframe lags such a feature before the read stays inside the session, with the `time` check that clears it; the check is recognised in the same condition (a lower bound of `time` in the top-level chain of `and`). Not marked ⚠: no text the owner approved is changed or refused, and a warning does not stop a compile (as in Amendment 11). **The stronger rule was not built:** make a lagged read of such a feature *unknown* across a session reset. It needs the frame to know the session of every history slot (a new member of `FeatureFrame`, which the architecture test of the compiled library lists by name), changes "k closed bars ago" for these features and the oracle with it, and needs a rule of its own for `prev_day_*` (whose lag across the open is a legitimate "yesterday against the day before"). It is open in the roadmap issues log as ISS-13; the owner may ask for it.
