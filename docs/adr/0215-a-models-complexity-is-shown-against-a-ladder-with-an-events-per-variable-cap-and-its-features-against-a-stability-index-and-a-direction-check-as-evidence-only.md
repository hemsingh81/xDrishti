# 0215 — A model's complexity is shown against a ladder with an events-per-variable cap, and its features against a stability index and a direction check, as evidence only

- **Status:** Proposed (built in CF-10 part 10b from the owner-Approved spec, 2026-11-03). Becomes Accepted when the owner has read it. **Safety slice: no** for this part (the figures are printed in the validation report and no verdict, eligibility rule, champion rule or trainer reads them; no switch, no rollback writer, no order). The safety parts of CF-10 (the influence predicate, champion / challenger, rollback) are 10c and 10d and are not built.
- **Date:** 2026-11-05.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** extends the validation report of [0067](0067-validation-harness-and-baselines-purged-folds-and-an-honest-eligibility-rule.md) and the explanations of [0068](0068-the-ml-net-trainer-adapter-pav-calibration-on-a-held-back-slice-and-honest-explanations.md); follows [0214](0214-a-models-calibration-is-chosen-by-sample-size-platt-below-500-calibration-rows-isotonic-from-500-and-the-error-is-reported-with-a-day-cluster-bootstrap-interval.md) (10a) and the programme rule of CF-00: a new figure is report-only.

## Context
The trainer fits trees on every feature of the catalog (41) with a few hundred trades behind them, and the harness judged the result on its score alone. Spec CF-10 D2 and D3 ask that a model be no more complex than its data justify (a ladder of rungs and an events-per-variable cap) and that its features hold from fold to fold (a stability index and a direction check).

## Decision
1. **Events are the smaller class (`EventsPerVariable`, pure, Domain).** `Cap = min(floor(events / EPV), 8)`, EPV 15 (proposal; the literature range is 10 to 20, Peduzzi et al. 1996, cited from memory and not fetched: UNVERIFIED). Hand tables in the unit tests: 120 trades at 40 % wins are 48 events, a cap of 3 at EPV 15 and 4 at 10 (the research note counted trades and said 8); 200 trades at 50 % are 100 events, a cap of 6; the cap never exceeds 8.
2. **The ladder (`ComplexityLadder`, `LadderRung`, `LadderAssessment`).** Rungs: base rate, shrunk cell rate, L2 logistic (at most the cap), trees (at least 100 events). A rung above the base rate is earned only with a probability of at least 0.95 of beating the rung below. That probability is **an input** (the paired day-cluster bootstrap that computes it is part 10c): a missing or non-finite one refuses the rung with the reason, so **an unavailable figure never counts as a pass**. The harness has the figure for no rung yet, so today its report says "not earned" for the logistic baseline and the candidate and gives every reason (the missing probability, the cap, the events). **The candidate also carries a structural reason**: the spec's trees rung is gradient-boosted trees with monotone constraints and depth at most 3, and the trainer has 8 leaves per tree and no monotone constraint, so the candidate is labelled rung 4 (trees) but can never read "earned" until the trainer meets the rung (`ComplexityLadder.Assess(..., structuralReason)`, unit-tested). When the trainer is changed to meet the rung, that reason goes in the same change. Units: 0.9258 is refused with the reason, 0.95 earns, 99 events refuse trees, 5 features on 48 events refuse for EPV.
3. **Population stability index (`Psi`).** `sum((a - e) x ln(a / e))` over equal-mass bins of the training distribution, with a share of zero lifted to 0.0001; 0.10 to 0.25 is "watch" and above 0.25 "alarm" (standard cuts, proposals). Hand check `e = (0.5, 0.5)`, `a = (0.7, 0.3)` gives 0.1695. Bins are **upper-inclusive**: a value goes up a bin when it is above the edge, and an edge is a training quantile (the value with `k / bins` of the rows at or below it) that lies below the largest value. A feature with no more distinct values than bins (a flag, a sign) gets one bin per value, so it is read **whichever value is the minority** (80 zeros and 20 ones is computed; a jump of the ones from 20 % to 50 % reads alarm, 0.416). The harness reads each fold's test rows against that fold's train rows and shows the worst fold per feature. Only a feature that never changes in training, or a side with fewer than `MinRows` finite values, is **not computed**, printed as such and never as 0. (A first version used lower-inclusive bins and edges above the smallest value, which lost every two-valued feature whose larger value was the minority; found in review.)
4. **Deviation: fewer bins on small samples (`Psi.MinRowsPerBin` = 20).** The index of two samples from one and the same distribution is about `(bins - 1) x (1/n_train + 1/n_recent)`; with 10 bins and a 90-row quarterly test window that is near 0.1, and a run over eight folds crossed 0.25 on pure noise in the first test. The effective number of bins is therefore the setting capped by what the smaller side fills with 20 rows each (at least 2). The spec says only "equal-mass bins"; the standard cuts were set for large samples. Unit test: 40 draws of 90 rows against 600 stay under 0.25.
5. **Direction of effect and its stability (`FeatureStability`).** In a fold the direction of a feature is the sign of the correlation, over the fold's test rows, between its value and its contribution to the model's margin (`IExplainableScorer.Explain`, the path-based attributions of 0068); no direction when fewer than `MinRows` rows, or either side does not vary. Across folds the flips are the folds on the minority side; a feature whose direction flips in more than one third of the folds that give one **would be dropped** (6 folds: 3 flips drop it, 2 keep it); fewer than two readable folds is `Unavailable`, never stable. **Interpretation, stated:** the spec says "contribution sign flips" and "permutation importance"; no permutation importance exists in the code, the contributions exist, and the mean signed contribution of a feature is near zero for any feature, so the sign of the value-to-contribution correlation is taken as its direction. A feature whose effect is not monotone (the day of the week) can flip without the model being wrong; the report says "would be dropped", not "bad". The features are not dropped: the trainer is unchanged.
6. **Where it shows.** `ValidationOptions.Ladder` (null: nothing) makes `WalkForwardValidator.Compare` collect the evidence in the fold loop it already runs (the contributions of each fold's test rows for a candidate that can explain, the index of every feature) and attach a `ComplexityReport` to the `ValidationReport`; `ToText` prints it before the limits. The events are those of the largest training set (the last used fold's, standing for the final model). `TrainModelHandler` passes the host setting, so the stored report of a model carries the block, with the catalog names of the features when the rows are the catalog's. **It reports only:** the verdict, `EligibilityRule`, `ChampionRule` and the trainer do not read it (architecture test), and the parameter text that keys the model version does not include it.
7. **Settings.** `Confidence:Ladder`: `Mode` (Off | ReportOnly, default ReportOnly; Gate is refused because nothing here enforces), `Epv` 15 (5..50), `MaxLogisticFeatures` 8 (1..50), `MinTreeEvents` 100 (10..100000), `MinProbability` 0.95 (0.50..0.999), `PsiWatch` 0.10 and `PsiAlarm` 0.25 (0 < watch < alarm <= 5), `PsiBins` 10 (2..100), `MinRows` 30 (5..100000), `MaxFlipShare` one third (above 0, below 0.5). Bound and validated at start; the binding and each refusal are tested. As in CF-01 to CF-10a the "versioned settings home" is the configuration section; a change takes a restart. The spec's `ml.ladder.*` database keys are not built.
8. **Isolation.** No type of Planning, Selection or Autonomy (Domain or Application) names a ladder type, and no verdict type does (architecture tests with a control that the rule sees the dependency of the validator); the calculators are pure (no clock, no I/O, no system random).

## Changed behaviour
A model trained from the host now has a `complexity (evidence only ...)` block in its stored validation report, so the report text, and with it the registration key (`VersionKey` hashes the report text), differs from the same rows before this change: a rerun of an older training counts as a new registration rather than deduplicating. Verdicts, hashes of the model file, and trainer behaviour are unchanged. Harness callers that do not set `ValidationOptions.Ladder` (the extension comparisons, the counterfactual, the meta-policy, the entry windows) get the report they always got. Cost: one `Explain` per test row per fold for a candidate that can explain, and a sort per feature and fold.

## What is not built (left for 10c, 10d)
The paired day-cluster bootstrap that gives the probability (10c; the ladder therefore reads every rung above the base rate as unavailable), its fold sign test (spec AC6, shown beside the bootstrap), uniqueness weights, `MlMayInfluence` and `IMlInfluenceGate` with their enforcement test (10c); champion / challenger, rollback, the regime rule (10d). The migration `AddMlSafety` and the `calibration_kind` column are **not** added: 10b stores nothing (the evidence is in the stored report text); they belong to the part that needs a table. A rung is not enforced: the trainer still fits all features, so a model that the ladder calls "not earned" can still be registered and (as before) cannot influence anything, because nothing reads a model score for a decision. Making the trainer use the ladder (fewer features, a rung choice) would change what it accepts and is a later, explicit step behind a switch.

## Limits
- The cuts (EPV 15, 8 features, 100 events, 0.95, PSI 0.10 and 0.25, one third, 20 rows per bin) are proposals; the PSI cuts were set for large samples and a few folds of a few dozen rows read noisily even with fewer bins.
- The logistic baseline of the harness uses every feature with a ridge penalty, so the ladder calls it over the cap on any history of today's size; that is the honest reading of the rule and is not a defect in the baseline's comparison role.
- The direction check is a correlation: it misses non-monotone effects and interactions, and with few test rows a weak feature flips by chance (which is the reason to distrust it).

- The index is noisy on small samples even with coarser bins: at 90 rows and 4 bins about a quarter of the features can reach "watch" on noise alone, and coarser bins also damp real shifts (a clear shift, every test row in the top half of the training distribution, still reads alarm at 90 rows, tested). Read "watch" as a prompt to look, not as a finding.
- The report prints the settings that produced it (`LadderOptions.KeyText`) on the first line of the block, and the bins as "up to N, at least 20 rows per side for each bin".
