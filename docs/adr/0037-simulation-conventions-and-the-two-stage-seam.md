# 0037 — Simulation conventions: how an entry is filled, how a trade leaves, and the two-stage seam

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-10-18: "yes to all recommendations" on [P3-03](../specs/P3-03-simulator-walking-skeleton-scan-walk-and-orb.md), open question 1, letters a to h as recommended; letter i, the bound of five minutes on the fill delay, was decided in the spec on research and the owner may revert it). **Decisions D3, D4, D5 and D7 are the first stage of a convention that [P3-03b](../specs/P3-03b-fill-realism-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-minute-order.md) changes (the entry becomes a limit order, `R` becomes `RExact`, the schema becomes 2): it does so with a new ADR that supersedes those four decisions and is linked both ways with this one, not with an amendment.** P3-03a (Amendment 1, 2026-10-18) and P3-04 add dated amendments to this ADR (new decisions; nothing is superseded).
- **Superseded in part, 2026-10-18:** decisions D3 (entry), D4 (exit), D5 (R, MAE, MFE) and D7 (what a trade says it is, the `SimTrade` schema) are superseded by [ADR 0038](0038-fill-realism-the-entry-limit-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-the-order-inside-a-minute.md) (P3-03b, `sim-2`); the text of this ADR is left as it was decided. D1, D6 (extended by 0038), D8 and D9 stand.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** builds on [ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md) (the frame that the scanner of P3-03a will drive; the walker does not use it) and on [ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md) (three-valued conditions; the trace of a decision). `Side` comes from the strategy model of ADR 0036 (P3-02b, written by that slice; not linked here because its file is added by the other change).
- **Date:** 2026-10-18 (built the same day: see *Consequences from the build*); Amendment 1 (the entry scanner, the `Simulator` and ORB v1, P3-03a) the same day, at the end; Amendment 2 (the window-independent replay and the run record, P3-04) the same day, after it

## In short
- **Two stages.** An *entry candidate* (where, when and on which side a strategy decided to enter, with its stop) is made first by the **entry scanner** (P3-03a); the **trade walker** (this slice) turns a candidate and the 1-minute bars after it into a simulated trade or the reason there is none. The candidate holds **no exit**, so the same entries can be replayed under every exit policy (P4).
- **The fill.** The first minute after the decision, at its **open**, with no entry limit and no slippage (P3-03b adds both), bounded to start at most **5 minutes** after the decision instant.
- **The exit.** Stop first, touching counts, a gap through the stop fills at the minute's **open**, the target fills at the target, the always-on square-off leaves at its minute's open, and the end of the data leaves at the last close and is **reported apart**.
- **The honesty.** Every trade says `Fills = Exact` and `Costs = None`; counters make a rule that never fires visible; a closed trade cannot depend on the minutes after it.
- **Placement.** The value types that a stored record holds (`SimTrade`, `ExitReason`, `FillBasis`, `CostBasis`, `NoTradeReason`, `WalkCounters`, `Side`) live in **`XDrishti.Domain.Research`**, because the run record of P3-04 holds them and a Domain type cannot name a type of `XDrishti.Simulation`. The walker, the candidate and `SimulationEngine` live in the new pure library **`XDrishti.Simulation`** (Domain only, no package).

## Context
The simulator is the second stage of the plan of [doc 07](../design/07-strategies.md) §2, "conditional trade plans": a ticket has a trigger, an entry, a stop and an exit, and history is replayed against it. Three risks of [doc 19](../design/19-risks-and-compliance.md) §1 decide most of the rules: look-ahead bias (a decision must not use a bar that had not closed), intrabar ambiguity (a 1-minute bar that holds the stop and the target does not say which came first) and unrealistic fills (the price of a trigger-bar close is a price no order could get). The first draft of P3-03 held the scanner and the walker (about 3 agent-days) and was split on review: the walker needs no compiler and no frame, and it is where the conventions are, so it comes first and is checked by hand-worked numbers before anything is built on it.

## Decisions
1. **Two stages that share one small value (the seam).** `EntryCandidate` carries the strategy id, the instrument, the side, the decision (the list index of the minute that closed the evaluation bar and the decision instant), the trigger bar (with the number of minutes it holds and whether it is complete), the stop level and the decision-time trace. It has **no exit policy and no exit field**, and the walker has no frame. The scanner (P3-03a) and the walker therefore share nothing else, and P4 can walk one set of candidates under every exit policy. A test reads the members of the type to keep it so.

2. **D3 — Entry.** The candidate's own minute is `bars[DecisionBarIndex]`; it starts at `DecisionEpochSeconds − 60`. The *fill minute* is `bars[DecisionBarIndex + 1]`. In this order: no such minute, one on another India date than the candidate's minute, or one that starts more than `MaxFillDelayMinutes` (5) after the decision instant is no trade, `NoFillBar`; one that starts at or after the square-off is `AfterSquareOff`. **Entry price = the open of the fill minute** (the bar that triggered has closed; an order cannot fill in it). The stop is used as given; risk per unit `r = |entry − stop|`. A BUY needs `entry > stop`, a SELL `entry < stop`: **a fill at or through the stop is no trade**, `EntryThroughStop`, counted. Target = entry ± `rr` × `r` when a reward-risk is given, otherwise none. The bound of five minutes exists because a market-type fill at the open of a minute far from the signal is a price unrelated to it, and the replay of P3-04 runs sessions with up to 37 missing minutes; it is an engineering bound, not a trading choice. A fill minute that starts later than the decision instant means that minutes are missing and the order rested through them; such a *trade* is counted in `EntriesAfterMissingMinutes` (a no-trade is not).

3. **D4 — Exit.** From the fill minute on, minute by minute, while the minute is on the entry's India date: (1) a minute that starts at or after the square-off minute exits `SquareOff` at its **open** and its range is not looked at; (2) a touched stop (BUY `low ≤ stop`, SELL `high ≥ stop`; touching counts) exits `Stop` at the stop level, or at the minute's **open when the market gapped through the stop** (BUY `min(stop, open)`, SELL `max(stop, open)`); **stop first: a minute that holds the stop and the target is a stop**; (3) else a touched target exits `Target` at the target level, with no price improvement when the minute opened beyond it; (4) else the minute is counted, and with no later minute on the entry date the position exits `EndOfData` at the **close** of the last minute. `EndOfData` is data, not a rule: it is counted apart and reported apart, never among the trades that statistics count. The minutes of the next date are never read (only their start, to see that the date changed). Stop checks start in the fill minute itself. `ExitEpochSeconds` is the start of the exit minute in every case.

4. **D5 — R, MAE, MFE.** `R = (exit − entry) / r` for a BUY, `(entry − exit) / r` for a SELL. Excursions are measured from the entry price over the minutes from the fill minute to the exit minute, in R of `r`, never below 0. The exit price is itself a visited price, so `−MAE ≤ R ≤ MFE` always. The path inside the exit minute is unknown and stop first is assumed, so that minute counts as follows. After a *Stop*: its adverse side is the exit's own distance (1.0 exactly at the stop level, more after a gap) and its favourable side is not counted, not even its open. After a *Target*: its favourable side is the target distance (MFE reaches `rr` exactly, whatever the minute's high) and its adverse side counts in full (only below the entry: a minute that gapped beyond the target is wholly favourable). After a *SquareOff*: the minute's range is not counted, its open is. After an *EndOfData*: it counts in full.

5. **D6 — Counters.** `WalkCounters` (summed by `Add`; `WalkOutcome.ToCounters()` gives those of one walk): candidates, trades, the three no-trade reasons, the four exit counts, `StopGapExits`, `TargetGapExits`, `EntriesAfterMissingMinutes` and `BarsExamined` (the listed minutes from the fill minute to the exit minute, both included; 0 for `NoFillBar` and `AfterSquareOff`, 1 for `EntryThroughStop`). Identities: `Candidates = Trades + NoFillBar + AfterSquareOff + EntryThroughStop`, and `Trades` is the sum of the four exit counts. A rule that never fires gives zero trades and zero losses and looks safe; these counters are how it is seen.

6. **D7 — What a trade says it is.** Every `SimTrade` carries `Fills = Exact` and `Costs = None`. *Exact* means the model's price with no slippage, no tick rounding and no entry limit. A figure from this engine is a signal-level result on invented data and says nothing about edge. The run header with its five labels (`fills: exact`, `entry limit: not applied`, `costs: none`, `portfolio limits: not simulated`, `sample: in-sample`) is P3-03a's; P3-03b replaces the first two.

7. **The trade record is a stored contract: `SimTrade` v1, 24 fields.** The fields and their order are in the spec (*Contract*) and pinned by a test that reads the constructor by reflection; `Key` (`StrategyId|Symbol|Side|TradeDate`, computed) is not one of the 24. Changing any field, its order or its type, or any enum member, fails that test until `SimTrade.CurrentSchemaVersion` is raised and this ADR is amended. Enum members are stored by name and never renamed. `EntrySnapshotJson` is reserved for the decision-time snapshot of [doc 09](../design/09-learning-engine.md) §3 step 3 and is always null in schema 1. P3-03b raises the schema to 2 (`R` becomes `RExact`; `RFilled`, `SlippageR` and the fill flags are added; `FillBasis` gains `Modelled`); P3-03c adds the net R and `CostBasis.Charged`.

8. **Placement.** `XDrishti.Domain.Research` holds `Side` (shared with P3-02b: whichever slice merges first adds it, byte for byte the same declaration), `SimTrade`, `ExitReason`, `FillBasis`, `CostBasis`, `NoTradeReason` and `WalkCounters` (with pure sums). `Side` is **not** `XDrishti.Domain.Portfolio.TradeSide`: that enum is a stored ledger fact with its own rule, a research side has its own schema versions, and a trade that meets a ledger row is mapped by name. `XDrishti.Simulation` (public namespaces `XDrishti.Simulation` and `XDrishti.Simulation.Walking`) holds `EntryCandidate` and its trace records, `InstrumentRef`, `SimulationEngine`, `WalkRules`, `WalkOutcome` and `TradeWalker`. P3-03a adds `.Scanning` and `.Running` and the references to `Indicators` and `Strategies`.

9. **`SimulationEngine.Version` = `"sim-1"`.** A constant bumped by any change of D3 to D5, of the `SimTrade` schema or of a replay rule of P3-04 (which stores it in the run record). The hand-worked fixtures are rendered to a golden file that starts with the version it was made with; a test fails when the rendered text differs from the file, so a changed rule needs a new version and a regenerated file, on purpose (`XD_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`).

## How the walker is built (decided in the build)
- **A static pure function.** `TradeWalker.Walk(candidate, bars, rules)` returns `Result<WalkOutcome>`. The failures that are not an outcome are data: `simulation.rules_invalid` (a stop that is not a finite number; a reward-risk that is not a finite number above 0), `simulation.candidate_not_in_bars` (the index is outside the list or the minute there does not start at `DecisionEpochSeconds − 60`) and `simulation.bars_not_ascending` (two equal epochs or a descending pair among the minutes the walk visits, including the fill minute against the candidate's own). The walker checks only the minutes it visits and does not scan the whole list for every candidate (a long list holds many candidates); the whole-list check is made once by `Simulator.Run` (P3-03a). The checks run in the order above, before anything is looked up.
- **Nothing per minute is allocated.** The loop reads a bar, compares and moves on; the result objects are allocated once. A test shows that positions that live 11 and 331 minutes allocate the same number of bytes.
- **One loop, one minute at a time, and it stops at the exit.** The only reads beyond the exit minute are of the *start* of the next minute (to see that it is on another date), so the look-ahead property of the tests can replace everything after the exit by extreme values and the result does not move.

## Consequences from the build
- **Tests.** `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests` holds the schema test, the 17 fixtures of the spec (F1 to F17b) and the walker table (W1 to W14), the SELL mirror of the BUY rows, the seam (the same F1 candidate under four exit rules), determinism and the golden file, 100 seeded series for the look-ahead property and the fill-timing property, the allocation test and an explicit benchmark; `SimulationArchitectureTests` (in `XDrishti.Architecture.Tests`) pins the references, the forbidden namespaces, the absence of clock, task, `Random` and environment, no mutable static field, sealed classes, the two public namespaces and the one-way dependency between them, with a sentinel for every rule. Exact counts are in doc 03 §4.3.
- **The look-ahead property and its control.** For a random cut `k`, every outcome *settled at* an index at most `k` (the exit minute of a trade; the fill minute of a no-trade, or the candidate's own minute when none follows) is the same when the minutes after `k` are removed and when they are replaced by zero and huge prices and volumes (epochs unchanged). The negative control is a doctored input and not a mutated walker (the walker is a static function): every low becomes the lower of its own and the next minute's and every high the higher of its own and the next one's; the property run on it reports violations on 35 of the 100 seeds, and the honest walker none on the very same inputs. The scanner and `Simulator.Run` half of the property is P3-03a's.
- **Throughput.** Explicit benchmark, Apple M4 Pro, Release, one cold run (the JIT included): 1,500 walks over 250 sessions (93,750 minutes) in about 16 ms, about 21 million visited minutes per second. Reported, not asserted. **Measured with the P3-03 walker (`double` arithmetic); since P3-03b the walker computes in `decimal` and is about four times slower (about 4.6 to 4.9 million visited minutes per second): see [ADR 0038](0038-fill-realism-the-entry-limit-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-the-order-inside-a-minute.md), *Performance*.**

## Open points carried to later slices
- Letter i (the bound of five minutes) is the owner's to revert; the constant is `SimulationEngine.MaxFillDelayMinutes`, and a changed value is a new `Version`.
- The favourable side of a stop minute is not counted, not even its open (D5). A stop minute that opens above the entry therefore does not raise MFE; P3-03b, which orders the prints inside a minute, may refine it in the ADR that supersedes this decision.
- Entry kinds that need an order across bars (`stop_above_high`, `limit_pullback`) are refused by name by P3-03a.

## Alternatives considered
- **One stage (a walker that also scans).** Rejected: the entry would then depend on the exit policy and P4 could not compare policies on identical entries.
- **Fill at the trigger-bar close (doc 07 §2, "Entry: at trigger-candle close").** Rejected: the bar has closed, so no order could get that price; the open of the next minute is the first price an order can get. The limit order at +0.1 % is P3-03b.
- **A gap through the stop fills at the stop.** Rejected: [doc 19](../design/19-risks-and-compliance.md) §1, "gap fills at open".
- **No bound on the fill delay.** Rejected: a fill at the open of a minute far from the signal is a price unrelated to it.
- **The research value types in `XDrishti.Simulation`.** Rejected: the run record of P3-04 is a Domain type and holds the trade, the reason and the counters.

## Amendment 1 — the entry scanner, the `Simulator` and ORB v1 (P3-03a, 2026-10-18)

New decisions; nothing in the decisions above is superseded. **A1 and A2 are D1 and D2 of [P3-03a](../specs/P3-03a-entry-scanner-simulator-and-orb-v1.md), A3 is its D10, A5 its D11 and A7 its D8** (the numbers of the spec are kept so that P3-03b and P3-04 can cite them; the ADR's own decisions 1 to 9 keep theirs). The scanner and the `Simulator` are built.

A1. **A decision is taken at a step where a bar of the evaluation timeframe `E` closed, and only there** (D1; [ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md), decision 15). The decision instant is the end of the minute that closed the bar, its India clock time is the compiled `time`, and `DecisionBarIndex` is the list index of that minute. A step that closes two `E` bars decides the second and counts the first as lost (`LostDecisions`). A step whose last closed `E` bar started on an earlier India date than its own minute (a bucket closed late by the first minute of the next date) is **stale**: counted, not evaluated, because no order may be placed on yesterday's bar. A bucket that closes late inside a date is decided at the minute that closed it and counted in `IncompleteBarsConsumed` (the bound of five minutes of decision 2 limits only the fill that follows). The date of a step is that of its own minute, even for a stale step.

A2. **The order at a decision step** (D2), per side: the invalidation latch (`invalidate` true at this step sets "no first entry for the rest of the date", this step included), the entry window (`first_entry ≤ decision time ≤ last_entry`, both ends included; outside it only `OutOfWindowSteps` and the `invalidate` items are counted), the eligible step (`setup` and `trigger` both true, unknown never true) and its fate, exclusive and in this order: a candidate already exists on the date (`LaterTriggers`), the latch is set (`BlockedByInvalidation`), `invalidate` is unknown (`UnknownInvalidation`), the stop level has no value yet (`StopNotReady`, the date stays open), otherwise a candidate. **At most one candidate per instrument, side and date: the first.** A stop-out does not arm the pattern again; BUY and SELL are independent scanners. *Decided in the spec, the owner may revert:* the candidate check comes before the latch, because an invalidation that turns true after an entry was taken must not turn a later trigger into a "blocked" one.

A3. **Scanner counters** (D10): `ScanCounters` and `ConditionCounter` live in `XDrishti.Domain.Research` next to `WalkCounters`, with pure sums (conditions merged by path; adding the counters of two sides is an `ArgumentException`). They count the bars and the ordering guard (`IgnoredBars`, 0 for a `Run`), the decision steps and what became of them (`StaleBucketSteps`, `OutOfWindowSteps`, `WindowSteps`), `FullyReadySteps` (the window steps at which every feature of every `setup` and `trigger` item has a value at lag 0, whatever the result of the item: a condition made false by logic while a feature is still empty is not ready), the fate of every eligible step, and per condition path (`buy.trigger[0]`) the counts `True`, `False`, `Unknown` and `NotReady` (an unknown in which a feature of the condition has no value at lag 0, told apart from any other unknown). Identities, checked on every fixture: `DecisionSteps = StaleBucketSteps + OutOfWindowSteps + WindowSteps`; the eligible steps add up to `Candidates + LaterTriggers + BlockedByInvalidation + UnknownInvalidation + StopNotReady`; the four counts of a `setup` or `trigger` item add up to `WindowSteps`, those of an `invalidate` item to `DecisionSteps − StaleBucketSteps`.

A4. **What is refused, by name.** `EntryScanner.Create` reads the stops that are a level on the evaluation timeframe with no buffer (`swing_low`, `swing_high`, `bar_low`, `bar_high`, `or_opposite`; the stop is the value of the level feature at the decision step, used as given) and refuses the rest with `simulation.scanner_stop_unsupported` (`fixed_pct`, `atr_mult`, a `buffer_atr` above 0, a stop timeframe other than `E`), and a daily evaluation with `simulation.scanner_timeframe_unsupported`. **`Simulator.Create` is stricter and runs `or_opposite` with no buffer, read on `E`, only.** It compiles every listed side through `StrategyCompiler` and refuses, with a located problem (the path of the field, the span of a model problem, a message that names the kind and the slice that will run it): `simulation.unsupported_entry` (`stop_above_high`, `limit_pullback`), `simulation.unsupported_stop`, `simulation.unsupported_exit` (anything but exactly one `FIXED_RR` with exactly one `rr` value, and any overlay: a silent ignore would make a result look like what it is not), `simulation.unsupported_style` (swing), `simulation.filter_not_available` (any filter name), `simulation.needs_context`, `simulation.needs_expiry_days` and `simulation.scanner_timeframe_unsupported`. The problems of the compile pass on unchanged; **a side that does not compile makes `Create` return no simulator, so a half strategy is never run**; the warnings of the compile are carried when a simulator exists.

A5. **What a result says it is** (D11). The header of a run carries `SimulationEngine.Version`, the `SimTrade` schema version, the strategy id, `Fills = Exact`, `Costs = None` and exactly five labels in this order: `fills: exact`, `entry limit: not applied`, `costs: none`, `portfolio limits: not simulated`, `sample: in-sample`. **The first two are the labels of this stage and are replaced by ADR 0038 (the fill rules of [P3-03b](../specs/P3-03b-fill-realism-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-minute-order.md)); this happened when P3-03b and the `Simulator` were merged: the header now carries the `FillRules` and `Fills = FillRules.Basis`, and its first two labels are `FillDescription.Labels(fills)` (`fills: exact` or `fills: modelled`, then `entry limit: +0.1 %`)**; the other three stay. A figure from this engine is a signal-level result per instrument on invented data and says nothing about edge.

A6. **`Run` and the shape of its result.** `Run(instrument, bars, calendar = null)` (now `Run(instrument, bars, tick, calendar = null)`, see the end of this paragraph) first checks, once and in O(n), that the epochs are strictly ascending (`simulation.bars_not_ascending`, before any step), then scans each listed side with its own frame (created with the calendar, so a special session closes its last buckets at its own last minute) and walks each candidate with `TradeWalker.Walk(candidate, bars, rules)`, where the rules were `new WalkRules(rr, squareOff)` as built in P3-03a (`rr` is the single `FIXED_RR` value of the side and `squareOff` its square-off minute; since P3-03b they are `WalkRules(rr, squareOff, fills, tick)`, see the end of this paragraph). The result (types in `XDrishti.Simulation.Running`, because `WalkOutcome` is `Walking`'s and the root namespace depends on neither stage) holds `Outcomes` (every candidate with its `WalkOutcome`, by decision instant, then Buy before Sell), `Trades` (those whose reason is not `EndOfData`, by entry instant, then Buy before Sell), `OpenAtEnd` (the `EndOfData` trades in the same order), the `ScanCounters` per side (Buy, then Sell) and the `WalkCounters` (the sum of those of the outcomes). A `Simulator` holds only compiled sides and is safe to share; every run makes its own frames. P3-03b added `FillRules? fills = null` to `Create` (null is `FillRules.Default`) and the instrument's `TickSpec` to `Run`, before the calendar, at two marked places (`RulesOf` builds `WalkRules(rr, squareOff, fills, tick)`, `HeaderOf` the header); both are wired, the walker's new counters are summed with the old ones, and `SimulationEngine.Version` stays `sim-2`.

A7. **ORB v1** (D8) is built as `OrbV1.Create(orMinutes = 15, rewardRisk = 2.0)` in `XDrishti.Strategies.BuiltIn`, a C# builder that is the interim source of the built-in ORB until P3-02d seeds it as stored text ([ADR 0002](0002-config-and-strategies-in-app.md)): a bare breakout (the owner's answer to plan question Q2, 2026-10-18), BUY `close > or_high(or_min) and close[1] <= or_high(or_min)`, SELL the mirror written out by hand, stop `or_opposite` with `minutes = or_min`, one `FIXED_RR`, window 09:30 to 11:30, square-off 15:15. The level form is used because `or_high` keeps the history of the session only ([ADR 0035](0035-per-session-features-keep-the-history-of-the-session-only.md)).

## Amendment 2 — the window-independent replay and the run record (P3-04, 2026-10-18)

New decisions; nothing above is superseded. They are decisions of [P3-04](../specs/P3-04-run-from-stored-bars-window-independent-replay.md) (the pure half of the first draft: bars in, record out, no I/O), as amended the same day by [P3-04a](../specs/P3-04a-run-from-stored-bars-over-the-ports.md) (`InstrumentReplayInput` lives in the Domain and carries a preset skip) and with the obligations of [P3-03b](../specs/P3-03b-fill-realism-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-minute-order.md) (the fill rules and the tick). Numbers about the owner's data come from the read-only scan of his 214 CSV files on 2026-10-18. **P3-04a, the use case that reads the bars from the ports and chooses the universe, has its own amendment when it is built.** The replay is built.

R1. **A trade does not depend on the window that was asked for.** `InstrumentReplayer.Replay` is given the raw bars from the instrument's first stored bar to the end of `To` and scans and counts from the **first session that runs**, whatever `From` is; only the **trades** are cut to `[From, To]` (a trade dated before `From` is dropped and counted, `TradesBeforeWindow`). So an `ema(10)` is seeded by the first stored bar and not by the first bar of the window: a different `From` only adds or removes the trades outside it, a different `To` only adds or removes the sessions after it. What can still change a trade is more history before the first stored bar (a backfill), which also changes the hash of the data and so the run key. A list that is not strictly ascending is a caller error (`InvalidOperationException` naming the instrument; the store cannot produce it), and so is a bar dated after `To` (`ArgumentException`).

R2. **Sessions: the data is never repaired, and what is left out is counted** (data notes, part one). Each date with bars is classified with `TradingCalendar.SessionOf` and the bars inside its window `[FromMinute, ToMinute)`: no session on the date is `closed_day`; a session that is not the regular 09:15 to 15:30 is `special_session`; a regular session whose window holds fewer than `MinSessionCompleteness` (default **0.90**) of the 375 minutes is `short_session` (compared in `decimal`, `count < 0.90 × 375`, so exactly the threshold runs); otherwise it runs. A trading day of the calendar between the first and the last bar with no bar is `missing_session`. An excluded session feeds nothing to the frame (a frame is always fed whole sessions, ADR 0035), has a note (date, reason, bars in the window, expected minutes; at most 100, the counts exact) and is read like a closed market: `prev_day_*`, `gap_pct` and the discontinuity check use the previous session that ran. Bars outside the window of a session that runs are dropped and counted (`BarsOutsideSession`); `BarsRead = BarsFed + BarsExcluded`. *Excluded* is on purpose and counted; *ignored* is the ordering guard of the frame (`IgnoredBars`, 0 on stored data). **Decided on research, not asked** (the scan: 108,481 of 109,704 instrument-dates have all 375 bars, 588 have 338 to 374, 635 have fewer than 188 on three dates, none between 188 and 337, so any threshold from 50 % to 90 % gives the same runs today; the owner may revert): 0.90. **The calendar decides the weekend:** the 427 complete Saturday and Sunday dates of the files are `closed_day` unless the calendar has a confirmed special session for them.

R3. **Prices are adjusted on a copy, once.** Each fed bar is multiplied by `AdjustmentFactors.FactorAt(its India date)` (prices `AdjustmentFactors.Price`, volume `AdjustmentFactors.Volume`, 4 decimals); a bar on or after every ex-date is copied as it is. An instrument with no applied action that changes the bars read (`ActionsAfter(date of the first bar)` empty) whose bars are all fed is **not copied**: the raw list is the fed list. The hash of the data covers the raw bars, before any session is dropped and before adjustment. The replay marks an adjustment `OnCsvBars` when `CsvAdjustedThrough` is given and the ex-date is on or before it; the guard against adjusting a CSV-adjusted series twice is the runner's (P3-04a), because the replay cannot tell which bars came from a CSV.

R4. **Discontinuities are flagged, never repaired; a flagged day gets no entry and restarts the indicators** (data notes, part two; open question 3 of the spec, decided by the owner on 2026-10-18 as recommended). On the adjusted series of the sessions that run, a day whose first open differs from the last close of the previous session that ran by more than `DiscontinuityThreshold` (default **0.20**) is flagged with its ratio `open / close`: the test is `abs(open - previousClose) > threshold × previousClose` in `decimal`, so a move of exactly the threshold is not flagged (the `double` form flags a close of 12.00 with an open of 9.60). The first session that runs is never flagged. A flagged day (a) gets no entry: the outcome of a candidate dated that day, a trade or a no-trade, is dropped and counted (`EntriesSuppressed`; the scanner's and the walker's counters still include it), and (b) starts a new **segment**: `Simulator.Run` is called once per segment with fresh frames, so the old price level cannot sit in an average or an ATR for weeks, and the counters of the segments are summed. A strategy that reads a daily average such as `tf("1D").ema(50)` takes no entry for about 50 sessions after the day. **Decided on research** (74 days of the files open more than 10 % from the previous close, 15 more than 15 %, 3 more than 20 %: SIEMENS 2025-04-07, TMPV 2025-10-14, VEDL 2026-04-30; 9 of the 12 between 15 % and 20 % fall on two market-wide dates and are real): 0.20. *Fail closed:* a previous close that is not above 0, or a price that is not finite or larger than 1e15, cannot be compared and is flagged; the threshold is clamped at 1e12 for the `decimal` product.

R5. **One list for the scanner and the walker.** `EntryCandidate.DecisionBarIndex` counts `Step` calls from the scanner's creation, so it is the index in the list only when the scanner steps the whole list from element 0 and the walker reads that same list. `Simulator.Run` does exactly that with the list it is given; the replay therefore builds **one list of fed bars per instrument** (the adjusted copy, windows of the sessions that run only) and gives each segment a **window** onto it (`BarSegment`, no copy; the whole list when there is one segment), so that the scanner and the walker of a call share the list and the index of a candidate is its index in it (test `The_decision_index_is_the_index_in_the_one_list_whatever_is_skipped_or_restarted`).

R6. **The end of the data is data, not a rule.** A trade that the walker closes with `EndOfData` is listed in `OpenAtEnd` and not in `Trades`, so that no statistic counts it by accident. The window cut comes first (a trade dated before `From` is `TradesBeforeWindow` whatever its exit). Where a trade may continue after `To` (swing positions) is P3-03d's decision; the read ends at `To`. A position that spans a flagged boundary is P3-03d's too (no overnight position exists yet, so the field `ExcludedAcrossDiscontinuity` of the first draft is not built).

R7. **Every candidate outcome has exactly one fate**, in this order: dropped by a flagged day (`EntriesSuppressed`); a trade before `From` (`TradesBeforeWindow`); an `EndOfData` trade (`OpenAtEnd`); another trade (`Trades`); a walker no-trade, by reason (`NoFillBar`, `AfterSquareOff`, `EntryThroughStop`, `LimitNotReached`). The identity *sum of the fates = the scanner's candidates (both sides, all segments)* is asserted on every replay of the tests. All counters cover the replay from the first session that ran to the last (`CountersCover`); the trades are cut to the window; each instrument also carries `CandidatesInWindow` (candidates whose decision date is in `[From, To]`, suppressed ones included) and `TradesInWindow` (`Trades + OpenAtEnd`), so that a rule that fires only before `From` is visible (ISS-24: a rule that never fires looks safe; here the reverse). **Skip reasons the replay decides**, in precedence: `no_bars`; the preset skip (`csv_adjusted_twice`, `tick_size_invalid`, decided by the runner: the replay classifies the sessions and hashes the bars and does not scan; an empty list gives `no_bars` whatever the preset says); `no_runnable_sessions` (no session ran, or no session that ran gave a window step); `no_sessions_in_window`; `features_not_ready` (window steps above 0 but `FullyReadySteps` 0 on every side). A skipped instrument that was read keeps its data facts and its counters and has no trades; `instrument_not_found` is the Domain factory `InstrumentRun.NotFound(id)`.

R8. **The hash of the stored bars** is the SHA-256, lower-case hex, of the concatenation, for each raw bar read in order, of six 8-byte little-endian fields: `EpochSeconds`, the 64-bit patterns of `Open`, `High`, `Low` and `Close`, then `Volume` (`BarStreamHash`, in the Domain; the known answers were computed with Python 3 for this layout and recomputed during the build).

R9. **The strategy as the record keeps it: an interim canonical JSON** (until P3-02c defines the canonical text). `StrategyPreparation.Prepare(definition, parameters, evaluationTimeframe, fills?)` calls `Simulator.Create` (the located problems pass through unchanged) and writes the definition with a hand-written writer (no `System.Text.Json`: the bytes are ours and not a framework version's): snake_case member names, `BUY`/`SELL`/`FIXED_RR`/`PARTIAL_TRAIL` as doc 11 spells them, kinds and modes in snake_case, timeframes by code, `HH:mm`, numbers by `CanonicalText` (round-trip form, **negative zero written 0**), only `"`, `\` and control characters escaped, no white space, absent members left out, each condition parsed and printed again so white space and redundant brackets do not change the hash. Two spellings of one feature (`rsi()` and `rsi(14)`) give two hashes, because the text is the version (ADR 0002). `BuiltInStrategies.Build(id, parameters)` turns `ORB` and `or_min`, `rr` into `OrbV1` with the defaults filled in (unknown ids and names are located problems); stored strategies replace it with P3-02c and P3-02d.

R10. **The run record and the run key.** `RunAssembler.Assemble(header, replays)` returns the `SimulationRun` (schema **2**): the totals (the figures of the instruments summed, the instruments by skip reason), `Trades` and `OpenAtEnd` ordered by entry time, then by the instrument's place in the universe, then Buy before Sell, each with the basket ids of the universe (none for a list), the **exceptions of the calendar** to the default week from the earliest first bar read to `To` (found by asking `SessionOf` for each date, because the calendar lists no days: a weekday holiday, a session other than the regular window, any session on a weekend) and the **run key**, the SHA-256 of these UTF-8 lines: `xd-run 2`, `engine=`, `strategy=` (the definition hash), `params=` (effective values, ordinal name order), `timeframe=`, `window=`, `options=`, `ack=`, **`fills=` (the canonical JSON of the rules actually used; the same for none and for `FillRules.Default`)**, `universe=` (the label), one `i=` per instrument (`id|bars hash or -|skip reason or -|ex-date:kind:numerator:denominator,…|tick or -|1 if the tick is the fallback else 0`) and `cal=`. The tick is written by `CanonicalText.Number(decimal)`, so `0.0500` and `0.05` are one tick. The record reads no clock and no random source: equal inputs give an equal record. The names of its public properties are pinned by a test; changing one needs the schema raised.

R11. **Where things live.** The run record, `InstrumentReplay`, `InstrumentReplayInput` (moved here from the replay namespace by P3-04a: its fields are Domain types and the Application port must name it), `RunHeader`, `BarStreamHash`, `RunAssembler` and `CanonicalText` are in `XDrishti.Domain.Research`, next to `SimTrade` and the counters, because Application references the Domain only. The preparation, the JSON writer, `BuiltInStrategies` and `InstrumentReplayer` are in the new public namespace **`XDrishti.Simulation.Replay`**, which may use `Running` and the Strategies library and which the root, `Walking`, `Scanning` and `Running` namespaces may not use. No project reference changed.

R12. **`SimulationEngine.Version` stays `sim-2`.** The replay adds rules and changes none of D3 to D5 or of the rule table of ADR 0038; nothing was recorded under `sim-2` before. From now on a changed replay rule needs a new version, and the golden record of the criterion 1 fixture (`run-record.golden.txt`, first line the version) has a table in its test that maps each version to the SHA-256 of its golden file, so a replay rule changed without a new version, or a new version without a new row, fails.

### Differences from the spec sketch (the names of the real code win)
- `CalendarException` is **`CalendarExceptionDay`** (rule CA1711 forbids a type named `…Exception` that is not one); the property of the record keeps the name `CalendarExceptions`. It is `(Date, IsHoliday, FromMinute, ToMinute)`.
- `RunHeader` gains `string Fills` (the canonical JSON; P3-03b's obligation) and `SimulationRun` gains `Fills`; `InstrumentRun` gains `TickSize` and `TickFromFallback`; `InstrumentReplayInput` gains `Tick` (`TickSpec?`, absent only with the preset `tick_size_invalid`), `PresetSkip` and `SkipDetail`; `RunCounters` needs no new field for the walker counters of P3-03b because they are fields of `WalkCounters`, summed.
- `UniverseRecord` is `(Kind, BasketId, BasketName, Label, Members)` with `UniverseKind { InstrumentList, Basket }` and `UniverseMember(Id, Symbol)`; the labels are constants of `UniverseLabels`. `SkipCount`, `RunFates`, `SessionCounts`, `SessionNote`, `DiscontinuityNote`, `AppliedAdjustment`, `DateSpan` are the small records the spec describes in prose. `CountersCover` is a `DateSpan?` and `ReplayFrom` is kept next to it.
- `RunAssembler.KeyText(header, instruments, exceptions)` is public so that the pre-image can be pinned; `CanonicalText` is a public Domain helper shared by the key, the JSON writer and the tests.
- `PreparedStrategy` also exposes `Warnings` and `Fills`; `BuiltInStrategies.Build` returns a `BuiltInBuild(Definition?, Parameters, Problems)` and its two refusal codes are `ReplayCodes.UnknownStrategy` and `ReplayCodes.UnknownParameter` (`simulation.unknown_strategy`, `simulation.unknown_parameter`).
- *The scanner and the walker share the list of a call, not one list per instrument:* see R5; `InstrumentReplayer` has an internal overload with an observer that the test of the decision index uses.
- `InstrumentReplayer` reads the `WalkOutcome` that `Running` hands it (a type of `Walking`), but never calls the walker; the architecture rule says so (the spec's rule list did not name `Walking`).
- For a skipped instrument that was not scanned (a preset, no runnable session) `BarsFed` is the bars of the sessions that run, which is what would have been fed, so that `BarsRead = BarsFed + BarsExcluded` holds for every instrument; its `Scans` list is empty.
- `no_bars` keeps the hash of the empty list (`e3b0c442…`); an instrument that has no row has no hash.

### Consequences from the build (Amendment 2)
- **Tests.** `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests` runs 590 tests and 4 explicit benchmarks (the replay adds 131 tests and one benchmark: the session table of 18 rows, the discontinuity table of 14 rows, window independence with its control, end of data, the restart (18 and 9 not-ready steps), the split, adjustment, the skip reasons A to G and the presets, the record against an independent frame and scanner, the definition JSON and its golden text under a second culture, the factory, assembly, the golden record and its table, the 100-seed look-ahead property, memory); `XDrishti.Domain.Tests` (1,205 in all) adds the hash (four known answers, every chunk boundary), the assembler (the pre-image pinned and hashed with Python 3, 18 changes of the key, order, totals, calendar exceptions) and the snapshot of the property names; `SimulationArchitectureTests` (168 architecture tests in all) gain the `Replay` namespace (documented, not used by the other namespaces, no `System.Text.Json` with a sentinel, the writer internal) and `DomainResearchSurfaceTests` the new types and a no-I/O rule.
- **The look-ahead property** (100 seeds of 10 sessions, a cut at the end of session 3 to 9): the trades, flagged days and notes of the prefix are equal when the series ends at the cut, goes on, and has extreme bars after the cut; the compared prefixes hold **457 trades, 72 flagged days, 66 excluded sessions, and 55 of the cuts lie after a flagged day** (the bars required were 250, 40, 40 and 40); the control with extreme bars in session 1 differs in 100 of 100 seeds.
- **Memory (criterion 16; the bound of 5 times the raw list was UNVERIFIED in the spec).** A synthetic instrument of 190,125 bars (9,126,000 bytes as a list of 48-byte bars): one replay allocates **1,107,064 bytes (0.12 times the raw list)** when nothing is copied and **10,117,560 bytes (1.11 times)** when an applied action makes the replay adjust a copy. The bound of 5 holds with a wide margin; the one list of fed bars is the only large allocation.
- **Throughput.** Explicit benchmark `Replay_phases_for_synthetic_instruments`, this machine (macOS arm64, Release), 20 invented instruments of 507 sessions: per instrument the hash takes about 5 ms, the adjustment of a copy about 3 ms and the whole replay about 34 ms with about 1.0 MB allocated, so the replay of the owner's 214 instruments of about 190,000 minutes is about 7 s of one core (the estimate of the spec was 15 s; UNVERIFIED on the owner's data until P3-04b measures it, and without the read, which is P3-04a's). The peak working set of the test process was about 200 MB. Reported, not asserted.
