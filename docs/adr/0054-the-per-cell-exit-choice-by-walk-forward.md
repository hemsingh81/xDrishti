- **Status:** Accepted (spec [P4-06](../specs/P4-06-per-cell-exit-choice-walk-forward.md), which the owner approved on 2026-10-22, questions 1 to 8 as recommended; built on 2026-10-22). The text of this ADR has not been read by the owner.
- **Date:** 2026-10-22.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** [ADR 0053](0053-the-same-entries-comparison-and-the-mae-mfe-diagnostics.md) (the comparison it reads: candidate ids, the entries key, the downside deviation, the stored comparison it points at), [ADR 0045](0045-the-result-store-sim-runs-and-sim-trades.md) (insert-only research tables), [ADR 0047](0047-the-oracle-and-the-rule-table-gaps.md) (the oracle), [ADR 0039](0039-an-engine-module-is-reached-from-an-adapter-in-infrastructure-never-from-application.md) (the ports).

## Context
Doc 08 §3 says the exit of a cell is chosen by out-of-sample risk-adjusted expectancy, with a minimum sample, stability across folds and a plateau preferred to a sharp peak. P4-05 produces the trades of every candidate on the same entries. The data are about 2.1 years (ISS-19), so doc 16's `walk_forward: train_years: 3` cannot run, and four to six folds are weak evidence however carefully they are cut. This slice builds the choice and the stability report and says at every presentation what they are worth.

## Decision
1. **A pure function of trades (D1).** `ExitSelector.Select(SelectionInput)` in Domain takes the closed trades of each candidate (entry date, exit date, R in the unit of the run), the sessions of the trading calendar, the candidates in the order of the comparison (the first is the default exit) and `SelectionOptions`; it reads no clock, no random and no store. The verb makes the input in memory from the comparison it just ran (`--select-exit`), from the **ungated** comparison walk (a swing cell says "overlap not gated"). Reading a stored comparison is a later slice.
2. **Folds (D2, D3).** Expanding train from the first entry date; test windows of 3 calendar months tiling from the first month start at least 9 months in; a final window shorter than one month is dropped and counted. The train set of a fold holds the trades that entered at least 5 sessions before the window start (the sessions are those of `ITradingCalendar` over the run's window) **and exited before it**: a swing trade still open at the window start is purged, one that entered inside the embargo is embargoed, and both are counted in the record. `walk_forward` in doc 16 becomes `{ mode: expanding, min_train_months: 9, test_months: 3, embargo_sessions: 5 }`; the 3-year rolling form stays as the later default for 5 years of data. Overrides: `--min-train-months`, `--test-months`, `--embargo-sessions`, `--lambda`, `--min-trades`, `--no-plateau`.
3. **The score (D4).** `mean_R - lambda * downside_dev_R` with lambda 0.5 and the semi-deviation of ADR 0053 (`sqrt(mean(min(R, 0)^2))`, target 0), on `RNet` for a costed run and `RFilled` for a gross one; the unit is in the record and never mixed.
4. **Minimum sample and "no choice" (D5).** A candidate takes part in a choice with at least `min_trades` (30) trades in the train set; a fold with fewer than 2 such candidates has no winner; a fold is **thin** (out of the stability fraction, listed) when its winner has fewer than 30 trades in the test window. The cell has a final choice only if the full-sample choice qualifies and at least 2 folds are neither thin nor without a winner; otherwise "no choice", the default exit is used and the banner says so.
5. **The plateau rule (D7), changed from the spec in one respect.** Candidates that differ in exactly one parameter and are adjacent in that parameter among those equal in the others are neighbours (a one-value line has none and is "isolated"). A candidate is **on a plateau** if every qualified neighbour scores at least its own score minus its standard error of the mean (`sd/sqrt(n)`). **The spec says the winner is the highest scorer among plateau candidates; read literally this picks the worst candidate of a line (all its neighbours are better, so it is "on a plateau") over a clear best, and it would fail the spec's own planted-signal criterion** (a planted exit better than its neighbours by more than one error is not on a plateau by that test). **As built, a plateau candidate is eligible only if it scores within one standard error of the best candidate; the highest eligible wins; if none is eligible the best wins and is flagged a lone peak** (an isolated best is both a lone peak and isolated). `--no-plateau` takes the best. Ties break by the lower candidate id in ordinal order (so `rr=2.5` sorts before `rr=2`). The rule reads only the train trades.
6. **Stability (D8).** From the non-thin folds with a winner: a fold agrees with the modal winner if it is the same candidate or a neighbour of it. **Stable** needs at least 60 % of those folds in agreement **and** at least 60 % of the agreeing folds with a positive out-of-sample score for their winner (a stable loss is not a stable edge); otherwise unstable, with the reason; fewer than 2 usable folds is "no stability statement". The key is a new `stable_folds_pct`; doc 16's `min_positive_folds_pct` keeps its other meaning. The report prints, in a fixed line, the **exact probability that uniformly random winners pass the agreement rule** for the same number of folds and candidates (every tally of winners enumerated, multinomial weights; not computed above two million tallies and then said so), the lone-peak count, the winner and the train and test score of each fold, and the pooled out-of-sample result of the walk-forward choice beside the default exit and every single candidate.
7. **The record (D9).** `ExitSelection` schema 1: the inputs, the dates and counts of the folds, a table of every candidate in every fold, the winners and flags, the final choice or "no choice" with its reason, the default, the pooled figures, the verdict and the banner (the in-sample line of P4-00 criterion 9, "k folds only", the chance-pass line, the unit, "overlap not gated", the no-choice line). Rendered by `ExitSelectionText`, `ExitSelectionCsv` (one row per fold and candidate, the in-sample line on every row) and `ExitSelectionJson` (six decimals, fixed member order); the same inputs give the same bytes. Folder: the verb names it from the **selection key** (a hash of the comparison key and every input of D3), so it never meets a plain comparison's folder, and adds `exit-selection.json` and `exit-selection.csv`.
8. **Storage (D10).** With `--store`, after the member runs and the comparison: one row in `research.exit_selections` (selection key unique, foreign key to `sim_comparisons`, the cell, the chosen candidate and the verdict, the record as `text`, its SHA-256) and one row for each fold and candidate in `research.exit_selection_folds`, in one transaction; migration `AddExitSelections`; insert-only for the runtime role. A selection whose comparison is not stored is refused (`store.write_failed`, nothing written); the same selection again is "already stored"; another record under the key is `store.run_conflict`. Port `IExitSelectionStore` (a sibling of `IRunStore`), handler `StoreExitSelectionHandler`, events 4428 to 4430. The regime is `*` until P6.
9. **The oracle (D11).** A second implementation from D2 to D8 and this ADR only (lists, two-pass statistics, neighbours by pairwise comparison, the chance by walking every sequence of winners) agrees with the selector on every field of the record over 5,000 seeded comparisons (random lines, a single-value candidate, a 2x2 grid, calendars with holidays, swing holds, options); the properties of D11 hold, and a chooser that scores on the test window is caught by the look-ahead property.

## Findings of the measurement (acceptance criterion 5)
The spec asked for "unstable on at least X % of 1,000 noise-only series" with X set by measurement. Measured on seeds 1 to 1,000, 540 sessions, one trade a session for each candidate, R with a common and an own noise term:
- **Zero edge** (every candidate loses on average once the downside penalty is taken): unstable on 1,000 of 1,000 for 3 candidates and for 6, by the second rule alone (the winners' out-of-sample scores are negative). The test fixes 99 %.
- **A profitable series in which the exit does not matter** (every candidate drawn from one distribution with mean 0.8 R): unstable on **0 of 1,000** for 3 candidates and **54 of 1,000** for 6. Neighbours count as agreeing, so on a line of candidates random winners agree with their modal neighbourhood most of the time, and the profit makes the second rule pass. **The verdict alone does not tell an exit that matters from one that does not**; the chance-pass line printed beside it does (its median among the series called stable is above 0.5 for 3 candidates and above 0.7 for 6; a test pins both). Making the verdict stricter (for instance requiring the chance-pass probability to be small, or the walk-forward result to beat the default) is an owner decision recorded as an open item, not done here.

## Consequences
- `simulate --exit FIXED_RR:rr=1.5|2|2.5|3 --select-exit --gross` prints the folds, the winners, the verdict and the banner, and writes the record; `--store` keeps it.
- Nothing is applied: no stored strategy exists to apply a choice to, and the choice is not evidence of an edge (it is in-sample on about 2.1 years, 4 to 6 folds).
- The three small corrections to the P4-05 report that the reviewer asked for are made with this slice: a median MFE above the target is censored; a costed trade without costing is refused by `UnitR` (the assembler already refuses such a run, so the guard is a second line); the diagnostics say that a winner there is `RExact > 0` while the table uses `RNet` for a costed run.

## Honest limits
- Four to six folds cannot establish stability; the report says so on every run and prints the chance of passing by luck.
- The candidates on a line are neighbours only by the order of one parameter; `PARTIAL_TRAIL`, `PYRAMID` and single-value candidates have no neighbours and can only win as a lone peak or, if they are the best, by the best-score fall-back.
- The sessions of the embargo are those of the trading calendar over the run's window; a session with no trade is still a session.
