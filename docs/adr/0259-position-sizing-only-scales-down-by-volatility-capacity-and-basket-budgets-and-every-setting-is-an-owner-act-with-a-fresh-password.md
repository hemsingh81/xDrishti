# 0259 — Position sizing only scales down, by volatility, capacity and basket budgets, and every setting is an owner act with a fresh password

- **Status:** Accepted (spec BK-10, approved by the owner 2026-11-03). **Safety slice: YES** (it changes what quantity a plan suggests; it can only lower it; no order is placed).
- **Date:** 2026-11-11.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** [0042](0042-costs-and-sizing-the-charge-sheet-the-quantity-net-r-and-simtrade-schema-4.md) (the fixed-risk quantity, unchanged), [0091](0091-auto-approval-is-an-option-behind-an-owner-setting-default-off-a-new-approval-kind-the-database-checks.md) (owner acts, fresh password), [0084](0084-portfolio-fill-greedy-with-every-limit-rechecked-and-unavailable-blocking.md) (the fill this runs after), [0242](0242-few-visible-settings-and-learned-changes-tighten-automatically-while-loosening-needs-the-owners-password.md) (descriptors), [0223](0223-the-lab-verdict-counts-each-trades-liquidity-tier-and-capacity-breach-from-day-bars-report-only-and-readiness-row-14-reads-them-with-unavailable-as-a-fail.md) (capacity share, see the open question below).

## Context
The owner asked for "more leverage": capital efficiency inside the existing risk rules. An upward scale is a bigger book in calm periods, which is the leverage risk this project's rules forbid; the research evidence for volatility-managed sizing is mostly a smoother drawdown, and out-of-sample or after-cost versions often fail to beat the unmanaged ones. The useful, safe reading is the opposite: suggest less when a share is unusually volatile, never more than a stated part of what trades, and never more than a basket's share of the day's open risk.

## Decision
1. **Down only, by construction.** The final quantity is `floor(q_fixed x min(volatility scale, gate scale) x budget ratio)` capped by the capacity pool. Every factor is at most 1: the volatility scale is `clamp(target / sigma, Floor, 1.0)` where the 1.0 is the constant `VolTarget.Ceiling`, not a setting; `SizingFactors.Combine` and the wiring take a minimum with the gate's factor; the fit ratio is at most 1. There is no `allow_upsizing`, no cap-raising setting and no word `upsize`, `leverage`, `margin` or `multiplier` in the slice's surface (an architecture test fails otherwise). An up-scaling what-if is a new spec, a new ADR and a hypothesis-loop experiment.
2. **Target is the share's own median volatility** (EWMA, lambda 0.94, seeded on 20 returns, 60 needed; target the median of the previous 252 sessions, 120 needed). The scale is below 1 only when the share is more volatile than usual. A share without the history is sized at the fixed quantity and the ticket says "volatility unknown": never a guess.
3. **The two market factors are combined by the minimum, not the product.** The gate's 0.75 (reduced mode) and the volatility scale answer the same question; the product double counts. `q_fixed` is the quantity before any gate factor; the wiring takes `min(gate, factor)` once (a test pins 375, not 281).
4. **Capacity cap** `floor(CapacityPct x ADV20 / entry)`, with `CapacityPct` defaulting to **0.005** and never above the configured `Confidence:CostStress:CapacityDailyShare` (the one capacity share of the app, ADR 0223; the owner can only tighten below it), ADV20 the median traded value of the 20 consecutive trading sessions (the strict rule of [ADR 0230](0230-the-min-turnover-filter-passes-on-a-20-day-median-traded-value-of-at-least-20-crore-the-tier-bps-and-range-share-are-tighten-only-settings-and-the-defaults-are-judgment-calls-to-be-replaced-by-measured-paper-fills.md)). It caps the ticket's pool; below one share the ticket goes to the reserve (`capacity_cap`). A value that cannot be computed is flagged `sizing:capacity_unavailable`, never a pass.
5. **Basket risk budgets** are a share (1 to 100 percent) of the total open risk limit. After the fill, in one place (`SizeScaler`), a candidate is scaled to the room left (the basket's book risk plus earlier picks); a ratio below the floor sends it to the reserve with code `basket_budget` and the numbers; a basket without a row has no budget; inputs that are unknown (the candidate's risk, a book position without baskets) are flagged `sizing:budget_unavailable` and the ticket keeps its fixed size. A share in two baskets is fitted to the tightest.
6. **All four settings and every budget are the owner's, with a fresh password for each change, in both directions.** `PUT /api/selection/sizing-settings` and `PUT /api/selection/basket-risk-budgets/{basketId}` take the session, the CSRF token and the password (checked on every change, the elevation window untouched); the actor is the session's owner (a body cannot name one); `SizingChanges` refuses a non-owner before the store, and the database check (`changed_by LIKE 'owner:%'`) says so again. Both tables are insert-only (`selection.sizing_settings_changes`, `selection.basket_risk_budgets`); each key that changes is a row with old and new value, direction (`tighten`, `loosen`, `neutral`), actor, database time and hash; the newest row per key wins; no row means everything OFF and no budgets. No rule, job, model or assistant writes them. Direction: disabling, raising the volatility floor (`sizing.vol_target.floor`), lowering the budget minimum fit (`sizing.budget.min_fit`, the lowest share a budget may cut a candidate to), raising the capacity percent, raising or removing a budget are `loosen`; the opposite are `tighten`. A change that changes nothing is refused, so there is no `neutral`. Each accepted change also writes one account event `SizingChanged` (no password).
7. **The auto option.** A ticket sized by an ON setting needs no per-ticket approval of the scale (it only reduces); turning the setting ON is the owner act of point 6; each ticket carries a structured `sizing_note` (JSON on the plan item: volatility scale, gate scale, factor, capacity shares, budget room and use, settings version); with every setting OFF and no budget nothing is written and the plan, its hash included, is what it was; a ticket whose scaled quantity is below one share for every paper account goes to the reserve as `sizing_zero`; a ticket with unavailable inputs is flagged. No setting allows an order (P12 is not touched).
8. **The study** (`GET /api/reports/sizing-study`) is a pure what-if over the stored closed trades of one run: each trade's net R times the scale its share's day bars up to the day before entry give (never above 1), max drawdown and total R with and without, the two halves (below 30 trades is insufficient). The floor is the stored setting, or one of 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 as a labelled sensitivity. It prints "descriptive, before costs, same trades", recommends nothing, writes nothing.

## Consequences
- OFF by default means every existing plan is unchanged (the builder takes the port optionally; with defaults and no budget the plan hash is identical, tested).
- One more serial day-bar read per primary ticket at plan build (about five).
- The scaling cannot tell the allocator's final per-account quantity: the ticket note states the factor, not the share count.

## Known limits
- The hold-out halves are a median split, not the locked hold-out of ADR 0207.
- The stricter capacity default (0.5 percent, the app's one capacity share) follows the review's Q6 recommendation; the owner may decide otherwise.
- The banner "loosened since the last plan, until dismissed" is moved to BK-10b (roadmap): the read shows the newest loosening.
- The audit event is a second write after the history row (the history is raw SQL, the account events are EF); its failure is logged, not a rollback, as in the other owner acts.
