# 0035 — Per-session features keep the history of the session only

- **Status:** Accepted — **decided on research under the owner's delegation (2026-10-16/17); the owner may revert.** The owner said "use your research and fix them as of now"; this issue (ISS-13) was designed three times independently, a judge chose between the designs, and the result was built, attacked by a second agent, reviewed and verified. **Two of the decisions change text the owner approved earlier** — a per-session feature read on a coarser timeframe is unknown until that timeframe has closed its first bar of the day (decision 3), and `prev_day_*` and `gap_pct` get no exemption (decision 5); *The owner may revert* (right after the decisions) says what reverting each would mean.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** Supersedes in part [ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md) decision 4 (the history of the frame runs on across sessions) and narrows its decision 2 (a value is the one "as of the last closed bar"); narrows [ADR 0032](0032-frame-history-closed-flags-and-the-remaining-indicators.md) decision 2 and criterion 2 of [P3-01b](../specs/P3-01b-frame-api-for-the-dsl-and-more-indicators.md) ("across session boundaries"); supersedes the paragraph *Lagged reads are by position, not by session* and Amendment 19 of [ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md), whose premise — that the stronger rule needs a new member of `FeatureFrame` — was false.
- **Date:** 2026-10-17 (built the same day: see *Consequences from the build*)

## In short
- **The bug.** Twelve features start again every morning (`vwap`, the opening range `or_high` and `or_low`, today's open, high and low, yesterday's open, high, low, close and volume, and the gap), but their history did not: one bar back on the first bar of a day was the last value of the day before. So `crosses_above(close, vwap)`, the headline example of the language, compared two different days. On a seeded random walk of 500 sessions it was true at the first evaluation of 138 sessions on 1m and 130 on 15m (the red run of the regression test, on unmodified code).
- **The rule.** For these twelve names, on 1m to 1h, the frame keeps the history of the current day only. Anything that would reach into the day before is *unknown*, and unknown never fires an entry. A crossing of the vwap can therefore first be true on the second bar of the day, and `rising(vwap, 3)` on the fourth.
- **What it costs.** Some rules fire later, or never, without saying so (the warning `lag_across_session` covers only a read that no session can hold). On a coarser timeframe (`tf("1h").prev_day_close`) the value is unknown for up to the first hour although it is known. The frame step got about 2 to 8 % slower (for example 0.066 to 0.068 µs against a budget of 1 µs).
- **What the owner may revert.** Two choices change text approved earlier and were decided on research under the owner's delegation (2026-10-16/17): the coarser-timeframe rule (decision 3) and "no exemption for yesterday's levels and the gap" (decision 5). *The owner may revert* (after the decisions) lists what each revert changes.
- **Words.** *Session* = one India date. *Pending* = the feature has no value yet today. *S* = how many bars the feature's timeframe has closed today. *Unknown* = the third value of the logic of [ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md): not true, not false, and never fires an entry.

## Context
The frame of [ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md) and [ADR 0032](0032-frame-history-closed-flags-and-the-remaining-indicators.md) keeps, for each feature, the values it had after each of the last `HistoryDepth` closed bars of its own timeframe, in a ring that is written once per closed bar and never cleared. That is right for the rolling features: an `ema(20)` at 09:30 wants the bars of the day before (ADR 0031 decision 4). Twelve features are not rolling — `vwap`, `or_high`, `or_low`, `today_open/high/low`, `prev_day_open/high/low/close/volume` and `gap_pct` start again at each India date. The indicators restart themselves at the first bar of a date, but the frame's ring ran on, so one bar back on the first bar of a session was the last value of the session before.

The final review of the rules language ([ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md)) found the consequence: `crosses_above(close, vwap)`, the headline example of the language, compared the first bar of a session with the last bar of the one before. On a seeded random walk of 500 sessions it was true at the first evaluation of 138 sessions on 1m and 130 on 15m (the red run of the regression test of this ADR, on unmodified code); a scratch run of the lint design measured 28.1 % (140 of 498) of the first evaluations on 15m against 8.75 % of the other bars. ADR 0034 Amendment 19 left the meaning of `x[k]` as it was and added a warning, `lag_across_session`, that named a `time >= "HH:MM"` check; the stronger rule — a lagged read across a session reset is *unknown* — was left open as ISS-13, with the premise that it needs a new member of `FeatureFrame` and a rule of its own for `prev_day_*`. The owner delegated the decision. Three agents designed it from three sides (the lint, the compiler, the frame) and a judge chose; this ADR records the choice, what was built and what it costs.

## The problem in numbers
Two sessions, worked out by hand in `SessionBoundaryExamplesTests` (1-minute bars, volume 10 each, so the vwap is the running mean of the typical prices). Monday has three bars that slide under their own vwap; Tuesday opens with a gap up.

| Bar | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|-----|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Session | Mon | Mon | Mon | Tue | Tue | Tue | Tue |
| `time` (close of the bar) | 09:16 | 09:17 | 09:18 | 09:16 | 09:17 | 09:18 | 09:19 |
| `close` | 100 | 98 | 96 | 122 | 121 | 119 | 130 |
| `vwap` | 100 | 99 | 98 | 120.667 | 120.833 | 120.222 | 122.667 |
| `today_open` | 100 | 100 | 100 | 119 | 119 | 119 | 119 |

On bar 4, `close` 122 is above Tuesday's first typical price 120.667. By position the bar before it is Monday's last: `close[1]` = 96 and `vwap[1]` = 98, so 96 ≤ 98 and the condition `crosses_above(close, vwap)` was true. Nothing crossed the vwap: Tuesday opened 23 points above Monday's close. Now `vwap[1]` does not exist in Tuesday (it has one bar), so the crossing is unknown. One letter per bar — T true, F false, U unknown:

| Condition | Before | Now |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| `crosses_above(close, vwap)` | UFFTFFT | UFFUFFT |
| `rising(vwap, 1)` | UFFTTFT | UFFUTFT |
| `crosses_above(close, today_open)` | UFFTFFT | UFFUFFT |
| `close > vwap and close[1] <= vwap[1]` (the crossing written out) | FFFTFFT | FFFUFFT |
| `close > vwap and close[1] <= vwap` (the level form: only the bar is lagged) | FFFTFFT | FFFTFFT |
| `rising(close, 1)` (`close` is continuous) | UFFTFFT | UFFTFFT |
| `crosses_above(close, vwap) and time >= "09:17"` | FFFFFFT | FFFFFFT (the check is no longer needed) |

Three sessions of three bars (same test) show what position does to a level that is constant within a session. `prev_day.high` is Monday's 105 for Tuesday and Tuesday's 109 for Wednesday; closes are 101 104 99 | 102 107 108 | 114 115 111. `crosses_above(close, prev_day.high)` is now UUUUTFUFF and the level form `close > prev_day.high and close[1] <= prev_day.high` is UUUFTFTFF: on Wednesday's gap-up bar (bar 7, close 114 above the 109 of Tuesday's high, Tuesday's last close 108 at or below it) the level form says true and the crossing is unknown. By position the crossing was FALSE there (worked out by hand from the old rule): the level one bar back was Tuesday's own 105, and 108 is not at or below 105 — the old reading missed the gap through the level.

## Decisions
The rule is the one the judge fixed; "the owner may revert" applies to all of it, and decisions 3 and 5 are the two that change approved text.

1. **The rule is in the data of the frame, in two parts.** (A) *Forgetting.* When a timeframe closes its first bar of a new India date, the frame fills the history slots of that timeframe's restarting features with NaN, so a lag that reaches before that bar is unknown. (B) *Pending.* From the first 1-minute bar of a new date until the timeframe has closed its first bar of that date, the restarting features of that timeframe are not ready at any lag (`Value` is NaN). The "restarting features" are the twelve names of decision 4 on the timeframes of decision 4. Nothing else changes: no new member of `FeatureFrame` (the seven members the compiled library may reference are the same), no check on the read path, no change to `At`, the compiled code, the binder or the parser.

2. **A session is the India date of the start of the bar a timeframe feeds to its indicators; S counts the bars the timeframe closed in it.** The date is `IndiaDays.DayOf`, the call that `Vwap`, `OpeningRange` and `SessionContext` already use to restart, so the frame's boundary and the indicator's reset cannot disagree. Let D be the India date of the last accepted 1-minute bar and S the number of bars the timeframe has closed whose start lies on D. Then for a restarting feature:
   - **S = 0 (pending):** not ready at any lag — `this[h]`, `At(h, k)` for every k, `IsReady` and `IsReadyAt`.
   - **S ≥ 1:** lag 0 is the indicator's value; lag k ≥ 1 is the value k closed bars ago when k < S (and it was ready then), otherwise unknown.
   - Equivalently: the feature is read as if the frame had been created at the first accepted bar of D — for `prev_day_*` and `gap_pct` at the first bar of the previous date that had bars, so the previous session's values are known.

   The text and the meaning of `x[k]` in the language do not change ("k closed bars of its own timeframe ago"), nor do the three-valued tables of ADR 0034 (true, false, unknown; Kleene's): a NaN operand makes a comparison, `crosses_*`, `rising/falling` and `between` unknown, and unknown is not true ([ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md) decision 3). Because a session is a date and S counts bars, the odd days need no rule of their own:

   | Case | What happens |
   |------|--------------|
   | Weekend, holiday | no bars, so no session; the first bar after it starts a new one; `prev_day_*` is the last date that had bars |
   | Half day, special session | a session with fewer bars (with a calendar its last bucket closes at its own last minute, without one it closes late: see the late bucket) |
   | Opening minutes missing (the first bar is 09:30) | the session starts at the first bar present; S counts bars the timeframe closed, never clock minutes |
   | Saturday session; Muhurat session on a date of its own | a session of its own, restarting at its first bar (in the owner's files a Muhurat session starts at 18:00 on 2024-11-01 and at 13:45 on 2025-10-21; the test fixture starts one at 18:15) |
   | A morning block and an evening block on one date | **one session** (a documented limit; the indicators already treat them so) |
   | A bucket of the old date closes late (the 15:29 minute is missing, so the next date's first minute closes it) | the bucket is consumed with its own, old date: it is neither wiped nor does it end the pending state of the new date; the new date stays pending until the timeframe closes its own first bar; at that step `BarClosed` is true, `close` and the continuous features are defined, the restarting features are unknown |
   | The next date's first minute closes the late bucket and the new date's own bucket | the timeframe is on the new date: lag 0 is the new date's value, lag 1 is unknown (a one-bar session so far), `ClosedBarsAt` is 2 |
   | A duplicate or older 1-minute bar | skipped by the ordering guard before any lane runs; it restarts nothing and the results equal those of the clean series |
   | `Reset`; a frame fed from the middle of a day | `Reset` returns to "no date seen"; the first bar a frame is given sets its date |

3. **A per-session feature on a coarser timeframe is unknown until that timeframe closes its first bar of the day.** *Delegated decision; it amends [ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md) decision 2 ("as of the last closed bar of its timeframe") for lag 0 of the twelve names.* Before, `tf("1h").prev_day_close` showed the value the 1h timeframe had after its last bar of the previous session until the 1h bar of the new day had closed — for `prev_day_*` that is the close of the day before yesterday, not yesterday's. Measured in a scratch run on the built libraries, after three sessions, at 09:15–10:14 of session 3: `tf("1h").prev_day_close` was 137.9 (session 1's close; session 2's was 237.9), `tf("1h").today_open` was 200.00 (session 2's open) and `tf("1h").vwap` 219.82 (session 2's last value). Now all three are unknown for those minutes (up to T minutes on a timeframe of T minutes: 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60) and then the right day's. The price is that the rule is **conservative**: `tf("1h").prev_day_close` is unknown for up to an hour although its value has been known since the open. A strategy is evaluated only when its own timeframe has closed a bar, so a read on the strategy's own timeframe, or on a finer one, never meets the gap; only a read on a coarser one does (a 5m strategy that reads `tf("15m").prev_day_close` has two unknown evaluations a day, 09:20 and 09:25).

4. **The twelve names, and where the rule applies.** `IndicatorInfo.RestartsEachSession(spec)` is true for exactly these names, and only on 1m, 5m, 10m, 15m, 30m and 1h:

   | Names | 1m, 5m, 10m, 15m, 30m, 1h | 1D | 1W, 1M |
   |-------|---------------------------|----|--------|
   | `vwap`, `or_high`, `or_low` | the history of the session only; pending on a timeframe coarser than the minute | refused (intraday only) | refused |
   | `today_open`, `today_high`, `today_low` | the same | accepted; nothing restarts, a lag is by position | refused |
   | `prev_day_open`, `prev_day_high`, `prev_day_low`, `prev_day_close`, `prev_day_volume`, `gap_pct` | the same, no exemption (decision 5) | accepted; nothing restarts: `gap_pct[1]` on 1D is yesterday's gap | refused |
   | the other 44 names (32 rolling, raw-bar and pattern names; the twelve date-driven names `day_of_week`, `is_expiry_day` and the ten context values) | untouched | untouched | untouched |

   A daily bar is a whole session and its bucket starts at India midnight, so on 1D the history is by position and nothing is pending. The refusals on 1W and 1M are those of [ADR 0033](0033-weekly-monthly-timeframes-calendar-features-and-the-market-context.md) decision 9 and are unchanged. An opening range of one bar of its timeframe (`or_high(15)` on 15m) is ready on the first bar like `vwap`; one of several bars (`or_high(30)` on 15m) is unknown at lag 0 until the range has closed, and at lag k for k more bars; if the first bar of a date lies after the window it is never ready on that date.

   What is known when, on a full session (time is the close of the evaluated bar, ADR 0034 decision 4):

   | Read | Known from | On 15m |
   |------|------------|--------|
   | `vwap`, `today_*`, a single-bar `or_*` at lag 0 | the timeframe's bar 1 | 09:30 |
   | `prev_day_*`, `gap_pct` at lag 0 | bar 1 of the second session on (they need a previous session) | 09:30 |
   | `x[k]`, and `crosses_*` against one of these (k = 1) | bar k + 1; crossings from bar 2 | 10:00 for `vwap[2]`; 09:45 for a crossing |
   | `rising(x, n)` / `falling(x, n)` | bar n + 1 | 10:15 for `rising(vwap, 3)` |

5. **`prev_day_*` and `gap_pct` get no exemption.** *Delegated decision; it amends [ADR 0032](0032-frame-history-closed-flags-and-the-remaining-indicators.md) decision 2 and criterion 2 of P3-01b ("across session boundaries") for these six names.* A lag across the open is unknown, like that of `vwap`. The exemption ("yesterday against the day before") would make `prev_day_close[1]` the day before yesterday on the first bar of a session and yesterday on every other bar — a meaning that depends on the position of the bar, which is how charting platforms produce the same artefact — and it would need a per-name flag in the catalog. By position `crosses_above(close, prev_day.high)` both invents and misses gap-throughs (4.8 % of the first evaluations true against 9.6 % for the level form, in the lint design's scratch run on 500 sessions; and the false at the gap-up bar of the example above). The legitimate readings have one-line spellings (*Idioms*).

6. **What stays continuous.** Rolling, trend, momentum, volatility, pattern and raw-bar features (`ema`, `rsi`, `atr`, `adx`, `macd`, `bb`, `supertrend`, pivots, `highest`/`lowest`, `rel_volume`, `roc`, `open high low close volume`, the candle patterns) keep running across sessions — `ema(20)` at 09:30 still uses yesterday's bars, `close[1]` on the first bar is yesterday's last close, and `rising(close, 1)` is true on a gap up. The twelve date-driven features (`day_of_week`, `is_expiry_day` and the ten context values) keep their lane of their own and their previous *date* ([ADR 0033](0033-weekly-monthly-timeframes-calendar-features-and-the-market-context.md), [ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md) Amendment 4). Every feature on 1D keeps positional lags. At lag 0 a coarser timeframe still shows the value "as of its last closed bar" for every non-restarting feature (`tf("1D").close` is yesterday's close until today's daily bar closes).

7. **`lag_across_session` stays a warning and narrows to a read that no session can hold.** For a restarting feature on the timeframe of T minutes at an effective lag L (the subscript plus the offsets of the enclosing `crosses_*`, `rising`, `falling`), the lint warns when `L × T ≥ 375`, the minutes of a regular session — that is, when L ≥ ceil(375 / T): 375, 75, 38, 25, 13 and 7 on 1m, 5m, 10m, 15m, 30m and 1h. Such a read reaches into a session before the current one, so that part of the condition is never known. The rule does not depend on the strategy's timeframe (`tf("5m").vwap[80]` on a 1D strategy is warned; a read on the 1D lane never is), is given once per call or lone subscripted feature, and is not changed by a `time` check; lag 0 is never warned. The message reads: "reads `vwap` up to N closed bars back, but a session holds at most n bars of 5m and a per-session feature has no value from an earlier session: that part of the condition is never known, and unknown is not true. Read at most n−1 bars back." Examples:

   | Warned | Compiles without a problem |
   |--------|----------------------------|
   | `vwap[75]` and `rising(vwap, 75)` on 5m; `rising(vwap, 7)` on 1h; `tf("5m").vwap[80]` on a 1D strategy | `vwap[74]` on 5m; `rising(vwap, 6)` on 1h; `crosses_above(close, vwap)` on any timeframe, with or without a `time` check |

   On 1m the warning always comes together with `lag_too_deep` (the boundary 375 is above the 256 values the frame keeps): `vwap[375]` gets both, `vwap[374]` only the error. A read on the 1D lane is never warned (`today_open[400]` on a 1D strategy is only `lag_too_deep`). The code and the 16 lint codes stay; the meaning changes from "the first bars of a session" to "never known", which is acceptable because nothing consumes the old meaning yet (no stored strategies, P3-02b is not built).

8. **The catalog answers; no other code knows the names.** `IndicatorInfo.RestartsEachSession(spec)` is the single predicate for the frame, the lint and the tests. `IndicatorInfo.HasValueAtSessionOpen`, its field and its `valueAtSessionOpen: true` constructor arguments (two in the source, for ten catalog entries), and the time-guard arithmetic of the lint (`TimeGuard`, `SessionRead.CleanFrom`, the clock text of the message) are deleted. `IRestartingIndicator` (internal, one member, `MarkPending()`) is implemented explicitly by `Vwap`, `OrHigh`, `OrLow` and `SessionContext`, so the public surface of the library gains only `RestartsEachSession` and loses `HasValueAtSessionOpen`.

## The owner may revert
Both delegated decisions were decided on research under the owner's delegation (2026-10-16/17). The owner may revert either; the rest of the rule — forgetting the history of the other ten names, `lag_across_session` narrowed, `RestartsEachSession` — does not depend on them.

**Decision 3 (pending on a coarser timeframe).** Reverting means dropping part B of decision 1 and keeping part A:
- *Code.* Remove `StartSession` and the fields `_restartLanes`, `_sessionDay`, `_sessionDayKnown` and their lines in `Reset`; remove the `_pending` flag and the explicit `MarkPending()` from `Vwap`, `OrHigh`, `OrLow` and `SessionContext` (and the `Value` gate of `OrHigh`/`OrLow`), `IRestartingIndicator` and `Lane.Restarting`. `ClearRestartHistory` in `Consume` and `RestartsEachSession` stay.
- *Tests.* The pending cases go: `FrameSessionRestartTests` H2 and H4, `RestartingIndicatorTests` (40) and `RestartingIndicatorsAdversarialTests` (33), `CoarserLaneAdversarialTests` (9), the S = 0 cases of `FrameColdStartTests`, `SessionHistoryReferenceAdversarialTests` and `FrameSemanticsTests`, the pending mask of `SessionReference` and the counters of the oracle, the 11 coarser-timeframe shapes of `SessionLagTruthTests`, the second window of `EvaluationAllocationTests`, the pending path of the allocation tests; mutants M2, M3, M3b, M6, M6b, M13 and M15 lose their meaning.
- *Documents.* Decision 3 and the "pending" sentences of this ADR, of the Lags paragraph of doc 11, of the `FeatureFrame` row of doc 03 and of the P3-01b note; the line "`tf("1h").prev_day_close` is unknown for up to an hour".
- *What comes back.* A coarser timeframe shows the previous session's values until its first bar of the day — with the numbers of decision 3: `tf("1h").prev_day_close` of session 3 would read session 1's close for the first hour — and a strategy finer than a feature's timeframe reads yesterday's final value (for `prev_day_*`, the value of the day before) during that timeframe's first bar of the day; the truth test must then leave out the shapes where the strategy's timeframe is finer than the feature's.

**Decision 5 (no exemption for `prev_day_*` and `gap_pct`).** Reverting means a per-name exemption in the catalog:
- *Code.* A per-name exemption in the catalog (a flag on the six entries) that the frame consults when it forgets the history (`ClearRestartHistory`), so that these names keep their positional history; whether they also stay pending on a coarser timeframe is a second choice for the owner.
- *Tests.* The `prev_day_*`/`gap_pct` parts of `FrameSessionRestartTests` H1, the second shadow of `FrameColdStartTests`, `SessionHistoryReferenceAdversarialTests`, `SessionReference`, `IndicatorInfoRestartTests` (the matrix), the three-session word UUUUTFUFF of `SessionBoundaryExamplesTests` and the unknown at the first evaluation for `crosses_above(close, prev_day.high)` in `SessionOpenRegressionTests`; the lint truth tests with these names.
- *Documents.* Decisions 4 and 5, the idiom for `prev_day.high`, and the lines of doc 11 that say a lag across the open is unknown for these names. The level form and `tf("1D").close[1]` stay valid spellings.
- *What comes back.* `prev_day_close[1]` is the day before yesterday at one bar per session and yesterday at all the others, `crosses_above(close, prev_day.high)` invents and misses gap-throughs as in the example above, and `rising(prev_day_close, 1)` can be true on the first bar only and is then never true.

## Why the frame, and not the lint or the compiler
Each of the three designs was written by an agent that could only read the code, then checked by the judge against scratch runs on the built Release libraries (the repository untouched).

| | Lint: an error and a clock guard | Compiler: a guarded read | Frame (chosen) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where the rule lives | `LagRules`, in the text | a new `FeatureFrame.SessionBars(handle)` member, `Kernel.SessionValueAt`, a guarded read emitted by `ExpressionBuilder` for lags ≥ 1 | the data of `FeatureFrame` |
| First bar of a session, `crosses_above(close, vwap)` | refused unless guarded | unknown | unknown |
| Lag 0 of a timeframe coarser than the strategy's | refused by a widened rule | **still the previous session's value** (verified) | unknown until its first bar of the day |
| A session that opens at 09:16, a Muhurat session | **fires on the first bar through the advised guard** (verified) | correct (counts bars) | correct (counts bars) |
| New `FeatureFrame` member (breaks the seven-name architecture list) | no | yes | no |
| Cost on the evaluation path | none | a branch on every guarded read, in the 255 ns path | none |

The judge's reasons, in order:
1. **Only a rule in the data can honour "never silently compare two sessions".** The lint design is unsound by its own counterexamples: a session that opens at 09:16 (09:15 missing) fires `crosses_above(close, vwap)` on its first bar with the guard the lint advises, `time >= "09:17"` — `time` is the close of the bar, so the guard is true on bar 1 —; a Muhurat session (18:00 on 2024-11-01 and 13:45 on 2025-10-21 in the owner's files; 18:15 in the test fixture) passes `time >= "09:45"` on its first bar (and `bad_time_literal` makes an evening guard unwritable); a daily bar that closes late reads the next session. The lint counts clock, the lag counts bars. Raising the warning to an error would also have refused text that compiled before.
2. **The compiler design has a verified defect.** It treats lag 0 as "`frame[handle]`, always the current session". That is false for a timeframe coarser than the minute — the numbers of decision 3 — and a guard on lags ≥ 1 cannot fix it; the pending state of decision 1 can.
3. **The premise that the stronger rule needs a new member of `FeatureFrame` is false.** The ring is written once per closed bar; clearing the restarting slots when the timeframe consumes its first bar of a date puts the invariant in the data, so `At`, `IsReadyAt`, `TryGet` and the compiled code get it without a check on the read path and without a change to the architecture test.
4. The frame's boundary and the indicators' reset are the same call (`IndiaDays.DayOf` of the start of the bar), so they cannot disagree.
5. Counting the bars the timeframe actually closed, not clock minutes, closes limits (b), (c) and (d) of Amendment 19 at once; limit (a) was never reachable (next section).
6. The cost is one date computation and a compare per consumed bar of a timeframe that has a restarting feature, plus one per step (measured below).
7. Uniform `prev_day_*` and `gap_pct` (decision 5).
8. The narrowed warning keeps a protection that would otherwise become a silent never-true condition (decision 7).

## Corrections to earlier claims
- **ADR 0034 Amendment 19 and ISS-13: "the stronger rule needs a new member of `FeatureFrame` (the session of each history slot)".** False. No member was added; the architecture test of `XDrishti.Strategies` is byte-identical and green (`FrameMembers` is still `get_Item`, `At`, `get_Steps`, `get_LastBarTime`, `HandleOf`, `get_HistoryDepth`, `Create`).
- **Limit (a): `close > or_high(30)[1]` on 15m compares with yesterday's range.** It could not be written (a subscript on a call is accepted since ADR 0108, which makes it writable): a subscript on a call was the syntax problem `strategy.syntax.subscript_on_non_reference` ([ADR 0034](0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md) Amendment 8), and `or_high` with parameters is always a call. The real exposure was a crossing or a run against a multi-bar opening range when a session's first bar is the last minute of the range: the range is then ready on bar 1 while its lag-1 slot still held yesterday's range. The frame rule closes that too. No example in the tests or the documents may use `or_high(n)[k]`.
- **Limits (b), (c), (d).** (b) a 1D strategy that reads an intraday timeframe (`tf("5m").vwap[80]`): now unknown by the rule and warned. (c) the `time` check is a clock, the lag counts bars: the rule counts the bars the timeframe closed. (d) a guard in another section of the strategy file: moot, because no guard is needed.
- **"A rule of its own for `prev_day_*`"** (Amendment 19): decision 5 is that rule — none.
- **`tf("1h").prev_day.close` is not accepted by the parser** (`strategy.syntax.unexpected_token` at the second dot); the accepted form is the flat `tf("1h").prev_day_close`. The notes of this issue used the dotted form; this ADR and the documents use the flat one.
- **The judge's mutation plan said that forgetting to reset the frame's session state (M9) turns `DeterminismAndResetTests` red.** It does not: the mutant is provably equivalent (see the mutation table under *Consequences from the build*).
- **ADR 0034 states the old behaviour as a finding and as the rule built** (the paragraph *Lagged reads are by position, not by session*, the `lag_across_session` row of the lint table, Amendment 19). They stay as the history of what was found and why, with a pointer here; the rule they describe is replaced.

## Idioms
| What a person wants | Write | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The close crosses the vwap, or today's open | `crosses_above(close, vwap)`, `crosses_above(close, today_open)` | known from the second bar of the session; no `time` check is needed for correctness |
| A gap through yesterday's level ("the open jumped over yesterday's high") | `close > prev_day.high and close[1] <= prev_day.high` | the level is read at lag 0 only and the bar is lagged; `close` is continuous, so the bar before the first is yesterday's last close. True on a gap-up first bar, where `crosses_above(close, prev_day.high)` is unknown |
| Yesterday against the day before | `tf("1D").close[1]`, `tf("1D").high[1]`, `tf("1D").gap_pct[1]` | the daily timeframe has one bar per session and nothing restarts on it |
| Yesterday's close, known at the first evaluation of the day | `prev_day_close` on the strategy's own timeframe, or on a finer one (`tf("1m").prev_day_close`) | `tf("1h").prev_day_close` on a 5m strategy is unknown until the first 1h bar of the day has closed (flat spelling; `tf("1h").prev_day.close` is refused by the parser) |
| Wait for the opening range, or avoid the open | `time >= "09:45"` | a trading rule now, no longer a correctness device |
| A filter that negates a crossing | accept that the first bar is skipped | `not crosses_below(close, vwap)` is unknown, not true, on the first bar of the session |
| A level that rises or is crossed | the level is the other operand | `today_open`, `prev_day_*`, `gap_pct` and a closed `or_*` are constant within a session, so `rising(today_open, 1)` is never true intraday; `rising(today_high, 1)` now means "the high of the day was raised on this bar" and needs bar 2 |

## Why it is safe
- **It can only turn a value into NaN.** It never returns a different or a nearer value, so everything that kept the language from reading the future still holds: the seven members of `FeatureFrame` the compiled library may reference, the audit of the expression trees (no negative lag, additive shape, kernel-only calls) and the oracle. The state of the frame at step t depends on the bars up to t only (the correctness review traced it).
- **Unknown is not true.** The worst outcome of the rule is a condition that does not fire where it used to fire by accident, never one that fires wrongly. The risk it creates is the opposite — a condition that is silently never true — and that is what the narrowed warning, the idioms and the documents are for.
- **The boundary is the indicators' own.** Frame and indicator use the same call on the same bar, so they cannot disagree about where a session starts.
- **Three independent checks agree.** (1) The cold-start property of the frame tests: at every step of long seeded series the long-running frame reads as a fresh frame created at the first accepted bar of the date. (2) A reference written from the definition (a bucket builder and the catalog indicator, S counted from the start dates of the buckets) behind the interpreter oracle. (3) The correctness review's own model — its own bucket arithmetic, no library indicators, 12 seeds of 45 days with holidays, Saturdays, sparse and partial sessions, six timeframes × seven names × lags 0 to 7 against `At` and `IsReadyAt` — 21,887,712 comparisons (5,988,843 unknown by the rule), no mismatch.
- **No allocation and no new nondeterminism.** Steady state allocates exactly 0 bytes, across date changes too. The new state (a pending flag in four indicators, a day on each restarting lane, the frame's session day) is cleared by `Reset`; the determinism and reset tests, and `Reset` while pending, cover it.

## Consequences
- **For rules.** Crossings, `rising/falling` and lags of these features are unknown for the first k evaluations of the feature's timeframe in each session: crossings from its second bar, `rising(vwap, 3)` from its fourth. A decision treats unknown as false and `EvaluateTruth` shows `Unknown`; `not` of an unknown stays unknown. `crosses_above(close, vwap)` and its siblings compile with no problem, with or without a `time` check. A `time >= "HH:MM"` check is a trading rule now.
- **For P3-03 and the live engine** (the same frame, the same code): the first evaluations of a session are unknown and count as "no signal". A frame must be fed whole sessions: a live restart replays the current session from its first bar, because a frame started mid-day takes that bar's date as its session (`prev_day_*`, `gap_pct` and the rolling features still need the earlier sessions). A bar of the old session that closes late is evaluated in the new session with its restarting features unknown — intended, conservative, and a case for P3-03's late-close policy. A hazard that predates this ADR and is not closed by it also belongs there: with the 15:29 minute missing, a 1D evaluation of Monday's bar happens at Tuesday's first minute, where a `tf("1m")` read is already Tuesday's. The P3-03 spec, when it is drafted, states these facts.
- **For the strategy file (P3-02b).** The check "a `time` guard in another section" has no purpose any more; nothing has to make a guard visible across sections.
- **For the code.** One public member is added (`IndicatorInfo.RestartsEachSession`) and one removed (`IndicatorInfo.HasValueAtSessionOpen`; no other user in the repository). The code and tests already cite "ADR 0035" (45 references in 32 files, 18 of them in `backend/src`), so the number is fixed.
- **Not affected.** The six flagged deviations of [ADR 0033](0033-weekly-monthly-timeframes-calendar-features-and-the-market-context.md) (its open question 8) are unrelated to the per-session features and are not touched by this decision.

## Consequences from the build
What was learnt while building ISS-13; the structure of the change first, then the measurements.

**The code.** `IRestartingIndicator` (internal, own file) with `MarkPending()`, implemented explicitly by `Vwap`, `OrHigh`, `OrLow` and `SessionContext`: each has a private `_pending` flag that `Update` and `Reset` clear, `IsReady` is `!_pending && …` and `Value` is `IsReady ? … : NaN` (`OrHigh`/`OrLow` gained the gate), so `IsReady` stays exactly "the value is not NaN" (ADR 0031 Amendment 8). In `FeatureFrame`, `Lane` knows its `RestartSlots` and `Restarting` instances (a catalog entry that restarts but forgets the interface fails at `Create`). `Consume` clears the restarting slots of the ring (`ClearRestartHistory`: `Span.Fill(NaN)` on `History[slot × depth …]`, no allocation) at the lane's first bar of a new date and records the date; `Step` ends with `StartSession`, which runs **after the lanes consumed the minute and after the ordering guard** and, when the minute starts a new date, marks the restarting indicators of every lane that has not consumed a bar of it pending and clears their history. The order is load-bearing: a bucket of the old date that closes late has been consumed with its own date by then, and a double close leaves the lane on the new date so the pass skips it. Mutations M6 and M6b below show the tests notice a change of that order. `Lane.Day`/`DayKnown` are reused for the restarting lanes. `CompilerWarmup`'s valid set gained `rising(vwap, 75)` (on its default 5m), the only source that still gets `lag_across_session`; `CompilerWarmupTests` requires the compiler warm-up to report that code.

**Where the build departed from the judge's binding design**, with the reason:
- The reference behind the interpreter oracle is **not** built from cold-start shadow frames. A never-reset frame already runs the new rule, so the design's "run-on" control could not disagree with anything; and the shadow frame fed the previous date for `prev_day_*` runs the code under test (`StartSession`, `ClearRestartHistory`) inside the reference. `SessionReference` is written from the definition: per feature a public `TimeframeBuilder` and the catalog indicator are fed every accepted bar, the start date and value of each closed bar are recorded, and a read is masked by counting S from those start dates with `SessionClock.DateOf` of the Domain. It has no clearing, no pending flag and no `FeatureFrame`, writes the ordering guard again and keeps its own list of the twelve names (a test pins it to the catalog). The shadow-frame form is kept where it is valid: `FrameColdStartTests` at frame level. Limit, as the judge said: the values *inside* a session still come from the same indicator code and `TimeframeBuilder` (the older reference-indicator tests cover those).
- The negative controls are built from raw pieces: a positional reference (the frame as it was read before) at frame level, and `SessionReference(runOn: true)` with the mask off at oracle level.
- Near-miss rows on 1m cannot be standalone examples (`vwap[374]` and `vwap[375]` are both `lag_too_deep`); the compile-clean boundaries are 74/75, 37/38, 24/25, 12/13 and 6/7, checked on several strategy timeframes including 1D. The message always says "up to N closed bars back" (a warned read is never fewer than 7 bars back) and carries no `Suggestion`: the advice is in the text.
- `StartSession` also writes `lane.Day`/`DayKnown`, as the design said; those two writes are behaviourally redundant (M14) and kept because the design specified them.
- The expectation of `EvaluationAllocationTests` was wrong in the plan: with an evaluation at every 1-minute step, condition 1 (`crosses_above(close, vwap)` on 5m) is unknown at minutes 0 to 8 of a session (pending until the first 5m bar closes at minute 4, then lag 1 needs the second at minute 9), and condition 6 (`tf("1D").close[1] < close and close > prev_day.high`) is also unknown at minutes 0 to 3 when its first conjunct holds. The test asserts both windows by arithmetic from the bars.

**Known limits of the rule** (honest list; ISS-17 in the roadmap issues log):
- **Pending is conservative** (decision 3): `tf("1h").prev_day_close` is unknown for up to an hour although the value is known.
- **A lag that fits a session but is large is silently unknown.** `rising(vwap, 60)` on 1m is unknown for the first 60 minutes of every session and is not warned; only a lag a whole session cannot hold is.
- **The warning ignores the warm-up of a multi-bar opening range.** `rising(or_high(30), 24) or falling(or_high(30), 24)` on 15m: `or_high(30)` is ready from bar 2, lag 24 at the 25th bar is bar 1, so the condition is unknown at all 250 evaluations of 10 sessions while `L × T` = 360 < 375 gives no warning (with lag 23 it is known once per session). Not a wrong value; the correction `lag + WarmUp(spec) − 1 ≥ bars in a session` is a follow-up (the boundaries of the other names would not move).
- **A late-closed bucket of the previous session is evaluated with its restarting features unknown**, a morning and an evening block on one date are one session, and a frame fed from mid-day starts its session there.
- **Hidden state.** The readiness of four indicators now depends on a flag the frame sets, not on the bars fed to the indicator alone; documented in `IIndicator` and `IRestartingIndicator`, and covered by `Reset`, determinism tests and the mutations above.
- **Independence.** The oracle's reference and the frame-level cold-start property share `TimeframeBuilder` and the indicator code with production; a bug inside an indicator or a builder is caught by the older reference-indicator tests, not by these.
- **Cost of the first bar** (above; ISS-15) and **no automated guard** for the frame-step, evaluation and cold-start budgets (ISS-16).
- **Open reviewer minors (ISS-17):** the bars-per-session formula `(375 + T − 1) / T` exists twice (the lint and `IndicatorCatalog`; `SessionLagTruthTests` guards the drift); the 480 tests carry the process word "Adversarial" in their file names, overlap the implementer's suites, and `XDrishti.Indicators.Tests` takes about 40 s wall (one 1,400-day case alone 12 s); the list of twelve names is repeated in several test files.
