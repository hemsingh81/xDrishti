# 0025 — Dhan trade history and ledger are read nightly; lots are rebuilt automatically where it is safe

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-10-14: "approved" on [P2-13](../specs/P2-13-dhan-trade-history-and-ledger-sync.md)) — builds on [ADR 0023](0023-trade-import-and-lot-reconstruction.md) (transactions, reconstruction, "a later story reuses these tables with `source = broker`"), [ADR 0012](0012-holdings-acquisition-dates-and-returns-model.md) and [ADR 0018](0018-services-framework-and-editable-schedules.md)
- **Date:** 2026-10-14

## Context
ADR 0023 stores trades from a CSV or by hand and rebuilds lots with real dates, but the owner still has to export a tradebook. Dhan serves the same facts (`GET /v2/trades/{from}/{to}/{page}`) and the account's cash movements (`GET /v2/ledger`). The roadmap's P2 row names both. The Dhan field names used here come from Dhan's public documentation and its published samples (amendment g), not from a real answer of the owner's account.

## Decisions
1. **Two read-only endpoints are added to the broker allow-list**, nothing else: the trade history (path with two dates and a page number, no query) and the ledger (exactly the query keys `from-date` and `to-date`). The holdings client's limits apply: HTTPS, no redirects, 30 s, answers ≤ 5 MB, token only in the header, no content logged.
2. **Broker trades are ordinary transactions** (`source = broker`, run id as provenance). Their identity is `t:yyyyMMdd:` + the exchange trade id — the same identity the CSV reader gives a tradebook's trade id — so the same trade from a CSV and from Dhan is stored once. Only NSE equity rows are kept, and of those every row that is not an intraday product; other segments and the intraday products are counted per reason (amendment c). A trade row carries its six rupee charge fields; their sum is stored as the transaction's `charges` and the ISIN is stored too (amendment g).
3. **Ledger entries are stored, immutable, with a signed amount and a class** (`funds_in`, `funds_out`, `dividend`, `charges`, `other`) from a pure classifier over the voucher description and narration. Unrecognised entries are `other` and keep their raw text (untrusted, length-capped, rendered as text). Dividends are account-level; they are not matched to holdings (narrations are free text). Dhan credits dividends to the bank account, so the trading ledger rarely holds one (amendment g).
4. **Returns do not change.** Dividends and charges are shown in a Cash & dividends panel, not added to cash flows, XIRR, returns or the value history; the UI says so. Putting them in was a separate decision, taken in P2-14 ([ADR 0026](0026-dividends-entered-by-the-owner-and-charges-in-net-returns.md)): the per-trade charges and the ledger's account-level costs count in a **net** return next to the unchanged gross one, as a separate line and never in the cost or the lots. As built in P2-13 the per-trade charges stored with broker trades were informational.
5. **Runs.** A backfill from the account's first-import date in windows of at most 30 days (a constant; a window Dhan rejects as too large, or answers DH-907 for on page 0, is halved once; amendments a and b), then incremental from a per-account watermark. Each window is one unit of work with its run row and the watermark move, so a failure re-reads the window and the identity dedupe makes that safe. Runs never overlap with each other or with a holdings sync of the account. A scheduled service (ADR 0018) runs on trading days at `sync.time` (16:30 IST); the manual action works any time.
6. **Automatic rebuild (owner's decision).** After a trade sync every holding whose rebuild result is `ok` is rebuilt with the same pure reconstructor and unit-of-work rules as ADR 0023, with no preview. Because a rebuild replaces all lots of the holding, three safeguards apply: a holding with a **hand-dated** lot is not rebuilt and is reported `needs_review` (the owner uses the existing preview-then-rebuild); a rebuild is skipped when the holding changed during the run (`xmin`), and the next run retries; every automatic rebuild is audited (`LotsRebuilt`, `trigger = broker_sync`, counts only). The rebuild is skipped when the run stored nothing, the previous finished trades run left nothing to retry and no holding appeared or gained a lot since (amendment e). `quantity_mismatch`, `ambiguous`, `no_holding` and `unchanged` are left alone and counted.
7. **A voided broker trade comes back** when its day is read again (its identity is free, as for CSV). A "never re-import" mark is a follow-up.

## Consequences
- The owner gets true dates and real sale prices without exporting anything; the hand-dated lots stay safe unless the owner asks for a rebuild.
- An automatic rebuild can change lot dates and therefore XIRR without a preview; the run result and the audit trail say what happened.
- Field names and Dhan's real limits (page size; date span and history depth are not documented by Dhan at all) are unverified until the owner's first real read; the mapping is tolerant and the window and page cap are constants.
- The ledger adds a second source of truth about cash; from P2-14 its account-level costs (and only those) count in the net returns.

## Amendments (2026-10-14)
The independent review of the first implementation led to six decisions (a–f), and the mapping against Dhan's published sample to one more (g); the spec criteria they change are marked there too ([P2-13](../specs/P2-13-dhan-trade-history-and-ledger-sync.md)).
- **a. A `from` that would leave a hole is refused** (`400 portfolio.invalid`, field `from`): later than the day after the older of the two watermarks, because it would leave dates that are never read. When exactly one kind was never read, `from` may not be later than the account's first-import date (the history of that kind would otherwise begin at the chosen date). An earlier `from` re-reads; the identities make that harmless. An account where neither kind was read may start at any date. The pipeline keeps a defensive guard behind this check and skips the rebuild for a run whose start would leave a hole.
- **b. DH-907 ("incorrect parameters or no data") is not always an empty result.** It ends the paging as empty only for page ≥ 1. On page 0 of a window longer than one day it is *unclear*: the window is halved once and the halves are used; a half that also answers DH-907 on page 0 is taken as empty and counted (`emptyByNoData` in the run's counts). This narrows the first version of decision 5 and of spec criterion 4, which read DH-907 as "no data". On a one-day window it stays an empty result (counted). **The ledger follows the same rule** (it has no paging, one call per window): a multi-day window answering DH-907 is halved once, and a half or a one-day window answering it is empty and counted. The status line says "Dhan answered "no data" for N date ranges" only when more ranges answered no data than windows were read, i.e. windows were halved and still empty. The holdings read keeps its own rule (doc 05 §8.2) and the chart client still treats DH-907 as a failure (doc 05 §6).
- **c. Every NSE_EQ row that is not an intraday product is kept.** Rows with the intraday products `INTRADAY`, `INTRA`, `MIS`, `CO`, `BO` are skipped and counted as `intraday` (they net out within the day), next to the other skip reasons of decision 2. Delivery rows (`CNC`, `MTF`), `MARGIN` (on `NSE_EQ` only a position carried overnight; skipping it would cause quantity mismatches) and rows with no product are kept.
- **d. Run bookkeeping and two documented limits.** Every exit of a run completes it (`failed` with a plain, safe error). The status figures (`holdingsRebuilt`, `holdingsNeedingReview`, `needsReviewSymbols`, at most 50 tickers) come from the latest **succeeded** trades run and no other, nothing carried forward: a quiet night whose rebuild was skipped shows 0 rebuilt, and a skipped (expired token), failed or running run neither changes nor erases them. A tradebook CSV row without a trade id has the `h:` hash identity, so the same Dhan trade (`t:`) is stored twice; this is a known limit and no attempt is made to match the two.
- **e. The automatic rebuild is skipped** when the run stored nothing **and** the previous finished trades run succeeded with nothing left to retry (nothing other than `no_holding`) **and** no holding of the account appeared, was reopened or gained a lot after that run ended (`IHoldingStore.AnyAppearedSinceAsync`); a run after a failed run always rebuilds. A quiet evening then changes no lots and leaves no `LotsRebuilt` event. (The skip for a start that leaves a hole is a defensive guard behind amendment a.)
- **f. The owner's lot-date edit and the automatic rebuild.** `UpdateLotDate` marks the holding as changed, so an automatic rebuild running at the same moment is skipped as `changed_meanwhile` (the next run retries) instead of overwriting the date just set; the edit is repeated once on what is stored, and a second concurrency conflict answers `409 portfolio.conflict`.
- **g. Mapping against Dhan's published sample (read 2026-10-14 from https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/statements/; fixtures `dhan-published-trade.json` and `dhan-published-ledger.json`).** It corrects decision 2 (which said Dhan's trade API carries no charges and no ISIN) and the trade and ledger mapping.
  1. *The published trade row* has `tradingSymbol` **null** (and `customSymbol`, a company **name**, which is never used as a ticker), `isin`, `securityId` and six rupee charge fields (`sebiTax`, `stt`, `brokerageCharges`, `serviceTax`, `exchangeTransactionCharges`, `stampDuty`); `createTime` and `updateTime` are `"NA"` (missing), the time is `exchangeTime`.
  2. *Charges and ISIN are stored.* `charges` = the sum of the six fields (4 decimals; numbers, strings or `"NA"` tolerated) on the transaction; the ISIN is stored. Informational in P2-13: lots, cost and XIRR do not use charges (P2-14 counts them in the net returns only, [ADR 0026](0026-dividends-entered-by-the-owner-and-charges-in-net-returns.md)).
  3. *Symbol resolution* when `tradingSymbol` is missing: Dhan's `securityId` through `market.instrument_broker_ids` (`dhan`, `NSE_EQ`), then the ISIN. Unresolved but ISIN known: stored ISIN-only (empty ticker), like the CSV path, and matched to holdings by ISIN. Neither: not stored, counted as skip reason `no_symbol`.
  4. *Ledger.* The published sample is a single object with string fields (`voucherdate` `Jun 22, 2022`, `voucherdesc` `PAYBNK`, `narration` `FUNDS WITHDRAWAL`); one object (or one under `data`) is read as one row. The classifier knows `PAYBNK` (`funds_out`), `FUNDS ADDED` / `PAYIN` (`funds_in`) and `DPC Interest` (`charges`).
  5. *Limits.* Dhan documents no maximum span and no history depth for either endpoint. An earlier `from` is accepted (floor 1990-01-02); a window Dhan rejects (DH-905) ends the run `failed` with the "history too large" text; far-back reads mean many 30-day windows.
  6. *Dividends.* Dhan credits them to the bank account; they are not in the trading ledger (a `dividend` entry is rare, e.g. rented stocks). The owner enters them from P2-14 ([ADR 0026](0026-dividends-entered-by-the-owner-and-charges-in-net-returns.md)); the Cash & dividends panel shows whatever the ledger holds.
- **h. Trades stored before amendment g are completed when read again (enrich step, added with P2-14, [ADR 0026](0026-dividends-entered-by-the-owner-and-charges-in-net-returns.md)).** The first implementation stored `charges = 0` and no ISIN, and dropped rows without a `tradingSymbol` (a read now stores those as new trades); a read that finds such a trade stored already now fills in what is missing and nothing else: the ISIN when the trade has none, the ticker when it is empty, the charges when they are 0 and the read has some (`Transaction.EnrichFromBroker`). It only looks at the account's non-voided trades of `source = broker` among the rows of the window read; it never changes the identity, side, date, quantity or price, never overwrites a value that is there, and leaves CSV and manual trades alone. The completed trades are counted as `enriched` (a part of `duplicates`) in the run's counts and in the counts-only audit text; a run that enriched trades re-works the lots like one that stored trades (an ISIN may now attach a trade to its holding). The nightly read starts at the watermark, so the owner completes older trades once with *Read from an earlier date*. Charges this adds are the ones the net returns count, which is why the step belongs to the same change as ADR 0026.

Consequences: a systematic parameter error cannot pass as "no trades" without a visible `emptyByNoData` count; an owner who chooses a late `from` cannot create an unread gap; the first real read still decides the window size, the DH-905 / DH-907 behaviour and the field names.
