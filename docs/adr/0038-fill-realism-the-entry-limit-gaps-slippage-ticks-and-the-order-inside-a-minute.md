# 0038 — Fill realism: the entry limit, gaps, slippage, ticks and the order inside one minute

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-10-18: "yes to all recommendations" on [P3-03b](../specs/P3-03b-fill-realism-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-minute-order.md), open questions 1 and 2 as recommended; the nine decisions the spec took on research when it was drafted — *Decisions this draft takes* — are the owner's to revert). **Supersedes decisions D3, D4, D5 and D7 of [ADR 0037](0037-simulation-conventions-and-the-two-stage-seam.md)**; D1 (the seam), D6 (the counters, extended here), D8 (placement) and D9 (the version constant, raised here) stand. ADR 0037 carries a dated pointer to this one and its decisions are not edited. **Decision 8 (`SimTrade` schema 2) is superseded by [ADR 0040](0040-swing-positions-held-across-sessions-the-maximum-hold-the-position-gate-and-simtrade-schema-3.md) (schema 3, 2026-10-18), which also extends decisions 2, 9 and 11 for the swing walk; this ADR's text is not edited.**
- **Relation to other ADRs:** builds on ADR 0037 (the two-stage seam, the walker, the 5-minute bound on the fill delay), [ADR 0010](0010-instrument-master-sources-and-scope.md) (`TICK_SIZE`) and [ADR 0002](0002-config-and-strategies-in-app.md) (trading configuration is data: in P3 the doc 16 numbers are code defaults and every run stores the values it used).
- **Date:** 2026-10-18 (built the same day)
- **Pointer (2026-10-19):** the entry of a candidate decided at or after the end of its session (a `NoFillBar` here) is changed by [ADR 0043](0043-the-daily-evaluation-and-the-overnight-entry-order-sim-5-and-run-record-schema-5.md): the order rests overnight and fills at the next session's open within the limit (E8, E9). The decisions above are not edited.

## In short
- **The entry is a limit order** placed when the trigger bar has closed: trigger close +0.1 % rounded down to the tick (a sell: −0.1 %, up). It lives for one minute and fills at the open when the open is within the limit, at the limit when the minute trades back to it, and is otherwise a counted miss, `LimitNotReached`, never filled later.
- **Gaps cost what they cost.** A stop the market gapped through fills at that minute's open; a target fills at the target and never better; a minute that holds the stop and the target is a stop, unless its open is already beyond the target; the square-off waits for the next minute that holds a trade.
- **Slippage on a price grid.** Market-type fills pay `(Ticks + extra) × tick + BarRangeFrac × range` against the trade and are rounded to the instrument's tick; a resting limit does not slip. The exact result stays beside the modelled one on every trade.
- **A minute with volume 0 holds no trade** and is read like an absent one.
- **Everything is `decimal`.** `double` arithmetic gets the grid wrong.

## Decisions
1. **D3b — Entry (supersedes D3).** E0: a minute with `Volume` 0 holds no trade; in every row below it is read like an absent minute and counted in `ZeroVolumeMinutes` (the real files have them: 740 of 192,591 minutes of one sampled file, 689 of them flat). E1: the *fill minute* is the first minute after the candidate's own that holds a trade and starts at or after the decision instant; in this order, none, or on another India date, or more than `SimulationEngine.MaxFillDelayMinutes` (5, unchanged from ADR 0037) after the decision instant is `NoFillBar`, and starting at or after the square-off is `AfterSquareOff`. E2: `limit = tickDown(trigger close × (1 + p))` for a buy, `tickUp(trigger close × (1 − p))` for a sell, `p = FillRules.EntryLimitPercent / 100`, computed in decimal and rounded **in both fill modes** (it is an order price). E3: open within the limit fills at the open (`AtOpen`). E4: the open beyond the limit and the minute's low (a sell: high) touching it fills at the limit (`AtLimit`); touching is a fill. E5: otherwise `LimitNotReached`, final: a later minute that would have been within the limit does not fill it (decision 4 of the spec's list, open question 2). E6: a fill at or through the stop is `EntryThroughStop`, which guarantees `risk > 0`; a risk under four ticks is counted (`RiskUnderFourTicks`). E7: only the first trigger of a date is a candidate (P3-03a), so a miss spends the day's ticket; that is the scanner's test. The walker reads one thing of the trigger bar: its close.
2. **D4b — Exit (supersedes D4).** From the fill minute, minute by minute on the entry date, passing over a minute without a trade; the first row that applies decides. **X1:** the first minute that holds a trade at or after the square-off minute `Q` closes the position at its open and its range is not used: an open beyond the stop exits at the open, reason `Stop`; else an open beyond the target exits at the **target**, reason `Target`; else `SquareOff` at the open (`LateSquareOffs` when the minute starts after `Q`). **X2:** a touched stop exits at `min(stop, open)` (a sell `max`), `Stop`; **X3:** a touched target exits at the target, `Target`. **X4:** a minute that reaches both is a stop, except that if its open is already beyond the target the first print decides for the target; it is *ambiguous* (`AmbiguousMinutes`) when both are reached and the open lies strictly between them. **X5:** in the fill minute of an `AtLimit` entry only the stop is checked (the high may have come before the fill). **X6:** with no minute that holds a trade at or after `Q`, exit at the close of the last minute of the date that holds one, `EndOfData`, reported apart. `StopGapExits` and `TargetGapExits` count exits whose minute opens *strictly* beyond the level.
3. **D5b — R, MAE, MFE (supersedes D5).** `RExact = d × (exit − entry) / risk` from the exact prices, `risk = |entry − stop|` of the exact entry (the unit of every R); `RFilled` uses the filled prices and the same risk, so `SlippageR = RFilled − RExact ≤ 0` is exact. MAE and MFE are those of ADR 0037 D5 (in R from the exact entry, never below 0, unaffected by slippage, `−MAE ≤ RExact ≤ MFE`; after a `Stop` the exit's own distance counts as adverse, after a `Target` MFE is `k`; a `SquareOff` minute counts its open and not its range) with the additions: a minute whose open is already beyond the level it exits at (a gapped stop or target, X1, the exception of X4) ends the trade at that first print and contributes no range, so after such a `Target` only MFE = `k` counts; X5 leaves the high of an `AtLimit` fill minute (a sell: the low) out of MFE; a minute without a trade counts nowhere. R, MAE, MFE, every level and every comparison of a bar price with a level are computed in `decimal` from doubles rounded to 8 decimals; only the `SimTrade` fields are doubles.
4. **Slippage (S1 to S4).** Market-type fills slip: the entry `AtOpen`, stop exits (including the stop of X1), `SquareOff` exits and `EndOfData` exits. A resting limit does not: the `AtLimit` entry and the target exit (`touch_is_fill`). Amount `a = (Ticks + extra) × tick + BarRangeFrac × (high − low)` of the minute the fill happens in, `extra = StopExitExtraTicks` for a stop exit; a flat minute slips by the ticks only. Always against the trade: entry buy `min(tickUp(entry + a), limit)`, sell `max(tickDown(entry − a), limit)` (the entry never passes the limit); exit of a long `tickDown(exit − a)`, of a short `tickUp(exit + a)`; a target exit only goes on the grid. **Decisions use the exact prices**: which minute fills, which row exits and the reason never depend on slippage or the grid. With `SlippageConfig.None` no fill price is moved or rounded and the fills are `Exact`; otherwise `Modelled`.
5. **`SlippageConfig`, `FillRules` and `TickSpec` (Domain.Research).** Sealed records with a private constructor and a validating `Create`: `SlippageConfig` (`Ticks`, `BarRangeFrac`, `StopExitExtraTicks`, `GapFill`; `Default` = 1, 0.05, 2, `AtOpen` from doc 16; `None`; `simulation.slippage_invalid`), `FillRules` (the limit percent, 0 to 5, and the slippage; `Default` = 0.1 %; `Basis`: `Exact` when the slippage is `None`, else `Modelled`; `ToCanonicalJson()`, invariant culture, fixed key order, shortest round-trip numbers, the slippage keys of doc 16: `{"entry_limit_percent":0.1,"slippage":{"ticks":1,"bar_range_frac":0.05,"stop_exit_extra_ticks":2,"gap_fill":"at_open"}}`; `simulation.fill_rules_invalid`). `WalkRules(RewardRisk, SquareOffMinute, Fills, Tick)` is positional and breaking by design; `TradeWalker.Walk` refuses a value that bypassed `Create` (`default(WalkRules)`) with `simulation.fill_rules_invalid` or `simulation.tick_size_invalid`, before anything else is checked.
6. **The tick and its unit.** The instrument master gives `TICK_SIZE` in **paise**: the committed extract of the real file (75 rows, `backend/tests/XDrishti.Application.Tests/Fixtures/dhan-master-sample.csv`) holds `1.0000`, `5.0000` and `10.0000` for NSE shares of series EQ and `10.0000` for the RELIANCE and NIFTY futures, which are ₹0.01, 0.05 and 0.10 (the four index rows hold `0.0500` and are no evidence). `TickSpec.FromMasterPaise(decimal?)` divides by 100 and is the **only** place that does; null gives `0.05` (the NSE equity tick) with `FromFallback = true` and the trade carries `TickFallback`; a size outside `0 < tick ≤ 1` rupee after the conversion is refused (`simulation.tick_size_invalid`) and **not** replaced by the fallback (the range is a net, not the guard of the unit: an unconverted `1.0000` passes it, which is why the conversion is one named function with its own tests). **Verified on the owner's data, 2026-10-19 (done; P3-04b2).** The stored table `market.instruments.tick_size` holds paise for shares and ETFs, read-only with `psql` on the local database: shares have the ticks `1.0000` (1,400 instruments), `5.0000` (1,343), `10.0000` (392), `50.0000` (38), `100.0000` (21) and `500.0000` (11); ETFs `1.0000` (400) and `5.0000` (8). Examples: `NSE:RELIANCE` `10.0000` (₹0.10), `NSE:TCS` `10.0000`, `NSE:SBIN` `5.0000` (₹0.05), `NSE:NIFTYBEES` `1.0000` and `NSE:GOLDBEES` `1.0000` (₹0.01). The smallest stored tick of a share is `1.0000` (a stored `0.0100` would have been rupees). The index rows hold `0.0000` to `0.0500` (rupees, not paise: no evidence, and the gate does not apply to them) and the futures rows `1.0000` to `500.0000`. A first run of the verb over three instruments used ticks of 0.05 (SBIN) and 0.10 (RELIANCE, TCS), as the conversion says. The open item of this decision is closed; `TickUnitCheck` (spec P3-04b1, E8) remains as a net: the verb refuses a run before any bar is read when a share or an ETF has a stored tick that converts to less than ₹0.01 ([ADR 0041](0041-the-simulate-verb-the-export-library-and-the-pipeline-in-the-host.md), decision 4).
7. **`TickGrid` (internal to `Walking`).** `Down` and `Up` in decimal; `TryConvert` turns a double price into a decimal rounded to 8 places and refuses what is not finite or beyond 1e12 in magnitude (a price, a level, a reward-to-risk or a slippage figure: far beyond any share price, and small enough that `reward-to-risk × risk`, at most 2e24, stays far inside the 7.9e28 of `decimal`; with a limit of 1e18 that product could be 1e36 and a decimal conversion would throw). Plain double arithmetic gets the grid wrong (`floor(100.00 × 1.001 / 0.05) × 0.05` is 100.05, not 100.10) and a double target is above the decimal one in about a third of the cases (`100.00 + 2 × 1.15` is 102.30000000000001), so a minute that touches the target exactly would be no touch. The grid never rounds a stop or a target level; equality is exact. `SimTrade.StopPrice` is the candidate's stop as converted (8 places), so that a stop exit equals it exactly.
8. **`SimTrade` schema 2 (supersedes D7).** `SimTrade.CurrentSchemaVersion` is 2 and the positional properties are P3-03's 24 in their order with #19 renamed `RExact`, followed by #25 `RFilled`, #26 `SlippageR`, #27 `EntryFilledPrice`, #28 `ExitFilledPrice` (doubles), #29 `EntryFill` (`AtOpen`, `AtLimit`) and #30 `TickFallback`. New meanings: #13 `EntryPrice` is the exact entry (the open of the fill minute or the limit); #14 and #15 are the levels, never rounded to the tick; #17 `ExitPrice` is the exact exit of the table; #27 and #28 are the same prices after S2 and S3 (equal to #13 and #17 when the fills are `Exact`). `FillBasis` is `Exact, Modelled`; `NoTradeReason` gains `LimitNotReached`. `Costs` is still `None` (P3-03c); `EntrySnapshotJson` is still null. A test reads the constructor by reflection and fails until the version is raised and an ADR says why.
9. **Counters (extends D6).** `WalkOutcome` gains `AmbiguousMinute`, `LateSquareOff`, `RiskUnderFourTicks` and `ZeroVolumeMinutes`; `WalkCounters` gains, appended at the end so that no positional use moves, `LimitNotReached`, `EntriesAtOpen`, `EntriesAtLimit`, `AmbiguousMinutes`, `LateSquareOffs`, `ZeroVolumeMinutes` and `RiskUnderFourTicks`. Identities: `Candidates = Trades + NoFillBar + AfterSquareOff + EntryThroughStop + LimitNotReached`, `Trades = EntriesAtOpen + EntriesAtLimit` and `Trades` is the sum of the four exit counts. Decided in the build: **`BarsExamined` counts the minutes that hold a trade** from the fill minute to the exit minute, both included (0 for `NoFillBar` and `AfterSquareOff`, 1 for `EntryThroughStop` and `LimitNotReached`); **`ZeroVolumeMinutes` counts the empty minutes passed over between the decision instant and the exit**, or, for a no-trade, before the minute that decided it; the empty minutes after the last minute that holds a trade at the end of the data are not counted.
10. **`SimulationEngine.Version` is `sim-2`**, and every golden file is regenerated on purpose (the walker's and the scanner's, which differs in its first line only). The run header's first two labels come from `FillDescription.Labels(rules)`: `fills: modelled` (or `fills: exact` when the slippage is `None`) and `entry limit: +0.1 %` (the percent in the shortest round-trip form); they replace `fills: exact` and `entry limit: not applied` of ADR 0037 D7; the other three labels and the header are P3-03a's.
11. **`FillLimits.NotModelled`** is the one list of what the fills do not model, in this order, each with a code and an English text that the banner of P3-04b prints: `auction_open`, `circuit_locks`, `touch_is_fill`, `no_queue_or_impact`, `grid_approximate`, `minute_path`, `hole_is_no_trade`, `one_minute_entry`, `no_latency`. They go to the issues log as *measure on real data* (ISS-18).
12. **Failures that are not an outcome** gain `simulation.fill_rules_invalid`, `simulation.tick_size_invalid` (before all others) and, decided in the build, `simulation.bar_price_invalid` for a visited minute whose price is not a finite number (a `decimal` conversion must never throw); a trigger bar whose close is not finite, or beyond the 1e12 above, is `simulation.rules_invalid`. The codes `simulation.bars_not_ascending`, `simulation.rules_invalid` and `simulation.candidate_not_in_bars` are declared once in `SimulationErrors` (as constants) and used from `TradeWalker` and `SimulatorCodes`; their values are unchanged.
13. **Normalised values, so that no text says `-0` or two scales** (decided in the review of the build). A decimal zero can carry a sign (`-1m * 0m`), and a plain cast turns it into the double `-0.0`; the walker builds `RExact`, `RFilled`, `SlippageR`, MAE and MFE through one function that maps decimal zero to `+0.0`, so the golden files and any JSON say `0`, never `-0` (the regenerated golden files differ from the previous ones in nothing else). `SlippageConfig.Create` and `FillRules.Create` do the same for a `-0.0` argument, so that `ToCanonicalJson()` never writes `-0`. `TickSpec` strips the trailing zeros of its size, so that `FromMasterPaise(5.0000m).Size` and `FromRupees(0.05m).Size` have the same scale and print `0.05`.

## Differences from ADR 0037 (criterion 17)
With `FillRules` of 0.1 % and `SlippageConfig.None` and the trigger close of each candidate at the open of its fill minute (so that the limit does not bind), every F-day and walker row of P3-03 gives its table: prices, R, MAE, MFE, reasons and counters (the new counters are additions). **Two rows differ, and only they:**

| Row | ADR 0037 (sim-1) | This ADR (sim-2) |
|-----|------------------|------------------|
| W13: the minute opens at 106.00, beyond the target 105.50, and reaches the stop 98.00 | `Stop` at 98.00, R −1.0, MAE 1.0, MFE 0 | `Target` at 105.50, R +2.0, MFE 2.0, MAE 0, `TargetGapExits` 1 (X4 exception: the first print decides) |
| W14, stop side: the 15:15 minute opens at 97.50, beyond the stop | `SquareOff` at 97.50, R −1.2 | `Stop` at 97.50, R −1.2, MAE 1.2, `StopGapExits` 1 (X1) |
| W14, target side: the 15:15 minute opens at 106.00, beyond the target | `SquareOff` at 106.00, R +2.2 | `Target` at 105.50, R +2.0, `TargetGapExits` 1 (X1) |

Rules that no row of P3-03 exercises and that therefore add behaviour without changing a P3-03 number: E0 (empty minutes), the high of an `AtLimit` fill minute left out of MFE (X5), and the gapped target with no adverse range (the minute contributes no range). The counters `AmbiguousMinutes` (F3: 1) and `LateSquareOffs` (F7: 1) are new additions to rows that already existed. No other difference was found.

## Consequences
- **Tests.** `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests` gains the 25 hand-calculated trades H1 to H25 (modelled and with no slippage), the entry boundaries B1 to B9, N1 to N5 and the hole and empty-minute cases, the grid arithmetic over 10,000 prices per tick and the 8,000 combinations of the exact-comparison trap in both directions, and the properties over **500 seeded days**: slippage never changes a decision, never improves a fill and is monotone in each parameter; fills lie where the rules say and in modelled mode on the grid within `a + tick`; a closed trade does not depend on the future (8,028 outcomes compared, with the default rules and with none); the unsplit ambiguous minute equals the worse of the two orders its path could have taken; two negative controls (a walker that peeks one minute ahead, caught on 474 of 500 days; one that decides a minute early, caught on all 500) while the honest walker is caught on none; and a non-vacuity test that every counter of the table is hit at least ten times (over the 500 days: 1,749 entries at the open, 21 at the limit, 102 misses, 273 ambiguous minutes, 981 empty minutes passed over, 67 end-of-data exits). The architecture tests list the new Domain types, and keep `TickGrid` internal. Criterion 19 is one check over the 500 days, `FillTimingCheck`: every fill and exit minute holds a trade, the fill minute starts at or after the decision instant and at most 5 minutes after it, before the square-off, the exit is on the entry date and not before the fill, a square-off is at the first minute that holds a trade at or after the square-off (the test's own scan), exact fill prices lie in their minute or are their order's own level, modelled prices are on the tick grid within `a + tick`, `risk > 0` and `−MAE ≤ RExact ≤ MFE`; the signal-minute negative control applies the same check against the true decision instant.
- **Culture tests.** The test host runs in globalization-invariant mode, so `de-DE` and `tr-TR` cannot be loaded; the canonical-JSON and label tests therefore use a clone of the invariant culture with comma decimals and dot grouping (the German separators), which is what changes the text of a number. The dotless-i behaviour of `tr-TR` is not exercised.
- **Allocation.** A walk allocates the same bytes for a position that lives 16 minutes and one that lives 331, with and without slippage; `decimal` is a value type.
- **Handed over.** To P3-03a: the `Simulator` takes `FillRules` and the `TickSpec`, builds `WalkRules`, sums the new counters, takes the first two header labels from `FillDescription.Labels`, and re-runs the F-days of the scanner column with the 0.1 % limit (the F11 day gives a first candidate with no trade, `LimitNotReached`). To P3-04 and P3-04a: the record stores `FillRules.ToCanonicalJson()`, the tick used and `TickFromFallback`, the run key changes with any slippage number, the limit percent or a tick, and the runner reads the tick with the instrument row and converts it with `TickSpec.FromMasterPaise`.
- **Performance.** The comparisons are decimal comparisons of prices converted from `double`, which costs more per minute than P3-03's; the explicit `WalkBenchmarks` prints the throughput and asserts nothing. Measured once, cold (Apple M4 Pro, Release, 1,500 walks over 250 sessions): about 4.6 million visited minutes per second with no slippage and 4.9 million with it, against about 21 million for the double arithmetic of ADR 0037 — about four times slower, still about 70 ms for those 1,500 walks.

## Alternatives considered
- **Fill the entry at the trigger-bar close (doc 07 §2).** Rejected in ADR 0037 and still: no order can get the price of a bar that has closed.
- **The entry order lives until the next evaluation-timeframe bar closes.** More trades and more chasing; one minute is the pessimistic choice, and `LimitNotReached` shows how many setups it drops. To be revisited from that counter on real data (ISS-18).
- **A fill at the carried-forward price in an empty minute.** Would add a tenth `FillLimits` code and one more test; an empty minute holds no trade.
- **Slippage on a resting limit.** Paying the base slippage on targets would take about 0.1 R off every winner, and a touch is still a fill: stated as `touch_is_fill`, not modelled.
- **`double` arithmetic with a tolerance.** A tolerance hides which side of the grid a price is on; decimal makes the equality exact.
