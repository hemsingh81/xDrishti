# 0033 — Weekly and monthly timeframes, calendar features and the market context: daily, as of the previous session

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-10-16: "approved" on [P3-01c](../specs/P3-01c-weekly-monthly-timeframes-and-market-context.md), with the seven recommendations below). The six build deviations that waited for the owner's confirmation (Amendments 1, 2, 5, 7, 13 and 14) were settled by the owner's approval of [P3-01d](../specs/P3-01d-context-data-job-index-and-vix.md) on 2026-10-27: five confirmed as built, Amendment 14 changed (the context now has a maximum age, `ContextOptions.MaxAgeDays`); see *Amendment (2026-10-27, P3-01d)* at the end — amends [ADR 0031](0031-streaming-indicators-and-the-feature-frame.md) and [ADR 0032](0032-frame-history-closed-flags-and-the-remaining-indicators.md) (both left the weekly and monthly builders and the regime and market-context features to this slice)
- **Date:** 2026-10-16 (built the same day: see *Consequences from the build* and *Amendments*)

## Context
The parser (P3-02) needs three more families of building blocks from doc 11 §2: features on the **weekly and monthly** timeframes (a swing strategy on 1D with a weekly higher timeframe, doc 07 §4), the **calendar** (`day_of_week`, `is_expiry_day`) and the **market context** (`regime()`, `index.trend`, `vix_pct`, breadth). The context is the first feature that is not computed from the instrument's own bars — it needs the index and the VIX — and it raises the look-ahead question in a new form: the regime of a day is computed from that day's close, so a strategy that runs during the day must not be able to read it. The weekly and monthly bars raise the older question of *when* a bar of a period that has no fixed last minute (a holiday on Friday, a Saturday session) counts as closed.

## Decisions
The owner's answers of 2026-10-16 to the seven open questions of the spec:
1. **The context is daily and read as of the previous session.** Every context feature changes once per India date and has the value of the latest context day **strictly before** that date: a strategy at 10:30 on a Tuesday sees Monday's close, never Tuesday's. Intraday index features (the index against its own VWAP, its move since the open) are a possible later addition.
2. **Trend rule.** `index_trend` is **+1 (up)** when the index close is above its 50-day average, that average is higher than 5 days ago, and the index's ADX(14) is at least 20; **−1 (down)** for the mirror image; otherwise **0 (range)**. The numbers 50, 5, 14 and 20 (and those of the next decision) are `ContextOptions` (later trading configuration in the database, [ADR 0002](0002-config-and-strategies-in-app.md)).
3. **Volatility rule.** The volatility score of a day is the **mean of two percentile ranks over 252 days: the VIX close and the index's ATR(14) as a percent of its close**. *As approved:* **LOW (0) below 33.3, HIGH (2) from 66.7**, NORMAL (1) in between; when the day has no VIX, the index percentile alone is the score. *As built (Amendments 1, 2 and 13, confirmed 2026-10-27):* the thresholds are exact thirds — LOW below 100/3 (33.33…), HIGH from 200/3 (66.67…) — so a score of exactly one third is NORMAL and a score of exactly two thirds is HIGH (it would be NORMAL under the literal 66.7); the VIX percentile ranks the last 252 days *that have a VIX*, and the index percentile alone is the score on **any** day where the VIX percentile is not ready — a day without a VIX, but also while fewer than 252 days with a VIX exist.
4. **`sector_rs(n)` and the computation of breadth are a story of their own, P3-01d**: the context data job that writes `market.context_daily` (index and VIX bars from Dhan, breadth and sector composites from the tracked stocks' daily bars) **and the reader that builds the `MarketContext` from that table** — P3-01d owns the data and its reader; the simulator (P3-03) only uses the `MarketContext` it gets. It is placed before the simulator needs `by_regime` (P3-03 / P3-04). Here `breadth` is a plain field of the day record (a percent, 0 to 100) that is read, never computed.
5. **Expiry dates are supplied by the caller** (`FrameOptions.ExpiryDays`, from the F&O contracts of the instrument master): NSE has changed its expiry weekdays more than once, so the library does not guess them.
6. **Weekly and monthly bars.** With a `TradingCalendar` a week or month is emitted at the step that closes the **last session of the period**; without one, at the **first bar of the next period** — later, never earlier (see *The closing rule* below). The simulator and the live path always pass the calendar.
7. **One regime index and the VIX.** Nifty 50 by default (the caller feeds the series it chooses); Bank Nifty and the sector indices are not part of the regime.

Design decisions of the spec, approved with it:

8. **A week or month is built from the days of an inner 1D builder**, so the rules that decide which bar closes a day, what a complete day is and which late bar is ignored exist once. Open = first day's open, high = highest high, low = lowest low, close = last day's close, volume = sum, `Bars` = the sum of the days' 1-minute bars, `Complete` = every day that has bars is complete (the running period is not incomplete for being unfinished); the bucket starts at India midnight of the Monday of the week (a Saturday or Sunday session belongs to the Monday-to-Sunday week it falls in) or of the 1st of the month — the stored rules of [doc 05 §4](../design/05-data-and-brokers.md).
9. **1W and 1M are valid timeframes for the continuous indicators** (rolling, trend, momentum, volatility, patterns, raw bars); warm-up is still counted in bars of the feature's own timeframe (`ema(10)` on 1W needs ten weeks). The indicators that restart at each session are refused with an error that names the feature and the timeframe: `vwap`, `or_high` and `or_low` on 1D, 1W and 1M (they were already refused on 1D); `prev_day_*`, `today_*` and `gap_pct` on 1W and 1M (a weekly bar has no session to restart at; they stay valid on 1D).
10. **Twelve new date-driven names, 56 in all.** The two calendar features (`day_of_week`, `is_expiry_day`) and ten that read the context (`index_trend`, `index_adx`, `index_vol_pct`, `vix`, `vix_pct`, `vix_chg`, `breadth`, `regime_trend`, `regime_vol`, `regime`). They take no parameters, are asked for on **1m only** (they change once per date), live in a lane of their own that the frame refreshes at the first accepted step of each India date, and write their history once per date.
11. **`MarketContext` is a pure, shared object.** It is built from `ContextDay` records (date, index high / low / close, VIX close if known, breadth if known) in strictly increasing date order. Each day's values are computed on arrival, from that day and the earlier days only, by this library's own streaming `Sma`, `Atr` and `Adx` and a percentile window. Invalid input returns a validation error with a code, never an exception. It is read-only after the build (any number of frames share it); `Append` is single-writer.
12. **Regime codes and names** follow doc 07 §6: `regime = (1 − trend) × 3 + vol + 1`, so `UP_LOW_VOL` = 1 … `DOWN_HIGH_VOL` = 9, both ways (`Regime.Name`, `Regime.Code`).

| Trend ↓ / volatility → | LOW (0) | NORMAL (1) | HIGH (2) |
|------------------------|--------:|-----------:|---------:|
| UP (+1) | 1 `UP_LOW_VOL` | 2 `UP_NORMAL_VOL` | 3 `UP_HIGH_VOL` |
| RANGE (0) | 4 `RANGE_LOW_VOL` | 5 `RANGE_NORMAL_VOL` | 6 `RANGE_HIGH_VOL` |
| DOWN (−1) | 7 `DOWN_LOW_VOL` | 8 `DOWN_NORMAL_VOL` | 9 `DOWN_HIGH_VOL` |

## "Strictly before today": why the context cannot look ahead
The guarantee has two layers, and both are tested.
1. **A day's values depend on that day and earlier days only.** `MarketContext` feeds each record to its streaming indicators in date order and stores the result; nothing is ever recomputed, so the values of the first *k* days are the same whatever follows them. Tests: the values of the first *k* days are bit-identical when the series is cut after *k* days, when a different future follows, and when the context is built by `Create` or by repeated `Append` (220 seeds).
2. **A reader at date *D* can only reach days before *D*.** `TryAsOf(D)` returns the latest record whose date is below *D* (a binary search on `Date < D`); a record dated *D* or later cannot be reached through the API. The frame asks once per India date, at the first accepted step, so the value is the same at 09:15 and at 15:29. A record dated *D* that is already in the context — a back-test that loaded the whole history, or a live run that appends today's close at 15:35 — is present and unread: the frame at any minute of *D* sees *D − 1*. Tests: a day record dated *D* is in the context while the frame sees the day before it at all 375 minutes of *D*; a frame stepped over a whole day shows the same context at 09:15 and 15:29; a day appended in the middle of a run is seen from the next date and not on its own; three families of 220 seeds each — changing day *D* or any later day leaves the frame's output up to *D* bit-identical, cutting the bar stream, and starting the frame late.

What this does **not** guard, and the data job must: the daily records must themselves be point-in-time — an index or VIX close restated after the fact, or a breadth computed with later prices, would be look-ahead in the *data*. The reading is also deliberately conservative: a strategy running at 16:00 on *D* does not see the close of *D*, although it is known. Dates inside a gap read the day before the gap (Monday sees Friday, or Thursday when Friday is a holiday); a gap is no day, never a zero. `day_of_week` and `is_expiry_day` read the date of the step itself: that is not look-ahead, both are known in advance.

## The closing rule for weeks and months
A weekly or monthly bar is emitted **once**, never while it is forming, and a feature on 1W or 1M reads the closed periods only (`tf("1W").close` is last week's close at every minute of the current week before its closing step; at the closing step itself — with a calendar, the step that closes the last session of the week — it already shows the week that has just closed).

| | With a `TradingCalendar` | Without a calendar |
|---|---|---|
| When | At the step that closes the **last session of the period** — the greatest date of the week (Monday to Sunday) or month with `IsTradingDay` — which is the step at which the 1D lane closes that date (its 15:29 bar, or the last minute of its special session) | At the **first bar of a later period**: the next Monday or the first of the next month |
| Holiday on Friday | Thursday's 15:29 bar | The first bar of next Monday |
| Month ending on a weekend | The 15:29 bar of the last weekday | The first bar of the next month |
| Month ending in the middle of a week | The month closes at that session (a Tuesday, say); the week closes later at its own last session | Each at the first bar of its own next period |
| Saturday special session the calendar knows | The last minute of the Saturday session | The first bar of next Monday (the Saturday bars are part of the week) |
| Saturday session the calendar did not know | The week was closed at Friday; the Saturday bars are **ignored and counted** (`Ignored`) | — |

The right-hand column also applies on top of the left: if the last session did not close a period (its final minute never came, the calendar's last session has no bars), the first bar of a later period does, so a period is never lost. A period without any bar is never emitted. Three cases that the spec left open are decided like the 1D lane decides its own:
- **A late last session.** If the last session's final minute never arrives and the next bar is of a later date of the same period (an unknown Saturday), that bar closes the period and is itself counted in `Ignored`, once.
- **A calendar session without bars.** If the calendar's last session has no bars in the data, the period closes late, at the first bar of the next period.
- **A calendar that disagrees with the data.** If the calendar says Friday is a holiday and the data has Friday bars, the week closes after Thursday and the Friday bars are ignored and counted.

**Where the streaming bars can differ from the stored `1W` / `1M`** ([doc 05 §4](../design/05-data-and-brokers.md)): the stored bucket merges every bar of the period, the streaming one is built from the days the 1D lane accepts, so (a) a bar the 1D lane ignores (an evening bar after the regular close of its date) is not in it; (b) a bar of a period already emitted (a special session the calendar did not know, a date after the calendar's last session of the period) is not in it; (c) it exists when the period is over, the stored one while it runs; (d) the last period of a stream is not emitted (no flush); (e) a stream that starts in the middle of a period gives that period only the days from its first bar. All of these are counted in `Ignored` where bars are dropped. To compare with stored data, pass the same `TradingCalendar`.

## Consequences
- The parser can compile every building block of doc 11 §2 except `sector_rs(n)`: weekly and monthly higher timeframes, `day_of_week`, `is_expiry_day`, `regime()`, `index.trend`, `vix_pct` and breadth all exist as catalog names (`regime` is the code 1 to 9; the parser maps its spellings, `index.trend` → `index_trend`, as it maps `prev_day.high`).
- A **context needs history before the first date of a test**: with the defaults, `index_trend` is ready after 55 context days, `index_vol_pct` and `regime` after 265, `vix_pct` after 252 days that have a VIX. The simulator must load more than a year of daily index and VIX data before the period it tests.
- A weekly lane costs a day builder as well: a frame with a 1D and a 1W lane runs two separate day builders (they are not shared).
- A back-test that wants a calendar-accurate weekly or monthly bar must pass the calendar; without it the bar comes at the first bar of the next period, which a strategy on 1W would see as a Monday-morning bar.
- Until P3-01d exists, nothing writes the context and nothing reads it from the database: the caller supplies `ContextDay` records, and the engine does not read the database (the reader that builds the `MarketContext` from `market.context_daily` is part of P3-01d). A context that is **not extended** keeps the values of its last day for ever and stays ready — a known risk for P3-01d and P7, described under *Threading and the end of the context* below.
- `ContextOptions` are arguments of the `MarketContext`, not of the frame: two contexts with different numbers can run side by side (the simulator's parameter-sensitivity runs), and the numbers move into the database configuration when that exists.

## Consequences from the build
What was learnt while building [P3-01c](../specs/P3-01c-weekly-monthly-timeframes-and-market-context.md); the deviations from the spec are listed under *Amendments* below.

**Definitions as built** (the numbers the tests pin; `ContextOptions` defaults in brackets):
- **`index_adx`**: the Wilder ADX (14) of the index's daily high / low / close — the same code as the `adx` feature; ready at day 28 (2n).
- **`index_trend`** as in decision 2, with the slope `sma[d] / sma[d − 5] − 1`; the comparisons are strict, the ADX test is `≥`. Ready at day 55 (`max(50 + 5, 2 × 14)`). `regime_trend` is the same number.
- **Percentile rank** of the newest value among the last `PercentileWindow` values: `100 × (#below + 0.5 × #equal) / window`, where #equal counts the newest value itself, so ties take the middle of their span (a window of identical values ranks 50; in a window of 4, the lowest value ranks 12.5 and the highest 87.5). Not defined until the window is full. The counts are integers compared exactly.
- **`index_vol_pct`**: the rank of `ATR(14) / close × 100` over the last 252 ATR values; first ready at day 14 + 252 − 1 = **265**.
- **`vix`** the close; **`vix_pct`** the rank of the VIX close over the last 252 days **that have a VIX**; **`vix_chg`** `(v − previous) / previous × 100` against the previous day *of the context*, not ready when that day has no VIX (a VIX of 0 is refused by validation, so there is no division by zero).
- **`regime_vol`**: 0 / 1 / 2 as built in decision 3 (exact thirds, Amendment 13: `score < 100/3` → 0, `score ≥ 200/3` → 2); the score is the index percentile alone on any day where `vix_pct` is not ready. Not ready while `index_vol_pct` is not (265). **`regime`**: not ready unless both parts are, so `max(trend, volatility)` = 265.
- **The thirds are exact.** The score is computed from the integer ranks (`2 × #below + #equal` of each percentile) with **one** division, `100 × (indexRank + vixRank) / (4 × 252)` (`100 × rank / (2 × 252)` for the index alone), so it is the double nearest to the true fraction — the same double as `100.0 / 3` and `200.0 / 3` — and a score that is exactly one third (two thirds) is never put on the wrong side by the rounding of two percentiles. Before the review the mean of the two rounded percentiles did: 268 of the 253,009 pairs of ranks of a 252-day window fell on the wrong side. A test enumerates every pair of ranks of nine window sizes against integer comparisons.
- **`breadth`** the value the record carries (0 to 100), not ready when it has none. It is not part of the regime; it is for the market gate (P7).
- **`day_of_week`** the ISO weekday of the India date (Monday 1 … Sunday 7, a Muhurat Sunday is 7), ready from the first step and correct at India midnight, not UTC midnight. **`is_expiry_day`** 1 / 0, ready from the first step once the set was given (an empty set is valid: always 0).

**Threading and the end of the context.** `MarketContext` is shared by any number of frames and has **one writer**: `Append` is not called from two threads at once, but readers may run while it appends (a live run adds today's close at 15:35 while frames exist). A day is written completely before it is counted, with a release, and the storage grows by copy, so a reader sees the days that were complete when it started and never a half-written one (the review found the count raised before the element was written; a test with a reader thread and 60,000 appends fails on that order). **Known risk of the first build (closed by P3-01d, Amendment 14): past the last day of the context a frame kept that day's values for ever.** A date after the last context day reads the last day, however far away it is, and the features stay ready; `ContextFeature` does not pass on the date the values belong to, and the context has no notion of the clock. If the data job fails for two weeks, `regime` keeps returning the last stored day and a strategy gated on it trades on a stale regime without a signal. *Settled by P3-01d (Amendment 14, changed):* `ContextOptions.MaxAgeDays` (default 7 calendar days) bounds the age: a date more than that many days after the last day is not ready, so a stale context never reads as current; the data job and the coverage endpoint make a stale context visible (`data_context_stale`).

**Bad input does not throw.** A bar whose date lies outside the range of `DateOnly` (years 1 to 9999; a feed that sends epoch milliseconds for seconds is the plausible cause) is built like any other by every timeframe builder: the month and week bounds are computed with integers (`IndiaDays.MonthBounds`, the Gregorian calendar continued without end in 400-year cycles), and a calendar, which cannot name such a date, knows no session on it, so a week or month there is closed by the first bar of a later period, as without a calendar. The date lane treats the same bar as "not ready".

**The public surface** (all in `XDrishti.Indicators`, still referencing `XDrishti.Domain` only):
- `ContextDay`, `ContextOptions`, `ContextValues`, `MarketContext` (`Create`, `Append`, `Count`, `Options`, `TryAsOf`), `Regime` (`Name`, `Code`), `ContextFeature`, `ContextFeatureKind`.
- `FrameOptions(Calendar, HistoryDepth, Context, ExpiryDays)`; a context feature without `Context` is refused at `Create` on `options.context`, `is_expiry_day` without `ExpiryDays` on `options.expiryDays` (each once, naming the features); a date-driven name on another timeframe than 1m is refused on `specs[i].timeframe`.
- `IndicatorInfo.Supports(Timeframe)`, a per-indicator `SupportedTimeframes` and `DateDriven`. `IndicatorCatalog.SupportedTimeframes` now ends with W1 and Mo1; each indicator supports nine timeframes (the continuous ones), seven (the other session-bound ones), six (`vwap`, `or_*`) or only 1m (the date-driven ones). The refusals read, for a session-bound name on 1W: "'prev_day_high' is a value of the trading session; it cannot be computed on 1W (read it on 1m or 1D)", and for a date-driven name on 1D: "'regime' changes once per date; it can only be asked for on 1m (the default), not on 1D."
- Validation codes of `MarketContext`: `indicators.context_options_invalid`, `context_day_missing`, `context_date_not_increasing`, `context_price_not_positive`, `context_high_below_low`, `context_close_outside_range`, `context_vix_not_positive`, `context_breadth_out_of_range`; and `indicators.unknown_regime` for `Regime.Code`. The field says where (`day.indexClose` for `Append`, `days[17].indexClose` for `Create`, `options.percentileWindow`). A refused record leaves the context unchanged. `ContextOptions` periods are 1 to 10,000 (the limit of the indicator parameters), the thresholds 0 to 100 with `LowVolBelow ≤ HighVolFrom`.

**The date lane in the frame.** The date-driven features are fed once per India date, at the first accepted step of the date; a bar the ordering guard skipped does not refresh the lane, `Reset` clears it, and its history has one entry per date, so `At(h, 1)` is the value the previous *date* of the frame had. A frame with a date-driven feature always has a 1m lane, so `BarClosed(M1)` works for it. The frame copies `ExpiryDays` into a `FrozenSet` at `Create` (a `FrozenSet` passed in is used as it is, so one can be shared). A date outside the range of `DateOnly` (a garbage epoch) makes the context and expiry features not ready; the weekday is still computed.

**Warm-up of the date-driven names is counted in days of the context**, not in bars: the number of context days that must lie before the date. The catalog declares it for the default `ContextOptions` (1 for `day_of_week`, `is_expiry_day`, `vix`, `breadth`; 2 `vix_chg`; 28 `index_adx`; 55 `index_trend`, `regime_trend`; 252 `vix_pct`; 265 `index_vol_pct`, `regime_vol`, `regime`); an instance inside a frame reports the number for the options of its own context. The generic catalog-consistency test cannot feed these features bars, so a test of its own measures the first ready date for five option sets.

**Tests** (`XDrishti.Indicators.Tests`, 4,275 tests: 4,267 run, 8 explicit benchmarks; `XDrishti.Architecture.Tests`, 111, unchanged):
- *Weeks and months:* 14 named fixtures (a holiday on Friday, a month ending on a weekend, a month boundary inside a week, a Saturday session, a half-missing day, a single-session week, a holiday-only week, year end, a leap and a common February, a stream starting mid-week at the open and mid-day, a Muhurat Sunday, a missing Friday close) and 40 seeded random series, each against an independent grouping reference for W1 and Mo1 with four calendar kinds (none; full; weekday sessions only; holidays only): bucket start, OHLC, volume, `Bars`, `Complete`, the closing step and the `Ignored` count, and minutes pushed = minutes emitted + the open period + ignored; hand-calculated closing steps; "never earlier than the calendar, never later than the first bar of the next period" over 40 seeds; the prefix property over 200 seeds; each W1 / Mo1 bar equals the sum of the 1D bars.
- *Frame on 1W and 1M:* the catalog warm-up measured over 1,100 sessions (about 52 months), the closed flags, history, raw bar features, ordering guard, determinism and `Reset` on W1 / Mo1, `tf("1W").close` at any minute of week N before its closing step is the close of week N − 1, and at the closing step itself the close of week N; the prefix property over 220 seeds, and a second family that changes every bar of one week or month and checks that no feature inside it moves (120 cases).
- *Market context:* every derived value against `ReferenceContext`, a plain-loop reference, within 1e-9 on 30 seeds with the default options and 30 with small periods (the warm-up days pinned, all nine regimes seen); hand-calculated examples of at most 12 days for the trend in each direction and the range, the slope distance, strict above / below, ties in the mid-rank, the terciles and their boundaries, the VIX-missing fallback, breadth and the nine codes; 22 refused records and 15 bad option sets with their codes and an unchanged state; concurrent readers; determinism on another thread.
- *Date-driven features in the frame:* `day_of_week` for every day of 2026 to 2030 and for special sessions, India midnight against UTC midnight; `is_expiry_day` on a Tuesday, a Thursday before a holiday and a date with no bars; the 24-seed comparison with a batch reference; frames sharing one context, also across threads; a missing VIX day does not stop `regime`; determinism under a comma-decimal culture.
- *Allocation:* steady-state `Step` allocates 0 bytes with W1 / Mo1 lanes and with context features, including the step that changes the date, with and without a calendar, on the gappy series, with the ordering guard skipping bars and after `Reset`; each run checks that weeks, months and dates really changed in the measured window. **The measurement itself is protected** (review finding: the tests were red at random in roughly one `check.sh` run in five): a garbage collection that another thread starts inside the window — the test runner reporting an earlier result, a background collection that began before the window — moves `GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread` by up to one allocation quantum (1 to 8 KB seen) although the thread allocates nothing. It was reproduced on demand with a second thread that only allocates, and the failing steps follow collections, not JIT events, so it is not tiered compilation (the first suspicion). `AllocationProbe` / `AllocationWindow` (test project) start a no-GC region around the window, say whether a collection ran anyway, and run such an attempt again from scratch (at most 5 times); zero bytes, or bytes with no collection, are final at once, and nothing is averaged or tolerated. All exact-zero allocation tests use it, the older ones too (same assertion, now protected); `AllocationProbeTests` proves that one object in 100,000 steps is still reported, that a disturbed attempt is repeated and never passes as clean, and that the weekly and monthly builders stay green beside a thread that makes the collector run (it failed in 4 of 6 runs with the raw counter).
- *Review round (minors M1 to M20 of the P3-01c review):* a bar outside the range of `DateOnly` (and the largest and smallest epochs of a `long`) on every builder, with and without a calendar, and in a frame (74 cases); `IndiaDays` month bounds against the platform calendar for **every day of years 1 to 9999**, the weekday for every day, the repetition every 400 years beyond the range; a calendar that calls the Friday (the last weekday of a month) a holiday although the data has its bars, and a Saturday session the calendar did not know at a month end; every pair of percentile ranks of nine window sizes against integer comparisons, and the two boundary cases of the terciles on paper; a reader thread against 60,000 appends (red on the old order of count and element); the number of dates the cursor read looks at (at most 3 per step for 60 and for 5,000 days, a search for a jump); `IndicatorInfo` has no writable field.
- The builders also changed the code on purpose to see whether the tests notice (about a hundred small changes, each restored): every change that altered behaviour was caught; the survivors were equivalent (for example clearing a cache in `Reset` whose content depends only on the day).

Both period builders swing **by a factor of 3 or more between runs of the same Release binary** (a review recorded seven runs of 1W and six of 1M), and 1W was the slow one in most of them. The cause was **not found**: a change of tier in the JIT (tiered compilation or PGO) is the first suspect, the zeroing of the `out ClosedBar` results through three `Push` layers (`TimeframeBuilder`, `WeekMonthBuilder`, the inner day builder) the second; neither was tested. The benchmark `Timeframe_builders_bars_per_second` has **no assertion** (it is explicit, only reports, and is noisy); the recorded figures are all above 10 M bars/s, and a regression of the builder to a third of its fast mode, or far below it, would still pass, so the harness cannot tell a slow mode from a regression — this is an open item in the roadmap issues log (ISS-11) and a follow-up in the spec. The frame figures are the ones that matter for the simulator; they carry noise too (in one run the frame with six more features measured faster than the one without them), so the date lane costs less than this harness can resolve; it does not cost "nothing".

## Amendments (2026-10-16)
Deviations of the build from the sketch in [P3-01c](../specs/P3-01c-weekly-monthly-timeframes-and-market-context.md); the guarantees of the acceptance criteria are unchanged. **Amendments 1, 2, 5 and 7 change text that the owner approved**; Amendment 13 clarifies a number of it and Amendment 14 records a behaviour it left open. All six were settled on 2026-10-27 (see the last section). The others add behaviour the spec left open or record how it was built. (The numbers 1 to 9 are those of the spec; from 10 on the spec numbers its own list — its Amendments 15 and 16 are Amendments 13 and 14 here.)
1. ✔ *Confirmed 2026-10-27 (P3-01d D1).* **`vix_pct` window.** The spec said a missing day makes the value not ready and that it is never filled. Built: the window holds the last 252 days **that have a VIX**, so a gap day reads not-ready on its own date and the history before it is not erased; the next day with a VIX is ready again if 252 such days exist.
2. ✔ *Confirmed 2026-10-27 (P3-01d D2).* **`regime_vol` fallback.** The spec: the index percentile alone when the VIX is missing *on that day*. Built: on **any** day where `vix_pct` is not ready — also while the VIX history is shorter than the window. `regime_vol` is not ready exactly while `index_vol_pct` is.
3. **`vix_chg`.** Compared with the previous day of the context; not ready when that day has no VIX (the spec's "or 0" cannot occur: validation refuses a VIX of 0).
4. **`regime` warm-up** is `max(trend, volatility)`, not only the volatility part; with the defaults both give 265 only because the volatility part is the later one.
5. ✔ *Confirmed 2026-10-27 (P3-01d D3).* **The cursor is in the reader, not in the context.** The spec described `AsOf` as O(1) amortised through "a cached cursor". The public `TryAsOf(date, out values)` is a binary search (O(log n)) that writes nothing, so any number of frames can share the context; the O(1) walk is an internal overload `TryAsOf(date, ref cursorHint, out values)` that each `ContextFeature` calls with its own position, falling back to the search for any other date. Whatever the hint, the answer is the same (tested from arbitrary hints). The review noted that nothing proved the walk: an internal overload now also returns how many dates of the context the read looked at, and a test asserts at most 3 per step (2 when the answer stays, 3 when it moves on) for a context of 60 and one of 5,000 days — a binary search would need 6 and 12 or more — and a search for a jump forward or back.
6. **`Regime.Name(int)`** throws `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` for a code outside 1 to 9 (a programming error: a not-ready regime is NaN and must be checked before it becomes a code); `Regime.Code(string)` returns `Result<int>` with `indicators.unknown_regime`, case-sensitive and exact. The spec's sketch did not say.
7. ✔ *Confirmed 2026-10-27 (P3-01d D4; the spec's test plan contradicted its scope; the build follows the scope).* **A stream that starts in the middle of a week.** The spec's test plan said the first partial week is emitted incomplete "as 1D today". Built: `Complete` is "every day that has bars is complete" (doc 05 §4), so a stream that starts at the open of a Wednesday gives a complete first week, and an incomplete one only when one of its days is (a start at 11:00 makes that Wednesday partial).
8. **Three behaviours the spec left open** for the weekly and monthly builders: a late last session, a calendar session without bars, and a calendar that disagrees with the data (see *The closing rule*).
9. **New public API the sketch did not list:** `IndicatorInfo.Supports`, `IndicatorInfo.SupportedTimeframes`, `IndicatorInfo.DateDriven`, `ContextFeature`, `ContextFeatureKind`, `MarketContext.Options` and the eight problem-code constants. `TimeframeBuilder.Ignored` became a read-only getter over two counters (the surface is the same). `BarsPerSession` returns 1 for a calendar timeframe (it would otherwise divide by zero for W1 and Mo1).
10. **Validation the spec did not list:** `ContextOptions` is validated in `Create` (`options.percentileWindow` and the like), a null day or sequence is `indicators.context_day_missing`.
11. **Existing tests changed.** The tests of P3-01 / P3-01b that asserted W1 and Mo1 are refused now use other refused values: `TimeframeBuilderTests` M1 and `(Timeframe)99`, `FeatureFrameTests` `(Timeframe)99` and `(Timeframe)(-1)`; the short-series theories of the frame loop over 1m to 1D (a short series cannot close a week) and have dedicated W1 / Mo1 theories on long series.

12. **Review round (minors).** Behaviour and surface changed after the review, none of it a change of an approved definition: (a) the weekly and monthly builders — and the day builders with a calendar — never throw for a bar whose date is outside the range of `DateOnly` (see *Bad input does not throw*); (b) `MarketContext.Append` publishes a day only once it is complete, so readers may run during the single writer's `Append` (see *Threading and the end of the context*); (c) the volatility score is computed from integer ranks with one division, so the exact thirds fall where the definition puts them; (d) `ContextOptions.MaxPeriod`, a public constant that the amendments did not list, is gone: the limit is the internal `IndicatorCatalog.MaxPeriod`, shared with the indicator parameters; (e) `IndicatorInfo.SupportedTimeframes` is built in the constructor instead of on first use; (f) the internal `TryAsOf(date, ref hint, out values, out datesRead)`; (g) the IIndicator and frame documentation say that the warm-up of a date-driven feature is counted in days of the context and that the date-driven features are a function of the dates of the bars and the context days before them.
13. ✔ *Confirmed 2026-10-27 (P3-01d D5; a clarification of a number the owner approved; spec Amendment 15).* **The volatility thresholds are exact thirds.** The owner's answer to open question 3 said "LOW below 33.3, HIGH from 66.7"; the contract sketch approved with it shows `LowVolBelow = 100.0 / 3` and `HighVolFrom = 200.0 / 3`, and the build uses exactly those doubles. The readings differ only for a score from 33.3 up to, not including, one third (LOW as built, NORMAL under the literal 33.3) and from two thirds up to, not including, 66.7 (HIGH as built, NORMAL under the literal 66.7): **a score of exactly two thirds is HIGH as built and NORMAL under the literal 66.7**; exactly one third is NORMAL under both.
14. ↻ *Changed 2026-10-27 (P3-01d D6; the spec was silent; spec Amendment 16).* **Past the last day of the context.** As first built, a date read the last day however far away it was, and the features stayed ready. Now `ContextOptions.MaxAgeDays` (default 7) bounds it: see the last section.

**What P3-01d built on this ADR.** Table `market.context_daily` (one row per India trading day: index high, low, close, VIX close, the bars each came from; insert-only for the runtime role); a post-step of the data job; `IContextFigures` (the adapter in Infrastructure is the only code that names `MarketContext`, so Application still names no engine type, ADR 0039); `GET /api/data/context`; the notices `data_context_stale` (more than one session behind the last closed one) and `data_context_gap` (sessions missing inside the stored range); `Data:Dhan:InitialDaysIndex` (default 1825) for the first fetch of an index. The daily close is **derived from the stored 1-minute bars** (`derived_1m`) through the one allow-listed chart endpoint (no allow-list change, [ADR 0133](0133-new-broker-endpoints-enter-the-allow-list-in-the-commit-of-the-slice-that-uses-them-index-and-vix-history-needs-none.md)); NSE computes an index's official close from its constituents, so the last 1-minute close can differ slightly and only a trend flag within about 0.05 % of its average could flip. A day is written only when final (at or before the last closed session and at least 96 % of the calendar's expected minutes, D9); a day waits up to `VixGraceSessions` (5) for its VIX leg and then goes without (D10), because a restated close would move earlier percentiles and the table is insert-only.
