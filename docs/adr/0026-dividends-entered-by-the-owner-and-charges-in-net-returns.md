# 0026 — Dividends are entered by the owner; charges and dividends form a net return next to the gross one

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-10-14: "approved, continue" on [P2-14](../specs/P2-14-dividends-and-charges-in-returns.md), after the research below) — builds on [ADR 0012](0012-holdings-acquisition-dates-and-returns-model.md) §4 (returns), [ADR 0020](0020-portfolio-value-history-benchmark-and-charts.md) (benchmark), [ADR 0024](0024-live-feed-xd-feed-and-live-prices.md) (live twins) and [ADR 0025](0025-dhan-trade-history-and-ledger-sync.md) (trade and ledger sync)
- **Date:** 2026-10-14

## Context
Doc 12 defines *total return* as (realised + unrealised + dividends) ÷ invested and a position's average cost as including charges, but ADR 0012 and ADR 0025 left both out. The plan was to take dividends from the Dhan ledger (class `dividend`) and match them to holdings by narration. Research into Dhan's published material (API docs `dhanhq.co/docs/v2/statements`, support pages, 2026-10-14) found:
- A dividend is **credited to the owner's bank account** for shares held on the record date; the only documented ledger dividend entry is the transfer for *rented* stocks. Documented ledger entries are funds added/withdrawn (`FUNDS WITHDRAWAL`, voucher `PAYBNK`), settlements and "other charges" such as `DPC Interest : … to … : …`. No ordinary-dividend narration is documented, and no API for the app's *Money → Dividends* list was found.
- The trade-history rows carry the charges per trade (`brokerageCharges`, `stt`, `sebiTax`, `serviceTax`, `exchangeTransactionCharges`, `stampDuty`) and an `isin`; `tradingSymbol` may be null. (The P2-13 mapper dropped such rows and stored `charges = 0`; corrected in the P2-13 follow-up before this story.)
- Neither endpoint documents a maximum date span or how far back history goes.

## Decisions
1. **Dividends are entered by the owner**, per holding (or left unallocated), in `portfolio.dividends` — immutable except for being voided, no DELETE for the runtime role, audited with ids and counts only. A ledger entry of class `dividend` (if one ever appears) is only a **suggestion** the owner accepts or dismisses; nothing is added automatically and no narration is matched. A dividend CSV import is a follow-up (P2-15).
2. **Charges are a separate line, not part of the cost.** Trade charges come from the stored transactions (Dhan rows, CSV, manual) and belong to a holding (matched to it by ISIN, else symbol, among the account's open holdings, then its closed ones; a trade that fits no single holding belongs to the account). Account-level costs come from ledger entries of class `charges`, at account and total scope only, and only those that **name an account-level cost** (`LedgerCosts.IsAccountLevel`): DP or demat charges, AMC, delayed-payment / DPC interest, penalties, platform or subscription fees, pledge, payout and withdrawal charges, call & trade. An entry that mentions a trade-related phrase (brokerage, STT, stamp duty, SEBI, transaction charges, settlements, bills) is never summed, even if an account-level phrase is also in it, because the trade rows already carry the costs of trades; the rule is conservative (a doubt leaves a cost out, where it stays visible in the ledger, rather than counting it twice) and its phrase table is expected to grow after the first real read. The class shown for an entry is not changed. Lots, Dhan's average cost and the cost basis are not changed.
3. **Gross and net side by side.** The existing gross figures stay as they are. New: `dividends`, `charges`, `unallocated`, total return % = (realised + unrealised + dividends) ÷ invested, a net total return (minus charges) and a **net XIRR** (lot flows plus dividend inflows and charge outflows on their dates). Only data that exists is counted; a scope with no dividends and no charges shows gross figures only, and each figure says from which date it is complete. **Coverage rule:** the parts are shown separately — `dividendsFrom` (the first dividend entered), `tradeChargesFrom` (the first stored trade that carries charges: a trade without charges is unknown, not free), `ledgerChargesFrom` (the first ledger date stored), `chargesFrom` (the later of the last two when both exist) — and `coverageFrom` is the **later start of the sources that exist** for the scope, so a net figure is never presented as complete from before any of its sources begins. A source with nothing for the scope has a null date and does not hold `coverageFrom` back.
4. **The benchmark stays a price return.** The Nifty 50 price index has no dividends, so alpha remains the gross XIRR minus the benchmark XIRR and its basis says "price return, before dividends and charges".
5. **History depth is the owner's to try.** The sync dialog offers an optional earlier start date; Dhan's refusal or an empty answer is reported in plain words.
6. **Live figures agree with a re-read where they move.** The TypeScript twins take the `income` of the live-base read; at a pushed price they move only the two total return percentages (the dividends, charges and dates do not depend on a price). The **net XIRR stays as the last read gave it**, like the gross XIRR and the benchmark, and is exact again at the 5-minute read. Golden fixtures (schema 3) and the 150-seed property test carry dividends, trade charges, ledger costs and an unallocated dividend and compare by exact equality.
7. **Trades stored before P2-14 are completed when read again.** P2-13 first stored `charges = 0` and no ISIN for broker trades. A read that finds such a trade stored already fills in only what is missing (ISIN, ticker, charges), only on broker trades that are not voided, never the identity, side, date, quantity or price (the *enrich* step, ADR 0025 amendment h); the count is `enriched`, audited with the other counts.
8. **Total return is over what is still held.** Total return % = (realised + unrealised + dividends) ÷ invested, where invested is the cost of the open lots, as for the gross P&L %; realised profit is in the numerator, so for a partly sold position the percentage can look high. It is not a return on all the money ever put in; the XIRR is the time-weighted answer to that.

## Load
Income is read in a fixed number of set-based statements, whatever the number of dividends, trades or holdings (`IIncomeQueries`; never one per holding): the accounts of the scope, the holdings' keys (not read when the caller hands over the holdings it has just read, and only needed to match a trade's security to a holding), the dividends that are not voided, the trade charges summed per account, security and day, and — for the summary and the groups, never for the rows — the ledger's costs summed per account, day and text and the first date of each account's ledger. A page of `GET /api/holdings` costs 6 statements (the session, the holdings, their lots and the three income reads: accounts, dividends, trade charges) and 7 when the holdings listed are not all the account's (a filter, a status or a group narrows them) and their keys are read once; it never reads the ledger. The summary adds the two ledger reads, so a scope reads each income table once (the ledger twice). The net XIRR is worked out for the rows of the page only, after ordering and paging, never to order by; `sort=dividends` orders by a cheap sum. This adds to, and does not change, the *Load of the live totals* of [ADR 0024](0024-live-feed-xd-feed-and-live-prices.md): the browser still reads the pages of 100 rows on its 5-minute timer, and each page now carries three more reads. The slim read endpoint named there as a follow-up would drop the XIRR and the income from that read and is still not built.

## Consequences
- Net figures are only as complete as what the owner enters and what the trade and ledger reads cover; the screen says from when.
- Typing dividends costs effort until a CSV import exists; in return holding, group and total figures are exact and need no guessing.
- Alpha does not move because of dividends, which keeps it an honest price-to-price comparison.
- Group figures at the default status (open holdings) leave out the dividends and the trade charges of closed holdings, while the summary tiles read every holding and include them; the two agree at `status=all`. The dividends and costs of no holding appear only in the total, in the account's own group, or in an extra `~unallocated` row, and only when neither a bucket nor a text search narrows the scope.
- A trade stored before P2-14 has no charges until its day is read again; the owner reads from an earlier date once (the enrich step), and the figures say from which date the charges are known meanwhile. CSV and manual trades usually carry none and stay unknown, not free.
- The net XIRR on the screen can lag a price move by up to the 5-minute read; the two net total return percentages move live.
- Not built: TDS on dividends, dividend reinvestment, a total-return benchmark, charges in the cost basis, matching ledger dividends to holdings by narration.

## Amendments after the first review (2026-10-14)
The independent review of the first implementation changed six points of the first reading of this ADR; the spec marks them too ([P2-14](../specs/P2-14-dividends-and-charges-in-returns.md)). (a) Ledger costs are account-level only (decision 2: a table of account-level phrases and a list of trade-related phrases that always exclude). (b) Coverage is given in parts and `coverageFrom` is the **later** start of the sources that exist (decision 3), not the earliest date. (c) The net XIRR is worked out lazily, for the page's rows only (Load). (d) The enrich step completes trades stored before this change (decision 7). (e) The total-return wording is exact about its denominator (decision 8). (f) The net XIRR is not moved live (decision 6). The `income` object also carries `ledgerCharges`, `dividendsFrom`, `chargesFrom`, `tradeChargesFrom` and `ledgerChargesFrom`.

**Update (2026-10-15):** the dividend CSV import named above as a follow-up is built in [P2-15](../specs/P2-15-dividend-csv-import.md), decided in [ADR 0028](0028-dividend-csv-import-with-name-matching.md); dividends read from a file count in every figure exactly like the ones typed in.
