# 0050 — `PARTIAL_BE`, `PARTIAL_TRAIL` and the `time_stop` overlay in the simulator

- **Status:** Accepted (spec [P4-02](../specs/P4-02-partial-exits-and-the-time-stop.md), which the owner approved on 2026-10-19 with "yes to all recommendations", questions 1 to 8; built on 2026-10-21 ahead of the P3 exit check because the owner pulled P4 forward). The text of this ADR has not been read by the owner.
- **Date:** 2026-10-21.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** ADR 0049 (the tape, the legs and the order inside a minute, which this builds on), ADR 0038 (the minute table X2 to X5 and the slippage S1 to S5, reused unchanged), ADR 0040 (swing positions, the calendar count of sessions, `MAX_HOLD`), ADR 0042 (costs per leg), ADR 0036 Amendments 1 and 2 (the tables of the three policies are final from this slice), ADR 0047 (the oracle). The number is 0050, not the 0049 the spec names: 0049 was taken by P4-01 on the same day.

## Context
P4-01 built the seam and proved it with a rule that lives in the test project. This slice is the first in which a production run makes a leg: a strategy whose exit is `PARTIAL_BE` (book part at T1, stop to break-even, the rest at T2) or `PARTIAL_TRAIL` (book part at T1, trail the rest by the ATR), with or without the `time_stop` overlay. It runs one exit set at a time; comparing candidates is P4-05.

## Decision
1. **The policies are data, not `IExitRule`s.** `LegPolicy` (internal to `XDrishti.Simulation`) holds the partial kind, the book share, T2, the trail multiple and the time stop; `Simulator.Create` builds it from the accepted exits and `Run` gives the walker an `ExitContext(null, tape, policy)`. No shipped type implements `IExitRule` (the architecture test still holds that); the contract stays the way to add a rule that needs an arbitrary function of the tape (P4-03). The code that applies a policy is a second file of `LegState` (`LegState.Policy.cs`) so that the shared file changes by a few lines.
2. **T1 is the target of the position.** The walker's reward-to-risk of a partial policy is `t1_rr`, so `TargetPrice` of the trade is T1 (D11), and the minute table decides T1 like any target: the open beyond it fills at the level and never at a better price (X3), a minute that touches the stop and T1 is the stop for the whole position unless the open is already beyond T1, and in the fill minute of an entry at the limit the target is not checked (X5). When the target the table gives is the first one of a partial policy, the loops call `LegState.FillT1` instead of leaving: a resting limit (no slippage, grid only) of the share `book_pct / 100` of the position, leg code `T1`.
3. **The order inside the T1 minute (D2).** The new levels are written by `FillT1` and read from the next minute on: `PARTIAL_BE` gets the stop at the **exact entry** and the target at `entry ± t2_rr × R0`; `PARTIAL_TRAIL` loses the target and keeps the initial stop. So the minute that fills T1 is never also a break-even stop, a T2 fill or a trail level. **The minute counts in full for the excursions** (decision of this ADR: ADR 0049 said "a minute with a partial exit counts in full until the exit"; for a target exit the table gives only the favourable side up to the level, but the position stays open here, so the whole range of the minute is an excursion of the part still held; hand rows B9 and B18 pin it).
4. **The trail (D5).** After T1, at each bar *k* the walker reads for the first time (close at or before the minute, and after the T1 minute: a bar that closes at the start of the T1 minute is read before T1 and gives no level), `level = H − trail_atr_mult × ATR_k` for a buy (a sell: the lowest low, plus), `H` the highest high of the minutes that hold a trade from the T1 minute through the end of bar *k*. `LegState` keeps `H` as a running extreme of the minutes it has walked (`Observe`); this equals the extreme through the end of the bar because a bar is read at the first trade minute at or after its close, and every minute between the close and that minute holds no trade. The oracle computes `H` by scanning the minutes from the T1 minute to the close of the bar, so the equivalence is tested over 19,000 series. A NaN ATR gives no level; the level is applied through the existing `MoveStop`, so a looser one is ignored and counted in `StopLoosenIgnored`; the level is not put on the tick grid (a level is exact, only a filled price is on the grid). `ATR_TRAIL` of P4-03 shares only the minute table.
5. **The intraday time stop (D8).** The tape records the closes only when the strategy has an intraday time stop and reads no feature (`EntryScanner`: a tape with no feature but `Close`). The check is at the close of the N-th bar read after the fill, where a bar counts when its close is **after** the fill minute's start (the bar that closes at the fill instant is the decision bar and is never read, ADR 0049 decision 2); N = 1 is the bar that contains the fill minute. Progress is `side × (close − entry) < min_progress_rr × R0` (a product, not a quotient, so the comparison is exact), a close that is not a number is no check, and the overlay is spent after the check. When it finds too little progress the open share leaves at the **open of the minute that reads that bar** (the first trade minute at or after the close), market-type, reason `TimeStop`. Precedences, in this order: the square-off (a check bar that closes at or after the square-off minute makes no check, because that minute is the square-off); a stop the open is already beyond; a target (T1, T2 or the plain one) the open has already reached; then the time stop. If the open fills T1, the time stop is not lost: the check minute is the T1 minute and the remainder leaves at the open of the next minute.
6. **The swing time stop (D9).** The checked session is session `swing_sessions` counted from the fill session by the calendar (`SwingHold.DueSession(entry, n)`). If the last minute of that session (the due minute of the calendar) holds a trade and neither level was reached, progress is judged against its close and the open share leaves at that close, reason `TimeStop`. If that minute holds no trade, the first minute of a later date (or after the due minute) shows the session has ended and the check is made against the close of the last minute that did, counted in `TimeStopAtLastClose`; a list that ends first is `EndOfData`. The T1 minute continues through these checks (it may be the checked minute; the P4-01 loop would have skipped them).
7. **What `Simulator.Create` accepts (D10).** Exactly one exit of `FIXED_RR`, `PARTIAL_BE` or `PARTIAL_TRAIL` with one value in every list, at most the `time_stop` overlay; everything else is `simulation.unsupported_exit` at `buy.exits` (the message names the policy and, for a list, the parameter) or `buy.overlays[i]`. `ExitTables.RunBySimulator` is the three policies and the new `OverlaysRunBySimulator` is `{time_stop}`; the warning `exit_not_evaluated` is not given for them, and a test holds the model's and the simulator's lists equal.
8. **`ExitReason.TimeStop`** is a new member (stored by name: `timestop`). `WalkCounters` gets `ExitsTimeStop` and `TimeStopAtLastClose` (34 counters; the goldens of `run.json` differ by these two zeros). The export spells it `time_stop`; the verification pack's table of edge categories lists it as "not a category yet". **The store gets one migration, `AddTimeStopExitReason`**: the check constraint `ck_sim_trades_exit_reason` is dropped and created again with `timestop`; the grants are untouched. **The legs are still not persisted** (the spec's contract asks for no column): `simulate --store` of a run with a partial exit is refused by name (`run.trades.legs`) and the export files keep the legs. The columns `legs jsonb` and `cost_leg_charges jsonb` (migration `AddSimTradeLegs`) come with the first slice that must read a stored trade with legs back (P4-05).
9. **Costs.** Unchanged from ADR 0049 for a trade with legs. Two findings of the review of P4-01 are closed: a trade whose legs have no exit leg is `costs.legs_invalid` (a validation error, not an index error), and a non-last exit leg whose `floor(Q × share)` is 0 is dropped and **is counted** — the replay counts `LegsMerged` as the legs of the trade minus its charged legs, so a dropped leg of any position is in it (a test with a one-share position pins it).
10. **Engine version.** `sim-6` stays (spec question 7): every request that existed gives the same entries, exits and R (the goldens differ only by the two new counters), and a strategy with the new exits is another definition with another hash, hence another run key.
11. **The oracle** (`OraclePolicy`, `OraclePolicyBar`, `OracleRules.Policy`) is extended in `WalkRun` from the rule tables of the spec and this ADR, with its own reading: it computes the trail anchor by scanning minutes, finds the checked session with the calendar, and keeps the exact decimal prices of the partial legs (the first run found that the round trip through a double lost digits of a T1 level that has more than 8 places; the oracle's, not the engine's). Compared with the engine on 19,000 series for each family, in both fill modes: every field of every trade, the legs included, bit-equal.

## Consequences
- A production run can now make a leg. `LegsGateAndProductionTests` still holds that a `FIXED_RR` run makes none. The gate holds the instrument to the last leg (ADR 0049), including when the last leg is a time stop.
- The scanner allocates a tape of closes (one `double` and one `long` per evaluation bar) for an intraday time stop; the walk allocates nothing per minute or per bar beyond its legs (`PartialWalkAllocationTests`).
- `simulate --store` cannot store a run whose strategy books T1. The Lab can run and export it; storing and comparing candidates is P4-05.
- The verbs `simulate` and `verify-pack` take a built-in strategy by id and have no way to choose another exit (only `rr` and `or_min` are parameters), so the end-to-end of the policies is tested through the library they call (`PolicyEndToEndTests`), not through the verbs. Choosing the exit from the command line, and the verification pack of the P4 exits, come with P4-05 and P4-06.
- The assisted check (`ChartReader`) reads the exit of a `FIXED_RR` trade from the bars; it does not know T1, the break-even or the time stop, so a pack drawn from a run with these exits would show differences that are the reader's, not the engine's. It is not extended in this slice.

## Honest limits
- The oracle, the engine and the hand rows are written by the same model family from the same spec. The 61 hand rows were computed before the engine ran and all agreed on the first run; the oracle and the engine agreed on the first run too after one difference that was the oracle's (the double round trip). That is evidence that the two readings of the text agree, not that the text is what the owner meant.
- Three readings are mine and are recorded here so that they can be changed: the minute of T1 counts in full for MAE and MFE (decision 3); a T1 fill in the check minute defers the time stop by one minute (decision 5); the bar of the intraday count is "closes after the fill minute starts", which makes N = 1 the bar that contains the fill (spec question 4).
- `T2`, the trail level and the break-even stop are exact decimals and not on the tick grid; the stop exit that fills at them is on the grid after slippage. A real stop order would be placed on the grid.
- The leak property for the swing time stop at the last close compares what the position did before the cut without the legs that only a later minute shows (end of data, `MAX_HOLD` and `TimeStop` at the last close); that exception is the same one the P3 swing properties make.

## Alternatives considered
- **Express the policies as `IExitRule`s.** Rejected: T1 and T2 are resting limits and the time stop is a market exit at a minute, which the rule contract (bars in, market shares out at the open) cannot say without more actions; each policy would have been a new action in a shared file that P4-03 edits too.
- **A new engine version (`sim-7`).** Rejected for the reason in decision 10.
- **Store the legs now.** Rejected: the spec asks for no column and nothing reads a stored trade with legs before P4-05.
- **Count the time stop from the first bar that starts after the fill.** Rejected by the owner's answer to question 4.
