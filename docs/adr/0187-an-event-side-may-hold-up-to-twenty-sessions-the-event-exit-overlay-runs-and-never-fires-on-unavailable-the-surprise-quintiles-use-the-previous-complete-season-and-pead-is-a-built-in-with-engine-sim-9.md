# 0187 — An event side may hold up to twenty sessions, the `event_exit` overlay runs and never fires on unavailable data, the surprise quintiles use the previous complete season, and `PEAD` is a built-in with engine `sim-9`

- **Status:** Proposed (built in P15-09 from the owner-Approved spec, 2026-11-03). Becomes Accepted when the owner has read it. **Safety slice: no** — a research strategy and simulator rules; it is in no plan, selection or paper account, it places no order, and no setting is added or changed.
- **Date:** 2026-11-03.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** [0037](0037-simulation-conventions-and-the-two-stage-seam.md) (engine version; Amendment 12 records `sim-9`), [0040](0040-swing-positions-held-across-sessions-the-maximum-hold-the-position-gate-and-simtrade-schema-3.md) (the swing walk; Amendment records the hold up to 20 and `event_exit`), [0043](0043-the-daily-evaluation-and-the-overnight-entry-order-sim-5-and-run-record-schema-5.md) (a decision at the close fills at the next open), [0181](0181-the-results-calendar-keeps-each-meeting-row-with-its-own-intimation-time-counts-days-in-trading-sessions-to-an-event-session-and-answers-unavailable-for-an-instrument-with-no-entry.md) and [0182](0182-the-event-risk-filter-reads-the-results-calendar-in-the-simulator-and-behind-an-owner-switch-in-the-plan-and-the-results-loss-tag-describes-the-past-and-unavailable-is-never-a-pass.md) (the calendar, unavailable is never a pass), [0185](0185-the-event-and-fundamental-feature-sets-read-only-what-was-public-at-the-decision-instant-store-nan-with-a-closed-reason-for-every-unknown-and-the-frame-gets-two-results-day-features-with-engine-sim-8.md) (`sue_yoy`, `results_car3`, the results-day features), [0186](0186-the-p15-evaluation-protocols-f-x-p-and-l-count-each-trial-in-the-ledger-before-any-number-store-insert-only-verdicts-with-the-missing-quantity-named-and-feed-the-event-filter-only-through-the-verdict-port.md) (protocol P, the `IPeadEventSource` port).

## Context
Protocol P pre-registers a post-earnings-drift test: long the top surprise quintile, entered at the close of the reaction session, held 5 or 20 sessions with a 2 × ATR(14) stop, against the same rule on the middle three quintiles. The simulator could not run it: a swing hold stopped at 5 sessions, the `event_exit` overlay was refused, an `atr_mult` stop was refused, there were no quintile features, and the port that feeds protocol P had only a default that answered "no events".

## Decision
1. **Event side.** A side whose setup or trigger reads `days_since_results` is an event side (a name list or a flag would be a second, forgettable marker). For it `time_rules.max_hold_sessions` and the swing `time_stop.swing_sessions` accept 1 to 20 (`ExitTables.MaxEventHold`); every other side keeps 1 to 5. The code is still `max_hold_out_of_range` and the message names both limits. `StrategyFileSchema` gains `maximum: 20`; the model check gives the exact bound. The draft sheet of P9-07 is unchanged (no prompt hash change). The walk (`SwingHold`) counts up to 20 and leaves the choice of who may use it to the model.
2. **`event_exit` runs.** At the close of a session whose `days_to_results`, as known at the start of that session, is at most `days_before_results`, the open position leaves at that close (`ExitReason.EventExit`, the mechanics of `MAX_HOLD`, X8; if the longest hold falls on the same close the longest hold wins). A session whose `days_to_results` is unknown (no visible entry, a cancelled meeting, a date the series does not hold) **never fires**: it is counted (`event_exit_unavailable` session closes and the trades that had one), and the walk goes on. This is the only safe reading of an exit after results: the next meeting is usually not intimated (`no_upcoming_entry`), and exiting on that would close every PEAD trade on its first day. The overlay is removed from the `unsupported_exit` refusals (an intraday side still refuses it: it would be ignored) and from the `exit_not_evaluated` warning; a strategy that lists it gets the same results-day inputs as one that names `days_to_results`.
3. **Quintile features.** `results_sue_q` and `results_car3_q` (1 to 5, NaN when unknown) are frame features read from the same per-date series as the results-day features. A value is the quintile of the `sue_yoy` or `results_car3` of the newest visible filing against the breakpoints of the **previous complete season**: the 20, 40, 60 and 80 % quantiles by the nearest-rank rule of all filings of the universe whose period end falls in the quarter before the filing's own and whose reaction session lies strictly before the date; at least 100 values, else NaN; ties go to the higher quintile. The plan's "the cross-section in the season" was look-ahead at the close of an early reporter's reaction session, and is corrected here. Nothing broadcast after the date, of any season, can change a bucket at it (`SurpriseBreakpoints`, `SurpriseInputs`; hand case 1..200 gives 40, 80, 120, 160). The numbers come from `fu-1` and `ev-1` through the extractor of P15-05 at the close of each filing's reaction session (`ISurpriseInputsReader`); a run that reads a quintile also records the hash, count and unverified count of those numbers and the rule version `sbp-1` in the `rdays` line of its key.
4. **A quintile is known as of the close of the date.** The CAR needs the close of the reaction session, so on that session the value is not "as known at the start of the date". The compiler refuses both features on any evaluation timeframe but 1D (`closing_feature_off_daily`), where a decision is taken at the close. This is the one place where a point of the series is not as of the start of the date, and it is written in the series' documentation.
5. **`atr_mult` stop.** The simulator runs the stop `atr_mult` on the evaluation timeframe: the close of the decision bar less (a buy) the multiple of the ATR the frame holds at that close. The scanner computes it; nothing else about stops changes. It was refused before, so no earlier run changes.
6. **`PEAD`.** A row of `BuiltInStrategies` (`PeadV1`): parameters `measure` (1 `sue_yoy`, 2 `results_car3`), `hold` (5 or 20) and `buckets` (1 top = quintile 5, 2 middle = quintiles 2 to 4); a closed set of values each (the registry refuses any other). Entry rule `days_since_results == 0 and results_<measure>_q` in the buckets, timeframe 1D, BUY only; the decision is the close of the reaction session and by ADR 0043 the order fills at the open of the next session, never the announcement jump. Stop 2 × ATR(14), target `FIXED_RR` 10 as a practical "no target" (20 ATRs in at most 20 sessions): exits by target are counted, and a run whose share is above 2 % of its trades is labelled "target not negligible". The strategy does not name `not_event_day`: under the fail-closed rule right after a results event the next meeting is rarely intimated, so the filter would block nearly every entry; a definition that combines them gets the warning `event_side_with_not_event_day`. The four variants and the control are the same definition but for these values: five different definition hashes, five different run keys. The short side is a research label; the simulator keeps refusing a swing SELL. `PEAD` is a built-in of the engine, not a seeded stored text: its parameters choose the feature name, which a stored text with named parameters cannot. It is offered to no plan, selection or paper account.
7. **The real `IPeadEventSource`.** `PeadRunEventSource` replaces the unavailable default: it finds, by definition hash, the newest stored run of each variant and bucket for the current engine version, turns each closed trade into a `PeadEvent` (entry date, the sessions of entry and exit counted in the trading calendar, gross R from the filled prices) and hands them to protocol P. With no stored run it answers an empty set with the reason, and with too few seasons protocol P states NOT_EVALUABLE with "seasons n of 20", as before. It reads; it runs nothing and stores nothing.
8. **Engine `sim-9`.** `SimulationEngine.Version` is `sim-9`; run-record schema stays 8 (the key only gains text for a run that reads a quintile). The goldens were regenerated on purpose; the `sim-8` files are frozen beside them (`Golden/*.sim-8.frozen.txt`) and a differential test shows every `sim-9` golden equals its frozen `sim-8` file but for the engine label and the key hash: a strategy that is not an event side and does not name `event_exit` has the candidates, trades and counters it had. `sim-6`, `sim-7` and `sim-8` frozen files and their differential tests stay. `WalkCounters` gains three counters at the end (`ExitsEventExit`, `EventExitUnavailable`, `EventExitUnavailableTrades`); the dry-read table of the oracle gets three SPEC ONLY rows.

9. **After review (2026-11-03).** (a) The event source reads **one coherent set**: the newest complete group of the eight runs sharing the window, universe, fills and costs; otherwise an empty set with the reason "no coherent set of the eight PEAD runs". The hash of the surprise numbers is in the run key but not in the stored header, so two sets differing only in it cannot be told apart. (b) The names `results_sue_q` and `results_car3_q` are **refused** by the BUY to SELL mirror (a top-quintile entry has no mirrored side), like the regime; `mirror-1` is kept because no stored definition can contain them. (c) The draft sheet is generated from the catalog: it gains two feature lines, its hash changes, it stays under 12 KB, and the assistant-eval baseline (ADR 0143) should be rerun by the owner before the next claim that rests on it. (d) A run label says "rules outside the reference" for an `atr_mult` stop, the `event_exit` overlay or a hold over 5 sessions (`OracleVerifiedFor` stays `sim-9`). (e) A run that reads a quintile labels the thin seasons ("NaN in k of n seasons") and the filings whose reaction session was never seen with `days_since_results == 0`. (f) `SurpriseInputs` keeps one filing for each instrument and season, the earliest broadcast, so a revision does not count twice in the breakpoints. (g) `comparison.csv` and `diagnostics.csv` do not show the `event_exit` count (it would change the bytes of every comparison); the run record's counters do. (h) `AddEventExitExitReason.Down` fails once an `eventexit` row exists, because `research.sim_trades` is append-only.

## Consequences
- The Lab can run the four variants and the control; protocol P can be fed. It is NOT_EVALUABLE until the bars and filings reach 20 seasons with `sue_yoy` computable in each (CF-04); the verdict states the missing seasons.
- A `FIXED_RR` of 10 is an approximation of "hold only"; the count and share of target exits make it visible.
- The run label of a `PEAD` run carries the target line and, while the parser is labelled synthetic, the count of filings whose `sue_yoy` rests on it.

## Known limits
- **A pre-open announcement is missed.** A filing broadcast before the open of its own reaction session is visible only from the next date, where `days_since_results` is already 1, so `days_since_results == 0` does not fire for it. The series can only see less than the instant (ADR 0185); a rule of `<= 1` would trade the announcement session itself.
- **The breakpoints come from the universe of the run, not from all tracked instruments.** A basket of fewer than 100 names with results in the previous season ranks nothing (NaN, no entry). The universe is part of the run record, so the run says what it was ranked against.
- **The per-trade flag of an unavailable event exit is a count, not a column.** The trade record (schema 5) has no spare field; the run counters carry the session closes and the trades that had one.
- **The oracle does not cover the new rules.** The independent walker reference has no results calendar and no ATR stop; `OracleVerifiedFor` is `sim-9` because the corpus of the oracle (every rule it models) still agrees, and `event_exit`, the 20-session hold of an event side and `atr_mult` have hand tables of their own. The label "agrees with a separately written reference" is therefore true of the rule tables it covers; the owner may prefer to leave the label unclaimed for `sim-9`.
- **Previous-season breakpoints lag by one season** when the surprise distribution moves; survivorship (ISS-22) flatters a long-only test; costs are gross until ISS-25.
- The comparison tables (P4-05) list the exit policies and do not show the `event_exit` overlay's count; the run record's counters do.
