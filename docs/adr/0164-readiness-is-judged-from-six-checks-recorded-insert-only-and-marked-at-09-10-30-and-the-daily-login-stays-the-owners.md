# 0164 - Readiness is judged from six checks, recorded insert-only and marked at 09:10:30, and the daily login stays the owner's

- **Status:** Accepted (decided in P12-11 under the owner's standing run instruction; spec [P12-11](../specs/P12-11-daily-login-token-and-static-ip-operations.md)).
- **Date:** 2026-10-30.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** builds on ADR 0008 and 0009 (the consent flow is the only login), ADR 0155 to 0163 (the execution host, the halt record whose `token_invalid` and `ip_not_allowed` codes clear on a ready fact, the mode trail whose gate waits for the attestation) and ADR 0027 (notices). No order endpoint, allow-list entry, network host or deploy file is touched; no other project references Execution.

## Context
An execution that could act on a token that ends mid-session, on an address Dhan does not know, or on a morning nobody logged in, would fail in the worst place. The system needs one place that says whether it can act today and why not, and a record of the morning that cannot be argued with.

## Decision
1. **The daily login is the owner's (spec D1).** No renewal loop, no TOTP login, no PIN or secret in the app. The app reads the stored connection and the broker's profile; it never obtains a token.
2. **Six checks, three outcomes.** `connected`, `token_covers_session`, `token_live`, `ip_configured`, `ip_attested`, `no_blocking_halt`, each pass, fail or unavailable. A broker rejection (`ip_rejected`, `token_rejected`) is not_ready whatever else is unavailable; otherwise any unavailable check makes the verdict unavailable; only all-pass is ready. Unavailable blocks exactly like not_ready and never counts as a pass. The rule is pure (`ReadinessPolicy`, `TokenCover`) and takes every fact as an argument.
3. **The token must outlive the session.** Default cut-off 16:45 India time (`exec.ready_token_until`, 16:45 to 18:00; earlier is riskier and not allowed) so the final reconciliation at 16:30 can still read; at least `exec.token_min_remaining_min` must remain. Order of judgement: expired, then expiring, then short.
4. **The IP is verified by comparison and attestation (D4).** `getIP` lists what is configured at Dhan, not where a request came from. Readiness compares the owner's declared address (`exec.expected_static_ip`) with `getIP` and requires an owner attestation of that address (orders allowed, at most `exec.attestation_max_age_days` old, default 30). An attestation is a claim recorded as such, not verified. A DH-905 voids readiness until a newer attestation, never by time.
5. **The 09:10:30 mark.** One `day_states` row per account and session; on time it stores the verdict of the probe made at that moment; if the service was not running (more than 5 seconds late) it stores `unavailable` with `late = true`, never reconstructed as ready; a later ready check leaves it as it was.
6. **One loop, two reads.** `ReadinessLoop` runs in `xd-exec` for accounts whose effective mode is assisted, on trading days from 08:00 to 16:30 (30 s from 09:00 to 09:15 while not ready, else `exec.readiness_interval_s`, default 300), through `IBrokerFacts` (profile and `getIP`, nothing else) and `IConnectionFacts` (status and expiry, never the token). It stores a row on a change and at least every 15 minutes. The owner's "check now" is a `readiness_now` command that wakes the loop. A ready verdict is the fact that lets the halts `token_invalid` and `ip_not_allowed` of that account clear (the P12-05 contract through `HaltAutoClearService`); a halt of another kind is untouched.
7. **`no_blocking_halt` counts the halts this slice does not clear.** A halt that readiness itself clears would keep it from ever being ready, so `token_invalid` and `ip_not_allowed` are not counted; any other active halt of the account or the system, or a halt record that cannot be read, is not ready or unavailable.
8. **Storage.** Three insert-only tables (trigger refuses UPDATE, DELETE, TRUNCATE for every role): `readiness_checks` and `day_states` written by `xd_exec`, `ip_attestations` written by `xd_app` (a trigger stamps the database time; actor must be `owner:`), each read by both. `day_states.check_id` is nullable (a late mark after the reading hours may have no check) and `readiness_checks.ip_modify_after` keeps the first day the IP can change again; the spec's table lists neither. A ready check without its three evidence columns is refused by a check constraint. The SEBI re-check stays P11-05's (`trading.compliance_records`); this slice reads it through `IComplianceRecords` and stores nothing for it.
9. **Read model and notices.** `GET /api/execution/readiness?date=` (no token, client id, key or secret), `POST /api/execution/ip-attestations`, `POST /api/execution/readiness/checks` (check now). The notice schedule (`exec_login_reminder`, `exec_not_ready` warning at 08:45 and error at 09:05, `exec_recheck_due`) is a pure rule in the shared domain, so P12-10 derives from one place; in-app only, nothing leaves the machine. Logs 6740 to 6749 carry an account hash, the verdict and reason words, never a token, an address or a note.

## Not built, owed
The Dhan adapter of the two reads (`GetProfileAsync`, `GetStaticIpAsync` through P12-02's `ExecTokenSource`): P12-02 has neither, so `IBrokerFacts` is the closed default (unreadable), every verdict is unavailable and no account is ready today. The tolerant parsers (`DhanReads.Profile`, `DhanReads.StaticIp`) exist and are tested; the HTTP calls need their entries in the call list and the allow-list, a later step. The send path does not yet call `NoteRejectionAsync` (nothing composes a sender). The mode gate of P12-07 still shows the static-IP fact as unavailable; reading `ip_attestations` there is the next change. `NotReadyDayTests` need P12-08's intent creator. The card, checklist and attestation form are the frontend slice.

## Consequences
Execution has one honest answer to "can it act today", a morning record, and a runbook; the default state (no record, closed broker facts) is not ready. Nothing new can send.
