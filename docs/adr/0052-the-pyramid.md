- **Status:** Accepted (spec [P4-04](../specs/P4-04-the-pyramid.md), which the owner approved on 2026-10-19, "yes to all recommendations", questions 1 to 8; built on 2026-10-22 ahead of the P3 exit check because the owner pulled P4 forward). The text of this ADR has not been read by the owner.
- **Date:** 2026-10-22.

## Context
Doc 08 §4 describes a position that enters part of its size and adds when a bar closes at a progress level, with a risk rule that keeps the open risk at or below the initial 1 R, and trails the combined position by the ATR. It is the first policy with more than one entry leg. This slice runs it, one exit set at a time (comparison of candidates is P4-05).

## Decision
1. **No new walker, no new rule type.** The ladder is data: `PyramidPlan` (initial share, levels, shares, `max_adds`) inside `LegPolicy`, built by `Simulator.Create` from the compiled `ExitPlan`. The trail of the combined position is the chandelier of ADR 0051 (`AtrTrailRule` with `activate_at_rr` 0, built by `TrailRules.Of`), so the stop of the risk rule and the trail are one stop that only tightens. The add itself is stateful (the next level, the add that waits) and lives in `LegState.Pyramid.cs` next to the policy of ADR 0050; `MinuteRule.AdvanceLegs` makes the add after the rule has read the bars of the minute. No type implements `IExitRule` that did not before.
2. **The ladder and the trigger (D2).** At a counted bar *k*, with no add waiting and a level left (`max_adds`, and the ladder), a close with `sign × (Close − entry₀) >= level × R0` (compared as a product, like every progress in P4) attempts the next level: the limit is the entry's (the close of the bar plus the entry limit percent, rounded down, a sell up, to the tick). Only one add waits at a time, so a close beyond two levels attempts the first now and the second at the next bar that closes there, even when one read sees two bars. A level is consumed when its add is made, missed or skipped; the next is armed at once.
3. **The add order (D3).** It fills only at the **open of the first minute that holds a trade** at or after the bar's close, and only if that open is within the limit; otherwise the add is **missed** (`AddsMissed`, never filled at the limit later: the order inside the minute is unknown and the stop would have no moment). It slips as an entry (ADR 0038 S3, never past the limit). Three things are decided here because the spec leaves them open or states two sentences that meet: **(a)** a minute that ends the position anyway makes no add and counts nothing: the square-off minute, a minute where the `time_stop` is due, and a minute that reaches the stop in force before the add (low at or through it for a buy); the spec's sentence "a minute that is also the minute of a stop exit is a stop exit and the add is not made" is read this way, and a minute that reaches only the **new** stop (the one the add would set) makes the add and then stops all the shares out, as D4 says; **(b)** the order of the three outcomes of an add that is made as an attempt is: missed (limit), skipped for quantity, skipped for risk, so that a counter says the first reason that applies; **(c)** a bar that closes at or after the square-off is read at a minute that is itself the square-off, so it needs no test of its own.
4. **The risk rule (D4) and a finding about it.** Before the add fills at `P`, `S_req = (Σ s·e + s_add·P − d·R0) / (Σ s + s_add)` (`d` +1 for a buy, −1 for a sell) and the stop becomes `max(S_now, S_req)` (a sell: the minimum), from the minute of the add. The branch "skip when `S_req >= P`" (`AddsSkippedRisk`) is kept as the spec writes it, but it **cannot be reached**: `S_req < P` is the same as `Σ s·(e − P) < R0`; the open risk at the stop in force is at most `R0` at every time (the initial leg holds `initial_pct/100 × R0`, and each add sets a stop that restores it), an add is made only when the minute does not reach the stop in force, so `P > S_now`, and `Σ s·(e − P) < Σ s·(e − S_now) <= R0`. The counter is therefore always 0; a property (`PyramidPropertyTests`) asserts it over 11,100 series and the hand table has no row for it, which differs from criterion 2 and D13 of the spec (they ask for a row and an oracle case). The risk cap itself is a property: at every bar the rule is handed, the legs filled so far at the stop in force hold at most 1 R of open risk.
5. **The exit (D6, D7).** All open shares leave together as one exit leg at the first of the stop in force (market-type, with the stop's extra ticks), the square-off, `MAX_HOLD`, the `time_stop` (progress against the **first** entry), or the end of the data. A pyramid is **always written with legs**, even with no add: the entry leg of `initial_pct / 100` and the exit leg of the same share. This differs from D7 and criterion 8 (a pyramid with no add has no legs) on purpose: `SimTrade` has no field for the share, and the costing sizes the first leg from the legs' shares; a trade without legs would be charged on the full size. `initial_pct` is always below 100 (the relation `initial_pct + Σ add_pct <= 100` with a non-empty ladder), so the case always arises.
6. **R, MAE and MFE (D9).** The unit is `Q × R0`, the risk of the full size. `RExact = sign × (Σ_exit s·x − Σ_in s·e) / R0` (exact prices) and `RFilled` the same on filled prices; a pyramid stopped at the cap shows −1.00, one stopped before any add shows −`initial_pct/100`. MAE and MFE are taken against the share-weighted average entry of the legs open **at that minute**, in units of `R0`: the extremes of a run restart at the new average at the minute of an add (`LegState.BeginSegment`, the finished segment's excursions are kept as maxima), the minute of the add counts against the new average, and every exit minute contributes what the rows X2 to X5 and the other exits already say, against the average in force. A pyramid with no add has the MAE and MFE of any one-fill trade.
7. **Costs (D10, D8).** Each leg is one order (ADR 0049 already charges per leg); the exit leg's quantity is the sum of the legs in. `TradeCostApplier` gained one check: when the first leg holds no whole share (`floor(Q × initial_pct/100) = 0`), the trade is a skipped quantity. The walker needs `Q` to see that an add holds no whole share, but `Q` is known only to the costing: `Simulator.Run` takes an optional function `(entry, risk) -> Q`, the replay gives it the sizing of its cost context, and `ExitContext` hands it to the pyramid. A **gross** run has no `Q`, so it skips no add for its quantity (`AddsSkippedQuantity` is 0 there). The cap on capital is applied to `Q` at the first entry's price (the sizing of ADR 0042, unchanged); the adds fill higher, so the notional of a pyramid can exceed it: a named limit, `pyramid_capital_cap`, the eleventh of `CostLimits` (and of the oracle's list), printed in the banner of a costed run and in `run.json`.
8. **What the simulator accepts (D11).** One exit candidate among the seven policies and at most `time_stop`; the two ladders `add_at_rr` and `add_pct` are not candidate lists and are accepted in full, every other list of more than one value is still `simulation.unsupported_exit`; `EVENT_EXIT` and two candidates are refused at their paths. `ExitTables.RunBySimulator` holds all seven policies, so `exit_not_evaluated` is no longer given for any exit; the tests that the model's and the simulator's lists agree are updated.
9. **Counters, versions, stores.** `AddsTaken`, `AddsMissed`, `AddsSkippedRisk`, `AddsSkippedQuantity` are appended to `WalkCounters` with default 0 (39 counters) and appear in `run.json`, so the golden files of `run.json` and the banners differ from before by those lines only (criterion 1 asked for byte identity; the goldens are regenerated as in ADR 0051). The engine stays `sim-6`; no migration, no new `ExitReason`. The store still refuses a trade with legs (ADR 0049, decision 12), so `simulate --store` refuses a pyramid; the export (CSV, `run.json`, the card, which spells the add leg) keeps the legs.
10. **The oracle (D13).** `OraclePyramid` and a `PyramidStep` in `WalkRun`, written from D1 to D10 and this ADR: the bars are read in order, the add waits in a variable, the average entry and the legs in are lists, and the excursions are taken minute by minute against the average at that minute (the engine folds segments instead), so the two formulations of D9 are compared. The trail is the oracle's own `OracleTrail` of kind ATR with no activation. The test tape is `TrailTape`, the same input to both sides; the full size is a number given to both (unknown, 3, 5, 40 or 100, so that adds of no whole share occur).

## Consequences
- A strategy with `PYRAMID` runs end to end through `Simulator.Run`, the replay and the export; no verb offers it yet, because a verb runs a built-in strategy by id and the three built-ins carry `FIXED_RR` (as in ADR 0051).
- `FIXED_RR`, the partials and the trails take the same code path as before; the only change they meet is `AdvanceLegs` testing one flag that is false for them. The `TrailAllocationTests` and `PartialWalkAllocationTests` pass unchanged; a pyramid allocates its legs and nothing per minute (`PyramidAllocationTests`).
- The claim that the risk rule never skips is a consequence of the other rules, not a separate guarantee: a change to the order of "stop reached" and "add made" would make the branch reachable, and the property would then fail.

## Honest limits
- The rule, the oracle and the test tape were written by the same model family from the same spec; the comparison finds differences between two readings, not a misreading they share. Reading (a) and (b) of decision 3 and the choice in decision 6 (the new average from the add's own minute) are such readings; they are written here so that the owner can overturn them.
- The oracle does not recompute the ATR or the closes: it takes the tape as given.
- A gross run does not skip adds for their quantity, so a gross and a costed run of the same pyramid can differ in the adds (the costed one is the one that means money). The named limit says so only for the capital cap; the difference between the two is stated here and in the run record's counters.
- The favourable extreme of the first (at-limit) fill minute follows X5 as for any trade; adds fill only at an open, so no add has that case.
- No verb runs a pyramid, the store cannot hold its legs, and the throughput of the `FIXED_RR` path against its P4-01 figure (criterion 10: 5 %) is not measured in this change: the explicit benchmark of ADR 0051 is the tool and runs on a quiet machine.

## Alternatives considered
- **A pyramid as an `IExitRule`.** Rejected: the add needs state between bars (the level, the add that waits) and a fill at an open, which the contract (a pure function of the view and the tape) does not carry; the policy record of ADR 0050 already holds such state.
- **Fill the add at the limit when the minute trades back to it.** Rejected by the spec (question 3): the order inside the minute is unknown, so the stop would have no moment.
- **A trade without legs for a pyramid with no add (D7).** Rejected, decision 5: the costing would need a share on the trade; adding a field to `SimTrade` for it would be a schema change for one policy.
- **Skip an add whose notional is above the capital cap.** Deferred (spec question 4): a named limit instead, until a result needs it.
