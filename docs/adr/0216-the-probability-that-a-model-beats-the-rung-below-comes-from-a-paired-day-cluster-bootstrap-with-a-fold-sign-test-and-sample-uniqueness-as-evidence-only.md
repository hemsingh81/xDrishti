# 0216 — The probability that a model beats the rung below comes from a paired day-cluster bootstrap, with a fold sign test and sample uniqueness, as evidence only

- **Status:** Proposed (built in CF-10 part 10c-1 from the owner-Approved spec, 2026-11-03). Becomes Accepted when the owner has read it. **Safety slice: no** for this part (the figures are printed in the validation report and no verdict, eligibility rule, champion rule or trainer reads them; no switch, no rollback writer, no order). The safety parts of CF-10 (the influence predicate with its gate, champion / challenger, rollback) are 10c-2 and 10d and are not built.
- **Date:** 2026-11-05.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** completes the input that [0215](0215-a-models-complexity-is-shown-against-a-ladder-with-an-events-per-variable-cap-and-its-features-against-a-stability-index-and-a-direction-check-as-evidence-only.md) (10b) left open (the probability of beating the rung below); follows [0214](0214-a-models-calibration-is-chosen-by-sample-size-platt-below-500-calibration-rows-isotonic-from-500-and-the-error-is-reported-with-a-day-cluster-bootstrap-interval.md) (10a, the same seeded generator and the same day resampling) and the programme rule of CF-00: a new figure is report-only. CF-12 reuses the bootstrap.

## Context
Spec CF-10 D2 lets a rung be used only if it beats the rung below with a probability of at least 0.95; D4 says how that probability is found (a paired bootstrap that resamples whole trade days); D5 asks for training rows to be weighted by how unique their labels are. 10b took the probability as an input and the harness had none, so every rung read "not available". This part builds the calculators and gives the ladder its figure.

## Decision
1. **The paired day-cluster bootstrap (`PairedDayBootstrap`, pure, Domain, a stable surface for CF-12).** Input: the gain of a model over a baseline on each of the same rows (baseline log loss minus model log loss, positive is better) and the trade day of each row. Whole days are resampled with replacement (as many picks as there are days), the mean gain over the resampled rows is recomputed (a day with more trades weighs more), the result is `ProbabilityBetter` (the share of resamples with a mean above zero) and the percentile interval of the mean gain (default 2000 draws, 90 %). Generator: the Domain's `SeededRandom` (SplitMix64, the one 10a's `EceInterval` uses), seeded from the sha256 of a key made of the two scorer names and the span, so a rerun repeats. **Oracle (spec AC5):** per-day gains `+2, +1, -1, +3`: of the 256 resamples 237 have a mean above zero, so `P = 237 / 256 = 0.9258`, "promising", not a pass; the unit test enumerates all 256 and the seeded run (20000 draws) agrees within 0.01. Two more hand cases in the tests: days `+1, -1` give 1/4, and a day of three rows of `+1` with a day of one `-1` give 3/4 (rows, not days, are averaged).
2. **Documented behaviour at the edges.** A resample mean within 1e-12 of zero is a tie and counts **against** (as the oracle does: the pair `(+1, +1)` with two `-1` sums to zero and is one of the 19 not-better). Fewer than `MinDays` (default 10, at least 2) distinct days is **not available** (`bootstrap.too_few_days`): the resamples would repeat a few values and the share would be 0 or 1. Gains that are all equal give a probability of 0 or 1 and an interval of one point (that is the honest answer for no variation, and the tests pin it). Non-finite gains, a length mismatch, draws outside 100 to 20000, a level outside (0.5, 1) are refused with `bootstrap.invalid`. Input order does not matter. Cost: `draws x days` random picks, no allocation per draw (day totals are built once; one array of the draws for the interval).
3. **The reading (`PairedReading`).** `Pass` from the pass bar (`Confidence:Ladder:MinProbability`, 0.95); `Promising` from `Confidence:PairedBootstrap:PromisingFrom` (0.90) and below the bar, **never a pass**; otherwise `Below`. The research note's two conditions (`P >= 0.90` and a lower bound above 0) collapse to `P >= 0.95` for a two-sided 90 % interval, so only the stricter is kept (spec D4).
4. **The fold sign test (`FoldSignTest`, spec AC6).** Per-fold gain (the fold's mean log loss of the rung below minus the scorer's); the one-sided exact binomial tail at one half over the folds with a non-zero gain (a tied fold is left out): 5 of 6 better is `7 / 64 = 0.109375`, 6 of 6 `1 / 64 = 0.015625`. A fold with a gain that is not a finite number is left out with the ties. No fold that counts, or more than 1000, gives no p-value. The existing helpers were not reused because none fits: `ClusterBootstrap` (P15-10) gives bounds of a difference of means and no share above zero or tie rule, and the existing binomial tails (`PairedComparison.PValue`, `SlippageStats.BinomialCdf`) are two-sided or built for other counts. It is shown beside the bootstrap and decides nothing.
5. **Sample uniqueness (`UniquenessWeights`, spec AC7 and D5).** A label spans the minute bars from its decision to its resolution; `u = mean(1 / c_t)` over its bars, `c_t` the number of labels spanning bar `t`; weights are `u` scaled to mean one; the sum of `u` is the effective count. A sweep over start and end points, `O(n log n)` whatever the size of the bar numbers. Hand case: bars 1-2, 2-3, 3-4 give `u = (0.75, 0.5, 0.75)`, effective count 2.0, weights `(1.125, 0.75, 1.125)`; labels that do not overlap weigh 1 each; the tests also compare it with a direct count over each bar on random spans. **Only the effective count is used** (printed next to the number of training rows in the complexity block); nothing trains on the weights, since that would change what the trainer accepts and is a later step behind a switch.
6. **The ladder reads the probability.** With the ladder on and `Confidence:PairedBootstrap:Mode` ReportOnly, `WalkForwardValidator` computes, on the pooled out-of-sample rows of the used folds, the logistic baseline against the cell rate and each candidate against the logistic baseline, and passes each probability to `ComplexityLadder.Assess`. A rung is now "earned" when it reaches the bar **and** is within its cap (logistic) or has the events (trees) **and** has no structural reason. **The trees rung keeps its structural reason from 10b:** the candidate carries "the trainer has 8 leaves per tree and no monotone constraints ..." whatever its probability, so it can never read earned until the trainer meets D2 rung 4 (unit test: a probability of 1.0 still reads not earned; and with the real ML.NET trainer). The cell-rate rung is not given its own line (as in 10b). An unavailable probability (the bootstrap off, too few days) is `null` and the rung is not earned: **an unavailable figure never counts as a pass.** The block gains a line per comparison with the probability, its reading, the mean gain, the interval, the days and draws, and the sign test; the settings line names the bootstrap settings.
7. **Settings.** `Confidence:PairedBootstrap`: `Mode` (Off | ReportOnly, default ReportOnly; Gate refused because nothing here enforces), `Draws` 2000 (100..20000), `Level` 0.90 (above 0.5, below 1), `MinDays` 10 (2..1000), `PromisingFrom` 0.90 (0.50..0.99, and not above `Confidence:Ladder:MinProbability`, checked at start; equal leaves no promising range). Bound and validated at start; the binding and each refusal are tested. As in CF-01 to CF-10b the "versioned settings home" is the configuration section; a change takes a restart. The bootstrap needs the ladder on (it shows inside the ladder block); with the ladder off nothing is computed.
8. **Isolation.** No type of Planning, Selection or Autonomy (Domain or Application) names a new type, no verdict type does, and the scorer types do not (architecture tests with a control that the rule sees the dependency of the evidence builder); the calculators are pure (no clock, no I/O, no system random).

## Changed behaviour
A model trained from the host with the ladder on now has a longer complexity block (the effective count line and, with the paired bootstrap on, the paired lines and the settings), so the stored report text, and with it the registration key (`VersionKey` hashes the report text), differs from the same rows before this change: a rerun of an older training counts as a new registration rather than deduplicating, as in 10b. Verdicts, hashes of the model file, parameter text and trainer behaviour are unchanged. Callers that do not set `ValidationOptions.Ladder` get the report they always got. Cost: two bootstraps (the logistic baseline and each candidate) of 2000 draws over the number of trade days, and one sort of the label spans.

## Limits
- Block length is one day, so serial correlation across days is ignored (spec UNVERIFIED); with few days the probability is coarse, and the bootstrap of a gain says how stable the gain is on this history, not that the history will repeat.
- The gain is the log loss, which is the quantity the margins of 0067 use; a model can win on log loss and lose on the money (the effect on realised R of the P6-13 policy is part of the predicate in 10c-2).
- The pooled rows are the used folds' test rows; the folds are cut once, so the same rows serve the logistic-against-cell-rate and the candidate-against-logistic comparisons (two looks at the same history; the multiple-testing factor of the trials is not applied to the probability).
- Two shortcuts, marked in the code: the label spans count calendar minutes (`HoldOut.BarSeconds`), not trading bars, so overnight and weekend gaps lengthen a span (switch to session bars when uniqueness weights feed training); and the block length is fixed at one day (the spec's `block_days` setting is not built; add it when serial correlation across days is measured).
- The tie tolerance (1e-12) and the cuts (2000 draws, 90 %, 10 days, 0.90 promising, 0.95 pass) are proposals.

## Open owner questions

## Consequences
The ladder of every stored model report now says, with a probability and an interval, whether the logistic baseline and the model beat the rung below on the same out-of-sample days, and how many independent labels the training rows are worth; nothing that chooses what to approve changes, because the probability stays advisory.
