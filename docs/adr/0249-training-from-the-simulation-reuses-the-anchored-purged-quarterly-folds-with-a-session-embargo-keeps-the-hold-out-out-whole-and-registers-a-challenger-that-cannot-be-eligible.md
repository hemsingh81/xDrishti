# 0249 — Training from the simulation reuses the anchored, purged, quarterly folds with a session embargo, keeps the hold-out out whole, and registers a challenger that cannot be Eligible

- **Status:** Accepted by delegation (owner, 2026-11-09: unsettled decisions are researched, the best chosen, recorded here).
- **Date:** 2026-11-10.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** Builds on [0243](0243-simulate-everything-is-training-data-fenced-from-the-hold-out-and-from-scorecard-evidence.md) (point 4 and point 5) and [0248](0248-the-simulate-everything-job-is-a-resumable-table-driven-queue-of-training-chunks-that-yields-to-market-hours-disk-and-the-broker.md) (point 5, point 9). Reuses ADR 0066, 0067 and 0070 unchanged. Does not change 0217, 0220 or 0221.

## Context
AU-13 learns from the whole simulation: train on the past, score on the next period, never touch the hold-out, never promote.

## Research and decisions (best practice, chosen by delegation)
1. **Walk-forward only, anchored.** Purged k-fold cross-validation (Lopez de Prado) shuffles which periods train, so a later period trains a model scored on an earlier one; the owner's rule is walk-forward only, so it is not used. Between anchored (expanding) and rolling windows the choice is anchored: it is the existing validator (ADR 0067: quarterly test windows, first test 9 months in, train on everything before), the history here is long and not known to be non-stationary enough to throw old rows away, and a rolling window would have to invent a length. No setting is added: a window length is a hyper-parameter, out of scope. If a later study shows decay, a rolling variant is a new ADR, not a switch.
2. **Purge by label horizon, plus an embargo in sessions.** Each train row is dropped when its label resolves after the first decision of the test window (the purge of 0067), and rows from the days just before the test window are dropped on top (the embargo). Best practice is an embargo of about 1 % of the sample or at least the longest label horizon; the longest holding period a strategy may declare is 20 sessions and the purge already covers the horizon exactly, so the embargo only has to absorb serial dependence at the boundary. `Training:EmbargoSessions` is 0 to 20, **default 5 sessions** (the same 7 calendar days as ADR 0067), converted to calendar days with the trading calendar (holidays and special closures included): the longest span of that many sessions over the job window, so a holiday week lengthens it (5 sessions = 7 days and 20 = 28 in a plain Monday-to-Friday stretch, more across holidays). Out of range stops start-up. No direction is declared in the settings catalog (none of the Training keys gates a verdict).
3. **The hold-out is out whole, by the label horizon.** A closed trade enters only if it was decided in the job window and its exit falls before the hold-out block start (`SimTrainingRules.Admits`). A trade decided before the block that exits in it is left out, so no label ever uses a day of the block; the count of those is in the summary. The training window already ends the embargo before the block (0243 point 3). The hold-out store is not named by the training code (architecture-pinned), and the database row has `CHECK (hold_out_read = false)`.
4. **Reading.** Runs are read only through `ITrainingRunReader`, which gained `StreamTradesAsync(runKey)` (cash trades of a run found under the `purpose = 'training'` fence, the trades read through that run id only). The one learning-side handler may name the reader (the architecture list of 0248 point 5 names it). Features come from the existing decision-time feature extractor (`ExtractTradeFeaturesHandler.ExtractAsync`, now shared): a trade is described to it by its decision instant, side, stop and target only. Labels, point-in-time cell statistics and the joined rows are the existing `TrainingSetBuilder`, `PointInTimeCells` and `TrainingDataBuilder`; the training set is stored in the existing table.
5. **One cell per strategy, side and timeframe across all runs.** The existing loader refuses two runs of one strategy; a job has one run per strategy and instrument, so the training loader merges the trades of all runs of a strategy into its cell (the cell parts that no model reads, style and exit, are fixed join keys).
6. **Cell statistics on their own basis.** The spec says a `basis` value `training_sim` on cell snapshots. Adding a column to `research.cell_stats` would have required a fence on eight readers and on the `aiv` view, and a missed one would silently mix bases. Decision: a separate table `research.training_sim_cell_stats` whose `basis` column is `CHECK (basis = 'training_sim')`, so no reader of the research and forward statistics can see it; the same intent, fail closed. Migration `AddTrainingSimBasis` (down drops both new tables).
7. **Not evaluable, not a weak model.** Fewer than `Training:MinRows` (default 1000, range 100..1,000,000) usable closed trades, or fewer than 6 usable folds (the verdict floor of 0067), stores a `not_evaluable` record naming the first missing quantity ("rows: 40 usable closed trades, 1000 needed" or "walk-forward folds: 2 usable, 6 needed (the history runs ... )"), trains and registers nothing.
8. **Challenger only, and not Eligible.** The model is registered through the existing registry as a challenger. Its verdict is capped at Inconclusive (a model learnt from simulated trades never reaches the Eligible that the champion rule of 0070 needs), the row `CHECK`s `verdict <> 'Eligible'`, and the training folder may not name designation, promotion, rollback, the influence gate or switch, or the hold-out (architecture-pinned). `MlMayInfluence` is untouched and false. It stays out of the scorecard unless it later meets the existing rules by their own path.
9. **Bounded memory.** Training runs in the 1 GiB xd-api container. Above `Training:MaxRows` (default 150,000, range 10,000..200,000; measured: 40,000 kept rows from 400,000 streamed trades peaked at 79 MiB of live heap, about 2 KiB a row. Training runs inside xd-api, whose container limit is 1 GiB (`deploy/compose.yaml`), so 150,000 rows is about 300 MiB and leaves room for the API itself; the range stops at 200,000 (about 400 MiB) until the container's memory budget is raised) rows are thinned by a stable hash of the trade key (same inputs, same rows), and the summary says every how-manieth trade was kept. Trades are streamed per run; only the admitted and sampled ones are kept, only their features are extracted, and only the stored features of the run at hand are read, so memory is bounded by `MaxRows` and one run. The cell statistics are computed on the kept (thinned) trades and the parent record states `sample_every` for them. The measurement on the real data is an owner step (it needs a finished job).
10. **Resumable, off by default.** `Training:Enabled` is false. When on, the job loop trains once per host start after a job completes (and `completed_with_failures`, on the finished chunks, stating how many did not finish), only while the market-hours, disk and bar-history yields allow; a failure is logged and not retried until the next start. While it runs, the market opening is a deadline and a watcher checks disk and the bar-history job every poll interval; either cancels it and releases the attempt so a later tick resumes it. The input hash covers the job, window, hold-out start, the settings, the feature version, the trainer parameters and the sorted run keys, so the same inputs write nothing twice and a changed embargo is a new training. Nothing here places, modifies or cancels an order or names the execution service.
11. **Linking a tuned strategy to its sweep (0243 point 5).** The link already exists as lineage: every stored strategy version has a parent hash. `CountTrialsAsync` now counts the `training_sweep` rows whose swept hash is a version of the family or one of its ancestors by parent hash (a strategy tuned from the swept version, in any family) as `LinkedSweeps`, and `TrialCounts.Total` includes them: each distinct swept version is one look (re-sweeping an unchanged version adds none, since the honest count is of versions looked at). The family's own swept versions outside the lineage are returned by SQL as `UnlinkedSweeps` and shown, not counted. The old distinct-hash count of the unswitched judgement is unchanged, so nothing moves for the owner until the honest-count switch is on. The sweeps of unrelated versions stay beside the count. The deflated Sharpe of 0205 counts variants with stored statistics and a sweep has none, so it is unchanged and says so through its existing limits text.

## Consequences
A model learnt here is a candidate for the existing evidence path only; it adds no look at the hold-out. A strategy tuned from a sweep is judged with a higher honest count and a tighter factor. Thinning above one million rows keeps memory bounded at the price of fewer rows, stated in the summary.
