# 0053 — The same-entries comparison, the MAE/MFE diagnostics, stored legs and stored comparisons

- **Status:** Accepted (spec [P4-05](../specs/P4-05-same-entries-comparison-and-mae-mfe-diagnostics.md), which the owner approved on 2026-10-22, questions 1 to 8 as recommended; built on 2026-10-22). The text of this ADR has not been read by the owner.
- **Date:** 2026-10-22.
- **Relation to other ADRs:** [ADR 0049](0049-the-exit-tape-trades-with-legs-and-sim-6.md) to [0052](0052-the-pyramid.md) (the walk rules, used as built; legs "not stored yet" ends here), [ADR 0045](0045-the-result-store-sim-runs-and-sim-trades.md) (the store, insert-only, the unique `(run_id, trade_key)` kept for this join), [ADR 0037](0037-simulation-conventions-and-the-two-stage-seam.md) and [ADR 0039](0039-an-engine-module-is-reached-from-an-adapter-in-infrastructure-never-from-application.md) (the port, which gains the exit choice), [ADR 0047](0047-the-oracle-and-the-rule-table-gaps.md) (the oracle), [ADR 0040](0040-swing-positions-held-across-sessions-the-maximum-hold-the-position-gate-and-simtrade-schema-3.md) (the gate).

## Context
P4-01 to P4-04 built seven exit policies that only tests could run: no verb could choose an exit, the simulator refused two candidates, and the store refused a trade with legs. Doc 08 §3 says the exit is chosen by replaying the same historical entries under every candidate, and reading the MAE/MFE of the trades to judge the stop, the target and the trail. This slice makes that possible and says, at every figure, how far it can be trusted.

## Decision
1. **A verb chooses an exit (D1).** `--exit POLICY[:name=value,...]` (repeatable; `a|b` expands a candidate list; the ladders of `PYRAMID` are not expanded) and `--time-stop` on `simulate` and `verify-pack`. `ExitFlags` (Domain, pure text) reads them into an `ExitChoice` of expanded candidates with a stable id (`FIXED_RR{rr=2}`, parameters in name order, `+time_stop` when the overlay is on); `ISimulationEngine.Prepare` gains an optional `ExitChoice?` and the built-in's exits and overlays are replaced by it (its entry, stop and time rules never are). Refusals are usage errors (exit 2) that name the flag and the value: an unknown policy or parameter, a list on a parameter that takes one value, `--param rr` together with `--exit`, `EVENT_EXIT`, two identical candidates, more than 16 candidates, `--time-stop` without `--exit`. No `--exit` is the built-in as before, byte for byte. A `verify-pack` takes **one** candidate (a pack is the hand check of one exit set); a comparison is a `simulate` matter. The run key needs no new input: the definition hash is of the definition with its exits, so a run with another exit has another key.
2. **One scan, N walks (D2).** `SimulatorComparison.Create` builds one `Simulator` for each candidate by the same `Simulator.Create` (so every refusal of P4-02 to P4-04 stands, with `candidates[i].` at the start of the path), and one scan side: the first member's compiled side with the exits of the others added (`CompiledSide.WithExitsOf`), whose features are the union and whose tape holds one column for each feature some candidate reads. `Run` scans each side once and walks every entry candidate once per member with that member's own rule, which finds its own column by name. **The spec's way, one compile of the strategy with all candidates as `exits`, does not work**: the model refuses two exits of one policy (`strategy.model.duplicate_exit`), which is what `FIXED_RR:rr=1.5|2|2.5` and two `PARTIAL_BE` candidates are, and a list expanded to one exit with a list parameter is not a product of independent candidates. `Simulator.Create` and `Run` are unchanged except that the walk of a side is a method both use.
3. **Same entries, ungated (D3, D4).** Every candidate walks the identical list of entry candidates; the trade key does not depend on the exit. A swing comparison is **not gated**: every candidate walks every entry, overlaps allowed (a banner line says so). The entries a gated run would have skipped under each exit are counted (`PositionGate.CountBlocked` over the ungated walks gives what the plain run's gate gives: a blocked entry is not walked and does not change the others) and printed as a column. A single-exit run is gated as before.
4. **The common set and the table (D5, D6).** The common set is the trade keys present under every candidate; every figure is printed on each candidate's own trades and on the common set, with the mean difference from the first candidate on the common entries and the count of trades that are not common. The unit is `RFilled` (gross) or `RNet` (costed), labelled. The figures: trades, wins (R above 0), win rate, mean, median, **downside deviation `sqrt(mean(min(R, 0)^2))` over all trades of the sample, target 0** (doc 08 and doc 16 name the term and never defined it; P4-06 reuses this definition), total, profit factor (null without a loss), average hold in minutes, share by exit reason, mean `CostR` if costed, and the counters of the policy. Open-at-end trades are in no statistic (ADR 0045).
5. **The MAE/MFE diagnostics and their censoring (D7, D8).** Per candidate: the shares of winners (`RExact > 0`) whose adverse excursion exceeded 0.8R and 1.2R; the median MFE and the shares reaching 1R, 2R and 3R; the median giveback (`MFE - RExact`) of the trades that reached 1R and the share that gave back more than half. Each figure carries its **censor level** (the stop at 1R for adverse figures, the target in R of a `FIXED_RR` or the `t2_rr` of a `PARTIAL_BE` for favourable ones, `none` for a trail) and is **censored** when it asks for more than the walk lets it show (a share reaching 3R under a 2R target, a dip beyond 1R under a stop at 1R): it is printed as such with no reading sentence. A cell of fewer than 30 trades is printed `n < 30` and no figure has a sentence. **The adverse excursion is the trade's `MaeR`, the largest over the whole trade, not "before its best point"** (the walker stores no order of the two); for a winner this is the dip on the way. **`--diagnose`, the optional walk with no target and a widened stop, is not built** (the spec's own first cut if the slice overran): without it "do winners dip beyond 1.2R" and "what share reaches 3R" cannot be answered from a `FIXED_RR` run, and the table says so for each.
6. **Legs are stored (D9).** Two child tables, not one: `research.sim_trade_legs` (a row for each `Leg`: epoch, price, filled price, share, kind, reason) and `research.sim_trade_leg_charges` (a row for each fill of `TradeCosting.LegCharges`: epoch, quantity, turnover, the eight amounts). One table with the charges beside the legs, as the spec sketches, would join two lists of different length (the fills of one minute are merged in the costing). Both are keyed `(run_id, open_at_end, ordinal, ordinal in the list)`, with a foreign key to `sim_trades` without cascade, insert-only for the runtime role (migration `AddSimTradeLegs`; `MakeAppendOnlyForAppRole`). The legs are written by a binary copy after the trades in the same transaction, with a count check of both tables; they are read in chunks of 2,000 trades beside the trade rows, on a second connection, and only when the run has a leg. `simulate --store` stops refusing a run with legs; what the tables cannot hold (a trade with fewer than two legs, an unknown kind, a reason over 64 characters) is still refused by name.
7. **A comparison is stored (D10).** One ordinary run for each candidate (its own run key) and one row in `research.sim_comparisons` (migration `AddSimComparisons`): the comparison key, the **entries key**, the strategy, the unit, the count of common entries, three parallel arrays (the member run keys, the candidate ids, the gated counts), the text of `comparison.csv` and `diagnostics.csv`, and the SHA-256 of that text. The entries key is the run key text with the definition hash replaced by the hash of the definition **without its exits and overlays** (`xd-entries 6`): equal for runs that differ only in the exit, different when any other input changes (a test changes each in turn). The comparison key hashes the entries key and the member run keys in order. Idempotent on the key; another record under a stored key is `store.run_conflict`; a comparison whose member is not stored is refused (`store.write_failed`). Events 4425 to 4427. The record is the text of the export, not a JSON record: the numbers are printed to four decimals in the one place that writes them.
8. **Records and versions (D11), changed from the spec.** `SimulationRun` stays at schema 6, `SimTrade` at 5, the engine at `sim-6` (no walk rule changed), the single-run export at `xd-sim-export-3`, and a single-exit run is byte-identical to before. The spec asked for record schema 7 with a `comparison` block and format `xd-sim-export-4` for every export; a new schema number would have changed the run key of every run and every golden file for a block that is null for a single run. The comparison is a record of its own instead: its folder is `xd-sim-export-4` (`comparison.csv`, `diagnostics.csv`, `trades.csv` and `run.json` of the first candidate, `trades-NN-<id>.csv`, `open_at_end-NN-<id>.csv` and `run-NN-<id>.json` of the others, the readme, the manifest with the entries key and the members), named by the comparison key, so it never meets the folder of a plain run. No trade card is written for a comparison (a card shows one run; a single `--exit` gives the cards of one policy).
9. **The banner of a comparison (D11)** says, besides the run's labels: candidates compared on the same entries and the best row in-sample and not evidence of an edge; the censor rule; and, for a swing strategy, that the gate is not applied.
10. **The oracle (D12 f).** `OracleComparison` computes the table, the paired figure and the diagnostics from the text of the spec and seven numbers of each trade, with plain loops and one sort; `ComparisonStatsOracleTests` agrees with the engine's report on 300 seeded instruments and seven candidates, gross and costed. The walk rules keep the oracles of P4-01 to P4-04.

## Consequences
- The seven policies run from the command line for the first time: `simulate ... --exit PARTIAL_BE:book_pct=50,t1_rr=1,t2_rr=2` is an ordinary run with legs that `--store` keeps, and a list is a comparison.
- A stored run can now hold legs; the store's reads return them, and the CSV made from the store equals the one from memory (the legs column).
- P4-06 reads what this stores: the candidate ids, the entries key and the join on `trade_key`.
- A swing comparison overstates simultaneous positions (the limit is named in the banner and counted).

## Honest limits
- Nothing here says that a strategy has an edge; the best row of a comparison is in-sample.
- The MAE of the diagnostics is over the whole trade; "adverse excursion before the best point" would need an order the walker does not record.
- `--diagnose` is not built, so the uncensored tail of the MFE and the dip beyond 1R are not readable from any run; the censor column says which figures this affects.
- A comparison of 8 candidates over 214 instruments is expected to cost about one scan and 8 walks; this is **not timed** (no timed P3 run exists, and the explicit benchmark of the single `FIXED_RR` path against its P4-04 figure has not been run on a quiet machine). The scan runs once is tested by the counters, not by a clock.
- The property "alone equals together" and its control share a generator with the comparison (the seeded sessions of the other P4 properties); the control is a comparison that walks a candidate with its neighbour's exit, which proves the check can see a shared exit, not that no shared state exists elsewhere.
- Costs differ by exit (the charge depends on the orders), so under a costed comparison an entry can be skipped under one candidate only; the common set and the "not common" count say so.

## Alternatives considered
- **One compile with all candidates as the exits of the strategy (the spec's D2).** Rejected, decision 2: the model refuses two exits of one policy.
- **One run holding every candidate's trades (a changed primary key).** Rejected by the spec (D10); not reconsidered.
- **A `jsonb` column of legs on `sim_trades`.** Rejected by the spec (question 6): queryable rows and exact number spelling.
- **A comparison record as canonical JSON with a schema number.** Rejected, decision 8: it moves every run key.
- **Gating the comparison walk.** Rejected by the spec (question 2): the entries would differ between candidates.
