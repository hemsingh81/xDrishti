## Goal
Take an entry candidate made by hand and the 1-minute bars after it and get a trade the owner can check by hand: where it entered, where its stop and target were, why it left, R, MAE and MFE, or the reason there is no trade. The walker is the second stage of the two-stage simulator; the first stage, the entry scanner (P3-03a), and the walker share nothing but a small value, the entry candidate, which carries no exit, so that P4 can replay the same entries under every exit policy. A property through the walker's own loop shows that a closed trade does not depend on the minutes after it, and every trade says what it is: fills exact, no costs. Pure library: Domain only; no frame, no compiler, no database, no YAML, no market context.

## Definitions (numbers and rules the tests pin)
All times are India time. A *minute* is a 1-minute `Bar`. The numbers D1, D2 and D8 of the first draft (decisions, the order at a decision step, ORB v1) are P3-03a's and keep their numbers there, so that the references in P3-03b and P3-04 hold; D3 to D7 and D9 are this slice's. The scanner parts of the first draft's D6, D7 and D9 (the scanner counters, the header labels and the scanner fixtures) are **D10, D11 and D12 of P3-03a**, so that no number means two things; where this text below says "D6", "D7" or "D9" for the walker it means the walker's.

**D3 — Entry.** The walker takes the candidate and the list `bars` (the 1-minute bars of one instrument, strictly ascending). The candidate's own minute is `bars[DecisionBarIndex]`: it starts at `DecisionEpochSeconds − 60`, because the decision instant is the end of the minute that closed the evaluation bar (the candidate says where the decision was taken; the walker does not recompute it). The *fill minute* is `bars[DecisionBarIndex + 1]`, the first minute that starts at or after the decision instant. In this order: there is no such minute, or it is on another India date than the candidate's minute, or it starts more than `MaxFillDelayMinutes` (5) after the decision instant: no trade, `NoFillBar`; it starts at or after `square_off` (`WalkRules.SquareOffMinute`): no trade, `AfterSquareOff`. A fill minute that starts later than the decision instant means minutes are missing and the order rested through them, at most five (`EntriesAfterMissingMinutes`). The bound exists because a market-type fill at the open of a minute far from the signal is a price unrelated to it, and the replay of P3-04 runs sessions with up to 37 missing minutes (its 90 % rule). **Entry price = the open of the fill minute** (the bar that triggered has closed; an order cannot fill in it). The stop is `candidate.StopLevel`, used as given; risk per unit `r = |entry − stop|`. A BUY needs `entry > stop`, a SELL `entry < stop`: **a fill at or through the stop is no trade** (`EntryThroughStop`, counted). Target = entry + `rr` × `r` (BUY) or entry − `rr` × `r` (SELL) when `WalkRules.RewardRisk` is given, otherwise none. The entry limit of [doc 07](../design/07-strategies.md) §2 (+0.1 % of the trigger close) is not applied here (P3-03b) and the label says so (D7).

**D4 — Exit.** From the fill minute on, minute by minute, while the minute is on the entry's India date:
1. A minute that starts at or after `square_off`: exit `SquareOff` at its **open** (the always-on `SESSION_EXIT` of doc 08 §2). Its range is not looked at.
2. Stop touched (BUY: `low ≤ stop`; SELL: `high ≥ stop`; touching counts): exit `Stop` at the stop level, or at the minute's **open when the market gapped through the stop** (BUY: `min(stop, open)`; SELL: `max(stop, open)`; [doc 19](../design/19-risks-and-compliance.md) §1, "gap fills at open"). **Stop first:** a minute that holds the stop and the target is a stop.
3. Else target touched (BUY: `high ≥ target`; SELL: `low ≤ target`): exit `Target` at the target level; a minute that opened beyond the target gives no price improvement.
4. Else the minute is counted and the walk goes on; with no later minute on the entry date, exit `EndOfData` at the **close** of the last minute (data, not a rule: counted apart, and P3-04 reports such trades apart, never among the trades that statistics count). The minutes of the next date are never read.

Stop checks start **in the fill minute itself** (its open cannot be beyond the stop: that is `EntryThroughStop`). `ExitEpochSeconds` is the start of the exit minute in every case.

**D5 — R, MAE, MFE.** `R = (exit − entry) / r` for a BUY, `(entry − exit) / r` for a SELL. Excursions are measured from the entry price over the minutes from the fill minute to the exit minute, in R of `r`, never below 0: MAE the worst adverse extreme, MFE the best favourable one. The exit price is itself a visited price, so `−MAE ≤ R ≤ MFE` always. The path inside the exit minute is unknown and stop first is assumed, so that minute counts as follows. After a *Stop*: its adverse side is the exit's own distance `|exit − entry| / r` (1.0 exactly at the stop level, more after a gap) and its favourable side is not counted. After a *Target*: its favourable side is the target distance (MFE = `rr` exactly) and its adverse side counts in full. After a *SquareOff*: the minute's range is not counted (the position left at its open), its open is. After an *EndOfData*: it counts in full.

**D6 — Counters** (`WalkCounters`, in `XDrishti.Domain.Research`, summed by `Add`; `WalkOutcome.ToCounters()` gives the counts of one walk, and the flags of `WalkOutcome` are the same facts): `Candidates` (walks), `Trades`, `NoFillBar`, `AfterSquareOff`, `EntryThroughStop`, the exits by reason (`ExitsStop`, `ExitsTarget`, `ExitsSquareOff`, `ExitsEndOfData`), `StopGapExits` (Stop exits whose minute opened beyond the stop: BUY `open < stop`, SELL `open > stop`), `TargetGapExits` (Target exits whose minute opened beyond the target: BUY `open > target`, SELL `open < target`), `EntriesAfterMissingMinutes` and `BarsExamined` (the listed minutes the walk looked at from the fill minute to the exit minute, both included: 0 for `NoFillBar` and `AfterSquareOff`, 1 for `EntryThroughStop`). Identities: `Candidates = Trades + NoFillBar + AfterSquareOff + EntryThroughStop`, and `Trades` is the sum of the four exit counts. A rule that never fires gives zero trades and zero losses and looks safe; these counters are how it is seen. The scanner's counters are P3-03a's.

**D7 — What a trade says it is.** Every `SimTrade` carries `Fills = Exact` and `Costs = None`. *Exact* means the model's price with no slippage, no tick rounding and no entry limit: the open of the fill minute at entry, the level of a stop or a target, the open of the minute when the market gapped through a stop, the open at the square-off and the close at the end of data. The run header with its five labels (`fills: exact`, `entry limit: not applied`, `costs: none`, `portfolio limits: not simulated`, `sample: in-sample`) is P3-03a's (D11 there); P3-03b replaces the first two. A figure from this engine is a signal-level result on invented data and says nothing about edge.

**D9 — The fixtures.** Date 2026-03-02 (a Monday) unless stated; one stream per instrument `TEST`, regular weekdays; dense from 09:15 to 15:29 unless stated; volume 1,000 per minute (a trigger bar of *m* minutes has volume 1,000 × *m*). `HH:MM O/H/L/C` is one listed minute; **a minute not listed repeats the previous close as its open, high, low and close.** *Absent* means no minute in the list. ## Acceptance criteria
Numbered, each testable; "the table" is D9 and the walker table. All through the real walker; nothing is mocked.

**Types and the draft**
1. **The trade record is frozen.** Given `SimTrade` when its positional (constructor) properties are listed by reflection, `Key` excluded (it is computed), then they are exactly the 24 of the *Contract* table, in that order and with those types; `SimTrade.CurrentSchemaVersion` is 1; `ExitReason` is `Stop, Target, SquareOff, EndOfData`, `FillBasis` is `Exact`, `CostBasis` is `None` (and `Side` is `Buy, Sell`); and the `Key` of the trade of F1 is `ORB|TEST|Buy|2026-03-02`. Changing any of it fails the test until the schema version is raised and the ADR is amended.

**The trade walker**
2. **Trades.** Given the days F1 to F7, F9, F12 to F17 and F17b when each hand-made candidate is walked then the trade, or its absence, is exactly the "Trade" column: entry and exit instants (start of the fill and exit minutes), entry, stop, target, exit price, `ExitReason`, R, MAE, MFE, `Fills = Exact`, `Costs = None`, `EntrySnapshotJson = null`, the trigger-bar fields carried over from the candidate, and the minutes examined. F1 to F5 together pin the exit-minute rule of D5 (and F5 the square-off minute, not its range); F13 walks two candidates on one list.
3. **Walker rules.** The rows W1 to W14 give the results of the walker table, in the walker's own unit test with hand-made candidates and bars (no frame, no compiler). W6 pins the gapped stop at the open; W13 and W14 pin the two cases in which P3-03b differs.
4. **The fill delay is bounded.** Given F15, F17, F17b, W9 and W9b then a fill minute 0, 1 and 5 minutes after the decision instant fills, one 6 minutes after, or on the next date, or none, is `NoFillBar`, whatever its price; `MaxFillDelayMinutes` is read from `SimulationEngine`.

**The seam, determinism and look-ahead**
6. **The candidate knows nothing of exits.** `EntryCandidate` has no member about exits, and the walker has no frame. Given the F1 candidate walked with `WalkRules` of `rr` 1.5, 2.0, 3.0 and none then: 1.5 gives target 104.25, `Target` at 10:30, R 1.5, MFE 1.5, 46 minutes; 2.0 gives F1; 3.0 gives target 108.00, not reached, so the position runs to `SquareOff` at 105.00 at 15:15, R 1.8, MAE 0.4, MFE 2.0, 331 minutes; none gives the same as 3.0 with `TargetPrice` null (W8). The same candidates walked under several exit rules are how P4 compares exit policies.
7. **Same input, same bytes.** Given the F-days of AC2 and the seeded days of AC8 when each is walked twice, once on a copy of the bars, then a plain formatter in the test project (invariant culture, round-trip doubles) renders identical text for the outcomes and the counters. The rendering of the F-days and the W-rows equals a golden file checked in with the tests; the file starts with the `SimulationEngine.Version` it was made with, and a test fails when the rendered text differs from the file while the version in the file equals the constant (a changed rule needs a new version and a regenerated file, on purpose).
8. **No look-ahead through the walker.** Given 100 seeded series of one to three sessions (seeds written in the test; the generator in the test project makes, per series, 2 to 6 candidates at random positions of both sides with a stop 0.5 % to 3 % on the right side of the decision minute's close, `rr` from {1.5, 2, 3, none} and square-off from {14:30, 15:15}; it is tuned until at least 300 outcomes are compared, at least 50 of each side, and every exit reason and every no-trade reason occurs at least 5 times, asserted; holes, gaps, spikes and decisions within three minutes of the square-off are planted so that each reason can occur) and for each a random cut `k`, when the minutes after `k` are (a) removed and (b) replaced by extreme values (zero and huge prices and volumes, epochs unchanged) then every outcome of the full run that is *settled at* an index at most `k` equals, field by field as rendered by the test formatter, the outcome of the cut run for the same candidate; an outcome settled after `k` is not compared. An outcome is settled at the list index of its exit minute (a trade), or of the minute that decided it (a no-trade: the fill minute, or the candidate's own minute when no minute follows). **Negative control**, built in the test project from the public API only (the walker is a static function, so a doctored input and not a mutated walker): a bar list whose lows (SELL: highs) are replaced by `min(low_i, low_i+1)` (a stop test that peeks one minute ahead; the transform is applied to the full list and to the cut list each on its own) is run through the same property and reports violations on at least 10 seeds, while the honest walker reports none on the very same inputs. This is the walker half of the look-ahead property that the P3-02 follow-ups ask for through the simulator's own loop; the scanner and `Simulator.Run` half is P3-03a's, and P3-03b AC20 extends this property to its new rules instead of repeating it.
9. **Fill timing and arithmetic.** Over the same seeded runs, for every trade: the fill minute is the minute after the candidate's own, on the decision's date, starting at most `MaxFillDelayMinutes` after the decision instant and before `square_off`; the entry price equals that minute's open; every exit price lies in `[low, high]` of its exit minute, or equals its open (`SquareOff`, and a stop gapped through) or its close (`EndOfData`), or the exit is counted in `TargetGapExits`; the exit minute is on the entry's date and not before the fill minute; R, and `MAE` and `MFE`, recomputed from the stored prices and the minutes by a plain reference loop in the test equal the stored ones, and `−MAE ≤ R ≤ MFE`.

## Non-functional
- **Performance:** the walk is O(minutes of the position) with a few comparisons per minute and no allocation per minute (AC11); the explicit benchmark reports minutes per second and nothing is asserted. The scan and replay figures are P3-03a's and P3-04's.
- **Determinism and purity:** no clock, no `Random`, no I/O, no ambient state in the library; two runs give identical results (AC7). Seeds and generators live in the test project.
- **Honesty:** every trade says fills exact and costs none (D7); counters make a rule that never fires visible (D6).
- **Security and privacy:** nothing is read from `data/`; tests use invented series and one invented instrument. No secrets, no network.
- **Observability:** the library returns counters and logs nothing.

## Open questions
1. **Fill and exit conventions (blocks Approval; one answer covers this slice, P3-03a and P3-03b).** This is the one place where the convention the owner approves is stated in full: [P3-03b](P3-03b-fill-realism-gaps-slippage-ticks-and-minute-order.md) question 1 and the plan question Q1 of the [roadmap](../design/18-roadmap.md) are this question, and the roadmap does not restate it. It is the final convention; this slice builds its first stage (the entry at the open without the limit, a stop in every minute that holds both levels, a square-off minute that exits at its open) and P3-03b the rest, as each letter says. Answer "yes to all" or name the letters you change.
   - a. An entry is a **limit order at the trigger close +0.1 %** ([doc 07](../design/07-strategies.md) §2, "limit +0.1 % max"; a BUY limit is rounded down and a SELL limit up to the tick), placed when the trigger bar has closed (a bar that closes at 09:45 gives an order that is live from the 09:45 minute). It fills at the **open of the first minute after the bar** when that open is inside the limit; at the **limit** when the minute opens beyond it and trades back to it; otherwise it is a **counted miss and is never filled later**. A bar that has just closed cannot be filled in, so this is more pessimistic than doc 07 §2, "Entry: at trigger-candle close", whose price no order could get. *Built:* this slice fills at the open and does not apply the limit (its trades say `entry limit: not applied`); the limit, the fill at the limit and the miss are P3-03b. How long the order lives is P3-03b question 2.
   - b. A minute that holds the stop and the target is a **stop**; **one exception:** when the minute's first print (its open) is already beyond the target, the target comes first. *Built:* this slice takes the stop in both cases (row W13); the exception is P3-03b.
   - c. **Touching a level counts** as hitting it. A stop that the market gapped through fills **at the minute's open** (doc 19 §1, "gap fills at open"); a target that it gapped through fills **at the target** (no price improvement). A square-off minute that opens beyond the stop fills the stop at that open, and one that opens beyond the target fills the target. *Built:* this slice exits at the open as `SquareOff` in both square-off cases (row W14); P3-03b fills the stop at the open and the target at the target.
   - d. A fill at or through the stop is **no trade** (counted).
   - e. **One trade per instrument, side and day: the first trigger**; a stop-out does not arm the pattern again, and neither does a miss (P3-03a builds it; P3-03b question 2 asks how long the entry order lives).
   - f. A position still open when the day's data ends is closed at the last close and **reported apart** (`EndOfData`), never among the trades that statistics count.
   - g. BUY and SELL may be **open on one instrument at the same time** (portfolio netting is not simulated; P3-03a).
   - h. A trigger bar with a missing minute **may still trade**, flagged and counted (`Complete = false`; P3-03a).
   - i. *Decided in this spec (2026-10-18) on research, not asked; the owner may revert:* an entry may wait **at most 5 minutes** past the decision for the next existing minute (`MaxFillDelayMinutes`); later is no trade (`NoFillBar`, counted). It is an engineering bound (D3), not a trading choice.

   **Recommendation:** yes to all of a to h (D3 to D5 and the rules of P3-03b). If not, say which letter.
