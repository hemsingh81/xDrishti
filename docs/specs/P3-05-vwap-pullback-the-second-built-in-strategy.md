## Goal
Add `VWAP_PULLBACK` to the built-in strategies so that `simulate --strategy VWAP_PULLBACK` runs it, BUY and SELL, on 15-minute bars: in an up-trend (hourly and daily), after two closes above the session VWAP, a dip bar that comes back to within `pull_atr` × ATR of VWAP and a bar that closes above VWAP and its own open is a BUY; stop under the pullback low, one fixed reward-to-risk target, out by 15:15. It is built the way ORB v1 was (a pure C# builder, a registry row, no engine change), checked by hand-calculated days, a plain reference loop and the look-ahead property, and it gives the owner a second set of trades for the early look at 3 to 5 cards and for the 20-trade check.

## Definitions (numbers and rules the tests pin)
All times are India time. A *bar* is a 15-minute bar; bar *n* of a session starts at 09:15 + 15(*n*−1) and is decided at its close, 09:30 + 15(*n*−1): bar 1 at 09:30, bar 3 at 10:00, bar 21 at 14:30, bar 25 at 15:30 (the step of the 15:29 minute). `x[k]` is the value *k* closed 15-minute bars ago (by position inside a session for `vwap`, ADR 0035). Prices are rupees.

**D2 — The setup, two items per side, evaluated together with the trigger at every closed 15-minute bar (doc 11 *How the sections combine*).**

| Side | Item | Text |
|------|------|------|
| BUY | `setup[0]` | `tf("1h").ema(20) > tf("1h").ema(50)` |
| BUY | `setup[1]` | `tf("1D").close > tf("1D").ema(20)` |
| SELL | `setup[0]` | `tf("1h").ema(20) < tf("1h").ema(50)` |
| SELL | `setup[1]` | `tf("1D").close < tf("1D").ema(20)` |

A higher-timeframe read is the value of the **last closed bar** of that timeframe (doc 11 *The feature catalog*, ADR 0031): at 10:00 on date *D* `tf("1D")` is the daily bar of the previous session (a daily bar closes at the step of the 15:29 minute, P3-03e) and `tf("1h")` is the last closed hourly bar. **The hourly rule, pinned (ADR 0031 and the `TimeframeBuilder` rules of P3-01, doc 05 §4: "09:15 anchoring, a partial 15:15 hour"):** hourly buckets start at 09:15, 10:15, 11:15, 12:15, 13:15, 14:15 and **15:15**; the last one is a 15-minute bucket that closes at the step of the 15:29 minute and is an hourly bar of its own. So there are **7 hourly bars per session** (6 full, 1 partial), and at 10:00 the last closed hourly bar is the previous session's 15:15 stub; at 10:15 it is today's 09:15 to 10:15 bar. (If the stub were not a bar there would be 6 per session; that is not what the ADR says. The reference loop of D12 takes this bucket rule as a stated input copied from the ADR, not from engine source.) **Consequences:** `tf("1h").ema(50)` is ready after the 50th hourly bar = the first bar of the 8th session (about 7.1 sessions; with 6 bars it would be 8.3), `tf("1D").ema(20)` after 20 daily bars, so the first possible trade of a replay is on session 21 (the daily item binds); on PU the margin `ema(20) − ema(50)` of the hourly closes at the start of the fixture day is +1.054 with 7 bars per session (+1.235 with 6), so the setup truth does not depend on the count but the figures in the test do; V6's bound is 5 sessions × 7 = 35 hourly bars < 50 (30 with 6). Until the averages are ready the items are `NotReady` and nothing trades (D8).

**D3 — The trigger, three items per side**, one idea each, so that the counters say which part keeps a rule from firing.

| Side | Item | Text | Reads |
|------|------|------|-------|
| BUY | `trigger[0]` | `close > vwap and close > open` | lag 0 |
| BUY | `trigger[1]` | `close[1] > vwap[1] and close[2] > vwap[2]` | lags 1, 2 |
| BUY | `trigger[2]` | `low[1] < low[2] and low[1] <= vwap[1] + pull_atr * atr(14)` | lags 1, 2; `atr(14)` at lag 0 |
| SELL | `trigger[0]` | `close < vwap and close < open` | lag 0 |
| SELL | `trigger[1]` | `close[1] < vwap[1] and close[2] < vwap[2]` | lags 1, 2 |
| SELL | `trigger[2]` | `high[1] > high[2] and high[1] >= vwap[1] - pull_atr * atr(14)` | lags 1, 2 |

Read (BUY), with *t* the bar that has just closed: bars *t*−2 and *t*−1 both closed above VWAP (as it stood at their own closes); bar *t*−1 is the **dip bar**: it made a lower low than bar *t*−2 and its low came down to at most `pull_atr` × ATR above VWAP (it may go below VWAP: a wick through VWAP that closed back above it counts); bar *t* closed above VWAP and above its own open (a bullish bar). Only the **level form** is used, no `crosses_above`: `vwap` keeps the history of the session only (ADR 0035), so `vwap[1]` is unknown on bar 1 and `vwap[2]` on bars 1 and 2, `trigger[1]` and `trigger[2]` are `Unknown` there and unknown never fires. **The first bar that can be known is bar 3, decided at 10:00 = the first entry**; that is why R14 starts at 10:00 and why a trigger "before the first entry" cannot be true (V7 shows it as `Unknown`). `atr(14)` is continuous across sessions (ADR 0031), so it is known from the first bar of a session once 14 bars have closed in all. All four lags are well under a session, so the lint `lag_across_session` (ADR 0034 table) does not warn, and no `crosses_*`, `rising` or `falling` is used, so `mixed_timeframes_in_lag` cannot apply to the `tf()` reads of D2.

**D4 — What "pullback" means here.** A bar-by-bar picture of the BUY: two closes above VWAP, then a bar that dips (lower low) to within `pull_atr` ATRs of VWAP but closes above it, then a bullish close above VWAP. The dip is measured with the **low** of the dip bar against VWAP at that bar's close, in ATRs at the trigger bar. It is a continuation entry, not a reversal: bars on the wrong side of VWAP make `trigger[1]` false. A shallow pullback (low more than `pull_atr` ATRs above VWAP) is not "to VWAP" and does not fire (V4 shows it). **There is no lower bound on the dip:** the low may be far below VWAP provided the bar still closes above it (a long wick), and then the `swing_low` stop is far below the entry: the risk can be several ATRs, the 2 R target correspondingly far, and the position size small (P3-03c sizes by risk). Q14 asks whether to bound it. The chosen pull is a distance in ATRs, not a percentage, so it scales with the instrument and the day.

**D5 — VWAP as the engine computes it.** On a 15m strategy `vwap` is the volume-weighted mean of the **15-minute bars' typical prices** (H + L + C) / 3 since 09:15, each weighted by the bar's volume, restarting each session (the indicator `Vwap` is fed the timeframe's bars; the F21 row of P3-03a computes (101 + 99 + 101)/3 for a 15m bar). It is **not** the minute-by-minute VWAP of a charting platform. For the hand days all bars have equal volume (15 minutes of 1,000), so VWAP after bar *k* is the plain mean of the *k* typical prices. Risk 3 and question 12 carry the consequence for the hand check.

**D6 — Entry, stop, target, exit (all existing engine rules, quoted for the examples).** The trigger bar closes at the decision instant; the order is placed in the minute that follows (ADR 0037 D1). BUY limit = tick-down(trigger close × 1.001) (SELL: tick-up(close × 0.999)); the open of the fill minute within the limit fills at the open, otherwise the minute that touches the limit fills at it, otherwise `LimitNotReached`, final (ADR 0038 E2 to E5). The stop is the value of `lowest(low, 2)` (SELL `highest(high, 2)`) at the decision step, that is the lower of the dip bar's and the trigger bar's lows, **used as given** (no buffer, not rounded to the tick). Risk = |entry − stop|; target = entry ± `rr` × risk. A touched stop exits at min(stop, open) (SELL max), a touched target at the target (touch is a fill), a minute that reaches both is a stop unless its open is already beyond the target, and the first minute that holds a trade at or after 15:15 exits at its open (ADR 0038 X1 to X4). A zero-buffer stop at a low is touched by a retest of that low: the fill of a stop at the exact low of a pullback is the engine's rule, not this strategy's choice, and Q3 offers the alternative. Examples use `SlippageConfig.None` (fills exact), `FillRules` 0.1 %, tick 0.05, costs none, so that every price is a number written below.

**D7 — Parameters and where their ranges are enforced.** `pull_atr`: declared range `0 < x <= 3` (`Min` 0 exclusive, `Max` 3), default 0.5; a finite value outside it makes `BuiltInStrategies.Build` return one located problem at `parameters.pull_atr`, code `simulation.parameter_out_of_range`, and no definition; a non-finite value is not judged by the range check and keeps the existing `ArgumentException` of `StrategyPreparation.Prepare` (finite check first, then the range, then the compile). `rr`: any finite number is passed to the compile, which accepts 0 < rr ≤ 10 (`ExitTables`, read 2026-10-19) and refuses the rest at `buy.exits[0]`'s `rr` as for ORB. An unknown parameter name keeps the existing refusal (`parameters.<name>`, the message lists `pull_atr` and `rr`). The ATR period (14), the trend periods (20, 50, 20) and the lookback (2) are **not parameters** in this draft (Q6).

**D8 — What the scanner counters will show for this strategy** (ADR 0037 D10; counts per condition path `buy.setup[0]`, `buy.setup[1]`, `buy.trigger[0..2]`). On a full 25-bar session: `DecisionSteps` 25; `OutOfWindowSteps` 6 (bars 1 and 2, bars 22 to 25); `WindowSteps` 19 (bars 3 to 21); each of the five paths counts True + False + Unknown + NotReady = 19. `FullyReadySteps` = 19 once the daily and hourly averages are ready, and 0 on a replay with fewer than 20 prior sessions (D2). The counters are how a rule that never fires is seen; the first real run of the owner's data should be read for `buy.setup[1]` NotReady (the cold start) and for which of the three trigger items is rarely True.

**D9 — Fixtures.** One stream `TEST`, volume 1,000 per minute, prices multiples of 0.25, a minute not listed repeats the previous close (the `Day` builder of `backend/tests/Shared`). **D12 — The plain reference loop and the generator behind AC13 and AC14.** *The loop* (test code, `Support`): it is built from the rule text of D1 to D6 and D2's bucket rule only; it **shares no production helper** (it may use the test/Domain types `Day`, `Bar`, `Side`, nothing from `XDrishti.Indicators`, `Strategies` or `Simulation`), and it carries a mutant control (AC14). The claim is an artefact rule, not a process claim: nobody can check the order in which files were read. It takes the minute bars, builds the 15-minute bars, the hourly and daily bars (closed bars only; the 7-bar hourly rule of D2 is a stated input), VWAP (D5, weighted by the bar volumes), Wilder ATR (seeded with the mean of the first 14 true ranges) and SMA-seeded EMAs with plain loops and decimals, applies the setup, the window, the three trigger items and the day's first-candidate rule, then walks the trade minute by minute with the limit, stop and target rules of D6 (the P3-08 oracle may be absent; the loop carries its own plain walk). It must reproduce the seeded days **exactly**: candidates (side, decision index, stop) and trades (entry, stop, target, exit price, reason, R). A difference is a defect in the engine or the loop, fixed against the spec text. *The generator* (test code, deterministic from a seed): each seed draws a prefix kind (up or down, 50/50) of 21 banded sessions that ends at a random level between 90 and 150 in quarter-rupees, then 1 to 3 random days (AC13: 100 seeds; AC14: 25 seeds of 2 days = 50 days). A random day is built bar by bar from a two-phase band-walk around the running VWAP so that dips to VWAP occur: a *run phase* (3 to 6 bars) drifts in the direction of the prefix trend by 0.3 to 1.0 times the previous ATR per bar, a *pull phase* (1 to 3 bars) drifts back toward the running VWAP by 0.5 to 1.5 ATRs, phases alternate at random, each bar's four prices and its minute path are noise around the drift (quarter-rupee grid), volumes are random between 500 and 1,500 per minute, now and then a minute is absent (rare). Seeds are written in the test; the generator's parameters are tuned until the floors hold, and the floors are asserted so that the checks cannot pass on empty days: AC13 compares at least 60 candidates with at least 20 of each side; AC14 shows at least 25 days with a candidate, at least 8 of each side, at least 5 days with a `LaterTriggers` count above 0, at least 3 stops, 3 targets and 3 square-offs.

**D13 — What the 20-trade check will see.** Trades with `StrategyId` `VWAP_PULLBACK`, both sides, intraday; exits `Target`, `Stop`, `SquareOff`, and the rare `EndOfData`; each card shows the bars, the entry, stop, target and exit (P3-04c). The sampler's strata (strategy, instrument, year) get a second strategy; the edge categories that can occur here are SELL, a stop in the entry minute (possible with a tight `swing_low` stop), a gap exit, the first evaluation of a session (D3: only from bar 3, so none), `end_of_data`, a hole through a stop, and an incomplete bar. ## Open questions
Each has a **Recommendation** first; none changes the scope of the slice except Q3 (a separate slice) and Q1 (changes the examples).
1. **What exactly is the "pullback"?** *Recommendation:* the proposal of R7: the dip bar makes a **lower low** than the bar before it *and* its low is at most `pull_atr` ATRs above VWAP *and* it closes above VWAP (the roadmap row reads "two closes above `vwap`, a dip to within `pull_atr` × `atr(14)` of it, then a close above `vwap` and `open`"; the lower-low clause is mine). Without it, a steady climb in the first hours satisfies the dip test on most bars (VWAP is close to price then; checked by hand on a staircase) and the rule fires on the first rise, which is not a pullback. Alternatives: the row's pure form (drop the lower low; one line of the definition); measure the dip with the dip bar's **close** instead of its low; allow the dip bar to close below VWAP (that is `VWAP_RECLAIM`, another name in doc 07).
2. **Does the dip bar have to close above VWAP (R6)?** *Recommendation:* yes (a wick through VWAP that closes back above it is the pattern; a close below is a failed trend, `VWAP_RECLAIM` territory).
3. **The stop.** *Recommendation:* `swing_low` over 2 bars (the pullback low: the lower of the dip bar's and the trigger bar's lows), zero buffer, **not** the row's `bar_low` (the trigger bar's own low alone). The trigger bar is by construction a bullish bar that closed above VWAP, so its low is often well above the dip's low; a stop at it is tight (risk under four ticks becomes frequent) and sits above the level the pattern says must hold. Both are accepted by `Simulator.Create` today (ADR 0043 D5), so the choice costs nothing; the roadmap word "below" cannot be written: a zero-buffer stop is *at* the low, and the engine fills a touch. The alternative the roadmap hints at (an ATR stop or a buffer under the low) needs a scanner and simulator change (`atr_mult`, `buffer_atr` are refused by name) and is a separate slice of about 1.5 agent-days with ADR 0045: say so if you want it.
4. **The entry window 10:00 to 14:30.** *Recommendation:* keep the row's window (bar 3 is the first bar the two lags can be known; 14:30 leaves 45 minutes to the square-off). Early-session VWAP sits close to price, so the first hour may fire easily even with the lower-low clause (Risk 2); if the first real counters show most candidates before 10:45, move `first_entry` to 10:30 (one line, a reopen of half a day at most).
5. **The trend setup.** *Recommendation:* hourly EMA(20) above EMA(50) and the last closed daily close above its EMA(20) (R3, R4): common and cheap, two lanes. Alternatives: `supertrend_dir` on 1h; `adx(14) > 20` as a third item; the daily close above EMA(50) (needs 50 prior sessions, about 10 weeks of cold start instead of 4).
6. **Which numbers are parameters?** *Recommendation:* two (`pull_atr`, `rr`), the rest fixed in the text (ATR period 14, the trend periods, the lookback 2, the window). The limit is four; promoting `atr` period and the hourly fast/slow averages to parameters costs one range check each (an arithmetic number is not judged by the compile, D7; the averages are judged by the catalog) and makes a later sweep (P5) possible, but each new knob is another thing to hand-check before the 20-trade check.
7. **Timeframes.** *Recommendation:* 15m only (R1). 5m or 30m change the meaning of "two bars", of the hourly and daily relationship and of the ATR; the other timeframe cells would need their own hand days.
8. **An `invalidate` rule?** *Recommendation:* none in v1 (as ORB v1 was a bare breakout, plan Q2). Candidates: close more than 1.5 ATR below VWAP, or the day's high-water more than N ATRs away.
9. **ISS-26: build the `rising`/`falling` cost lint now, or keep it deferred?** *Recommendation:* keep it deferred. The 35 % vs 20 % figure measures the work around the frame (window, counters, two truths per step), not a run, and this strategy has no run; the lint would guard a shape no built strategy has. This slice measures the share for `VWAP_PULLBACK` and writes it next to the 35 % (D10, AC16). If you want a slope filter (for example `rising(vwap, 3)` as a trend item), say so: it is the first use of a run, it would make the stated condition for revisiting true, and the cost of three reads is about 5 ns per step, so the lint would still not be the leading term (ADR 0034 Amendment 18).
10. **SELL: keep the explicit block in v1?** *Recommendation:* yes (the roadmap row says so, P3-02e's mirror is not built, and a short-side sample is one of the edge categories of the 20-trade check). The SELL texts are the exact mirror of D2 and D3 (comparison reversed, `high` for `low`, `−` for `+`).
11. **Reference loop and the P3-08 oracle.** *Recommendation:* write the reference loop here in full (entry rules and a plain walk) so that the slice stands alone; if P3-08 is merged first, call its oracle for the walk and keep only the entry rules (about 0.15 day less). The two are different checks: the loop proves this rule set was built as written; the oracle proves the walker.
12. **Which VWAP will you compare with on your chart?** *Recommendation:* keep the engine's bar-based VWAP for v1 (D5; it is what the rest of the platform computes and what P3-01 pinned) and let the recompute sheet of P3-09 print, for each sampled trade, the engine's VWAP and the 15-minute typical prices behind it, so that the hand check recomputes the number instead of reading a chart's different VWAP. A minute-based VWAP is a catalog change (P3-01), not a strategy change; say so if the owner's charting platform's number is the one you want.
13. **Where do the parameter ranges live?** *Recommendation (settled by the coordinator, kept here for the record):* declared on the public Domain record `BuiltInParameterInfo` and checked in `BuiltInStrategies.Build`, so that the listing can print them and P3-06 reuses them; the alternative (a private validator on the internal entry) was dropped because the listing could not show it.
14. **Should the depth of the dip be bounded below?** *Recommendation:* no bound in v1 (the row has none); read the first run's distribution of risk in ATRs and add, if wanted, `low[1] >= vwap[1] - 1.0 * atr(14)` as a fourth trigger item (one line; it keeps the stop within about 1 ATR of VWAP). Without it a long wick far below VWAP that closes above gives a wide stop and a distant 2 R target (D4).

## Risks
1. **A proposal, not a result.** The rule set may fire too rarely or too often, or on the wrong bars; the first real run's counters (D8) are the first evidence, and an edit after the early look reopens this slice (half a day, as the roadmap says). Passing the 20-trade check says the mechanics are right, not that `VWAP_PULLBACK` has an edge; every figure is in-sample and gross until the charge sheet passes its bill check (`--gross`, `costs: none`).
2. **Early-session VWAP.** In the first hour VWAP is close to price by construction, so "a dip to within 0.5 ATR of VWAP" is easy to satisfy; the lower-low clause and the 10:00 start reduce but do not remove it. Watch the time-of-day histogram of candidates in the first run.
3. **The engine's VWAP is not a chart's VWAP** (D5). The owner comparing a trade with a charting platform will see different VWAP and ATR values (bar typical prices vs minute prices; the platform's ATR seed and period may differ); the tolerance and the recompute sheet of the 20-trade protocol (P3-09, Q6/Q7) must say which number is the reference. UNVERIFIED how the owner's platform computes VWAP.
4. **Higher-timeframe warm-up and the window.** The daily setup needs 20 closed daily bars and the hourly one about 8 sessions; a replay window that starts near the first stored bar loses its first sessions to `NotReady` (visible in the counters). How P3-04's replay reads history before `From` for these lanes is UNVERIFIED here; V6 pins the engine-level behaviour, the replay-level behaviour is the first real run's to show. Window independence of a result for these lanes is a P3-04 property and is expected to hold; the property of AC13 does not prove it for a `From` cut.
5. **A tight stop.** Two bars' lowest low is usually close to the entry in a quiet stock; `RiskUnderFourTicks` and stops hit in the entry minute are the counters to read. A target at 2 R on a tight stop is a small price move: slippage and costs (when the sheet is checked) may dominate.
6. **Fixtures are artificial.** ATR exactly 1.0 and equal volumes are chosen so that every number can be checked by hand; the seeded random days (AC14) are what tests the rest. The banded prefix makes the trend setup true or false by construction; its value in the engine is asserted against plain arithmetic, not hand-derived to the last decimal.
7. **`vwap` and ADR 0035.** Every VWAP lag is unknown on the first bars of a session; a future change to the history rule would silently change when the strategy can fire. The golden file and V7 would catch it.
8. **Registry change touches shared tests.** The id-list assertions (`StrategyPreparationTests`, the verb listing test) are the only expected edits; an unexpected red test in the architecture suite is a sign that a new type slipped into the `Replay` namespace (Scope 3 forbids it); the Domain change (three record members, one constant) is public surface and is meant to move `DomainResearchSurfaceTests`.
