## Goal
A strategy becomes a typed value that the simulator and the live engine can compile and run. `StrategyCompiler.Compile(definition, evaluationTimeframe, side)` checks the model (schema 1: id, style, timeframes, sides, at most four numeric params, and per side the setup, trigger and invalidate lists, the entry, the stop, the exits and the overlays, plus the time rules and the filter names), compiles **every condition of every list as its own condition** with the rules language of P3-02, and returns one `CompiledSide`: the single feature list a frame is created from (the features of the conditions, of the stop and of the exits, plus a sentinel feature on the evaluation timeframe), the history depth, what the frame needs, the warnings, and a `BoundSide` that evaluates the lists at list level and shows the three-valued result of every item. Every problem is data with a **path** (`buy.trigger[1]`) and the span of the language **inside that item**. No YAML, no storage, no simulator.

**How the sections combine** (this settles [doc 07](../design/07-strategies.md) §1, "setup … evaluated tonight", against the example of [doc 11](../design/11-strategy-lab.md) §2, which puts `time` and `rsi(…)` in `setup`): at every closed bar of the evaluation timeframe E the sections are evaluated together and ANDed: an entry is possible when `setup` is true, `trigger` is true and `invalidate` is not true. Which parts of a setup are knowable at the previous close, for the nightly shortlist, is the business of the planner (P7), not of the model.

## Scope
**In:**
1. **The model**, new documented namespace `XDrishti.Strategies.Model` (no new project, no new package), schema version 1: sealed records and enums for the strategy (`StrategyDefinition`, `StrategyParam`, `SideRules`, `ConditionList`, `EntrySpec`, `StopSpec`, `ExitSpec`, `OverlaySpec`, `ExitParameter`, `TimeRules`, `Quantity`) and the closed sets of doc 11 §2 and doc 08 §2 (entry kinds, stop kinds, exit policies, overlay kinds, filter names). A field that holds a number may name a param instead of giving a literal (`Quantity`), as `lookback: swing_lookback` does in doc 11. `Side` (Buy, Sell) is added to `XDrishti.Domain.Research` (new namespace; P3-03 adds `SimTrade` and `ExitReason` next to it and reuses this enum). `Side` is not `XDrishti.Domain.Portfolio.TradeSide`: that enum is a stored ledger fact with its own storage rule ("stored by name, never rename a value"); a research side has its own schema versions (the model, `SimTrade`) and the two are mapped by name where a trade meets a ledger row. Names and bounds are pinned by a snapshot test (criterion 1), and the records compare by value (criterion 2).
2. **`StrategyCompiler.Compile(definition, evaluationTimeframe, side)`** in `XDrishti.Strategies.Compilation`, returning a `StrategyCompileResult`: a `CompiledSide` or the located problems (codes `strategy.model.*`, plus the codes of the language, unchanged, with a path). Errors are reported in the order of the model, the first 100 of them; warnings and information follow, at most 100 in all; one `too_many_problems` says that something was dropped (criterion 27; a cap of its own, in the manner of ADR 0034 Amendment 7).
3. **List-level evaluation** (`BoundSide`): an `all` list is the Kleene AND of its items, an `any` list the Kleene OR; every item gets its own `EvaluateTruth`, written into a caller's span with no allocation; the decision treats unknown as false and is **defined once**, in `SideEvaluation.Fires`: the simulator uses it and does not combine the three truths again. Lists are **never joined into one text**, so the 64-level limit (about 60 conditions in a chain, ADR 0034 Amendment 3) is never reached, no span points at text the author did not write, and no type error mentions an `and` the author never wrote. This replaces the note of Amendment 3 for P3-02b ("join the lists with parentheses up to about 60 entries, split at list level beyond that"): the join is not used at all, so there is no threshold, and the aggregate of the problems of many items has a cap of its own (criterion 27).
4. **The feature list**: the merged plan of all conditions, then the features read by the stop kind and by the exit kinds, then one feature on E when no feature is on E, as THE list a frame is created from; the history depth; `NeedsContext` and `NeedsExpiryDays`.
5. **The entry window** (`BoundSide.InEntryWindow`): `first_entry` and `last_entry` of the time rules against `time`, the close time of the evaluated step, read through the same function as the `time` keyword so that the convention exists once.
6. **Kinds the simulator cannot run yet** are accepted by the model and the compile (`stop_above_high`, `limit_pullback`, the partial, trailing and pyramid exits, both overlays, the two filter names) and are **refused by name by the simulator** (P3-03 and later); the compile reports the filters as warnings and refuses nothing for want of a simulator.
7. **`CompilerWarmup`** gains a valid and an invalid strategy, so the compiler warm-up covers the new path (criterion 29).
8. **Internal seams, one sealed class each** (so that the 46 codes do not end in one god class): `ModelChecks` (the definition level), `TimeRulesCheck`, `StopCompiler`, `ExitCompiler` (candidates and overlays; the policy table as data, name to parameter specs, so a new policy is a row), `SideCompiler` (the items, the merge, the sentinel). The catalog check is **extracted** from `BindingScope.CatalogAnswer` into a shared internal `CatalogCheck` used by the binder and by the stop and exit compile (a refactor that changes nothing in the language: its tests stay green unchanged); did-you-mean uses `Suggestions.Closest` (reused, not copied); stop and exit specs are built with `IndicatorInfo.DefaultSpec(S) with { P1 = … }`, whose source is already the canonical one (a catalog entry that ignores the source defaults to `close`), as `Binder.Features` builds them.
9. Tests, the architecture test (one more documented namespace), ADR 0036 and the documentation (Done when).

**Out (not in this slice):**
- **P3-02b2 — Exit policy tables of schema 1 (about 1 agent-day, before P4; spec to be drafted).** This slice checks the parameters of `FIXED_RR` and `PARTIAL_TRAIL` (the doc 11 example) and only the *shape* of the rest (criterion 11): `PARTIAL_BE`, `ATR_TRAIL`, `STRUCTURE_TRAIL`, `EMA_TRAIL`, `PYRAMID` and the overlays `time_stop` and `event_exit`. P3-02b2 writes, together with P4 so that the names are not guessed, their full tables (names, required, ranges, the features each reads on E) with a failing row and a near miss per row, and the doc 08 and doc 16 wording. It must settle at least: the doc 16 names that the draft tables do not carry (`cap_total_risk_to_initial` and `trail` of `PYRAMID`; `intraday_bars` and `swing_sessions` of the time stop; `EMA_TRAIL` and `PARTIAL_TRAIL` are in doc 08 §2 only, not in the doc 16 candidates) against doc 11's `trail: { type, mult }`; `PYRAMID`: `initial_pct` plus the sum of `add_pct` at most 100 (`value_out_of_range` at `buy.exits[i].add_pct`; doc 08 §4: 50 % + 30 % + 20 %) and `max_adds` at most the length of `add_at_rr`; `time_stop`: `intraday_bars` from 1 to `(375 + T − 1) / T`, T the minutes of E (a 15m session has 25 bars); a non-whole value in a whole-number field of an exit gives `field_shape` at the field (the stop fields keep the catalog's `feature_invalid`).
- **YAML, the JSON Schema, canonical text and the hash of a strategy (P3-02c).** One input for it, from the P3-02 Follow-ups: `ExpressionPrinter` prints `rsi()` and `rsi(14)` differently, so a hash that must be equal for equal meaning is taken from the plan (the canonical `FeatureSpec`s and the bound tree), not from the printed text; P3-02c decides it. This slice keeps each item's text as written (`CompiledCondition.Source`), keeps no field that depends on a spelling, and until P3-02c the run record of P3-04 stores the definition as canonical JSON.
- **Storage** in `lab.strategies` / `lab.strategy_versions` and the seeding of ORB, VWAP_PULLBACK and BASE_BREAKOUT (P3-02d); a built-in registry (the three built-ins are C# builders in the test projects until then; the ORB text is owned by the P3-03 spec, the fixtures here are copies and follow it).
- **The BUY → SELL mirror (P3-02e).** A strategy that lists SELL without a sell block is a located problem that names P3-02e.
- **The simulator and everything it decides**: what `at_close` fills at (doc 07 §2 says "at trigger-candle close, limit +0.1 %"; P3-03 fills at the open of the next minute within that limit), the formulas of a stop price and of an exit, the latch of an invalidated day, the counters and the decision-time trace, the check that the caller evaluates only at steps where `frame.BarClosed(E)` (the library may not reference `BarClosed`: an architecture rule), the `StrategyWarmupService` hosted service (P3-03: the first project that references `XDrishti.Strategies` will be `XDrishti.Simulation`, and the architecture rule that makes it call `CompilerWarmup.Run` stays vacuous in this slice because no `src` project references the library yet).
- **Late and doubled bars, holidays, restarts.** `InEntryWindow` answers for the clock of the step (`time`, ADR 0034 decision 4), not for the clock of the bar; telling a late bar from yesterday's, and a step that closed two bars of E, is the scanner's (P3-03 D1: stale and lost steps), because the library may not read `TryLastClosed` or `ClosedBarsAt` (criterion 24 shows the consequence). Holidays and special sessions: only the simulator knows the calendar (`max_hold_sessions`, P3-03d). A `BoundSide` holds no state of its own; a host that restarts rebuilds a frame and replays bars (a per-session feature needs only the bars of its session, ADR 0035).
- From the YAML of doc 11 §2: `universe`, `test`, `version` (a run setting, a Lab setting and a storage fact), a description. Cross-session tickets (`signal_valid_sessions`). An unused-param warning. A computed stop level (doc 11 lists `level`, nothing defines it: it is **not a stop kind of schema 1**, and `CompileValue` is not built), a subscript on a feature call and a never-true lint (P3-02f, only if wanted).
- **Hand-offs to the other Drafts** (reconciled in the build; the other three are Approved too): (a) P3-03 D2 uses `SideEvaluation.Fires` and does not combine the three truths itself; a step where setup and trigger are True and invalidate is Unknown is not a candidate and is counted there as `UnknownInvalidation`; the latch on a True invalidate stays P3-03's. (b) P3-03 AC18 drops `level` from its list of stop kinds (it is no kind of schema 1) and refuses a side with overlays by name like any exit other than one `FIXED_RR`. (c) P3-03 and later specs reuse `XDrishti.Domain.Research.Side`. (d) P3-02c refuses `type: level` and any `trail` type other than ATR by name, and writes doc 11's `trail: { type: ATR, mult }` as `trail_atr_mult`.

**Size.** 3 agent-days, the upper bound of a slice (the roadmap's 2.5 days was written before the review; P3-02b2 is about 1 more). If it overruns, cut in this order: the optional `Timeframe` of the stop (no built-in of P3 uses it; criterion 10 loses its rows), the 16-thread test (criterion 28), the fuzz from 1,000 to 200 mutations (criterion 27), the post-warm-up benchmark; never list-level evaluation, the sentinel feature or the model snapshot.

## Acceptance criteria
Numbered, each testable. "Pinned" values are asserted in tests, calculated by hand from the definitions here. Every row of every table in *Contract* has a failing example (exact code, path and severity) and a near miss that compiles without that problem; a convention test walks the constants of `ModelCodes` by reflection and fails for a code that has no failing example.

**The model**
1. Given the model of schema 1, when the snapshot test lists every public type of `XDrishti.Strategies.Model` and the enum `XDrishti.Domain.Research.Side` with its members, the enum values, the policy-to-parameter table of the exit policies this slice checks (name, scalar or list, bounds) and the problem codes, then the list equals the raw-string literal in `ModelSchemaSnapshot.cs` (a C# constant, no file I/O: unit tests never touch the disk), and `StrategyDefinition.CurrentSchema` is 1. Renaming or removing a member, an enum value, a parameter name or a code, or changing a bound, fails it with "a stored contract (ADR 0002)". The snapshot does not list the rest of `XDrishti.Domain.Research`, so the types P3-03 and P3-04 add there do not break it. The validator's tables and the tests' tables are separate literals, the tests' written from this spec, so a renamed parameter fails one of them. Every model type is a sealed record, a readonly record struct or an enum.
2. Given two `StrategyDefinition`s built twice from the same data, when they are compared, then they are `Equal` and have the same `GetHashCode` (lists compared element by element; `Quantity`, nullable fields and overlays included), and changing one item text, one param, one exit value or one time rule makes them unequal. (C# records compare a list by reference; the model therefore uses lists that compare by element, or writes `Equals` and `GetHashCode` in its records; the choice is the build's and is recorded in ADR 0036. P3-02c's round trip and P3-02d's "the seeded text equals the C# builder's definition" compare definitions.)

**The definition** (checked on every call, whichever side is compiled; the exceptions are `side_rules_missing` and `sell_block_missing`, criterion 5)
3. **Params.** Four params compile. A fifth gives exactly one `strategy.model.too_many_params` at `params`. A repeated name gives `duplicate_param` at the index of the second. `RSI_LEN`, `1x`, an empty name and a name of 33 characters give `bad_param_name` at `params[i]`; NaN and ∞ give `bad_param_value`. A param named `rsi`, `time`, `close` or `rising` gives the warning `param_shadows_name` at `params[i]`. The language's own `strategy.lint.shadowed_name` warning stays on **each item** that uses such a param (criterion 9), and a condition `rsi(14) > 55` compiled with a param `rsi` gives the language's `bad_arguments` error at that item next to it, not merged with it: `param_shadows_name` is the notice at the declaration, so one cause can appear as one declaration warning plus one warning per item that uses it. Criteria 13 and 14 use params that shadow nothing. Every declared param is offered to the conditions as written (the first of a duplicate), so a fault in a declaration (a duplicate, a bad name) is reported once, at the declaration, and does not cascade into the items.
4. **Identity, timeframes, style.** `Schema` 2 gives `schema_unsupported` at `schema`; an id that is empty, starts with a digit or has 65 characters gives `bad_id` at `id`; an empty `timeframes` gives `timeframes_empty`; `1m`, `1W` and `1M` give `bad_timeframe` at `timeframes[i]` (the entry timeframes are 5m 10m 15m 30m 1h 1D: doc 07 §4); a repeated timeframe gives `duplicate_timeframe`; `Compile(…, 15m, …)` for a definition whose timeframes are `[5m]` gives `timeframe_not_declared` at `timeframes`; an intraday strategy that lists 1D, and a swing strategy that lists 5m, 10m or 15m, give `style_timeframe_mismatch` at that `timeframes[i]` (a swing strategy may list 30m, 1h and 1D: doc 07 §3 and §4; the rule is this spec's proposal, open question 6); a style or any other enum field holding a value the closed set does not define (`(Style)7`) gives `undefined_value` at that field's path.
5. **Sides.** An empty `sides` gives `sides_empty`; a repeated side `duplicate_side`; compiling a side that `sides` does not list gives `side_not_declared` at `sides`; `[BUY]` with a `Sell` present gives the warning `side_rules_unused` at `sell`; a swing strategy that lists SELL gives `swing_sell_unsupported` at that `sides[i]`, whose message cites doc 07 §7 (swing SELL is futures only; there are no futures in P3). `side_rules_missing` and `sell_block_missing` are reported **only by the compile of that side**: `Compile(d, E, Buy)` of `[BUY]` with `Buy` null gives `side_rules_missing` at `buy`; `Compile(d, E, Sell)` of `[BUY, SELL]` with `Sell` null gives `sell_block_missing` at `sell`, whose message names P3-02e and says to write the sell block, and `Compile(d, E, Buy)` of the same definition succeeds (doc 11's example declares `sides: [BUY, SELL]` with no `sell:` block).
6. **Time rules.** The table in *Contract* is pinned row by row, in minutes of the India day: `first_entry` 09:30 = 570, `last_entry` 11:30 = 690, `last_entry` 14:30 = 870, `square_off` 15:15 = 915. 09:15 and 15:30 are accepted (the range of `bad_time_literal`); 09:14, 15:31 and any time with seconds give `bad_time`. The order is `first_entry ≤ last_entry < square_off`: 11:30 then 09:30 gives `time_rules_out_of_order` at `time_rules.last_entry`; `last_entry` equal to `square_off` gives it at `time_rules.square_off`.
7. **Filters.** `[]` compiles. `not_event_day` and `min_turnover` each give the warning `filter_not_evaluated` at `filters[i]` (the message says what data is missing: the results calendar, P15; the liquidity data of the universe layer, doc 10 §2); `not_event_dy` gives `unknown_filter` at `filters[i]` with the suggestion `not_event_day`, and `zzz` gives it without a suggestion (the rule of `Suggestions.Closest`: edit distance at most 2, reused, not copied); a repeated name gives `duplicate_filter`.

**The sections**
8. **Lists.** A null `Trigger` gives `trigger_missing` at `buy.trigger`; a present list with no items gives `list_empty`; 101 items give `list_too_long`; a null `Setup` or `Invalidate` is no gate and no invalidation. **100 items of `rsi(14) > 55` in an `all` list compile**, while the same 100 conditions joined by `and` into one text give `strategy.syntax.too_deep` (the test shows both, so the lifted limit is proven and not assumed); a list mode that is not All or Any gives `undefined_value`.
9. **Each item is compiled alone.** Given `buy.trigger` = [`crosses_above(close, vwap)`, `rsi(14) > 55 and closee > 1`], when compiled, then exactly one error is returned: `strategy.lint.unknown_name` with suggestion `close`, path `buy.trigger[1]`, span Start 17, End 23, Line 1, Column 18 (relative to the item). Given [`close > 1`, `rsi(14)`], the `type_mismatch` at `buy.trigger[1]` has a message that does not contain "`and`" (a joined text would have said "the right side of `and`"). Three faults in `buy.setup[2]`, `buy.trigger[0]` and `buy.stop` are reported together, in that order. The problems of an item (warnings included) equal those of `ConditionCompiler.Compile` of the same text with the same context, with the path added, for 500 seeded valid and invalid items.
10. **Entry and stop.** A null `Entry` gives `entry_missing`, a null `Stop` `stop_missing`, a null or empty `Exits` `exits_empty`. For every stop kind in the *Contract* table the valid example compiles and every failing row gives its code at its path: a missing required field (`lookback` of `swing_low`) `field_missing` at `buy.stop.lookback`; a field the kind does not use (`pct` on `atr_mult`, `timeframe` on `fixed_pct`) `field_not_used`; `swing_high`, `bar_high` on BUY and `swing_low`, `bar_low` on SELL `stop_wrong_side` at `buy.stop` / `sell.stop`; `pct` 0 and 51 `value_out_of_range` at `buy.stop.pct`; a feature the catalog refuses (`lookback` 0, `lookback` 2.5, `or_opposite` with `Timeframe` 1D, `minutes` not whole bars of the stop timeframe) `feature_invalid` at the field, with the catalog's own message. A stop with `Timeframe` 1D at E = 15m reads the last closed day, today excluded, as `tf("1D").lowest(n)` does.
11. **Exits and overlays.** For `FIXED_RR` and `PARTIAL_TRAIL` (the two tables in *Contract*) the valid example compiles and each parameter has a failing row: missing `field_missing` at `buy.exits[i].<name>`, unknown name `field_not_used`, a list where a scalar is wanted (two values for `book_pct`), the reverse (no value, or 11 values, for `rr`) or a name given twice `field_shape`, out of range `value_out_of_range` (at `buy.exits[0].rr[1]` for a value of a list), a whole-number field that is not whole (`atr_period` 14.5) `feature_invalid` (the catalog's), a candidate policy twice `duplicate_exit` at `buy.exits[1]`. The other five candidates (`PARTIAL_BE`, `ATR_TRAIL`, `STRUCTURE_TRAIL`, `EMA_TRAIL`, `PYRAMID`) and the overlays (`time_stop`, `event_exit`) compile when they pass the **shape check only**: every parameter name `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*` of 1 to 32 characters and given once, one to ten values each, each value a finite number or a declared param (`field_shape`, `bad_quantity`, `unknown_param`); no name, range or relation is checked and they read no feature (provisional until P3-02b2); a candidate twice is `duplicate_exit`, an overlay twice `duplicate_exit` at `buy.overlays[1]`. `SESSION_EXIT`, `MAX_HOLD`, `TIME_STOP` and `EVENT_EXIT` are not members of `ExitPolicy` (the first two come from the time rules, always on: doc 08 §2; the last two are overlays: open question 5).
12. **Quantity.** A `Quantity` is a literal or a param name. `Quantity.Named("swing_lookbak")` with `swing_lookback` declared gives `unknown_param` at the field (`buy.stop.lookback`) with the suggestion `swing_lookback`; `Quantity.Named("zzz")` gives the error without a suggestion (the rule of `Suggestions.Closest`, edit distance at most 2, reused, not copied); a quantity with neither or both set, or a non-finite literal, gives `bad_quantity`; a named param is resolved to its number before the ranges are checked, so `swing_lookback` = 0 gives the same `feature_invalid` as a literal 0.

**The compiled side**
13. **The example of doc 11.** The BUY side of VWAP_RSI_TREND (15m, intraday, `Sides` = [Buy, Sell] as in doc 11 with no sell block, so the BUY compile is the one that succeeds; params `rsi_len` 14, `rsi_min` 55, `swing_lookback` 5; setup `all` [`time >= "09:45"`, `tf("1h").ema(20) > tf("1h").ema(50)`, `rsi(rsi_len) > rsi_min`]; trigger `all` [`crosses_above(close, vwap)`]; entry `at_close`; stop `swing_low` lookback `swing_lookback`, `buffer_atr` 0.1; exits FIXED_RR `rr` [2.0] and PARTIAL_TRAIL `book_pct` 50, `t1_rr` 1.0, `trail_atr_mult` 2.0; time rules `last_entry` 14:30, `square_off` 15:15; filters `not_event_day`, `min_turnover`) compiles with no error. `Features` is exactly [`tf("1h").ema(20)`, `tf("1h").ema(50)`, `rsi(14)`, `close`, `vwap`, `lowest(5, source=low)`, `atr(14)`] (7, conditions in list order, then the stop's level and ATR, then the exits'), `HistoryDepth` 2, `NeedsContext` and `NeedsExpiryDays` false, the item paths `buy.setup[0..2]`, `buy.trigger[0]`, the stop plan (level `lowest(5, source=low)` on 15m, ATR `atr(14)`, buffer 0.1), the exit plans (FIXED_RR `rr` [2.0] reading nothing; PARTIAL_TRAIL reading `atr(14)`, `atr_period` 14 by default), no overlay, the session rules (first entry none, last entry 870, square-off 915, max hold none) and two warnings (`filter_not_evaluated` at `filters[0]` and `filters[1]`).
14. **A bare ORB** (params `or_min` 15; 15m and 5m; trigger BUY `close > or_high(or_min) and close[1] <= or_high(or_min)`, SELL `close < or_low(or_min) and close[1] >= or_low(or_min)`; stop `or_opposite` with `minutes` = `Quantity.Named("or_min")`; FIXED_RR `rr` [2.0]; time rules 09:30 to 11:30, square-off 15:15). At E = 15m and at E = 5m, `Features` for BUY is [`close`, `or_high(15)`, `or_low(15)`] and for SELL [`close`, `or_low(15)`, `or_high(15)`], `HistoryDepth` 2, no warning. With 10m and 30m added to the timeframes, E = 10m and E = 30m (15 minutes is not a whole number of bars) give the language's `bad_arguments` problems at `buy.trigger[0]` (equal to the standalone compile of that text) and `feature_invalid` at `buy.stop.minutes`: the cell is "not applicable", reported, not thrown. The sell block compiles without the buy block (BUY with five faults does not change the SELL result) and the reverse: a fault in the rules of one side never changes the result for the other.
15. **The sentinel.** A side that reads only `tf("1h")` features at E = 15m (setup [`tf("1h").ema(20) > tf("1h").ema(50)`], trigger [`tf("1h").close > tf("1h").ema(20)`], stop `fixed_pct` 1.0) has `Features` = [`tf("1h").ema(20)`, `tf("1h").ema(50)`, `tf("1h").close`, `close` on 15m], the last one being the sentinel; a frame made from it has `HasTimeframe(15m)`, and `BarClosed(15m)` is false at the step of the 09:28 minute and true at the 09:29 minute. A side whose only condition is `time >= "09:45"` has `Features` = [`close` on 15m] and `HistoryDepth` 1 (`FeatureFrame.Create` refuses an empty list, so this is the case the sentinel exists for). A side that already reads a feature on E has no sentinel and no duplicate `close`.
16. **One feature list.** A stop `swing_low` with lookback 5 and the triggers `close > lowest(5)` and `close > lowest(5, source=low)` give exactly one `lowest(5, source=low)` in `Features` (the specs the stop and the exits build are the binder's canonical specs). Property: for every stop kind, `FIXED_RR` and `PARTIAL_TRAIL`, on each of the six entry timeframes and both sides, whenever `Compile` succeeds, `FeatureFrame.Create(side.Features, …)` succeeds (with the context and expiry dates when the side says so), `HandleOf(Features[i]) == i` for every `i`, and the list has no two specs the frame would treat as one.
17. **Needs.** A trigger `regime() == "UP_LOW_VOL"` gives `NeedsContext` true and the information `strategy.lint.needs_context` at `buy.trigger[0]` in `Problems` (not in `Warnings`); `is_expiry_day == 1` gives `NeedsExpiryDays`; a frame made from the features without the context or the expiry dates is refused by `FeatureFrame.Create` as before.
18. **Layout.** `Bind(frame)` succeeds for a frame created from `Features` with `FrameOptions.HistoryDepth` = `HistoryDepth`, and for a frame whose list has more features after them; it throws `ArgumentException` that names the feature when the frame has the features in another order (`HandleOf(Features[i]) != i`), when one is missing, and the depth when the frame keeps less history; a null frame throws `ArgumentNullException`. (A `BoundCondition` alone does not check the layout, ADR 0034 Consequences; the side does.)

**Evaluation**
19. **The fold.** The internal fold of item truths is pinned exhaustively for 1 to 4 items (3 + 9 + 27 + 81 assignments) against the Kleene tables for `all` and for `any` (the model forbids empty lists, so the fold of none is internal and pinned too: `all` true, `any` false). A mutant that folds `all` as `any` fails it.
20. **List-level equals the joined text.** For 200 seeds, 2 to 6 conditions drawn from the generator of the interpreter oracle, at every closed bar of E of a seeded series of several sessions, the truth and the decision of an `all` list equal those of the one condition `(c1) and (c2) and …` compiled by `ConditionCompiler` (and of an `any` list those of `(c1) or (c2) or …`), unknown included; the truth of every item equals the item compiled alone; the number of unknown results compared is asserted above zero.
21. **The decision.** For setup in {absent, True, False, Unknown} × trigger in {True, False, Unknown} × invalidate in {absent, True, False, Unknown} (48 cases, items `close > 0`, `close < 0` and `rsi(14) > 0` on a fresh frame whose RSI is not ready): `Fires` is true exactly when setup is absent or True, trigger is True, and invalidate is absent or False; `Invalidated` is true exactly when invalidate is True; an unknown invalidation does not fire and is not invalidated. `Fires` is the one definition of the entry condition (hand-off (a) in *Out*). The latch ("once true, no entry that day") is the simulator's.
22. **The first evaluations of a session** (ADR 0035). Fixture (new, not that of `SessionBoundaryExamplesTests`, which runs on 1m and has a Tuesday bar that is not flat): E = 5m, each 5-minute bar built from five 1-minute bars held flat (O = H = L = C), volume 10 per minute; closes Monday 100, 98, 96 and Tuesday 122, 121, 119, 130. The vwap of the closed bars of the session is 100, 99, 98 | 122, 121.5, 120.667, 123 (the running mean of the closes, equal volumes). Trigger [`crosses_above(close, vwap)`]: the item truth is U F F | U F F T (unknown whenever one of the four values is not ready; on the fourth Tuesday bar 130 > 123 and 119 ≤ 120.667), so on the first closed bar of Tuesday the list is Unknown (not False) and `Fires` is false; on the fourth it is True and `Fires` is true (the window is a separate question, `InEntryWindow`, criterion 23). With an `any` list [`crosses_above(close, vwap)`, `close > 100`] the first Tuesday bar is True. With [`not crosses_below(close, vwap)`] it is Unknown. No guard is needed in the model.
23. **The window.** E = 5m, time rules `first_entry` 09:30, `last_entry` 11:30: `InEntryWindow` is false for the bar that closes at 09:25, true at 09:30, true at 11:30, false at 11:35, and false before the first step; with `first_entry` absent it is true at 09:20 and with `last_entry` absent true at 15:10; both absent, always true after the first step. The answer is the inclusive `first ≤ time ≤ last` with `time` as in ADR 0034 decision 4 (the start of the last accepted 1-minute bar plus one minute).
24. **A late bar** (the clock of the step, not of the bar). E = 5m, time rules `first_entry` 09:30, `last_entry` 11:30, `square_off` 15:15; the 1-minute bars of 09:15 to 09:28 and then 09:30, the 09:29 minute missing: the 09:25 bucket closes at the step of the 09:30 minute, `time` at that step is 09:31 and `InEntryWindow` is true; with `last_entry` 09:30 it is false at that step although the bar would have closed at 09:30. With `first_entry` and `last_entry` both absent, `InEntryWindow` is true at the first step of the next date (the 09:15 minute, `time` 09:16) for a 15:25 bucket of the date before that this minute closed late, because `BoundSide` cannot tell it from a bar of the day: the scanner guards it (P3-03 D1), the library may not read `TryLastClosed` or `ClosedBarsAt`.
25. **Zero allocation.** `BoundSide.Evaluate` and `InEntryWindow` allocate 0 bytes in steady state over 100,000 steps of a 20-feature frame with a side of ten items, a date change and a higher-timeframe close included (the `AllocationProbe` of the P3-02 tests).
26. **No look-ahead through the side.** The truncation and future-perturbation property of P3-02 criterion 10 is run through `BoundSide.Evaluate` and `InEntryWindow` for 60 seeds (random sides of 2 to 3 items per list, on 5m, 15m, 1h and 1D, with random time rules on the intraday ones): the three truths, `Fires` and the window at every closed bar of E are identical when all later bars are removed and when they are replaced by arbitrary values, and `NextBarFeeder`, the negative control, is reported as a violation. This guards the feed through the new layer; the library opens no new door to the frame (criterion 30) and the exact meaning stays with the oracle and the hand examples (ADR 0034, Amendment 14). Look-ahead through the caller is P3-03's property.

**Problems as data**
27. **Order, cap, never an exception.** Problems come in the order of the model (schema, id, style, timeframes, sides, params, then for the side that is compiled setup, trigger, invalidate, entry, stop, exits, overlays, then time rules, filters), and in source order inside an item; errors come first, warnings and information after them, each group in that order. The cap counts errors: a side whose `buy.setup` has 100 items `closee > 1` and whose `buy.trigger` has one faulty item as well (101 errors in all) gives the first 100 and then one `strategy.model.too_many_problems` with severity Error (101 entries, path empty). Warnings and information are listed after the errors, at most 100 in all: a valid side with 100 setup items and one trigger item, all `tf("15m").close > 1` at E = 15m (a `redundant_timeframe` warning each), compiles, lists 100 warnings and one `too_many_problems` with severity Information; the compile fails on errors only. 1,000 seeded mutations of the example definitions (null fields, empty and 5,000-character item texts, enum casts, NaN, lists of 101 items, a null item) never throw, every problem has a code that starts with `strategy.`, a path and (for the language's problems) a span, and two compiles of one definition give equal problem lists.
28. **Independence and immutability.** `Compile` does not change the definition (compared element by element before and after), keeps no reference to the caller's lists, and two compiles for two timeframes share nothing mutable; 16 threads that bind one `CompiledSide` to 16 frames at once give the same decisions as one thread.
29. **The compiler warm-up covers the strategy compiler.** `CompilerWarmup.Run()` compiles valid intraday and swing definitions on both sides (every entry kind, exit policy, overlay and stop kind that compiles appears in a valid one) and invalid ones (a stop on the wrong side, a model fault, a fault in an item with its path, the problem cap), through `StrategyCompiler.Compile` and `Bind`; the coverage tests of the warm-up fail for a stop kind, entry kind, exit policy or group of `strategy.model.*` codes the sets do not reach. With these definitions the compiler warm-up stays within `WarmUpMilliseconds` (150 ms, explicit benchmark) and the first strategy compile after it within 20 ms; the measured figures go to ADR 0036; `CompilerWarmup.Outcome` gains the counts of strategy definitions compiled and refused (the pinned counts of `CompilerWarmupTests` change with it).

**Structure**
30. **Architecture.** `StrategiesArchitectureTests` stay green with one change: `XDrishti.Strategies.Model` joins the documented public namespaces. The seven `FeatureFrame` members the library may reference are unchanged (`BoundSide` reads the clock through `Steps` and `LastBarTime` only, the layout check uses `HandleOf` and `HistoryDepth`); there is no new project, package or Dockerfile line; `Side` is the only public type added to `XDrishti.Domain`; no `src` project references `XDrishti.Strategies`, so the compiler warm-up rule stays vacuous (it fails the first project that does until P3-03's `StrategyWarmupService`); every new class is sealed; the model has no `Random`, clock, I/O or mutable static.

## Contract
No endpoint, table, screen or job: a pure library (API: none; Data: none, no migration; UI: none; Events/jobs: none). The shapes below are the **public surface as a sketch**: names are the contract, member details follow the build and are recorded in ADR 0036. The model has one public type per file.

**The model** (`XDrishti.Strategies.Model`; stored names are the doc 11 spellings, written by P3-02c)

**Names.** An id is 1 to 64 characters, `[A-Za-z][A-Za-z0-9_]*` (`ORB`, `VWAP_RSI_TREND`); a param name is 1 to 32 characters, `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*` (so it can never be one of the upper-case constants `MON … SUN`), and its value is a finite number. A section that is null in the model is "absent as written"; the compile decides whether that is allowed. `Trigger`, `Entry`, `Stop` and `Exits` are required; `Setup`, `Invalidate` and `Overlays` are optional; a present list has at least one item. `Invalidate` is the `invalidation` row of doc 07 §1 ("cancel if…"): it covers "gap beyond trigger" (for example a condition on `today.open` against a level); "stop level hit before trigger" cannot be written as a condition and is not covered. A stored strategy is **self-contained**: nothing is read from configuration, and the numbers of the `styles` block of doc 16 are not defaults for a missing time rule (the same hash must mean the same behaviour: NFR-3 of doc 02); the doc 16 names `first_entry_time`, `last_entry_time`, `square_off_time` and `max_holding_sessions` are the config spelling of the same four time rules.

**The compile** (`XDrishti.Strategies.Compilation`)

- **What it builds on (verified in the code):** `ConditionCompiler.Compile(string, CompileContext)` → `CompileResult(Condition, Problems)`; `CompiledCondition` (`Source`, `Features`, `HistoryDepth`, `NeedsContext`, `NeedsExpiryDays`, `Warnings`, `Bind(frame)`); `BoundCondition.EvaluateTruth`; `Truth`; `ConditionSet.Merge` → `CompiledPlan`; `CompileContext(Timeframe, IReadOnlyDictionary<string, double>)`; `Problem(Code, Message, Span, Suggestion, Severity)` and `SourceSpan`; `FeatureSpec`, `IndicatorCatalog.TryGet`, `IndicatorInfo.DefaultSpec`, `FrameOptions`; `BindingScope.CatalogAnswer` (to be extracted) and `Suggestions.Closest`; the seven frame members of the architecture test.
- **Every item is compiled alone** with `new CompileContext(E, parameters)` (the declared params, ordinal names), so a problem's code, message, suggestion and span are the language's. `ConditionSet.Merge` unites the conditions' features in list order (setup, trigger, invalidate; each in its own order). The stop's features follow, then the exits' (in `Exits` order), then the sentinel `close` on E if nothing is on E; duplicates are removed by equality of the canonical spec. A spec the stop or an exit builds is checked by the catalog the way the binder checks a feature (the shared `CatalogCheck`, a one-spec `FeatureFrame.Create`), so the catalog is the one rule for periods, whole numbers, whole bars of an opening range and timeframes, and its messages come back as `feature_invalid`.
- **The decision of a bar** is `SideEvaluation`: `Setup` is True when the side has no setup list; `Invalidate` is False when it has no invalidate list; `Fires` = `Setup == True && Trigger == True && Invalidate == False` (Kleene `setup and trigger and not invalidate`, a decision that treats unknown as false, as ADR 0034 decision 3) and is the one definition of the entry condition; `Invalidated` = `Invalidate == True`. Every item is evaluated at every call (no skipping across items), so the simulator can count true, false and unknown per path; the cost of a side is the sum of its items' (each bounded by its text, ADR 0034 Amendment 18; `rising`/`falling(x, n)` cost n reads).
- **When to evaluate** is the caller's: only at the steps where `frame.BarClosed(E)` (the sentinel guarantees the frame has a lane for E). `BoundSide` is a pure function of the frame as it is now; `InEntryWindow` answers for the clock of the step (criterion 24).

**The stop kinds** (doc 11 §2 Stops row; `S` is the stop timeframe, E when `Timeframe` is null; the formulas of the stop price are P3-03's; numbers are sanity bounds of schema 1)
| Kind | Side | Required | Optional | Level / ATR feature read on S |
|------|------|----------|----------|-------------------------------|
| `fixed_pct` | both | `pct` in (0, 50] | none | none |
| `atr_mult` | both | `mult` in (0, 20] | `atr_period` (14) | ATR `atr(atr_period)` |
| `swing_low` | BUY | `lookback` | `buffer_atr` in [0, 5], `atr_period` | level `lowest(lookback, source=low)`; ATR when `buffer_atr` is above 0 |
| `swing_high` | SELL | `lookback` | as `swing_low` | level `highest(lookback, source=high)` |
| `bar_low` | BUY | none | `buffer_atr`, `atr_period` | level `low` |
| `bar_high` | SELL | none | as `bar_low` | level `high` |

`swing_low` and `swing_high` are the lowest low and the highest high of the last `lookback` closed bars of S, the trigger bar included (open question 4). `lookback`, `atr_period` and `minutes` are whole numbers in the range the catalog gives the feature (a value that is not whole is the catalog's `feature_invalid`). **`Timeframe` on the stop is optional (default E).** No built-in of P3 uses another timeframe (`BASE_BREAKOUT` is evaluated on 1D, so its stop is read on E); it exists so that a 15m strategy can stop on the last closed day (`tf("1D")` rule, today excluded), and it is the first thing cut if the slice overruns. `Timeframe` on `fixed_pct` is `field_not_used`. ## Non-functional
- **Performance (targets, measured in the build by explicit benchmarks that `scripts/check.sh` skips, like ISS-12; the ISS-12 text then names these three as unguarded budgets):** a warm `Compile` of the example side of doc 11 (4 items, 7 features) takes at most 3 ms, the model checks and the merge adding under 0.5 ms to the compile of the items; the first compile after the compiler warm-up at most 20 ms; evaluating a side of ten simple items costs at most 0.4 µs per call (explicit benchmark, not in `check.sh`) and allocates 0 bytes (criterion 25, asserted in `check.sh`). All items are evaluated at every call on purpose (the trace), so the worst case is the sum over the items; with the 100-item cap and the 4,000-character limit a pathological side is bounded (3 lists × 100 items × about 0.3 ms at worst per ADR 0034), and a cost lint is reopened only if P3-03 shows evaluation in the profile.
- **Bounds (work is bounded by the input, never by a clock):** at most 100 items per list, 4 params, 10 values per exit list, 100 listed errors and 100 listed warnings and information; each item at most 4,000 characters and 64 levels (the language's limits, per item).
- **Purity and safety:** no I/O, clock, `Random`, mutable static or reflection beyond what P3-02 allows; sealed types; the compile never throws for a bad definition (a null argument or an undefined timeframe enum for `evaluationTimeframe` is a programming error and throws); nothing is executed as text except through P3-02's compiler; param names are identifiers, so they cannot carry text. Untrusted YAML is P3-02c's problem; here the model arrives from C# builders and tests.
- **Determinism and thread safety:** the same definition gives the same sides and the same problem lists in the same order; a `CompiledSide` is immutable and shared by any number of frames; a `BoundSide` belongs to its frame's thread, like a `BoundCondition`.
- **Observability:** none (library). The host logs the located problems it receives.

## Open questions
To answer by number, as for P3-02. Questions 1 to 3 and 5 to 6 are the model's design; question 4 is trading judgement because the stop sets the size of R in every result. Decided in the spec as engineering consequences and recorded in ADR 0036, not asked: stored strategies are self-contained, with no config defaults for a missing time rule (NFR-3: the same hash must mean the same behaviour); the optional `invalidate` list (it is the `invalidation` row of doc 07 §1, already approved there, and P3-03 F19 exercises it).
1. **Setup is evaluated together with the trigger.** At every closed bar of the evaluation timeframe all sections are evaluated and ANDed, `setup` being the gate; doc 07 §1 (setup "evaluated tonight", trigger "evaluated tomorrow") and doc 11 §2 are reworded to say so. The alternative is two moments (a nightly setup, an intraday trigger), which the doc 11 example contradicts (it puts `time >= "09:45"` and `rsi(…)` in `setup`) and which the simulator would need two evaluation loops for. **Recommendation:** yes. The nightly shortlist of P7 can still classify which setup items are knowable at the previous close.
2. **`time_rules` is the one place for windows** (`first_entry`, `last_entry`, `square_off`, `max_hold_sessions`); there is no separate `valid_window` section (doc 07 §1 lists one), and the windows are inclusive at both ends (09:30 and 11:30 admit the bars that close at 09:30 and at 11:30). `square_off` (intraday) or `max_hold_sessions` (swing) is required because SESSION_EXIT and MAX_HOLD are always on (doc 08 §2). **Recommendation:** yes.
3. **Filters are names that are accepted and not evaluated.** `not_event_day` and `min_turnover` (doc 11) compile with a warning and the simulator refuses a run that uses one, because there is no results calendar (P15) and no liquidity data (the universe layer, doc 10 §2); reports say that event days are not excluded. **Recommendation:** yes. The alternative, a compile error, would make the example of doc 11 unusable.
4. **`swing_low` and `swing_high` are the lowest low and highest high of the last N bars** of the stop timeframe (trigger bar included), not the last confirmed pivot (`pivot_low(n)`, which exists in the catalog and is known only n bars late and may not exist yet). Doc 11 says "stop below last swing low"; a pivot is the stricter reading, an N-bar extreme is always ready after N bars, is easy to check on a chart and is what BASE_BREAKOUT needs on 1D (the base low). A pivot-based stop can be added later as a kind of its own (`pivot_low`), which breaks nothing stored. **Recommendation:** the N-bar extreme.
5. **`TIME_STOP` and `EVENT_EXIT` are overlays, not candidates.** `Exits` lists the candidate policies (`FIXED_RR`, `PARTIAL_BE`, `PARTIAL_TRAIL`, `ATR_TRAIL`, `STRUCTURE_TRAIL`, `EMA_TRAIL`, `PYRAMID`); `time_stop` and `event_exit` are optional overlays on every candidate (doc 16 `exits.overlays`, doc 08 §5 lists the time stop among the time rules), stored as `SideRules.Overlays`, with the doc 16 names `min_progress_rr`, `intraday_bars`, `swing_sessions` and `days_before_results`. Doc 08 §3 chooses among candidates by replaying the same entries, and a time stop alone has no stop or target to replay. **Recommendation:** yes. The alternative (both as members of `ExitPolicy`) freezes a `TIME_STOP` that cannot be run alone; if the answer is no, `ExitPolicy` gains `TimeStop` and `EventExit` and `Overlays` is dropped from the model.
6. **A swing strategy lists 30m, 1h and 1D only; an intraday strategy does not list 1D.** Doc 07 §3 gives swing as 1h and 1D and §4 gives 30m as "intraday / short swing", 1h as "intraday / swing" and 1D as "swing"; the compile refuses the rest as `style_timeframe_mismatch`. The rule is this spec's proposal, written down in ADR 0036. **Recommendation:** yes. The alternative is no check, so a swing strategy on 5m compiles and the simulator decides.
