## Goal
A strategy can be replayed over the stored bars of one instrument with a result that does not depend on the window that was asked for, and the replay says what it did: which sessions ran and which were left out, which days are price discontinuities, what each rule saw, and enough about the data (a hash of the bars read) and the strategy (its text and hash, its parameters, the engine version) for the run to be checked again. The data is never repaired: special sessions, short sessions and unadjusted price breaks are skipped or flagged, and counted. This slice is pure: it takes bars, adjustment factors and a calendar as values and returns values; reading them, choosing the instruments and the skip list are P3-04a.

2. **The strategy as the replay takes it.** `StrategyPreparation.Prepare(definition, parameters, evaluationTimeframe)` calls `Simulator.Create` (it refuses what P3-03a refuses; the located problems pass through unchanged) and builds the `StrategyRecord`: the id, the parameters the caller built the definition with (kept as given, in ordinal name order; the replay does not interpret them), the **canonical JSON** of the definition (interim, until P3-02c defines the canonical text) and its SHA-256, the timeframe, the sides. The writer is this slice's and hand-written over a `StringBuilder`, not `System.Text.Json`: the bytes are ours and not a framework version's, and the engine may not use `System.IO`. Member names are the snake_case of the model's property names; `Side` and exit policies are written as doc 11 spells them (`BUY`, `SELL`, `FIXED_RR`, `PARTIAL_TRAIL`), entry, stop, overlay and style kinds and list modes in snake_case (`at_close`, `swing_low`, `time_stop`, `intraday`, `all`), timeframes by their code (`15m`), so the YAML reader of P3-02c and this text agree; a `Quantity` is a JSON number or the param name as a string; times `HH:mm`; numbers `double.ToString("R", CultureInfo.InvariantCulture)`; strings UTF-8 without BOM with only `"`, `\` and control characters escaped (`<`, `>`, `&` and `'` stay as written); no white space; absent members left out; lists in model order. Each condition is parsed with `ConditionParser.Parse` and printed with `ExpressionPrinter.Print`, so white space and redundant brackets in a condition do not change the hash. Two spellings of one feature (`rsi()` and `rsi(14)`) give two hashes, because the text is the version (ADR 0002); a hash that is equal for equal meaning is P3-02c's decision (P3-02b *Out*).
3. **The replay starts at the instrument's first stored session, whatever `From` is; only the trades are cut to the window.** `InstrumentReplayer.Replay` is given the raw bars from the first stored bar to the end of `To` (a bar dated after `To` is a caller error and throws `ArgumentException`). It scans and counts from the first session that runs to `To`, and only the **trades** are cut to `[From, To]`: a trade whose date is before `From` is dropped and counted (`TradesBeforeWindow`). So a trade does not depend on the window (an `ema(10)` is seeded by the first stored bar, not by the first bar of the window); a different `From` only adds or removes the trades outside it, and a different `To` only adds or removes the sessions after it. What can still change a trade: more history before the first stored bar (a backfill), which also changes the hash of the data and so the run key. The replay checks that the epochs are strictly ascending before it feeds anything (one pass) and throws `InvalidOperationException` naming the instrument if not; the store cannot produce it (`market.bars_1m` has one row per instrument and minute and `ReadAsync` orders by `ts`). It does not rely on `Simulator.Run`, which makes the same check first (P3-03a: `simulation.bars_not_ascending` before any step).
4. **Sessions (data notes, part one).** Each date with bars is classified with `TradingCalendar.SessionOf(date)` and the bars inside its window `[FromMinute, ToMinute)`: no session on the date is `closed_day`; a session that is not `IsRegular` (window 09:15–15:30) is `special_session`; a regular session whose window holds fewer than `MinSessionCompleteness` (default 0.90) of `SessionClock.RegularBars` (375) minutes, compared in `decimal` so that exactly the threshold runs, is `short_session`; otherwise it runs. A session that does not run is **excluded**: all its bars are excluded, none is fed to the frame, and it is counted with its reason in a note (date, reason, bars in the window, expected minutes). A trading day of the calendar between the instrument's first and last bar with no bar at all is counted as `missing_session`, with a note; nothing is fed (the owner's files have none: the scan found no instrument-day missing inside its own range, and 31 weekdays with no bar at all, the holidays). An excluded or missing session is treated as a day the market was closed: `prev_day_*`, `gap_pct` and daily averages read the previous session that ran, and the discontinuity check compares with it. Bars outside the window of a session that runs (an evening bar on a regular date, a bar at 15:30) are dropped and counted (`BarsOutsideSession`); `BarsExcluded` is the bars of the excluded sessions plus these, so bars read = bars fed + bars excluded. *Excluded* is on purpose and counted; *ignored* is the ordering guard of the frame (`IgnoredBars`, 0 on stored data): two separate counters (ISS-20). A frame is always fed whole sessions (ADR 0035). The window test is the one `CompletenessChecker` makes (minute of the day in `[FromMinute, ToMinute)`, expected minutes `TradingSession.Minutes`, 375 as `SessionClock.RegularBars`); the checker itself is not reused, because it holds the first day of an instrument against no one and reports a percent missing and not a fraction present. **Decided on research, not asked** (the scan of 2026-10-18; the default of `ReplayOptions`, recorded in every run; the owner may revert): the threshold 0.90. In the scan (109,704 instrument-dates with bars, 40,946,344 rows) 108,481 dates have all 375 bars, 588 have 338 to 374 (they run; their missing minutes show as incomplete higher-timeframe bars), 635 have fewer than 188 bars and lie on three dates (2024-05-18, 2024-11-01, 2025-10-21), and none has between 188 and 337; so any threshold from 50 % to 90 % gives the same runs on today's data, and 100 % would also skip the 588 (0.54 %). The rule counts the whole session, which is hindsight about the data and not about prices, and it matters for data that Dhan fetches later. **The calendar decides the weekend:** 427 of the complete dates are a Saturday (2025-02-01, 214 files) and a Sunday (2026-02-01, 213 files; a 214th has fewer bars) with 375 bars. A calendar without a confirmed special session for them excludes them as `closed_day`; one with a confirmed special session of the regular window runs them like any session. The record carries the calendar's exceptions, so the run says which.
5. **Prices.** Bars are raw in the database; the replay adjusts a copy: each bar by `AdjustmentFactors.FactorAt(its India date)` (prices `AdjustmentFactors.Price`, volume `AdjustmentFactors.Volume`, 4 decimals). An instrument with no applied action that changes the bars read (`ActionsAfter(date of the first bar)` empty) is not copied. The replay cannot tell which bars came from a CSV (`IBarStore.ReadAsync` returns no source per bar), so the guard against adjusting a CSV-adjusted series twice (doc 05 §4.2, ADR 0017 §5) is the runner's (P3-04a). The replay only marks each recorded adjustment `OnCsvBars` when `CsvAdjustedThrough` is given and the ex-date is on or before it, the comparison of `CsvAdjustment.AdjustsTwice`.
6. **Discontinuities and segments (data notes, part two).** On the adjusted series of the sessions that run, a day D whose first open differs from the last close of the previous session that ran by more than `DiscontinuityThreshold` (default 0.20) is **flagged** with its ratio `open / close` (informational). The test is `abs(open - previousClose) > threshold × previousClose` computed in `decimal` on the adjusted prices (the `decimal` cast keeps a price written with at most four decimals as written, and the adjustment rounds to four); a move of exactly the threshold is not flagged. The `double` form `abs(open / close - 1) > 0.2` is not used: it flags a close of 12.00 with an open of 9.60 and a close of 1.50 with an open of 1.20 (both exactly −20 %, because `1.2 / 1.5 - 1` is 0.20000000000000007), and, over the closes that are multiples of 0.25 from 10.00 to 2,500.00, 5,430 of the 19,922 moves of exactly ±20 % that land on the 0.05 grid (counted in the review). The first session that runs is never flagged. A flagged day (a) gets **no entry**: the outcome of a candidate dated D, a trade or a no-trade, is dropped and counted as `EntriesSuppressed` (the scanner's counters still include it); (b) **starts a new segment** (subject to open question 3): `Simulator.Run` is called once per segment, each with fresh frames, so the old price level cannot sit in an average or an ATR for weeks, and the counters of the segments are summed. The jump is not repaired. An applied action that explains the jump makes the adjusted series continuous, so it is not flagged. **Decided on research, not asked** (the scan of 2026-10-18; the default of `ReplayOptions`, recorded in every run; the owner may revert): the threshold 0.20. Overnight opens more than 10 % from the previous close: 74 days (38 of them on 2025-04-07 alone); more than 15 %: 15; more than 20 %: 3; more than 25 %: 2. The three beyond 20 % are the breaks that ISS-20 lists, SIEMENS 2025-04-07 (ratio 0.656), TMPV 2025-10-14 (1.229) and VEDL 2026-04-30 (0.413, the last day of the data); whether each is a missing adjustment or a real gap was not checked against the corporate actions. Of the 12 between 15 % and 20 %, 9 fall on two dates (2025-04-07 and 2024-11-21) on which other instruments moved the same way: real market days, the worst ones, which a lower threshold would take out of a long-only test.
7. **The end of the data.** A trade that P3-03's walker closes with `ExitReason.EndOfData` (D4: no later minute on its entry date, for example a session that ends at 15:09) is data and not a rule: it is listed in `OpenAtEnd`, not in `Trades`, so no statistic counts it by accident. The window cut comes first (a trade before `From` is `TradesBeforeWindow` whatever its exit), so `OpenAtEnd` holds the `EndOfData` trades dated in the window. Where a trade may continue after `To` (swing positions) is P3-03d's decision; the read ends at `To`.
8. **Fates, counters and the record** (NFR-3, doc 11 §6). Every candidate outcome of the replay has exactly one fate, decided in this order: dropped by a flagged day (`EntriesSuppressed`); a trade before `From` (`TradesBeforeWindow`); an `EndOfData` trade (`OpenAtEnd`); another trade (`Trades`); a walker no-trade, by reason. The identity *sum of the fates = the scanner's `Candidates` (both sides, all segments)* holds on every fixture. All counters (per condition, `ScanCounters`, `WalkCounters`) cover the replay from the first session that ran to the last (`CountersCover`), while trades are cut to `[From, To]`; so each `InstrumentRun` also carries `CandidatesInWindow` (candidates whose decision date is in `[From, To]`) and `TradesInWindow`, and the totals repeat them, so that a rule that fires only before `From` is visible (ISS-24: a rule that never fires looks safe; here the reverse, a rule that fires only before `From` looks alive). The walker's own counters include the outcomes a flagged day dropped, as the scanner's do. Per instrument the record holds: symbol, skip reason or the range of data (first and last bar), the replay start (first session that ran), bars read, fed and excluded, the **SHA-256 of the stored bars read**, the applied actions that change the bars, the session counts and notes, the flagged days, the counters (P3-03a's `ScanCounters` per side and P3-03's `WalkCounters`, summed over the segments, with `IgnoredBars`, `IncompleteBarsConsumed`, `LostDecisions` and the per-condition true, false, unknown and not-ready counts) and the fates. A skipped instrument that was read keeps its data facts and has no trades. **Skip reasons the replay decides**, in this precedence: `no_bars` (the read was empty); `no_runnable_sessions` (no session ran, or no session gave a window step); `no_sessions_in_window` (sessions ran, none dated in `[From, To]`); `features_not_ready` (window steps above 0, but `FullyReadySteps` is 0 on every side: no window step had all the items of `setup` and `trigger` ready). The runner adds `instrument_not_found` and `csv_adjusted_twice` before them (P3-04a).
9. **The hash of the stored bars** is the SHA-256, lower-case hex, of the concatenation, for each raw bar read in order, of six 8-byte little-endian fields: `EpochSeconds`, the 64-bit patterns of `Open`, `High`, `Low` and `Close`, then `Volume`. It uses `SHA256`/`IncrementalHash` (not among the namespaces that P3-03's architecture rule forbids) and covers the bars as stored, before any session is dropped and before adjustment.
10. **Run assembly and the run key.** `RunAssembler.Assemble(header, instrumentReplays)` returns the `SimulationRun`: the totals (`RunCounters`: the figures above summed over the instruments, the instruments by skip reason, `OpenAtEnd`, `EntriesSuppressed`, `CandidatesInWindow`, `TradesInWindow`); `Trades` and `OpenAtEnd` ordered by entry time, then by the instrument's place in the universe, then Buy before Sell, each wrapped in a `RunTrade` with the basket ids of the universe; the **calendar's exceptions** to the default week (holidays on weekdays; special sessions; any session on a weekend) from the earliest first bar read to `To`, found by asking `SessionOf` for each date, because `TradingCalendar` lists no days (it names the next holiday, not its special sessions); and the **run key**, the lower-case hex SHA-256 of these UTF-8 lines (no BOM), each ending in `\n`:

    | Line | Content |
    |------|---------|
    | `xd-run <SchemaVersion>` | the version of the key follows the record's |
    | `engine=` · `strategy=` | `EngineVersion` · `DefinitionSha256` |
    | `params=` | `<name>=<value>` pairs joined by `,` (effective values, ordinal name order, doubles in `R` form) |
    | `timeframe=` · `window=` | the code (`15m`) · `<From>..<To>` as `yyyy-MM-dd` |
    | `options=` · `ack=` | `<MinSessionCompleteness>,<DiscontinuityThreshold>` in `R` form · `0` or `1` (`AcknowledgeCsvAdjusted`) |
    | `universe=` | the label |
    | `i=` (one per instrument, run order) | `<id as Guid "D">\|<BarsSha256 or ->\|<skip reason or ->\|<ex-date:kind:numerator:denominator,...>` (the applied actions that change the bars; kind is the enum name) |
    | `cal=` | `<yyyy-MM-dd>:holiday` or `<yyyy-MM-dd>:<FromMinute>-<ToMinute>` joined by `,`, in date order |

    The record reads no clock and no random source, so equal inputs give an equal record; the wall-clock facts (when, how long) belong to the caller.

## Acceptance criteria
Numbered, each testable. Fixture series are invented; nothing is read from `data/`. "F1", "F2" and "F6" are the days of P3-03's fixture table (D9: dense 375-bar days, date shifted to the one given); their numbers are P3-03's and move with it. "The ORB draft" is P3-03a's `OrbV1`. Tests call `InstrumentReplayer.Replay` with the default calendar, the default options and `[From, To]` as stated; "the replay for `[a, b]`" is a call whose bars run from the first given day to `b`. **P3-03b:** the absolute numbers of criteria 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8 are P3-03's exact-fill numbers. When P3-03b exists they are produced with `FillRules` of 0.1 % and `SlippageConfig.None` and `R` reads `RExact` (P3-03b criterion 17); until then there are no fill rules. Criterion 8 compares with a direct run of `Simulator.Run` on the same (adjusted) bars, and criteria 12 to 14 compare runs with each other, so they hold with or without P3-03b.

**Window and replay**
1. **The replay starts at the first stored session.** Given one instrument with three days: d1 = 2026-03-02 flat at 99.00; d2 = 2026-03-03 the F1 day (BUY entry 100.50 at 09:45, stop 98.00, target 105.50, `Target` exit 105.50 at 11:10, R +2.0); d3 = 2026-03-04 flat at 105.00; and the test-only strategy "the ORB draft whose BUY side has the extra setup `close > ema(10)`" on 15m. When the replay is made for `[d2, d2]` (bars of d1 and d2) and for `[d1, d3]`, then both hold exactly this one trade, field for field. *Control that the fixture can fail:* a series of d2 and d3 alone holds none, because `ema(10)` on 15m is ready only at the tenth closed bar (11:45), after the last entry (11:30).
2. **Only the trades are cut to the window.** Given the bare ORB draft and the days d1 = the F2 day (stop, R −1.0), d2 = the F1 day, d3 flat at 105.00, when the replays for `[d2, d3]` and `[d1, d3]` are made, then the replay counters (per side, per condition, bars, sessions, notes) are equal field by field; `Trades` of the first is the F1 trade and of the second the F2 and F1 trades; the first has `TradesBeforeWindow` 1; and the replay for `[d1, d1]` holds the F2 trade only.
3. **A trade without a later minute is not a statistic.** Given the F6 day (355 bars, 09:15 to 15:09, 94.7 % of 375, so it runs), when the replay is made, then the trade has exit `EndOfData` at 103.00 at 15:09, R 1.0, it is in `OpenAtEnd`, `Trades` is empty, and the assembled totals have `OpenAtEnd` 1. The same F6 day dated before `From` gives `TradesBeforeWindow` 1 and an empty `OpenAtEnd`.

**Data notes**
4. **Sessions.** Given the cases of the *session table* (Contract), when each is classified, then it runs or is excluded with exactly the reason given; an excluded session is not fed to the frame (for every instrument, bars read = bars fed + bars excluded); each has a note; bars outside the window of a session that runs are dropped and counted (`BarsOutsideSession`); after an excluded or missing session `prev_day_close` and the discontinuity comparison read the previous session that ran.
5. **Discontinuities.** Given the cases of the *discontinuity table* (Contract), when the open of D is compared with the previous close, then the day is flagged with its ratio exactly where the table says, for the default threshold and for 0.10; the first session that runs is never flagged.
6. **A flagged day restarts the replay and gets no entry** (the restart is subject to open question 3). Given d1 flat at 99.00, d2 the F1 day at twice the price (opens at 198.00, ratio 2.0) and d3 flat at 210.00, then (a) with the bare ORB draft: d2 is flagged with ratio 2.0, `EntriesSuppressed` is 1 and there is no trade; the scanner counters equal the sums of those of the separate replays of d1 alone and of d2 with d3 alone, and the trades equal theirs without the trade of d2. (b) with the strategy of criterion 1 (prices of d2 and d3 as in this case): the not-ready count of `buy.setup[0]` is 18 (9 window steps of d1 and 9 of d2, because `ema(10)` restarts at d2), against 9 for the same series with d2 at the F1 prices (ratio 1.0), which shows that the old level is not carried over (counts hand-calculated from P3-03a D1 and its scanner counters).
7. **A split makes the series continuous.** Given Dhan-source bars with a raw close of 100.00 on d1 and a raw open of 50.05 on d2, and an applied 1:2 split with ex-date d2, then the adjusted d1 close is 50.00, the ratio 1.001 and d2 is not flagged; without the action the ratio is 0.5005 and d2 is flagged.

**Prices**
8. **Adjustment, applied once.** Given an applied 1:2 split (factor 0.5) with ex-date 2026-03-03 and a bar of 2026-03-02 with open 100.10, high 100.30, low 99.90, close 100.20 and volume 1,000, then the adjusted bar is 50.05, 50.15, 49.95, 50.10 and 2,000, and a bar of 2026-03-03 is unchanged; with a 1:2 bonus (factor 0.666666666667) a price of 100.00 becomes 66.6667 and a volume of 1,000 becomes 1,500; an instrument whose applied actions all have an ex-date on or before the date of its first bar is not copied (the same list is used). Given the three days of criterion 1, the bare ORB draft and an applied 1:2 split with ex-date 2026-03-05, after the last day, then the replay gives the F1 trade with every price halved (entry 50.25, stop 49.00, exit 52.75) and R +2.0, equal to a direct `Simulator.Run` on the halved bars. Given `CsvAdjustedThrough` 2026-03-04 and two applied actions with ex-dates 2026-03-03 and 2026-03-05, then only the first is marked `OnCsvBars`.

**Hashes and the definition**
9. **The bar-stream hash: known answer, determinism, sensitivity.** Given the bars (1760000040; 100.5, 101.25, 99.75, 100.0; 1234) and (1760000100; 100.0, 100.5, 99.5, 100.25; 987), the hash is `5028e51da21ebd7fb6f911b4fde15bd16587816fcc70376d5b656e5f608a1a24`; the first bar alone gives `d3022bcc4be32bfe31fca53ccb19bda16b655cda6573491f05267cf378fce29b`; with the second close changed to 100.5 it is `c9b1f5f0ba2e05ddb9c92d7b065de60fd1f70cc65c0d06fafcae219c8eff1dfe`; no bars give `e3b0c44298fc1c149afbf4c8996fb92427ae41e4649b934ca495991b7852b855` (computed with an independent tool for this layout, 2026-10-18, and recomputed in the review). Two calls on equal lists give equal hashes; a close changed by one tick (0.05) changes it.
10. **The definition text and its hash.** Given `OrbV1.Create()`, the canonical JSON equals a golden text checked in with the tests (the rules of Scope 2: no white space, snake_case names, doc 11 spellings, absent members left out); two builds give equal text; the record's SHA-256 is that of its UTF-8 bytes; `or_min` 30 gives another text and another hash; the same definition with extra white space and brackets in its conditions gives the same text and hash, and with `rsi()` written once as `rsi(14)` it gives another; a condition text with `>` and `<` is written as it is; the golden text and its hash are identical when the suite runs under `de-DE` and `tr-TR`.

**Skip reasons of the replay**
11. **The replay's skip reasons.** Given the test-only ORB variant whose BUY and SELL sides both have the setup `close > ema(30)`, with `[From, To]` as stated: A has three runnable sessions in the window (75 closed 15m bars, so `ema(30)` is ready from the 10:30 bar of the second) and runs; B has no bars (`no_bars`); C has only the 60-bar evening session (`no_runnable_sessions`); D has runnable sessions only before `From` (`no_sessions_in_window`); E has one runnable session (25 closed 15m bars, fewer than the 30 `ema(30)` needs, so no step has every feature ready: `features_not_ready`); G runs on a variant with `first_entry` 15:05 and `last_entry` 15:10, in which no 15-minute bar closes, so no session gives a window step (`no_runnable_sessions`). A skipped instrument that was read keeps its bars, hash, session counts and notes and has no trades.

**The record**
12. **The run record and the fates.** Given the F1 day with the 10:07 minute missing, then the record holds, for the instrument, the replay start, `CountersCover`, the first and last bar, the bars read, fed and excluded, the hash of criterion 9 for the stored bars, the applied actions, the session counts and notes, the strategy JSON with its SHA-256, the parameters and the engine version. `Bars` and `IgnoredBars` of each side equal those of a frame that the test creates from the same features and steps over the same fed bars (`IgnoredBars` is 0 on stored data: the store holds one row per minute and the replay never feeds a bar twice); `IncompleteBarsConsumed` is above 0 and equal to the frame's; `LostDecisions` and the condition counts are those of the scanner. An excluded bar is never counted as ignored: given one excluded session, `BarsExcluded` equals its bars and `IgnoredBars` is 0. A list that is not strictly ascending (two equal epochs; a descending pair) makes the replay throw `InvalidOperationException` that names the instrument. The identity of the fates (Scope 8) holds on every fixture of criteria 1 to 8 and 14, and `CandidatesInWindow` and `TradesInWindow` equal a count of the lists.
13. **Assembly, determinism, the run key and the schema.** Given two instruments whose trades share an entry time and both sides, the assembled `Trades` and `OpenAtEnd` are in the order of Scope 10, every `RunTrade` carries the basket ids of the universe (none for a list), the totals are the sums of the instruments, and the calendar exceptions are exactly the holidays, special sessions and weekend sessions of a fixture calendar between the earliest first bar and `To`. Two replays of one input give equal `InstrumentReplay`s and two assemblies equal records and the same run key (compared by a canonical text of every field, doubles in `R` form, lists in their defined order: universe order, date order, path order). The key pre-image of a two-instrument fixture equals the text pinned in the test and its SHA-256 equals a known answer computed with an independent tool, as in criterion 9; the key changes when one close changes by one tick, when an option, a parameter, the window, the acknowledgement or the universe label changes, when an applied action is added and when a calendar exception is added within the range. The public property names of every type of the run record are pinned by a snapshot test; changing one needs `SchemaVersion` 2. A golden rendering of the full run record of the criterion 1 fixture is checked in with the tests; its first line is `SimulationEngine.Version`, and a table in the test maps each version to the SHA-256 of its golden file, so a replay rule changed without a new version, or a new version without a new row, fails.
14. **No look-ahead through the replay.** Given 100 seeded series of 10 dates, date 1 a plain F1-shaped day and each later date the F1 shape scaled so that it opens within 5 % of the previous close, with about one date in seven a planted jump of ratio 1.5 or 0.6 (a flagged day, which carries no trade) and about one in seven a 60-bar evening session (excluded, which carries no trade), and a cut at the end of a random session c from the third to the ninth, when the replay for `[first, c]` is compared with the replay for `[first, last]` and with the same replay after the sessions after c are replaced by extreme bars (highs ×10, lows ÷10, zero volume, gaps of ±50 %), then the trades with entry dates up to c, the flagged days up to c and the notes of the sessions up to c are identical in all three. The test asserts that it is not vacuous: over the 100 seeds at least 250 trades, 40 flagged days and 40 excluded sessions fall into the compared prefixes and at least 40 cuts lie after a flagged day (the generator is tuned until these hold). *Control:* the same comparison with the extreme bars placed in session 1 shows a difference in every seed. The session filters use the bars of their own session and the previous close only, so the cut is at a session boundary; this carries the properties of P3-03 (AC8, the walker) and P3-03a (through `Simulator.Run`) to the replay's own reading and filtering. (The replay counters are not compared: they cover the whole replay to `To`.)

**Structure**
15. **Architecture.** P3-03's `SimulationArchitectureTests` (AC10) hold for the new `Replay` namespace (no I/O, clock, `Random`, tasks or `System.Text.Json`; sealed types; the list of documented public namespaces gains `XDrishti.Simulation.Replay`; the root and `Walking` namespaces do not use it); the new Domain types are in `XDrishti.Domain.Research` with no I/O and the Domain rules stay green; no project file changes (no new reference); `LayerDependencyTests` and `LogEventIdTests` stay green; the compiler warm-up rule stays satisfied by P3-03a's `StrategyWarmupService`.
16. **Memory.** Given a synthetic instrument of 190,000 bars (about two years of minutes; 9.1 MB as a list of 48-byte `Bar`s), the replay allocates at most 5 times the size of the raw list (about 45 MB) in all, measured with `GC.GetTotalAllocatedBytes(precise: true)`. The bound is a design bound from the review of this spec and UNVERIFIED against an implementation; the first measurement may move it, and the figure goes to the ADR amendment.

## Non-functional
- **Performance:** no budget is asserted. The explicit benchmark `Replay_phases_for_synthetic_instruments` (invented seeded series, 20 instruments of 2 years, not in `scripts/check.sh`) prints the time of hashing and adjusting and of the replay, the bytes allocated and the peak working set per instrument; the read is P3-04a's. The plan's estimate for the 214 instruments is about 15 s of one core per variant for the replay (the timings of ADR 0034); it and the end-to-end figure are UNVERIFIED and are first measured on the owner's data by P3-04b. Every replay evaluates the whole history before `From`, which is what makes a trade independent of the window; one instrument at a time keeps the memory flat. The share of evaluation in the replay is for P3-03's benchmark to report (the cost lint for long `rising`/`falling` runs, ADR 0034 Amendment 18, waits for it).
- **Memory:** see criterion 16. Peak memory is the lists of one instrument; the raw list, the adjusted copy and the segment lists are several, which is why the bound is a multiple and not 1.
- **Determinism and fail closed (NFR-4, NFR-7):** no clock, `Random` or `Guid.NewGuid` in the result; every list has a defined order; a caller error or an inconsistent input throws, never a partial record; every expected condition is a skip with a reason or a note.
- **First evaluations of a session:** the replay adds no guard for the unknown first evaluations of a session (ADR 0035, decided on research under the owner's delegation (2026-10-16/17); the owner may revert: unknown is not true); they show in the unknown counters. A fresh frame after a flagged day reads the per-session features as if created at that day's first bar, by the same rule (subject to open question 3).
- **Security and privacy:** the strategy is given as a definition, never as text, in this slice; the run key and hashes are not secrets. Tests use invented series only.
- **Observability:** the skip reasons, the session notes and the counters are the user-facing explanation; the library logs nothing.

## Open questions
Only one needs the owner. It keeps the number 3 that the roadmap already uses; questions 1 and 2 of the first draft, the completeness and discontinuity thresholds, are decisions now (Scope 4 and 6: decided on research from the scan of 2026-10-18, defaults of `ReplayOptions` recorded in every run, the owner may revert). The roadmap's plan questions 1 and 2 reach this slice through P3-03.
3. **A flagged day restarts the indicators** (a fresh frame from that day), besides giving that day no entry. The plan only gave the day no entry, which leaves the old price level in every average and every ATR for weeks. The cost: a strategy that reads a daily average such as `tf("1D").ema(50)` takes no entry for about 50 sessions after the day (on today's data that touches SIEMENS and TMPV; VEDL's day is the last).
