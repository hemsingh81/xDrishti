## Goal
Answer, with its n and its honest state, two questions about the counted paper trades of an epoch: is their mean R (after costs) positive and not worse than the backtest allows by chance for **these cells in these proportions**, and is their largest drawdown no worse than the 90th percentile of what the same number of backtest trades would give. Draw the paper line over the backtest fan (R14).

## Scope
**In:** pure `Domain/Paper` `BacktestBand` (stratified bootstrap), `BandOptions`, `BandResult`, `CellReference`, `PaperExitStatistics` (the two criteria as verdicts with labels), `FanPoint`; `Application/Paper` `GetBacktestVsPaperHandler` (reads the cell snapshot in force at the epoch start, its source runs through the existing `CellSourceReader`, and the counted paper trades); endpoint `GET /api/paper/backtest-vs-paper`; verb `xd paper compare`; frontend tab **Backtest vs paper** (lazy chart, table fallback); `scripts/gen-api.sh`.
**Out:** a stored result (recomputed, deterministic), real-fills slippage distribution (P11-03), live line (P11), regime splits, any change of cells or settings, reading the Lab's stored Monte Carlo (see Decision 3).

## Acceptance criteria
1. **Counted set.** The paper side is exactly the counted trades of the current epoch: closed, primary group, `measure_status = measured`, one per ticket (`trade_key`), `r_sim` in exit order (ties by entry instant, then ticket id). Reserve, straddle, open and earlier-epoch trades never enter; the response lists their counts apart.
2. **Reference is point-in-time.** The reference for a cell is its closed backtest trades with exit date **before the epoch's start date**, read from the runs named in the newest stored snapshot whose as-of date is before the start (`ICellStatsStore.ListLatestBeforeAsync`, `Sources`), unweighted. A run window that extends into the paper period is cut at the start date, so no paper day is also a backtest day.
3. **Composition-matched.** For each draw, the i-th paper slot draws one R with replacement from the reference list of the cell of the i-th paper trade. The band is the 5th to 95th percentile of the draw means; the paper position is `below` (mean < P05), `within`, or `above` (mean > P95), plus the percentile rank of the paper mean among the draws.
4. **Drawdown at the paper's n.** The same draws, taken in slot order, give a largest peak-to-trough fall of cumulative R (from 0) each; the reference is their 90th percentile; the paper value is its own largest fall in exit order; `within` is paper <= P90.
5. **Determinism.** Seeds come from SHA-256 of `epoch id | snapshot key | n`, through the platform-independent `SeededRandom` (SplitMix64); the same inputs give the same numbers; draws default 10,000 (`BandOptions.Draws`, 1,000 to 100,000).
6. **Honest states.** Fewer than 30 counted trades: every figure is `insufficient` (count shown). 30 to 59: figures are `provisional` with the word `interim`. 60 or more: `value`. A counted cell with no reference or fewer than 30 reference trades makes the band and the drawdown `insufficient` and lists the cells (never silently dropped). A paper basis (net/gross) different from the cell's costing, or a different charge sheet id where the run record names one, is `not_evaluable: basis differs`. No snapshot before the start is `unavailable`.
7. **Positive after costs (revised 2026-10-25).** The paper mean and its own 90% percentile-bootstrap interval are reported; "after costs" requires `r_basis = net` and a fact that the owner confirmed the sheet. That fact comes through a new port `ICostConfirmationFact` (`Application/Abstractions/Paper`) whose only implementation in this slice is `NotConfirmed` (always false): `IChargeSheetConfirmationSource` (ADR 0100) may be read by no other Application type, so P10-04 cannot read it. Until the unwritten slice that writes the charge-sheet confirmation (widen the allow-list, supply the real fact) exists, the production default `NotConfirmed` makes criteria E3 and E4 `not_evaluable: costs not confirmed` and gross figures are labelled gross; therefore every test that expects `met` or `not_met` injects a stub `ICostConfirmationFact` returning true. A test pins the port and that the handler has no dependency on the confirmation source.
8. **Criterion E3 (expectancy).** `not_evaluable` below 60 counted trades or when 6/7 say so; `not_met` when the paper mean <= 0 or `below` the band (stated as a fact with the numbers); otherwise `met`, whose label carries `(point estimate; 90% interval [lo, hi] includes 0: positive expectancy is not statistically established)` when lo <= 0, and `(above the band: luck or a leak is possible)` when `above`. **Criterion E4 (drawdown)** has the same gates, `not_met` when paper > P90, else `met` with both numbers.
9. **Early warning (not a verdict).** With 30 or more counted trades and the paper 90% interval's upper end below the band's lower edge, the view sets `earlyWarning` with the sentence `paper is clearly worse than the backtest allows: stop and review before more trades`.
10. **Fan.** The view returns, per trade index 1..n, the 5th, 50th, 95th percentile of cumulative R of the draws and the paper cumulative R, with the table form of the same numbers.
11. **Sensitivity beside.** The plan-price grades of **all** primary tickets of the epoch (approved or not, P7-13) give a second mean R with its n, labelled `simulated on plan prices, all proposals`; it is shown beside, never used for E3/E4.
12. Hand fixtures hold exactly (degenerate) and within the stated tolerance (random); a property: a permutation of the paper trades leaves the band unchanged and changes only the paper drawdown; adding a cell reference with identical trades does not change the band.
13. Read only: no write port is reachable from the handler; 422 `paper.invalid` for a non-positive epoch id or a draw count out of range; 404 `paper.not_found` for an epoch id that is not recorded.

## Decisions (researched)
1. **Q: Which statistic is "within the backtest band"?** A: the paper mean against the 5th to 95th percentile (nearest-rank percentiles, as `MonteCarlo` computes them) of the mean of **n backtest trades drawn from the same cells in the same proportions**, plus the paper mean's own 90% bootstrap interval. Why: the paper set is a mixture of cells, so one cell's band or the pooled backtest mean answers the wrong question; stratifying removes the composition effect. A bootstrap reuses the repo's seeded generator and the Monte Carlo already in the Lab (ADR 0062), needs no normality, and is explainable in one sentence. Rejected: a t-test of the mean against 0 or against the backtest mean (assumes near-normal R; says nothing about the mixture); Mann-Whitney (ranks ignore the size of wins and losses, which is the point of R); a sequential probability ratio test (efficient, but peeking and a new design for a roadmap row that says "band"). Source: ADR 0062; Wikipedia, Bootstrapping (statistics), read 2026-10-24: the percentile method works when the bootstrap distribution is roughly symmetric and centred, and percentile intervals from small samples (under about 50) are too narrow, which is why n >= 60 is required before `value` and the limits say the interval is approximate.
2. **Q: One-sided or two-sided band?** A: failure only below P05 (being better than the backtest is not a failure, but `above` is flagged because an unusually good paper result is more often luck or a leak than skill). Positive expectancy is a separate test (mean > 0).
3. **Q: Use the Lab's stored Monte Carlo drawdown (P95 at the OOS N)?** A: no. It is for the backtest's trade count and one strategy; the largest drawdown of a random walk grows with the length of the sequence (about with the square root for zero drift, only logarithmically for a positive drift; Magdon-Ismail et al., J. Applied Probability 41(1), 2004, via a secondary summary read 2026-10-24: https://www.mathworks.com/help/finance/emaxdrawdown.html and the paper listing https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/on-the-maximum-drawdown-of-a-brownian-motion/F9E3B8A454B020DDEBF0AC3390EF7807), so a 60-trade paper drawdown compared with a 300-trade backtest drawdown would pass almost always. The roadmap says 90th percentile; the Lab stores the 50th and 95th. The pure `MonteCarlo` primitives (generator, percentile, drawdown) are reused; the draws are made at the paper's n over the paper's cells. The exact growth rates are from a secondary description, not the paper (UNVERIFIED).
4. **Q: Reference trades' weighting?** A: unweighted, all closed pre-epoch trades of the cell. Recency weights (P6-01) shape the cell's estimate for ranking; a band should describe the whole history the cell was selected on.
5. **Q: Selection bias?** A: stated, not corrected. Cells were chosen because they looked good on this very history (winner's curse), so the reference mean is optimistic and the band is a stricter test than a fair-coin world. This can only make `below` more likely, which is the safe direction.
6. **Q: Why n >= 60 for `value`, 30 for `provisional`?** A: 60 is the roadmap's count; 30 is the repo's `min_trades` and the P8 insight minimum is 15, which is too thin for a distributional test. Below 30 only the count is shown.
7. **Q: Clustering by day?** A: draws are independent; trades of one session are correlated (same market day), so the interval is somewhat too narrow. Stated; a session-block bootstrap is a follow-up if the owner wants it (Findings).

## Risks and honest limits
- With 60 trades and a spread near 1.5R the 90% interval is about +-0.3R: only an expectancy above about +0.3R can be shown positive, and the band can only reject a result worse than about -0.3R from the backtest. A `met` is therefore weak evidence, and the label says when the interval includes 0. This is the arithmetic of the sample, not a defect of the method.
- The reference is simulated (modelled fills, in-sample, about 2.1 years, no hold-out, members as of today); the paper side is also simulated. Agreement between them proves the pipeline behaves as the research did on new days, not that real fills will.
- Clusters, regime shifts and a changing market are not modelled.
- Cells with thin references make the result `insufficient`; the owner cannot widen the sample without a new epoch.
