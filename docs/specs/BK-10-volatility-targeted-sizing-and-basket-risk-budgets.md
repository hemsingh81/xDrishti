## Goal
In a turbulent basket or on a crowded day the plan suggests a smaller quantity than the fixed-risk rule would (never larger), no basket can use more than its owner-set share of the day's open risk, a suggested quantity never exceeds a stated fraction of the share's daily traded value, and the owner can see on stored trades what these rules would have done to drawdown and return before switching any of them on.

## Scope
**In:** pure `VolTarget` (EWMA volatility and the scale in [0.5, 1.0]), `BasketRiskBudget` (fit-to-room), `CapacityCap` (quantity cap from ADV20), their wiring at ticket build as three **down-only** factors, `SizingStudy` (a pure what-if over stored trades), tables for the budgets and the settings history, owner-act API, a read-only line in the ticket's reasons, tests, ADR.
**Out (stated so no one adds them):** any increase of size above the fixed-risk quantity (no `allow_upsizing` setting exists in this slice; if the owner ever wants it, it is a new spec, a new ADR and a hypothesis-loop experiment); margin figures, broker leverage multipliers and MIS/MTF eligible lists (broker data, P12 and later; the research note's items 4 and 5 are not built here); a UI form for the settings (the owner-act form pattern of P8-19, a follow-up BK-10b; this slice ships the API and read-only display); automatic changes to stored budgets or to the settings by any rule, job, model or assistant; ML output changing size (needs the hypothesis loop and approval); equal-risk-contribution or HRP weighting across baskets; basket kill switches (U15, separate); any order or order-shaped field.

## Definitions
- **EWMA variance:** σ²_t = λ σ²_{t-1} + (1 - λ) r²_{t-1}, λ = 0.94, on the share's close-to-close returns, last value as of the previous close; seed σ²_0 = sample variance of the first 20 returns; needs ≥ 60 returns, else `insufficient`.
- **Target volatility** σ* = the median of the share's own EWMA volatility over the previous 252 sessions (needs ≥ 120). Relative to its own history by design: the scale is below 1 only when the share is more volatile than it usually is.
- **Volatility scale** s_v = clamp(σ* / σ_t, `Floor`, 1.0), `Floor` default 0.5. The upper bound 1.0 is a **constant in code, not a setting**.
- **Gate scale** s_g = 0.75 in reduced mode, 1.0 otherwise (the built gate).
- **Capacity cap** q_cap = floor(`CapacityPct` × ADV20 / entry) (ADV20 = median traded value of 20 sessions); `CapacityPct` default 1 %.
- **Budget room** room_b = budget_b × total open risk limit − open risk already used by basket b (today's book plus earlier picks).
- **Final quantity** q = floor(min(q_fixed × min(s_v, s_g), q_cap, fit-to-room quantity)); q ≤ q_fixed always; 0 means the ticket goes to reserve with the reason.

## Acceptance criteria
1. **EWMA by hand.** Given σ²_0 = 0.0001 (1 % a day) and returns +2 %, then 0 %, then σ²_1 = 0.94 × 0.0001 + 0.06 × 0.0004 = 0.000118 and σ²_2 = 0.94 × 0.000118 + 0.06 × 0 = 0.00011092, σ_2 = 0.010531 (within 1e-6). With fewer than 60 returns the state is `insufficient` (n, min 60), no number, no scale (the quantity is the fixed one and the ticket says "volatility unknown").
2. **Scale by hand.** Given σ* = 0.0080 and σ_t = 0.0105, then s_v = 0.7619; σ_t = 0.0200 gives 0.4 → floored to 0.5; σ_t = 0.0060 gives 1.3333 → **capped at 1.0**. A property test over random σ proves s_v ∈ [0.5, 1.0] always and q ≤ q_fixed always.
3. **Quantity by hand.** Given capital 1,000,000, risk 0.5 % (5,000), entry 100, stop distance 10 (risk per share 10), the fixed quantity is 500 (`Sizing.Quantity`); with s_v = 0.7619 the quantity is floor(500 × 0.7619) = 380 and the risk taken is 3,800 ≤ 5,000. With s_g = 0.75 and s_v = 0.7619 the factor is min(0.7619, 0.75) = 0.75 → 375 (Decision 3).
4. **Capacity cap by hand.** Given ADV20 = 20,00,000 rupees and entry 100 and `CapacityPct` 1 %, then q_cap = floor(20,000 / 100) = 200; a fixed quantity 380 is cut to 200 and the reason says "capped at 1 % of 20-day traded value". A share without 20 sessions has no cap and the ticket says "capacity unknown" (a limit that cannot be computed is shown as unavailable, never as a pass).
5. **Budget fit by hand.** Given a total open risk limit of 3.0 % of capital (30,000), a basket budget of 40 % (12,000), basket open risk used 9,000 and a candidate whose risk is 5,000, then room = 3,000; the candidate is scaled by 3,000 / 5,000 = 0.6 (≥ `Floor` 0.5) and taken at 60 % of its quantity with the note "uses 100 % of basket budget"; with used = 10,000, room 2,000, the ratio 0.4 is below the floor so the candidate goes to reserve with code `basket_budget` and the numbers. A basket with no budget row has no budget (off).
6. **Off means no change.** Given `sizing.vol_target.enabled` = false and no budget rows and `capacity.enabled` = false (all defaults), then every plan on the P7-04/P7-07/P7-09 fixtures is byte-identical to today (regression), and each ticket still shows the computed scale as "not applied (setting off)".
7. **Only down.** Given any combination of settings and inputs, then the final quantity is never larger than the quantity the fixed-risk rule gives, and the total open risk of the plan never exceeds the existing limits (property test over random books, scales and budgets).
8. **No upsizing path.** A static test (architecture rule) fails if `VolTarget` can return a value above 1.0 or if any settings key, DTO or method of this slice contains the words `upsize`, `leverage`, `margin` or `multiplier`; the `Floor` setting is validated to [0.25, 1.0] and a `Cap` setting does not exist.
9. **Owner act, password, history.** Given `PUT /api/selection/sizing-settings` or `PUT /api/selection/basket-risk-budgets/{basketId}`, then each change needs a session, the CSRF token and a fresh password in the body; an actor that is not `owner:<name>` is refused before the store; every change appends a row (old value, new value, actor, database time, hash, `direction tighten|loosen|neutral`); a budget that is raised, removed, or a `Floor` lowered, or a cap percent raised is `loosen` and is also shown in the next plan's banner until dismissed; the password is never logged or echoed; the history keeps the last change visible. With no rows everything is OFF.
10. **Auto option.** The standing rule "anything needing manual approval also runs automatically by a setting" holds: tickets sized by an ON setting need **no per-ticket approval of the scale** (it only reduces); turning the setting ON is the owner act of criterion 9; the plan item records the setting snapshot and the scale (`sizing_note`: s_v, s_g, q_cap, room, the setting version); a ticket sized with `unavailable` inputs is sized at the fixed quantity and flagged, never scaled by a guess; no setting allows an order to be placed (the P12 gate is not touched).
11. **Sizing study by hand.** Given six stored trades with net R +1, −1, −1, +2, −1, +1 and scales 1, 1, 0.6, 0.6, 1, 1, then the unscaled cumulative R is 1, 0, −1, 1, 0, 1 (max drawdown 2.0 R, total +1.0 R) and the scaled one is 1, 0, −0.6, 0.6, −0.4, 0.6 (max drawdown 1.6 R, total +0.6 R): the study prints "max drawdown 20 % lower, total R 40 % lower" with both numbers, the hold-out halves (sign of the change in each half) and the trade count; fewer than 30 trades in a half is `insufficient` for that half. It prints "descriptive, before costs, same trades" and recommends nothing.
12. **Study reads only stored data.** The study uses stored trades (`SimTrade` schema 5: net R, decision time) and the scale computed from daily bars up to each decision date (no look-ahead, truncation property); it writes nothing and places nothing; the scale ceiling in the study is also 1.0 (no up-scaling "what-if" is produced; the owner's Q4 about studying up-scaling is a separate decision, see Open questions).
13. **Fit-to-room is one place.** The budget fit and the volatility scale are applied in one function after the fill (so the cluster cap of BK-08, the per-sector and per-basket counts keep their order); the reserve reason lists the first limit that failed, with numbers.
14. **Performance and logs.** Sizing for a 5-ticket plan adds under 30 ms; log events (scale computed, budget fit, capacity cap, settings changed) carry counts only, never amounts of capital; next free id block at build time.

## Decisions (researched)
1. **Down-scaling only; the ceiling is a constant, not a setting.** Question: should the owner be able to let the scale go above 1.0? Chosen: no, in this slice. Reason: the owner asked for capital efficiency, but an upward scale is a bigger book in calm periods, which is the leverage risk this project's rules forbid (research §3 item 2, §4 item 6); the evidence for volatility-managed sizing is mostly a smoother drawdown, and out-of-sample or after-cost versions often fail to beat unmanaged ones. Rejected: a setting `allow_upsizing` with a password (the research note's first sketch): making it a setting makes it reachable by a mistaken click; a new spec is the right friction. Source: Moreira and Muir (NBER 22208) and Barroso and Santa-Clara (JFE 2015) for the benefit; Cederburg et al. (2020) for the failure; all read as snippets only (UNVERIFIED primary).
2. **Target = the share's own median volatility, not a rupee or a fixed percent.** Reason: a fixed target would shrink every size permanently (a hidden haircut) or need a number nobody can justify; relative to its own history the scale is 1 in normal times and below 1 only in unusually volatile ones. Rejected: a global target volatility setting (a tuning knob that would be fitted to results).
3. **Combine the down factors by the minimum, not the product.** Reason: the gate's 0.75 and the volatility scale answer the same question (the market is risky now); the product would double count (0.75 × 0.7619 = 0.57) and could cut below the floor the owner chose. Rejected: product (more conservative but unexplained). Open question below.
4. **EWMA 0.94, seed from 20 returns, min 60.** Reason: the standard RiskMetrics decay for daily data; the minimums stop a new listing from getting a made-up volatility. Source: J.P. Morgan RiskMetrics Technical Document (1996), not opened (UNVERIFIED); research note §U7.
5. **Budgets as a share of the day's total open risk limit, fit by scaling.** Chosen: one rule that scales a candidate to the room and reserves it when the ratio is under the floor. Reason: same shape as the volatility scale (down only), numbers visible ("uses 55 % of budget"). Rejected: hard block only (loses a good ticket that fits at 60 %); equal-risk-contribution across baskets (needs a covariance model for a small book).
6. **Capacity cap at 1 % of ADV20, off by default.** Reason: the owner's size is small, but a thin share with a large suggested quantity cannot be filled at the assumed price; a cap makes "more size" impossible where fills are implausible (research §3 item 6). The 1 % is a default for the owner to confirm, not a regulatory number.
7. **All changes are owner acts; the direction is recorded.** The repo's rule (ADR 0091) makes the owner, with a fresh password, the only author of settings that change what a plan suggests; recording `tighten|loosen` lets a review find every loosening. Rejected: letting tightening be automatic (a rule could shrink the plan to nothing unseen).
8. **The study is on stored trades and scales only to 1.0.** Same trades, common scale function, before costs (net costs unconfirmed: ISS-25), reporting max drawdown and total in R and the hold-out halves. Reason: R multiples are scale-free per trade so the what-if is a transform, no new simulator mode is needed (research asked for a sim option; this gives the same comparison without a rerun). Rejected: a new sim `sizing` option (touches `Sizing` for a result a transform gives).
9. **Regulatory and margin facts stay out.** Peak-margin, the 20 % upfront floor, MIS multipliers and the 80 % sale-proceeds credit are broker and SEBI facts quoted from secondary sources (research §3, UNVERIFIED); they are inputs the broker provides, stored dated, shown as `unavailable` when absent, and not part of this slice at all.

## Risks and honest limits
- The scale reduces size when volatility is high, which is often when the best breakouts occur; the study may show a lower total R as well as a lower drawdown (criterion 11 does by construction). The owner decides; nothing is recommended.
- EWMA needs 60 returns; new listings and long halts are sized at the fixed quantity and flagged.
- A basket budget uses the basket(s) a candidate belongs to; a share in two baskets counts against both (P8-09 overlap note); the fit uses the tightest.
- The capacity cap uses stored daily volume, which includes block and auction volume.
- Nothing here has been run on paper trades; it is advisory until P10 paper fills confirm slippage and P12 exists.
