## Goal
Run one rule set, Opening Range Breakout v1, BUY and SELL, over invented 1-minute bars and get a trade list the owner can check by hand: where each trade entered, where its stop and target were, why it left, R, MAE and MFE, and which conditions were true, false or unknown at the decision bar. This slice builds the **first stage** of the two-stage simulator, the entry scanner, and the `Simulator` that composes it with P3-03's walker; the two share nothing but the entry candidate, which carries no exit, so that P4 can replay the same entries under every exit policy. A look-ahead property runs through the simulator's own loop, and every result says what it is: fills exact, no entry limit, no costs, portfolio limits not simulated, in-sample. Pure library: no database, no YAML, no market context.

## Scope
**In:**
1. **The scanner** `EntryScanner` in the new namespace `XDrishti.Simulation.Scanning` (one instrument, one side). It creates and steps its own `FeatureFrame` with 1-minute bars; only at a step where `frame.BarClosed(E)` (E = the evaluation timeframe) it asks the bound side of P3-02b (`BoundSide.Evaluate`, `InEntryWindow`) and decides (D1, D2). It emits `EntryCandidate`s (P3-03's value: trigger bar, stop level, the decision-time trace) and `ScanCounters` (D10). Feature handles are resolved **once at `Create`** (the layout guarantee of `BoundSide`: `HandleOf(Features[i]) == i`), never per step. **The stop the scanner supports** is the one of the kinds that read a level on the evaluation timeframe: `swing_low`, `swing_high`, `bar_low`, `bar_high` and `or_opposite`, with `buffer_atr` 0 and the stop timeframe absent or equal to E; the stop is the value of the level feature at the decision step, used as given. `EntryScanner.Create` returns an error for the rest: `fixed_pct` and `atr_mult` (no level), a `buffer_atr` above 0, a stop timeframe other than E → `simulation.scanner_stop_unsupported` (P3-05 adds buffers and the other kinds); and **E = 1D** → `simulation.scanner_timeframe_unsupported` (a daily bar is always "stale" by D1; the daily evaluation is P3-03d). `Simulator.Create` is stricter (AC11). ## Definitions (numbers and rules the tests pin)
All times are India time. A *minute* is a 1-minute bar; `E` is the evaluation timeframe (15m in the fixtures unless stated). The numbers D1, D2 and D8 keep the numbers of the first draft of P3-03, so that the references in P3-03b and P3-04 hold; D3 to D7 and D9 (the walker's) are P3-03's. The scanner parts of the first draft's D6, D7 and D9 are **D10, D11 and D12** here (so that no number means two things): the scanner counters, the header labels and the scanner fixtures. The fixtures F1 to F7, F9, F12 to F17 and F17b, the prefix **P** and the bar **B**, and the walker table W1 to W14 are P3-03's D9 and are not repeated.

**D1 — Decisions.** The scanner steps the frame with the bars of one instrument in order. A *decision step* is a step at which `frame.BarClosed(E)`; nothing is evaluated at any other step ([ADR 0034](../adr/0034-the-rules-expression-language-lint-and-compiler.md), decision 15). The *decision instant* is `frame.LastBarTime + 60 s`, the instant the E bar closed; its India clock time is the compiled `time` (ADR 0034, decision 4), so a bar that closes at 09:45 is decided "at 09:45". `DecisionBarIndex` is the list index of the step's minute (the minute that closed the bar). A step that closed two E bars (one minute closing a late bucket and its own bucket) decides the second and counts the first as lost (`LostDecisions += ClosedBarsAt(E) − 1`). A step whose last closed E bar started on an earlier India date than the step's own minute (a bucket that the first minute of the next date closed late) is *stale*: it is counted and not evaluated, because no order may be placed on yesterday's bar. A bucket closed late on the same date is decided at the minute that closed it. `TriggerBarComplete` is the closed bar's own `Complete` flag (false for a bucket with a missing minute, whether it closed on time or late: F15, F16). A late close inside a date is not bounded by the scanner (a hole of 100 minutes closes the 09:30 bucket at 11:20, still inside the window, and the decision is accepted; the fill delay of P3-03 bounds only the entry that follows); the count of such decisions is `IncompleteBarsConsumed`. `DecisionBarIndex` is the number of `Step` calls before the one that made the decision (the list index of the minute when `Simulator.Run` steps the whole list).

**D2 — The order at a decision step, per side.** The date of a step is the India date of **its own minute**; the per-date flags (the invalidation latch, the day's candidate) reset at the first step whose minute is on a new date, even if that step is stale. At every step that is not stale, `BoundSide.Evaluate` evaluates every item of `setup`, `trigger` and `invalidate` (no skipping across items; the lists combined at list level, `all`/`any`, unknown as in ADR 0034) and returns a `SideEvaluation` of three truths. Then, in this order:
1. **The latch.** If `Invalidate == True` at this step, the side takes no *first* entry for the rest of the date, this step included; `InvalidatedDays` counts the dates on which the invalidate list was True at least once (even if a candidate already existed that day). The items of `invalidate` are counted at every non-stale decision step, window or not.
2. **The window.** `BoundSide.InEntryWindow`: `first_entry ≤ decision time ≤ last_entry`, both ends included (09:30 and 11:30 admit the bars that close at 09:30 and at 11:30). Outside it the step is counted (`OutOfWindowSteps`) and the items of `setup` and `trigger` are not counted.
3. **Eligible.** Inside the window, a step is *eligible* when `Setup == True` and `Trigger == True`, read from the fields of the `SideEvaluation` (setup absent is True). `SideEvaluation.Fires` (which also needs `Invalidate == False`) is the entry condition of the model; this scanner reads the fields too, because it has to tell **why** an eligible step makes no candidate, and that is the whole point of the counters. Unknown is never true (ADR 0034, decision 3). Setup is the gate: it is evaluated together with the trigger at each closed bar of E, not "tonight" (P3-02b question 1).
4. **The fate of an eligible step**, exclusive, in this order: the side already has a candidate on this date → `LaterTriggers`; the latch is set (step 1, this step or an earlier one) → `BlockedByInvalidation`; `Invalidate == Unknown` → `UnknownInvalidation`; the stop feature has no value → `StopNotReady` (the date stays open); otherwise → a **candidate**. *Decided in this draft, on the review of the first draft; the owner may revert:* the candidate check comes before the latch, because an invalidation that turns true after an entry was taken does not make a later trigger a "blocked" one; `BlockedByInvalidation` counts the entries that the latch kept from being the day's first (F19, F19b). **Identity:** the eligible steps of a side add up to `Candidates + LaterTriggers + BlockedByInvalidation + UnknownInvalidation + StopNotReady`, checked on every fixture against a hand count.

So there is **at most one candidate per instrument, side and date: the first**. A stop-out does not arm the pattern again, and both sides may have a candidate on the same date (BUY and SELL are independent scanners).

**D10 — Scanner counters** (`ScanCounters` per side; the walker's `WalkCounters` are P3-03's D6). `Bars` (= `frame.Steps`), `IgnoredBars`, `IncompleteBarsConsumed` (the frame's, per lane), `DecisionSteps`, `LostDecisions`, `StaleBucketSteps`, `OutOfWindowSteps`, `WindowSteps`, `FullyReadySteps` (the window steps at which **every feature** of every item of `setup` and `trigger` has a value at lag 0, whatever the result of the item: a condition made False by Kleene logic while one of its features is still empty does not count as ready, so P3-04's `features_not_ready` skip fires), `Candidates`, `LaterTriggers`, `BlockedByInvalidation`, `InvalidatedDays`, `StopNotReady`, `UnknownInvalidation`, and per condition path (`buy.trigger[0]`, `sell.setup[1]`, `buy.invalidate[0]` …) the counts **True, False, Unknown, NotReady**. `NotReady` is an unknown result in which at least one feature of the condition has no value at lag 0 (warm-up, a pending per-session feature); `Unknown` is any other unknown (a lag the history cannot give, a division by zero). Identities: `DecisionSteps = StaleBucketSteps + OutOfWindowSteps + WindowSteps`; the eligible-step identity of D2; the four counts of a `setup` or `trigger` item add up to `WindowSteps`, those of an `invalidate` item to `DecisionSteps − StaleBucketSteps`. A rule that never fires gives zero candidates and looks safe; these counters are how it is seen. `IgnoredBars` is the frame's ordering guard (0 for a `Run`, which checks the order first); it stays a counter of the scanner for the live feed.

**D11 — What the result says it is** (the header part of the first draft's D7; the walker's part is P3-03's D7). The header of a `Run` carries `SimulationEngine.Version`, the `SimTrade` schema version, the strategy id, `Fills = Exact`, `Costs = None` and exactly these five labels, in this order: `fills: exact`, `entry limit: not applied`, `costs: none`, `portfolio limits: not simulated`, `sample: in-sample` (the first two are replaced by P3-03b, *Order with P3-03b*; the amendment of ADR 0037 says so, and ADR 0038 replaces them). A figure from this engine is a signal-level result per instrument on invented data; it says nothing about edge.

**D8 — ORB v1** (the owner approved it as a bare breakout on 2026-10-18, plan question Q2; the filters of doc 07 are added one at a time after the first hand check). Id `ORB`; style intraday; entry timeframes 5m and 15m (the 10m and 30m cells do not compile with `or_min` 15 on those bars: "not applicable"; this slice runs 15m and one 5m check); sides BUY and SELL; one parameter `or_min = 15`; entry `at_close` (realised as P3-03 D3); no setup, no invalidate, no filters; stop `or_opposite` with `minutes` = the parameter `or_min` and no buffer; exits exactly one `FIXED_RR` with `rr` 2.0 (square-off is always on); `time_rules` first entry 09:30, last entry 11:30, square-off 15:15.

| Side | Trigger (one condition) | Stop (`or_opposite`) |
|------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| BUY | `close > or_high(or_min) and close[1] <= or_high(or_min)` | `or_low(or_min)` |
| SELL (explicit sell block, written out by hand) | `close < or_low(or_min) and close[1] >= or_low(or_min)` | `or_high(or_min)` |

Read: a 15-minute bar closes above the high of the first 15 minutes while the bar before it did not (SELL: below the low). The level form is used because `or_high` keeps the history of the session only ([ADR 0035](../adr/0035-per-session-features-keep-the-history-of-the-session-only.md)): the level is read at lag 0 and the close, whose history is continuous, is lagged, so the condition is known from the first bar of the day; `crosses_above(close, or_high(15))` would be unknown there and a subscript on a call is refused. On 15m `or_high(15)` is the first bar, ready after it closes; `or_min = 30` is two bars and `or_min = 20` is refused on 15m (not a whole number of bars).

## Acceptance criteria
Numbered, each testable. All through the real compiler and frame; nothing is mocked. "The table" is P3-03's D9 and walker table and the D9 above.

**The strategy**
1. **ORB v1 compiles.** Given `OrbV1.Create()` when each side is compiled through P3-02b for E = 15m and for E = 5m then there are no errors and no warnings; the features of the BUY side are [`close`, `or_high(15)`, `or_low(15)`] and of the SELL side [`close`, `or_low(15)`, `or_high(15)`] (the lists P3-02b pins), the history depth is 2, no market context and no expiry dates are needed, and there is one parameter. `Create(orMinutes: 30)` reads `or_high(30)` and `or_low(30)`; `Create(orMinutes: 20)` is refused on 15m by the compiler with two problems, `bad_arguments` at `buy.trigger[0]` (and its SELL twin) and `feature_invalid` at `buy.stop.minutes` (20 is not a whole number of 15-minute bars; P3-02b criterion 14). **The definition is pinned:** the result of `OrbV1.Create()` equals a literal `StrategyDefinition` in the test (id `ORB`, style intraday, timeframes 5m and 15m, sides Buy and Sell, one param `or_min` 15, the stop `minutes` as `Quantity.Named("or_min")`, `FIXED_RR` with `rr` [2.0], time rules 09:30 / 11:30 / 15:15, no filters and no overlays, the two trigger texts of D8), because this definition is the interim version of the built-in ORB until P3-02d.

**The entry scanner**
2. **Candidates.** Given the days F1 to F7, F9, F12 to F17 and F17b, and F11, F19b and F21 when each is scanned then the candidates are exactly the hand-made candidates of P3-03's tables and of D9 above: how many, side, `DecisionBarIndex`, decision instant, stop level and trigger bar (start, open, high, low, close, volume, minutes, complete); and none on F8, F10, F18, F19, F20 and, in F21, days A and B. F13 yields a BUY and a SELL candidate on one date.
3. **The decision trace.** Given the candidate of F1 then its trace lists the items in the order of `CompiledSide.Items` (setup, trigger, invalidate; here `buy.trigger[0]` only), each with its path, its source text, its truth (`True`) and, per feature it reads, the values at lags 0 up to its history depth − 1: `close@15m` [100.50, 99.00], `or_high(15)@15m` [100.00, 100.00]; for the SELL candidate of F12: `close@15m` [97.50, 99.00], `or_low(15)@15m` [98.00, 98.00]. A value with no history is `NaN`. The trace is built only when a candidate is made.
4. **Window and one candidate per side and day.** F9 is taken (decision 11:30) and F10 is not (decision 11:45: not evaluated, counted); F11 emits one candidate, counts `LaterTriggers` 1 and the second trigger makes no trade; F13 has candidates on both sides of one date.
5. **Setup, invalidate and the order of D2.** F20: the trigger is true at 09:45 while `setup` is false, so there is no candidate. F19: the invalidate list is true at the step of the trigger, so the entry is blocked at that very step (`BlockedByInvalidation` 1). F19b: the invalidation turns true after the entry was taken, and the later trigger is `LaterTriggers`, not `BlockedByInvalidation`. F23: setup and trigger are True and invalidate is Unknown at 09:45, so there is no candidate and `UnknownInvalidation` 1, and the latch is set at 10:00. The identity of D2 holds on every fixture. F22: a stop that has no value yet is `StopNotReady`, the date stays open, and the candidate comes at the first step with a value. The counts are those of the tables.

The identities of D10 hold on every fixture. With `or_min = 30` on the F1 day (`or_high(30)` is 100.50, the high of 09:44, and `or_low(30)` is 98.00) `buy.trigger[0]` is NotReady 1 (09:30: `or_high(30)` needs two bars), True 1, False 7, `FullyReadySteps` 8: bar 2 closes at 100.50, which is not above 100.50; the first break is bar 6, which closes at 104.00 after `close[1]` 100.50, so the candidate is BUY with decision 10:45 (index 89), stop 98.00, trigger bar 10:30 (O 100.50 H 104.50 L 100.50 C 104.00, 15 minutes, complete). On the first bar of a stream `close[1]` is unknown but `close > or_high(or_min)` is false, so the condition is False, not Unknown (Kleene). **The same F1 minutes at E = 5m** (the 5m cell of the same definition) give the same decision instant 09:45, the same entry, stop, exit and R as F1, with the trigger bar 09:40 (5 minutes: O 99.00 H 100.50 L 99.00 C 100.50), `DecisionSteps` 75, `WindowSteps` 25, `OutOfWindowSteps` 50, `buy.trigger[0]` True 1, False 24.

A stream that joins late (its first minute 10:00) has no opening range on that date: every window step of that date is NotReady on both sides and nothing trades.

**The simulator**
10. **`Run` composes the stages.** Given F1 when `Simulator.Run` is called then `Trades` holds the trade of P3-03's table (field for field) and `Outcomes` the candidate with it; F6 gives `Trades` empty and one `OpenAtEnd` trade; F13 gives two trades ordered by entry instant, then Buy before Sell; F14 gives a candidate with no trade (`EntryThroughStop`) in `Outcomes`; the header carries the labels of D7 in order. `Run(A)` then `Run(B)` on one `Simulator` equals `Run(B)` on a fresh one, and 4 threads running 4 instruments give the results of one thread. The walker's counters in `Walk` equal the sums of the `WalkOutcome.ToCounters()` of the outcomes (each `CandidateOutcome` carries its `WalkOutcome`).
11. **Refused by name.** Given the definitions below when `Simulator.Create` is called then it returns no simulator and a located problem with exactly this code, whose path names the field and whose message names the offending kind and the slice that will run it: entry `stop_above_high` or `limit_pullback` → `simulation.unsupported_entry`; a stop kind other than `or_opposite`, or an `or_opposite` with `buffer_atr` above 0 → `simulation.unsupported_stop` (`level` is no kind of schema 1); `Exits` other than exactly one `FIXED_RR` with exactly one `rr` value, or any overlay → `simulation.unsupported_exit` (a second candidate policy or several `rr` values are P4's same-entries comparisons); style swing → `simulation.unsupported_style`; any filter name in `CompiledSide.Filters` → `simulation.filter_not_available`; a side that needs a market context or expiry dates → `simulation.needs_context` / `simulation.needs_expiry_days`. E = 1D → `simulation.scanner_timeframe_unsupported`. `EntryScanner.Create` itself returns `simulation.scanner_stop_unsupported` for `fixed_pct`, `atr_mult`, a `buffer_atr` above 0 and a stop timeframe other than E, and `simulation.scanner_timeframe_unsupported` for E = 1D (a test per case, and the supported kinds of F22 run). The problems of the compile itself (P3-02b) are passed on unchanged, with their paths and spans; `Problems` also carries compile warnings when a simulator exists; a `simulation.*` problem carries the span of the model problem (0, 0, line 1, column 1). **A half strategy is never run:** a side that fails to compile makes `Create` return no simulator, and that side's problems appear with their `buy.*` or `sell.*` paths (test: ORB whose SELL trigger is misspelt gives exactly the language's own problem at `sell.trigger[0]`).
12. **The order of the bars is checked once.** Given a list with two equal epochs or a descending pair, `Run` fails with `simulation.bars_not_ascending` before any step (O(n) once), so `IgnoredBars` of a `Run` result is always 0; a test steps an `EntryScanner` directly with a repeated minute and finds `IgnoredBars` 1 (the counter stays for the live feed).

**The seam, determinism and look-ahead**
13. **The scanner knows nothing of exits, and candidates do not depend on them.** `EntryScanner` has no parameter, member or dependency about exits. Given the F1 to F13 days run with `OrbV1.Create(rewardRisk: 1.5)`, `Create()` (2.0) and `Create(rewardRisk: 3.0)` then the candidate lists, including their traces, are equal field by field and the trades differ as they must (F1: target 104.25 at 10:30 with 1.5; 105.50 at 11:10 with 2.0; 108.00, not reached, so the position runs to the square-off with 3.0). The same holds for the scanner (not `Simulator.Create`, which refuses them) run on the `CompiledSide`s of ORB with `ATR_TRAIL` and `PARTIAL_TRAIL` exits.
14. **No look-ahead through the loop.** Given 100 seeded series of one to three sessions (seeds written in the test, a generator tuned until the property compares at least 60 candidates, at least 20 of each side, asserted) and for each a random cut `k`, a cut at the decision bar of a candidate and a cut one bar before it, when the bars after `k` are (a) removed and (b) replaced by arbitrary values (zero and huge prices, zero and huge volume, epochs unchanged) then, for the scanner and for `Simulator.Run`, both sides: the candidates with `DecisionBarIndex ≤ k` (trace and counters up to `k` included) are equal field by field to those of the full run, and a trade whose exit minute index is at most `k` is equal too; the cut at a candidate's own bar keeps it and the cut one bar before removes it. **Negative controls**, built from the public API only, each a doctored input run through the same property: a *peeking feed* in which bar *i* is given the open, high, low, close and volume of bar *i*+1 with its own epoch (the scanner decides on the next minute's data; the transform is applied to the full list and to the cut list each on its own), and the peeking walker input of P3-03 AC8; both are reported on at least 10 seeds while the honest run reports none on the very same inputs. "Counters up to `k`" are observable on the direct-scanner path only (a `Run` exposes final counters); the `Run` path compares candidates, trades and the outcomes settled at or before `k`. The property is run for `OrbV1` and for the strategies of F19, F20 and F21 (invalidate, setup, per-session features). It guards the feed, the frame and the loop (the scanner is a pure stepper, so only a doctored input can show a leak); the compiler is guarded where ADR 0034 says.
15. **Same input, same bytes.** Given F1 to F22 (including F19b) and the seeded days of AC14 when each is run twice, once on a copy of the bars, then a plain formatter in the test project (invariant culture, round-trip doubles) renders identical text for the trades, the candidates with their traces and the counters; the rendering of F1 to F22 equals a golden file checked in with the tests, which starts with the `SimulationEngine.Version` it was made with and is regenerated on purpose when a rule changes (a changed rule needs a new version); P3-03's golden file of the walker is extended, not replaced.
16. **Fill timing through the simulator.** Over the seeded runs of AC14, every trade of `Simulator.Run` satisfies the properties of P3-03 AC9 (fill minute, entry price, exit price bounds, R recomputed from the stored prices), and its `StrategyId`, side, date and trigger bar equal those of its candidate.

**Structure, performance, host**
17. **Architecture and work.** (a) *Allocation:* after one warm-up session, scanning six further sessions that fire nothing (the F8 shape on consecutive dates) allocates exactly 0 bytes, measured with the `AllocationProbe` that P3-03 adds to the same test project (no new copy). (b) *Work without a clock:* one frame step per minute (`Bars` equals the number of minutes); the counts of an invalidate item sum to `DecisionSteps − StaleBucketSteps` and those of a setup or trigger item to `WindowSteps`; the handles are resolved at `Create` (a test that reads the frame's handle lookup count, or an allocation check of 0 bytes per step, fails if a lookup per step comes back); the walker visits each minute of a trade once (`BarsExamined` of F1 is 86, of F5 331). ISS-15's own figure (a frame of 20 features at depth 256) is not taken here. ## Contract
- **API:** none. No endpoint is added or changed; `backend/contracts` is untouched; `scripts/gen-api.sh` is not needed. The one host change is a hosted service.
- **Data:** no table, no migration. New values in `XDrishti.Domain.Research`: `ScanCounters` and `ConditionCounter` (below), with pure sums.
- **UI:** none.
- **Events/jobs:** `StrategyWarmupService` (`internal sealed`, `XDrishti.Api`), an `IHostedService`: `StartAsync` starts the warm-up on the thread pool (`Task.Run(CompilerWarmup.Run)`) and returns at once, `StopAsync` does not wait for it. Test infrastructure the first draft left out: a `PackageReference` to `Microsoft.Extensions.TimeProvider.Testing` in `XDrishti.Api.IntegrationTests` (the central version exists), a hand-written `ILogger<StrategyWarmupService>` that captures the line, and a `TaskCompletionSource` that the service completes after it logs, awaited with `WaitAsync` of 30 s real time (no sleeps, `backend/tests/CLAUDE.md`).

What the scanner takes from P3-02b's `CompiledSide`: the one feature list a frame is created from (the features of the conditions, of the stop kind and the sentinel on E), `HistoryDepth`, `NeedsContext` / `NeedsExpiryDays`, `Filters`, `Items` (setup, then trigger, then invalidate: each `CompiledItem` with its `Path`, `Condition.Source`, `Condition.Features`, `Condition.HistoryDepth`), the `StopPlan` (here: `Level` and `BufferAtr`), the `ExitPlan`s and `TimeRules.SquareOffMinute`; and from `BoundSide` the item truths (`Evaluate(frame, Span<Truth>)` → `SideEvaluation`) and `InEntryWindow(frame)`. ## Non-functional
- **Performance:** scanning allocates 0 bytes in steady state (AC17a); conditions are evaluated at decision steps only; the frame's budget of 1 µs per step applies, so one instrument-year (about 93,750 minutes) takes under 0.1 s per side at the budget (two frames per instrument, one per side, double the frame cost: the owner's whole set is then about 80 s at the budget, not the 15 s of P3-04's UNVERIFIED estimate, and the benchmark says which); the explicit benchmark reports the real figure. The owner's 214 CSVs hold about 190,000 minutes each; the end-to-end time of a whole-set replay belongs to P3-04.
- **Determinism and purity:** no clock, no `Random`, no I/O, no ambient state in the library; ties ordered by entry instant, then side; two runs give identical results (AC15). Seeds and generators live in the test project. A `Simulator` holds only the compiled sides (immutable); every `Run` makes its own frames.
- **Honesty:** every result says what it is (D11); the counters make a rule that never fires visible (D10).
- **Security and privacy:** nothing is read from `data/`; tests use invented series and one invented instrument. No secrets, no network. The warm-up service logs one line with a duration, never a strategy text.
- **Observability:** the library returns counters and logs nothing; the host logs the warm-up line.

## Open questions
None needs the owner before the build: plan questions Q1 and Q2 (fills and exits; ORB v1 as a bare breakout) are answered and are the conventions of P3-03 and D8. The decisions below were taken in this draft on the review of the first draft of P3-03 and on research; the owner may revert any of them, and each is cheap to change before the build:
1. **The candidate check comes before the invalidation latch** (D2 step 4): an invalidation that turns true after the day's entry was taken does not turn a later trigger into a "blocked" one. The alternative (latch first) counts it as `BlockedByInvalidation`, which hides the fact that an entry already exists. F19 and F19b pin the choice.
2. **The trace lists the items in the order of `CompiledSide.Items`** (setup, trigger, invalidate), not the order of the first draft (invalidate first), so that the order of the truths, the counters and the trace is one.
3. **`EntryScanner` supports the level stop kinds with a zero buffer and E other than 1D, and refuses the rest with its own codes** (Scope 1) instead of guessing a formula that P3-05 and P3-03d must decide; `Simulator.Create` is stricter (`or_opposite` only).
4. **`Simulator.Create` refuses a side with an overlay** (`simulation.unsupported_exit`) like any exit other than one `FIXED_RR`: P3-02b accepts `time_stop` and `event_exit`, and a silent ignore would make a result look like what it is not.
