## Goal
Run the system on the owner's real money at **25 % to 50 % of the intended risk**, with **the owner placing every order by hand** at the broker, and answer one question with evidence: **do the owner's real fills match what the research and the paper phase promised?** The system captures the owner's fills and orders (read only), matches them to the approved tickets, grades the trades on what was executed, measures slippage per liquidity bucket and a capacity score, compares live with paper on the same tickets, enforces a ramp of the risk (25 % -> 50 % -> 100 %) that goes up only on stated evidence and falls back by itself on a breach, raises the daily and weekly loss halts at the human level, and settles the static IP and SEBI question for P12. The phase exit is a report, not a feeling: "live within expectations" is a stated non-inferiority test on paired tickets (P11-06) that is **NOT EVALUABLE on thin data and says so**. P11 proves the measurement machinery; what eight weeks of small live trading can conclude is bounded (section "Risks and honest limits").

## What P11 may and may not do
- **P11 may:** read the owner's Dhan trade book, trade history and order book (GET only); store them; match fills to approved tickets; compute slippage, capacity and the waterfall from stored records; size tickets for a `manual_live` account at a stored fraction of its risk profile; record halts and ramp moves; show all of it; produce proposals (a slippage model estimate) that **change nothing** until the owner applies them through the hypothesis loop.
- **P11 may not:** place, modify or cancel an order at any broker (the architecture tripwire of ADR 0083 stays green: no order verb, no `orders` route segment, no broker write on the allow-list); hold an order-capable action; present "approved" or "armed" as "an order is resting at the broker" (a ticket is a plan; the order is the owner's act in Dhan); apply a learned change to trading automatically; raise the risk step without the owner's act (the optional auto step-up below is a setting that ships OFF and is capped at 50 %).
- **Orders arrive only in P12** (`xd-exec`), behind the owner's per-account mode setting (default OFF) and the safeguards of doc 20 §A.

## The seam (the decisions the slices share)
**D1. The system places no order; the broker reads grow by exactly two GETs.** P11-01 adds `GET /v2/trades` (today's trade book, no query) and `GET /v2/orders` (the day's order book, no query) to the allow-list, both read-only, both under the existing HTTPS/no-redirect/30 s/5 MB/throttle rules, and changes the P7-18 pin and its refused probes in the same commit (the probe `GET /v2/orders` is removed; `POST`, `PUT /{id}`, `DELETE /{id}`, positions and funds stay refused). That change is the owner's, which is why the spec's Approved is the owner's act.
**D2. One live account; paper shadow in parallel, allocated independently.** The owner's `manual_live` Dhan account takes the plan at a ramp fraction. A paper account keeps following the same tickets (P10) so each live ticket has a paired paper outcome. The allocator treats live and paper as **two pools** (live accounts share the ticket quantity; each paper account is replicated, as doc 10 `replicate`), so paper never takes quantity from the real account.
**D3. Capture is its own store; the journal reads it.** Intraday fills never enter `portfolio.transactions` (lots, XIRR and cost would be disturbed; ADR 0025 c). P11-01 stores every NSE_EQ trade of a live account in `trading.live_fills` (all products), stores order-book revisions in `trading.live_orders`, and lets `trading.journal_fills` point at either source (additive migration). The P7-14 matcher, grader, endpoints and screens are extended, not replaced.
**D4. Matching is P7-14's key (account, instrument, side, planned day) with explicit flags, never a guess.** Tickets are valid for their planned session only (P7-11), so a day key is exact. A fill that fits two tickets stays unmatched. Quantity above the allocation, a price outside the ticket's band and a fill with no ticket are **flagged and kept**, not silently dropped (P11-01).
**D5. Every approved live ticket gets exactly one outcome**: `filled`, `partial`, `over_allocation`, `not_triggered` (the replay says nothing was there to take), `missed_no_order`, `missed_unfilled`, `rejected`, `missed_unknown` (no order evidence), `unknown`. A missed trigger is therefore a measured thing with a cost in plan-price R, never a guess (P11-01).
**D6. Slippage is side-signed (positive = worse for the owner) and decomposed**: total = delay (arrival price versus the reference) + execution (fill versus arrival). In rupees, ticks, basis points of the reference and R. Reference kinds (ticket's numeric level, plan-grade replay price, paper fill) are never pooled in one number (P11-02).
**D7. Statistics are plain and deterministic**: mean with a two-sided 95 % Student-t interval, median with a distribution-free order-statistic interval, minimum n = 10 for any interval, no figure below it (`insufficient`, with n), gates use one-sided 95 % bounds. No bootstrap, no random, no pooling across buckets to reach n (P11-02, P11-03).
**D8. Liquidity buckets** are fixed in advance from the 20-session average daily turnover in rupees crore: **L1 >= 500, L2 150-500, L3 50-150, L4 10-50, L5 < 10**, taken at the ticket's decision date. Participation (order value / turnover) is stored as a covariate. Edges are configuration with the same validation as other options; moving them after seeing the result is forbidden in the report (the edges are printed on it).
**D9. Capacity score = the shrunk share of orders that were filled in full** (`Beta` posterior toward the account's own rate, prior strength 10, n and 95 % interval beside it), by account, bucket and cell; `unavailable` without order evidence; advisory only, not wired into selection in P11 (P11-02).
**D10. The risk ramp** has three steps: **S1 = 25 %, S2 = 50 %, S3 = 100 %** of the profile's intended risk. S1 is where every live account starts; S2 needs the S1->S2 gates; S3 needs the P11 exit check MET and is never reached inside P11. A step goes **up only by the owner's act** (password, reason, audited, the gate numbers frozen in the record); it goes **down or on hold by itself**, recorded as `rule:ramp`. An optional `live.auto_ramp_up` switch (default OFF, owner act, capped at S2) satisfies the standing rule "approvals have an auto option" (P11-04).
**D11. Halts at the human level.** The system measures the account's loss against the profile's daily and weekly limits, raises a halt record, a bell item and a Today tile, invalidates the account's still-unfilled tickets, blocks approval of new tickets for the halted account (a non-confirmable checklist check), flags every fill taken after the halt started (`after_halt`), and counts such fills as a breach that holds the ramp. It cannot stop the owner; P12-05 turns the same rules into a system halt (P11-04).
**D12. Static IP and SEBI: not needed to place orders by hand; settle it for P12 now.** Orders typed by the owner in Dhan's own app are not API orders. The retail algo framework (in force since 1 April 2026 per broker notices) concerns API orders: a registered static IP, daily 2FA, an order-per-second cap, exchange algo ids. Doc 19 §5 and Q6 (2026-10-09) say: ask the ISP and re-check the circulars at the P11 exit. P11-05 does the enquiry early (an ISP lead time and a week-long Dhan IP lock make the end of the phase too late) and stores a decision record the phase check reads. One factual conflict is put to Dhan in writing because it also affects today's read sync (question D1 of the P11-05 runbook).
**D13. No automatic learning change.** The weekly slippage estimates and the capacity scores are stored and shown; the backtest slippage model, the selection and the cell states change only through the owner (hypothesis loop P6-10, one knob). This keeps the owner's 2026-10-23 decisions (no automatic learning change in P7) true in P11.
**D14. Honest states everywhere.** The six states of P8-00 D3 (`value`, `provisional`, `insufficient`, `unavailable`, `not_evaluable`, `empty`) and an Evidence block on every live number (basis, n, date range, reference kind, limits); gross versus net only on the owner's stored charge sheet (ISS-25); no composite score; no number without n.

## Acceptance criteria (of the phase; each slice carries its own)
1. Given a `manual_live` account at S1 and an approved ticket, when the nightly plan is built then the allocation has `mode = manual_live`, `risk_scale = 0.25` and a quantity equal to the paper rule at 25 % of the risk; no allocation exists above S2 before the P11 exit is MET.
2. Given the owner's hand-placed orders and fills for a session, when capture and settle have run then every approved live ticket has exactly one outcome from D5, every fill has exactly one role (entry, exit, discretionary) or is listed unmatched with its reason, and running again writes nothing new.
3. Given a graded round trip then the waterfall (plan R, entry slippage, exit difference, charges, actual R) adds up exactly to the actual R; a trip without plan levels is shown in rupees with no R.
4. Given fewer than 10 observations in a bucket then the report shows `insufficient` and n, never a mean; given 10 or more then it shows mean, median and their intervals, and the bucket edges.
5. Given a halt (daily or weekly) then a record, a notice and a Today tile exist, the account's unfilled tickets are invalidated, new approvals for it are refused with a non-confirmable check, and a later fill is flagged `after_halt` and holds the ramp.
6. Given the step-up gates not all passing, or any `unavailable`, then the owner can still read the numbers but the step-up act is refused with the failing gates listed; a step-down or hold never needs the owner.
7. Given no order evidence for a session then capacity, delay and the unfilled-order classes read `unavailable`, never zero.
8. `xd_app` can insert and select every new table and cannot update, delete or truncate any (grants tests); the allow-list equals its pin and every order write, position and fund probe is refused; no order-named type or route exists.
9. P11-06 prints MET only with the stated minimum evidence, never on fewer sessions or pairs, and prints its limits in every outcome.
10. Every slice ends with `scripts/check.sh` green, `scripts/gen-api.sh`, a `reviewer` pass, `scripts/build-docs.sh`, an ADR where it decides; the bundle guard `npm run size` stays at 200 KB (one nav entry only is eager).

## Non-functional
Reads are tiny (one order book and one trade book per poll, a page of rows each); 120 s polls (about 195 polls in the 09:10-15:40 window) give about 390 GETs a day (one order book and one trade book each), far below Dhan's non-trading limit of 20 per second (documented per second 20, none per day) and spaced by the existing 250 ms throttle. Statistics are O(n log n) over at most a few thousand rows. Every number carries n; no log line carries a price, quantity, symbol list, account number, order id or token (counts, ids of rows and milliseconds only). Time through `TimeProvider`; India dates through `IndiaTime`. Frontend: one lazy chunk, no new dependency.

## Decisions (researched)
1. **Q: Orders by hand, so how do real fills enter the system?** A: Read the broker's trade book and order book (D1, D3). Why: the existing sync deliberately skips intraday products and runs once at 16:30, so MIS fills and same-day halts would be invisible; the order book is the only place a rejection, a partial fill or an unfilled limit exists. Rejected: asking the owner to type fills (error-prone, defeats the goal), Dhan postback webhooks (needs an inbound port; ports stay on 127.0.0.1). Source: ADR 0025 amendment c, `DhanTradeMapper`; https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/orders/ (read 2026-10-24: order book `GET /orders`, trade book `GET /trades`, fields `orderStatus`, `averageTradedPrice`, `filledQty`, `createTime`, `exchangeTime`, `omsErrorDescription`).
2. **Q: Is a read of the order book "an order endpoint"?** A: No: GET, no body, no state change; the static-IP rule is for placement, modification and cancellation. Why: refusing it would remove the evidence the phase needs. Safeguard: the allow-list pin changes in the same commit, the probes for POST/PUT/DELETE/positions/funds stay refused, the spec's Approved is the owner's consent. Rejected: no order evidence (capacity and missed-trigger classes become guesses). Source: Dhan authentication page (read 2026-10-24): "Static IP is only required while using Order Placement APIs including Orders, Super Order, Forever Order."
3. **Q: How many ramp steps and what fractions?** A: 25 / 50 / 100 % (D10). Why: the roadmap says 25-50 % inside P11 and "scale gradually" after; the doubling steps keep each step's worst case within the previous step's loss limit scale. Rejected: 10 % steps (too many reviews for 8 weeks), a continuous formula (not auditable).
4. **Q: What evidence lets the step go up?** A: Execution and discipline evidence, **not** realised profit (P11-04). Why: 20 to 40 trades cannot distinguish an edge of +0.15 R from zero (standard error of the mean R with sd 1 R is 0.16 to 0.22 R); a gate on profit would reward luck and punish noise. What 20 trades can show is whether the fills match the model, whether the owner follows the plan and whether the risk limits held. Rejected: "net positive after N trades".
5. **Q: What is "live within expectations" for the exit?** A: A paired non-inferiority test: for tickets with both a live and a paper outcome, the one-sided 95 % upper bound of the mean shortfall (paper R minus live R) is at most 0.10 R, with at least 30 pairs (P11-06). Why: pairing cancels market noise, so a few dozen pairs can answer the execution question; 0.10 R is two thirds of the +0.15 R qualification gate of doc 09 §7. Rejected: comparing live mean R with the backtest mean (independent samples, far too noisy), requiring positive live P&L (see 4).
6. **Q: Which slippage reference?** A: Priority: ticket numeric level (P7-19), else plan-grade simulated entry (P7-14 uses it), else paper fill; stored as `reference_kind`, never pooled (D6). Why: the owner's live entry is judged against the plan he approved, and the replay price already contains the modelled slippage, so "live minus plan" is exactly the model error. Rejected: the previous close (not what the plan promised).
7. **Q: Liquidity edges?** A: D8. Why: 20-session turnover is the repo's own definition (`TicketDraft`); the NSE cash market traded about Rs 1 lakh crore a day in 2025 across roughly two thousand stocks (https://tradingqna.com/t/nse-annual-highlights-calendar-year-2025/190178, read 2026-10-24: average daily cash turnover Rs 99,622 crore), so a stock above Rs 500 crore is in the top tens, and Rs 50 crore is the doc 16 `min_turnover_cr`. Edges are checked against the owner's tracked universe at build and printed. Rejected: quantile buckets (they move every week and cannot be compared across weeks).
8. **Q: How is the slippage interval computed with small n?** A: Mean with Student-t, median with order statistics (D7). Why: no random, hand-checkable. Rejected: bootstrap (needs a seed convention and more n than is available).
9. **Q: Does the ramp need an auto option?** A: Yes, because the owner's standing rule says anything that needs manual approval must be able to run automatically; two switches `live.auto_approve_tickets` and `live.auto_ramp_up`, both OFF, owner-only with a password, recorded as `rule:` actors with the settings snapshot, never able to place an order, step-up capped at S2, `unavailable` never a pass. Rejected: no auto option (breaks the standing rule), an uncapped auto ramp (real money rising without the owner).
10. **Q: Where do halts live?** A: In the app as records and invalidations (D11). Why: the owner places orders by hand, so only a visible, hard-to-ignore state is possible; the loss is measured with realised plus open positions marked at the last price, and `unknown` is shown when an open position cannot be marked. Rejected: silently blocking tickets (the owner would not know why), counting realised loss only (understates).
11. **Q: When must the static IP be settled?** A: Start now, decide by the P11 exit (D12, P11-05). Why: ISPs sell it as an add-on and Dhan's whitelist cannot be edited for 7 days after it is set (Dhan page, read 2026-10-24); P12 should not wait on it. Rejected: wait for P12 (a week-long lock plus an ISP lead time on the critical path).
12. **Q: Do SEBI's rules apply to hand-placed orders?** A: Not to orders the owner types in Dhan's app; they apply to API orders. UNVERIFIED against the circular text (not fetched; broker and blog summaries only: https://support.fyers.in/portal/en/kb/articles/what-are-the-new-sebi-rules-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026, https://algobulls.com/blog/industry-insights-and-updates/sebi-new-algotrading-regulations-for-retail-investors-2026, read 2026-10-24). P11-05 puts this to Dhan in writing.

## Risks and honest limits
1. **Small n.** Eight weeks at up to three entries a day is at most about 120 tickets, realistically 40 to 80 round trips spread over cells and buckets. Most buckets and nearly every cell will read `insufficient`. That is the right answer, not a defect.
2. **Fixed costs dominate a small R.** At 25 % of 0.5 % risk on a small capital, 1 R may be a few hundred rupees; Dhan's per-order brokerage and the statutory lines are partly fixed, so the charges can be a large share of 1 R and make live net R look worse than paper for reasons unrelated to execution. P11-02 prints charges per round trip as a share of 1 R and the ramp never judges net R; the owner's capital choice (below) is the lever.
3. **The owner changes when the money is real.** Discretionary trades, skipped tickets and late entries are the largest expected gap; the discipline measures show them, they are not execution noise.
4. **Eight weeks is one market.** A live period inside one regime says little about another; it cannot confirm an edge, only that fills and discipline were not worse than assumed.
5. **Paired tickets are a selected sample** (only tickets the owner took and the paper account also filled). The report prints how many tickets were left out and why.
6. **Broker data shapes are unseen.** The order and trade book field names come from documentation; a real answer may differ (a recorded-fixture test gates P11-01). The history endpoint's behaviour for the current day is unverified; the trade book is used for today.
7. **Arrival price comes from 1-minute bars** that exist only after the end-of-day load; delay and execution components appear in the evening, not live.
8. **Token and clock.** A lapsed Dhan token or a sleeping Mac creates capture gaps; gaps are detected, shown and hold the ramp; they are not repaired by guessing.
9. **Static IP behaviour is unverified** for read-only calls (D12).
10. **The 8-week floor is calendar time**; nothing here speeds it up, and a hold or a halt extends it.
