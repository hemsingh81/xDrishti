## Goal
Make a simulated trade fill the way a real order can, so that an R the owner reads is not better than the market could have given. The entry is the limit order of doc 07 §2 and can be missed; a gap through a stop costs the gap; a target gives no free price improvement; a minute that holds both the stop and the target is a stop (unless the first print was already beyond the target); the square-off waits for the next minute that holds a trade; every market-type fill pays slippage on the price grid of the instrument's tick. The exact result stays beside the modelled one on every trade (`RExact`, `RFilled`, `SlippageR`), so the price of realism is visible per trade. The rules are one table (*Definitions*), each row pinned by hand-calculated fills.

## Definitions (numbers the tests pin)
Prices are rupees. A *minute* is a 1-minute `Bar(EpochSeconds, Open, High, Low, Close, Volume)`; its date is `SessionClock.DateOf(EpochSeconds)`, its time of day `SessionClock.MinuteOfDay(EpochSeconds)`. A missing minute is *absent*, and the walker never invents one. `tick` is the instrument's tick size. `trigger close` is `candidate.TriggerBar.Close`; `stop` is `candidate.StopLevel`, used as given. `Q` is the square-off minute of the day (15:15 is 915: `WalkRules.SquareOffMinute`, from `SessionRules.SquareOffMinute` of P3-02b). **Beyond** a level: for BUY the stop is beyond when a price is ≤ stop and the target when it is ≥ target; SELL mirrors. `tickDown(x)` and `tickUp(x)` are the nearest grid price at or below / at or above `x`. In the hand-calculated cases (P3-03's notation, D9) a listed minute is written `HH:MM O/H/L/C` and has Volume 1,000, `(v0)` after it means Volume 0; a minute not listed repeats the previous close as its open, high, low and close, with Volume 1,000; *absent* means no minute. The candidate's own minute is the one that starts 60 s before the decision instant and repeats the trigger close; the date is 2026-03-02.

### Entry: a limit order that lives for one minute
This is how the entry kind `at_close` (the default kind of P3-02b) is run: the ticket of doc 07 §2, "at trigger-candle close, limit +0.1 % max", becomes an order placed when the trigger bar has closed, which can first fill in the next minute.

| # | Rule | BUY | SELL |
|---|------|-----|------|
| E0 | **Empty minutes.** A minute with Volume 0 holds no trade. The owner's CSVs have them (27 files sampled on 2026-10-18: 360ONE 740 of 192,591 minutes, PATANJALI 3,022, PIIND 961, DELHIVERY 269; 689 of the 740 are flat, `O = H = L = C`, 51 have a range) and the bar rules keep them (only a negative volume is rejected). In every row of this table such a minute is read like an absent one: the order rests through it, the square-off waits for the next minute that holds a trade, a stop or a target is not tested in it, and it is not counted in MAE or MFE. Counted: `ZeroVolumeMinutes` | same | same |
| E1 | **Fill minute.** The order is placed at the decision instant. The fill minute is the first minute after the candidate's own that holds a trade (E0) and starts at or after the decision instant. In this order: none, or on another India date, or starting more than `SimulationEngine.MaxFillDelayMinutes` (5, P3-03) after the decision instant: no fill, `NoFillBar`; starting at or after `Q`: no fill, `AfterSquareOff`. A start later than the decision instant means absent or empty minutes the order rested through (`EntriesAfterMissingMinutes`) | same | same |
| E2 | **Limit.** `p = FillRules.EntryLimitPercent / 100` (0.1 %, doc 07 §2 "limit +0.1 % max"). Rounded so that it never exceeds the 0.1 %; computed in `decimal`; rounded in both fill modes (it is an order price) | `limit = tickDown(trigger close × (1 + p))` | `limit = tickUp(trigger close × (1 − p))` |
| E3 | **At open.** The market opens within the limit: the order fills at the open (`AtOpen`) | `open ≤ limit` → entry = `open` | `open ≥ limit` → entry = `open` |
| E4 | **At limit.** The market opens beyond the limit but trades back to it during the minute: the order fills at the limit (`AtLimit`). Touching the limit is a fill | `open > limit` and `low ≤ limit` → entry = `limit` | `open < limit` and `high ≥ limit` → entry = `limit` |
| E5 | **Missed.** Otherwise no fill, `LimitNotReached`. The order ends with the minute: a later minute that would have been within the limit does not fill it (open question 2) | otherwise | otherwise |
| E6 | **Through the stop.** An entry at or beyond the stop is no trade, `EntryThroughStop` (P3-03), counted. It guarantees `risk > 0`, not a sensible risk: a stop within four ticks of the entry is counted (`RiskUnderFourTicks`), because one stop exit pays three ticks and more (S2) | `entry ≤ stop` | `entry ≥ stop` |
| E7 | **A miss spends the ticket.** Nothing to build in the walker: only the first trigger of a date is a candidate (P3-03a, D2), so a miss, like a stop-out or a fill through the stop, does not arm the pattern again (open question 2); the test is P3-03a's, with the F11 day (*Handed to P3-03a*) | same | same |

After a fill: `risk = abs(entry − stop)` per share, from the **exact** entry; it is the unit of every R below. For `FIXED_RR k`, `target = entry + k × risk` (BUY) or `entry − k × risk` (SELL). The stop and the target are used **unrounded**: the grid never changes a decision (S4, T4). A policy without a target (`SESSION_EXIT` alone) has no X3 and X4.

### Exit: the first row that applies, minute by minute
The walker visits the minutes of the entry's India date in order, starting with the fill minute, and passes over a minute without a trade (E0); for each other minute the first row that applies decides, and the trade ends there. Order of the rows: X1, then X5 for the fill minute of an `AtLimit` entry, then X2, X3, X4; X6 when the minutes run out.

| # | Rule | BUY | SELL |
|---|------|-----|------|
| X1 | **Square-off minute** = the first minute that holds a trade (E0) and starts at or after `Q` (only after the fill minute). The position is closed at its open and the minute's range is not used. If the open is beyond the stop: exit at the open, reason `Stop` (the stop order was resting before the square-off order existed). Else if the open is beyond the target: exit at the **target**, reason `Target`. Else exit at the open, reason `SquareOff`. After absent or empty minutes the next minute that holds a trade decides. P3-03 (W14) exits at the open as `SquareOff` in all three cases | stop if `open ≤ stop`; target if `open ≥ target` | stop if `open ≥ stop`; target if `open ≤ target` |
| X2 | **Stop.** A minute before `Q` that reaches the stop exits at the stop, or at the open when the open is already beyond it (a gap: P3-03 D4, doc 08 §5, doc 19 §1, doc 16 `gap_fill: at_open`). Absent minutes behave the same: the first minute after them is compared with the stop at its open. Reason `Stop` | `low ≤ stop` → exit = `min(stop, open)` | `high ≥ stop` → exit = `max(stop, open)` |
| X3 | **Target.** A minute that reaches the target exits at the target, never at a better price: a gap through the target gives no improvement (P3-03 D4). Reason `Target` | `high ≥ target` → exit = `target` | `low ≤ target` → exit = `target` |
| X4 | **One minute that reaches both the stop and the target is a stop**: the path inside a minute is unknowable and doc 19 says to assume the worse outcome. **Exception:** if the open is already beyond the target, the first print was beyond it and the target comes first (P3-03 W13 takes the stop). The minute is *ambiguous* (counted, `AmbiguousMinutes`) when both are reached and the open lies strictly between them | `low ≤ stop` and `high ≥ target` → stop, unless `open ≥ target` → target | `high ≥ stop` and `low ≤ target` → stop, unless `open ≤ target` → target |
| X5 | **The fill minute.** The stop is checked in the fill minute (the entry is at its open, or later in it). After an `AtLimit` entry only the stop is checked: the minute's high (SELL: low) may have come before the fill, so it is used neither for the target nor for MFE; the low (SELL: high) can only have come after the fill and is used for the stop and for MAE | same | same |
| X6 | **No minute that holds a trade at or after `Q` on the date** (the stored data ends, a day's data ends early, a special session): exit at the close of the last minute of the date that holds a trade, reason `EndOfData` (P3-03 D4), counted, treated as a market-type fill. Its R is measured at an arbitrary last price, so it is reported apart (P3-03a: `OpenAtEnd`), never among the trades that statistics count | same | same |

### Slippage
| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| S1 | **Which fills slip.** Market-type fills: the entry `AtOpen`, stop exits, square-off exits (`SquareOff`), `EndOfData` exits. **A resting limit does not slip:** the entry `AtLimit` and the target exit. A limit order fills at its limit or better; the model gives no better (E4, X3) and, by this rule, takes nothing away either (decision 1) |
| S2 | **Amount** `a = (Ticks + extra) × tick + BarRangeFrac × (high − low)` of the minute the fill happens in; `extra = StopExitExtraTicks` for a stop exit (including the stop of X1), else 0. A flat minute (`high = low`) slips by the ticks only |
| S3 | **Direction and rounding, always against the trade.** Entry BUY: `min(tickUp(entry + a), limit)`; entry SELL: `max(tickDown(entry − a), limit)` (the entry never passes the limit, doc 07 §2). Exit of a long: `tickDown(exit − a)`; exit of a short: `tickUp(exit + a)`. Target exit: `tickDown(target)` for a long, `tickUp(target)` for a short (the grid only, no `a`). Entry `AtLimit`: the limit, which is already on the grid |
| S4 | **Decisions use the exact prices.** Which minute fills, which row exits and the reason never depend on slippage or on the grid. When all three numbers are zero (`SlippageConfig.None`) no fill price is moved or rounded and the fills are `Exact`; otherwise they are `Modelled`. `Exact` means the prices of this table: no slippage and no grid for fills |

### Tick size
| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| T1 | `tick` comes from the instrument master, which gives it in **paise** (see *The unit of the master tick*: the committed rows 1.0000, 5.0000 and 10.0000 are ₹0.01, 0.05 and 0.10). `TickSpec.FromMasterPaise(decimal? paise)` in `Domain` divides by 100 and is the only place that does. A null value gives `0.05`, the NSE equity tick, with `FromFallback = true`; the trade carries `TickFallback` (the run record that lists the instruments is P3-04's) |
| T2 | A tick must satisfy `0 < tick ≤ 1` rupee after the conversion. Anything else is refused (`simulation.tick_size_invalid`) and **not** replaced by the fallback. This range is a net, not the guard of the unit: an unconverted `1.0000` (one paisa) passes it, which is why the conversion is one named function with its own tests |
| T3 | All tick arithmetic is in `decimal`; a `double` price is converted rounded to 8 decimals. Plain `double` arithmetic gets the grid wrong: `floor(100.00 × 1.001 / 0.05) × 0.05` is 100.05 (100.05000000000001), not 100.10, and `floor(2950.00 × 1.001 / 0.05) × 0.05` is 2,952.90, not 2,952.95. Measured while drafting (Python, `double`): of the 199,999 multiples of 0.05 up to 9,999.95, 26,893 fail the round trip `floor(x / 0.05) × 0.05 == x` or the same with `ceil` |
| T4 | The tick is used for the entry limit in **both** fill modes (it is an order price) and for the fill prices in `Modelled` mode only. It never touches the stop and target levels or any decision |
| T5 | **Levels and comparisons are decimal too.** `risk`, `target`, every R, MAE and MFE, and every comparison of a bar price with the limit, the stop or the target are computed in `decimal` from doubles rounded to 8 decimals (T3); only the `SimTrade` fields are doubles. The grid never rounds a level (T4), but equality is exact: a high equal to the decimal target is a touch. In `double` it is not: for entry 100.00 to 100.95 and stop 95.00 to 99.95 on the 0.05 grid and k in {1.5, 2, 2.5, 3}, the target `entry + k × abs(entry − stop)` is above the decimal target in 2,640 of 8,000 combinations (entry 100.00, stop 98.85, k 2: 102.30000000000001, not 102.30; H21), and a SELL target is below it in a similar number (entry 199.85, stop 200.90, k 2: 197.74999999999997, not 197.75; H22), so a minute that touches the target exactly would be no touch |

### R, excursions, counters
- `RExact = d × (exit − entry) / risk` from the exact prices (`d` = +1 BUY, −1 SELL). It is the `R` of P3-03 (renamed) and does not depend on the slippage configuration or the tick.
- `RFilled = d × (exitFilled − entryFilled) / risk` with **the same `risk`**: the unit of R is the exact ticket, so slippage changes the numerator only. `SlippageR = RFilled − RExact`, never positive. With `SlippageConfig.None`: `RFilled = RExact` and `SlippageR = 0`.
- **MAE and MFE** are those of P3-03 D5 (in R, from the exact entry, never below 0, not affected by slippage, `−MAE ≤ RExact ≤ MFE`: after a `Stop` the exit's own distance `abs(exit − entry) / risk` counts as adverse, 1.0 at the stop level and more after a gap; after a `Target` MFE is `k`; the open of a `SquareOff` minute counts, its range does not), with these additions of the table: a minute whose open is already beyond the level it exits at (a gapped stop or target, X1, the exception of X4) ends the trade at that first print and contributes no range, so after such a `Target` only MFE = `k` counts and no adverse side (H7 has MAE 0.2, not 1.1); X5 leaves the high of an `AtLimit` fill minute out of MFE; a minute without a trade (E0) counts nowhere.
- **Counters.** P3-03's `NoTradeReason` gains `LimitNotReached`; `WalkOutcome` gains `AmbiguousMinute`, `LateSquareOff` and `RiskUnderFourTicks` (flags) and `ZeroVolumeMinutes`; P3-03's `WalkCounters` gain `LimitNotReached`, `EntriesAtOpen`, `EntriesAtLimit`, `AmbiguousMinutes`, `LateSquareOffs` (a `SquareOff` at a minute after `Q`), `ZeroVolumeMinutes` (minutes with Volume 0 passed over between the decision instant and the exit, or the deciding minute of a no-trade) and `RiskUnderFourTicks` (trades with `risk < 4 × tick`). The identities of P3-03 D6 become `Candidates = Trades + NoFillBar + AfterSquareOff + EntryThroughStop + LimitNotReached` and `Trades = EntriesAtOpen + EntriesAtLimit`; `BarsExamined` of `LimitNotReached` is 1. `StopGapExits` and `TargetGapExits` (an exit whose minute opens strictly beyond the stop / the target) keep their meaning; X1 can now count them. `NoFillBar`, `AfterSquareOff`, `EntryThroughStop`, `EntriesAfterMissingMinutes` and `BarsExamined` are P3-03's.

### Limits that stay (stated, not modelled: `FillLimits`)
In this order: `auction_open` (the first 1-minute open of the CSVs can be the auction price, doc 05 §3 and §4; no P3 strategy fills in the 09:15 minute because `first_entry` is 09:30, and a repaired opening bar has a widened range, so its slippage is larger, never smaller) · `circuit_locks` (doc 19 says no fills at locked circuits; there is no price-band data) · `touch_is_fill` (a resting limit fills when the price touches it; paying the base slippage on targets would take about 0.1 R off every winner) · `no_queue_or_impact` (no queue, partial fill, market impact or liquidity cap) · `grid_approximate` (the tick is today's tick from the master; old prices can lie on a finer grid or off the grid, so rounding is approximate: in a read-only sample of 27 of the 214 CSV files, every 8th in file-name order, on 2026-10-18, every price has at most two decimals and 4 files have minutes with a price off the 0.05 grid: TATASTEEL 97.6 % of its minutes, VMM 96.9 %, ADANIPOWER 26.4 %, DELHIVERY 3.5 %; the cause was not checked) · `minute_path` (inside one minute only the open, high, low and close are known: stop first) · `hole_is_no_trade` (an absent minute and a minute with Volume 0 are read as a minute without a trade; the order rests through it, up to the 5-minute bound of P3-03) · `one_minute_entry` (the entry gets one minute; a miss is never retried) · `no_latency` (the order is live at the instant the trigger bar closes and fills at the first print of the next minute; decision and network latency are zero).

### Decisions this draft takes (decided on research when it was drafted, 2026-10-18, outside the owner's delegation of 2026-10-16/17; the owner approves them with the spec and may revert them)
1. A resting limit never slips (S1). It is a modelling convention, not a question: a run can measure it (`touch_is_fill` is listed in every run, and the issues log asks to measure the touch-versus-trade-through effect on real data).
2. An open already beyond the target wins over the stop (X4 exception) and a square-off minute that opens beyond a level fills that level (X1): the first print decides, so this is not a second ambiguity rule. Part of the fill convention (P3-03 question 1, letters b and c).
3. Absent and empty minutes before the entry do not cancel the order (E1), up to P3-03's bound of 5 minutes.
4. A miss spends the day's ticket (E7). Open question 2.
5. No minute that holds a trade at or after `Q`: exit at the last close of the date, `EndOfData`, reported apart (X6, as P3-03).
6. The unit of R is the exact ticket (`risk` from the exact entry) and `RFilled` uses the same unit, so `SlippageR ≤ 0` is exact, not approximate.
7. The tick: the master gives paise and is converted once; the fallback is 0.05 with a flag when the master has none; a tick outside (0, 1] rupee is refused.
8. A minute with Volume 0 holds no trade (E0). The alternative, a fill at the carried-forward price, would be a tenth `FillLimits` code, `carried_minute_is_a_fill`, and one more test.
9. The order has no latency (`no_latency`).

**Slippage and tick**
11. **`SlippageConfig` and `FillRules`.** The types are sealed, have get-only members and a private constructor: `Create` is the only way to build one, and `with` cannot change a member. Given `SlippageConfig.Default`, then it is `Ticks` 1, `BarRangeFrac` 0.05, `StopExitExtraTicks` 2, `GapFill` `AtOpen` (doc 16); `None` is 0, 0, 0 with `IsNone` true. `SlippageConfig.Create` refuses NaN, an infinity, a negative number, a `BarRangeFrac` above 1, ticks or extra ticks above 100 and any gap rule other than `AtOpen` (an undefined enum value), each with `simulation.slippage_invalid` naming the field; `FillRules.Create` refuses NaN, an infinity, a limit percent below 0 or above 5 and a null slippage with `simulation.fill_rules_invalid`. `FillRules.Default.ToCanonicalJson()` is exactly `{"entry_limit_percent":0.1,"slippage":{"ticks":1,"bar_range_frac":0.05,"stop_exit_extra_ticks":2,"gap_fill":"at_open"}}` (numbers in the shortest round-trip form, the slippage keys of doc 16), under any culture (the suite also runs under `de-DE` and `tr-TR`). `TradeWalker.Walk` returns `simulation.fill_rules_invalid` for a `WalkRules` whose `Fills` is null and `simulation.tick_size_invalid` for one whose `Tick` is null (a test builds both with `default(WalkRules)`), so a value that bypassed `Create` cannot run.

Given 0, −5 and 101 (₹1.01), then `simulation.tick_size_invalid`, and no walk; `TickSpec.FromRupees` accepts 0.05 and refuses 0 and 1.01. `TickSpec` is a sealed record with a private constructor: no value with a zero or negative size exists.
13. **Grid arithmetic and exact comparisons.** Given 10,000 grid prices `i × 0.05` (and `i × 0.01`, `i × 0.10`) built in `double`, then `tickDown` and `tickUp` return each unchanged; given an off-grid price, `tickDown` is the grid price at or below and `tickUp` the one at or above, and the two differ by exactly one tick. **The record and compatibility**
16. **`SimTrade` schema 2.** `SimTrade.CurrentSchemaVersion` is 2 and the positional properties are exactly P3-03's 24 in their order with #19 renamed `RExact`, followed by #25 `RFilled`, #26 `SlippageR`, #27 `EntryFilledPrice`, #28 `ExitFilledPrice` (all `double`), #29 `EntryFill` (`EntryFill`: `AtOpen`, `AtLimit`) and #30 `TickFallback` (`bool`). Meanings: #13 `EntryPrice` is the exact entry (the open of the fill minute, or the limit for `AtLimit`); #14 `StopPrice` and #15 `TargetPrice` are the unrounded levels; #17 `ExitPrice` is the exact exit of the table (`min(stop, open)` for a long stop and `max` for a short one, the target for a target, the open at the square-off, the last close for `EndOfData`); #27 and #28 are the same prices after S2 and S3 (equal to #13 and #17 when the fills are `Exact`). `FillBasis` is `Exact, Modelled`; `Fills` is `Modelled` exactly when the rules' slippage is not `None`; `SlippageR = RFilled − RExact` and is ≤ 0; for every `Target` exit `RExact` equals `k` and for every `Stop` exit it is ≤ −1, equal to −1 exactly when the exit is at the stop level (1e-9). `SimulationEngine.Version` (P3-03) is raised because the rules changed, and P3-03's schema test and golden file are regenerated with the ADR.
17. **P3-03 compatibility.** Given P3-03's F-days that its walker runs by hand-made candidate (F1 to F7, F9, F12 to F17 and F17b) and its walker rows W1 to W12 and W9b, run with `FillRules` of 0.1 % and `SlippageConfig.None` (each candidate's trigger close set to the open of its fill minute, so that the limit does not bind), then every trade and every no-trade equals P3-03's table: prices, R, MAE, MFE, reasons and counters (the new counters are additions). **W13 and W14 differ, and only they:** W13 (the minute opens at 106.00, beyond the target 105.50, and reaches the stop) is `Target` 105.50, R +2.0, MFE 2.0, `TargetGapExits` 1 (P3-03: `Stop` 98.00, R −1.0); W14 on the stop side (the 15:15 minute opens at 97.50) is `Stop` at 97.50, R −1.2, MAE 1.2, `StopGapExits` 1 (P3-03: `SquareOff` at 97.50, the same R, no counter), and on the target side (opens at 106.00) is `Target` 105.50, R +2.0 (P3-03: `SquareOff` at 106.00, R +2.2). Rules that no row of P3-03 exercises (E0, the high of an `AtLimit` fill minute left out of MFE, a gapped target with no adverse range) are listed in the ADR with their reason. Any other difference found is a defect of this slice or of P3-03 and is listed in the ADR with both numbers.

**Properties** (seeded generator in the test project: see *Test plan*)
18. **Slippage never improves a fill, never changes a decision, and is monotone.** For 500 seeded days, each walked BUY and SELL with `None`, `Default` and a ladder in which each parameter in turn takes four increasing values with the others at `Default` (`Ticks` 0, 1, 2, 3; `BarRangeFrac` 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2; `StopExitExtraTicks` 0, 2, 4, 5): the set of trades, the entry and exit minutes, the reasons and the exact prices are identical in every configuration; `EntryFilledPrice` is never below `EntryPrice` for a BUY and never above it for a SELL, `ExitFilledPrice` is never above `ExitPrice` for a long exit and never below it for a short exit; so `RFilled ≤ RExact`; and raising one parameter never raises `RFilled`.
19. **Fill timing and range** (P3-03's AC9, extended). For the same 500 days: every fill minute holds a trade, starts at or after the decision instant, at most 5 minutes after it and before `Q`; every exit minute is on the entry's date, holds a trade and is not before the fill minute; every `SquareOff` is at the first minute that holds a trade at or after `Q` (checked by the test's own scan of the minutes); every **exact** fill price lies in its minute's [low, high] or is that order's own level (a target gapped through lies outside the minute by design); in `Modelled` mode every fill price is a multiple of `tick` and `abs(filled − exact) ≤ a + tick` (with `a` = 0 for a resting limit); `−MAE ≤ RExact ≤ MFE`.
20. **A closed trade does not depend on the future, and the order inside an ambiguous minute never improves a trade.** (a) For every ambiguous minute of the 500 days, replace it by the two orders of its path (high first: the half minutes `o,h,o,h` and `h,h,l,c`; low first: `o,o,l,l` and `l,h,l,c`; at +0 s and +30 s) and walk both: the unsplit result equals the worse of the two in `RExact` and in the reason. (b) P3-03's AC8 property (for a random cut `k`, every outcome settled at an index at most `k` is equal, as rendered by the test formatter, when the minutes after `k` are removed and when they are replaced by extreme values) is run again with the rules of this slice over the 500 days, with `FillRules.Default` and with `None`, so that it covers the limit, the empty minutes, the `AtLimit` rows, X1 and X4; it is the walker half of the look-ahead property that P3-08 extends to the whole loop. (c) **Negative controls** are test-only wrappers around the real walker, and the property is applied to the wrapped function `f(X) = TradeWalker.Walk(candidate, doctor(X), rules)`. (i) Peek control: `doctor` sets each minute's low (SELL: high) to the minimum (maximum) of itself and the next minute; (b) compares `f(X)` with `f(truncate(X))`, the cut made after the exit minute (or the deciding minute) that `f(X)` reports, and must report a difference on at least 10 seeds, while the plain walker reports none on the same `X`. (ii) Signal-minute control: the wrapper moves the candidate one minute earlier (`DecisionBarIndex` − 1 and `DecisionEpochSeconds` set to that earlier minute's start plus 60 s, so that P3-03's W12 does not fire) and the check of criterion 19 uses the true decision instant of the series; it must fail on at least 10 seeds.
21. **The properties are not vacuous, and the walk is deterministic.** Over the 500 days each of `EntriesAtOpen`, `EntriesAtLimit`, `EntriesAfterMissingMinutes`, `LimitNotReached`, `NoFillBar`, `AfterSquareOff`, `EntryThroughStop`, `StopGapExits`, `TargetGapExits`, `AmbiguousMinutes`, `LateSquareOffs`, `ZeroVolumeMinutes`, `RiskUnderFourTicks` and the exits by reason `EndOfData` is at least 10, and both sides and the three ticks (0.01, 0.05, 0.10) occur; a generator that stops producing a case fails the test. Two walks of the same input, one on a copy of the minutes, render identical text in a plain formatter (invariant culture, round-trip doubles).

## Non-functional
- **Performance:** the walk stays O(minutes of the position) with no allocation per minute. Since T5 the comparisons are decimal comparisons of prices converted from `double`, which costs more per minute than P3-03's `double` comparisons; the explicit benchmark prints trades and minutes per second before and after the rules (not in `check.sh`; no asserted number, the baseline is P3-03's `WalkBenchmarks`). The tick arithmetic of the grid runs at fills only (at most four per trade: the limit, the two fill prices and the target).
- **Determinism:** no clock, `Random` or culture dependence; two walks give identical text (criterion 21). Tick arithmetic and comparisons are exact in `decimal`.
- **Security / privacy:** tests use synthetic series only; nothing is read from `data/`; no secret is involved.
- **Observability:** the new counters in `WalkCounters` and the flags of `WalkOutcome`; `Fills`, `EntryFill`, `TickFallback` and `SlippageR` on every trade; no logging in the pure library (the run record of P3-04 stores `FillRules` and the counters).

## Test plan
Unit only (`XDrishti.Simulation.Tests`, xUnit v3 and Shouldly as elsewhere, no mocks), plus the Architecture tests. Families: (1) the rule table as data: B1 to B9, H1 to H25 and N1 to N5 as one theory each, hand-calculated above and written before the code, through `TradeWalker` with hand-made candidates and P3-03's builder that turns table rows into bar lists; (2) `TickGrid` and the exact comparisons (criteria 1 and 13); (3) `SlippageConfig`, `FillRules` and `TickSpec` validation, `default(WalkRules)` and the canonical JSON snapshot under three cultures; (4) the seeded properties (criteria 18 to 21); (5) the negative controls (criterion 20c); (6) `FillLimits` and the labels; (7) allocation; (8) architecture; (9) the compatibility with P3-03's F-days and W-rows (criterion 17).

**The seeded generator** lives in the test project (`System.Random` is forbidden in the engine projects). Seed = the day's index. A regular session of 375 minutes from a random walk with a per-minute move up to 0.15 %; a price log-uniform from 20 to 5,000; a tick from {0.01, 0.05, 0.10}; about 15 % of the days on a 0.01 grid whatever the tick (as 4 of the 27 sampled CSV files have minutes off the 0.05 grid); 0 to 15 % of the minutes removed in runs of 1 to 6; 0 to 3 gaps of 0.3 % to 5 % between consecutive existing minutes; 5 % flat minutes (`open = high = low = close`); 2 % spike minutes (a range of 1 % to 6 %); 1 % of the minutes with Volume 0; 1 to 3 candidates per side at decision instants (the start of an existing minute plus 60 s) between 09:30 and 14:30, 10 % of them within 3 minutes before `Q`; the stop 0.2 % to 3 % from the trigger close; `FIXED_RR` k from {1.5, 2, 2.5, 3}; `Q` from {14:30, 15:15, 15:29}. **Planted cases** (starting rates, tuned until every counter of criterion 21 reaches at least 30 over the 500 days, asserted at 10): 8 % of the candidates have the fill minute rewritten as a gap of 0.05 to 3 stop distances, up or down, so that the limit is missed, the stop is passed or the target is gapped; 6 % have a minute 5 to 60 minutes after the decision rewritten to open at the entry price and to reach half a risk beyond the target and half a risk beyond the stop (an ambiguous minute); 10 % of the days end before `Q` (at 14:10 or 14:50); 12 % of the days lose the minutes `Q` to `Q` + 1 to 5; 4 % of the days get one more candidate whose decision instant is the start of the last existing minute plus 60 s. A throwaway replica of this generator and of the table, written for this review and not the project's, gave at least 31 of every counter over 500 days; the thresholds are met by the planted cases, not by the random walk alone (without them `AmbiguousMinutes` was 1 and `EntryThroughStop` 3).

**Edge cases to cover:** a flat minute (range 0: slippage by the ticks only); a stop level on and off the grid; a limit equal to the trigger close (0.1 % under one tick); a fill minute that is the 09:15 minute (the repaired opening bar, a widened range); `Q` equal to the last existing minute and `Q` after it (15:29 absent); a decision at the last minute of the day; a hole of exactly 5 and of 6 minutes before the entry; an empty minute at the start, inside the position and at the square-off (H24); a tick of 0.01 and of 0.10; a policy without a target; a trade whose fill minute is also its exit minute (H8); equality everywhere (open equal to the limit, low equal to the limit, high equal to the target, open equal to the stop); the SELL mirror of every BUY row; `default(WalkRules)`; the same input twice.

## Open questions
1. **The fill convention (blocks Approval).** Not a separate question: **[P3-03](P3-03-simulator-walking-skeleton-scan-walk-and-orb.md) open question 1 states the convention once, in full, and the answer to it covers this slice** (the plan question Q1 of the roadmap is the same question). Its letters a to c are what this slice builds beyond P3-03: the entry as a limit order at the trigger close +0.1 %, rounded to the tick so that it never exceeds the 0.1 %, which fills at the open when that is inside the limit, at the limit when the minute trades back to it and is otherwise a counted miss (E2 to E5); the first-print exception of stop first (X4); and the square-off minute that opens beyond a level (X1). Nothing is restated here, so there is one wording to approve. **Recommendation:** yes, as there: a bar that has just closed cannot be filled in, and every result you check against a chart should be one the market could have given; the results will be lower than with a fill at the close.
2. **How long does the entry order live?** As drafted it lives for the minute after the trigger bar (E5, E7): if that minute opens above the +0.1 % limit and does not trade back to it, nothing is entered and the side's ticket for the day is spent (later triggers are counted as `LaterTriggers`). Doc 07 §2 gives a ticket its cancel rules (open above 2,960, stop touched before the trigger), not a one-minute life, and the choice changes the number of trades. The alternative: the order lives until the next evaluation-timeframe bar closes and the ticket is spent only by a fill or a cancel; more trades, more chasing. **Recommendation:** one minute (pessimistic; the `LimitNotReached` counter shows how many setups it drops); revisit it from that counter on real data (ISS-18).
