## Goal
A finished simulation run can be kept in the database, exactly as it was, and read back. The owner runs `scripts/simulate.sh … --store` and the run header (its run key, engine version, schema versions, window, universe, counters, costs and the whole canonical record) and **every trade** (all 33 fields of `SimTrade` plus the baskets it came from) land in `research.sim_runs` and `research.sim_trades` in one transaction. Storing the same run key twice does nothing; a different run under a key already stored is refused and logged as a corruption signal. The files of the export stay the audit copy; the store is what the Lab (P5), the same-entries comparison of P4 and the evidence ledger of P7 query. No endpoint, no screen.

## Definitions (decisions the tests pin)
Names of P3-04, P3-04c and P3-03c are theirs: `SimulationRun`, `RunTrade`, `SimTrade`, `TradeCosting`, `LegCharges`, `CostRecord`, `RunAssembler`, `CanonicalText`, `TradesCsv`, `SimExportJson`.

**D1 — What is stored: the header as columns plus the canonical record text; the trades normalised.** `sim_runs` holds one row per run: the facts a query filters or sorts by as **typed columns**, and the whole record **without its trades** as the canonical text, so that nothing the record knows is lost. `sim_trades` holds one row per trade, every field of `SimTrade` as a typed column. Reasons: (a) the Lab, P4 and P7 ask questions of trades (by side, symbol, date, exit reason, R) across runs; a trade inside a document can only be asked of by reading the document; (b) a run of 214 instruments is about 120,000 to 217,000 trades, and the file `run.json` with them is about 100 to 180 MB (the owner's figures; UNVERIFIED here), too large to hold as one value that every query must parse; (c) the header part (strategy text, universe, instruments with their sessions, notes and counters, the costs record, the fills) is read as a whole or not at all, and has no relational query, so it stays one text; (d) a copy of the trades in both places would double the bytes for no reader. Rejected: one jsonb document per run (not queryable by trade, too large to parse for a page); one jsonb per trade (jsonb below the TOAST threshold of about 2 kB is stored uncompressed and bigger than typed columns, and no index serves a field without an expression index per field).

**D3 — `research.sim_trades` (the trades).** One row per `RunTrade` of `SimulationRun.Trades` (`open_at_end = false`) and of `OpenAtEnd` (`true`: the `EndOfData` trades, apart on purpose because statistics must not count them). Key columns: `run_id bigint` (FK to `sim_runs(id)`, no cascade: nothing is ever deleted by the runtime role), `open_at_end boolean`, `ordinal integer` (the place in its list of the record, 0-based: it carries the record's order, by entry instant, then place in the universe, then Buy before Sell, so the read gives back `trades.csv` in its order), **primary key `(run_id, open_at_end, ordinal)`**, `trade_key varchar(160)` (`SimTrade.Key`, `StrategyId|Symbol|Side|DecisionDate`, computed in the Domain and stored for the joins of P4), **unique `(run_id, trade_key)`**. Then the 33 fields, each a column of its own, in the order of `SimTrade`:
- text and ids: `strategy_id varchar(64)`, `instrument_id uuid`, `symbol varchar(40)`, `entry_snapshot_json text` (null: reserved, always null so far);
- enums by their lower-case names with the existing `EnumConversions.Lowercase` pattern and a `CHECK` on the closed set: `side`, `trigger_timeframe`, `exit_reason`, `fills`, `costs`, `entry_fill`;
- dates and instants: `trade_date date`, `decision_epoch_seconds`, `entry_epoch_seconds`, `exit_epoch_seconds` (`bigint`; epoch seconds as the Domain has them, no conversion on the way in or out);
- the trigger bar flattened: `trigger_bar_epoch bigint`, `trigger_bar_open`, `_high`, `_low`, `_close double precision`, `trigger_bar_volume bigint`; plus `trigger_bar_minutes integer`, `trigger_bar_complete boolean`;
- prices and results, `double precision` (the Domain's type; exact round trip): `entry_price`, `stop_price`, `target_price` (null), `exit_price`, `r_exact`, `mae_r`, `mfe_r`, `r_filled`, `slippage_r`, `entry_filled_price`, `exit_filled_price`;
- `tick_fallback boolean`, `sessions_held integer`, `sessions_unseen integer`;
- `basket_ids uuid[]` (`RunTrade.BasketIds`, empty for an explicit list; not a field of `SimTrade`);
- **the costing, flattened and all-or-nothing**: `cost_segment` (enum, lower-case), `cost_quantity bigint`, `cost_risk_taken numeric`, `cost_entry_turnover numeric`, `cost_exit_turnover numeric`, the eight lines of the entry leg and the eight of the exit leg (`entry_brokerage`, `entry_stt`, `entry_exchange_txn`, `entry_sebi_fee`, `entry_stamp_duty`, `entry_other_charges`, `entry_dp_charge`, `entry_gst`, and the same eight with `exit_`), `cost_total numeric`, `cost_r double precision`, `r_net double precision`. The costing is **24 columns** (5 leading: segment, quantity, risk taken, the two turnovers; 16 charge lines; 3 trailing: total, `cost_r`, `r_net`; the sheet id is not one): one `CHECK ((costs = 'charged') = (cost_quantity IS NOT NULL))` and one `CHECK` that the 24 are all null or all present, both generated from the mapping table so the list cannot drift. `numeric` without a scale, so that a `decimal` round-trips with its value (the Domain's `decimal` equality ignores the trailing zeros). The sheet id of `TradeCosting` is **not** a column: it is the run's `costs_sheet_id`, and the read puts it back (a test pins that every costed row of a run carries the run's sheet id).
Why typed columns for the 16 lines and not a jsonb: the lines are numbers of fixed size (about 6 to 8 bytes each as `numeric`), a jsonb would be larger than the columns it replaces, the sums over them (the cost by line, the cost of one class of trades) are exactly what P7 will ask, and the CHECK keeps the all-or-nothing rule in the database. The price is a wide table (about 65 columns); it is mechanical, generated from one mapping table (D8) and covered by the round trip.
Indexes: the primary key (serves every query of one run, in the record's order, and the stream of a whole run), the unique `(run_id, trade_key)` (serves the same-entries join of P4: two run ids, one key). **No index on symbol, date, exit reason or R**: a run is at most about 217,000 rows, a filter inside one run reads its range of the primary key (about 100 to 150 MB, tens of milliseconds to a few hundred, UNVERIFIED), and an index that no measured query needs costs bytes on every one of the 217,000 rows. A test records the timing of the paged filter on the 200,000-row synthetic run; an index is added by a later migration when a measured query needs one.

**D4 — The canonical record text and the digests.** `record_json` is the run object in the canonical form of `run.json` (the converters of `XDrishti.SimExport/Json`: sorted parameters, canonical numbers, spelled enums) **with `Trades` and `OpenAtEnd` omitted**, written by a new public `SimExportJson.WriteRecord(IBufferWriter<byte>, SimulationRun)` that shares the converters with `Write` (additive: the bytes of `run.json` do not change; the existing golden tests pass untouched). It is stored as **`text`, not `jsonb`**: jsonb does not keep key order or number spelling, so the hash of what was written could no longer be checked; `text` is compressed by TOAST above 2 kB without changing a byte. `record_sha256` is the SHA-256 of its UTF-8 bytes; `trades_sha256` is the SHA-256 of the two canonical trade files, computed as `SHA-256(sha256(trades.csv) ‖ ":" ‖ sha256(open_at_end.csv))` over the lower-case hex of the two figures that `manifest.json` already holds (`TradesCsv` is the one canonical trade text). **The library stays Api-only** (`SimExportArchitectureTests` pins that nothing in `src` but `XDrishti.Api` references `XDrishti.SimExport`): the verb produces the text and the digests and hands them to the handler as `RunRecordText`; the adapter and the handler verify what they can without the library (the hash of the text, the key format, the counts, the schema versions) and never serialise.

**D5 — Size, bytes and the choice of plain tables.** Estimated, **not measured** (UNVERIFIED until the size test of the test plan): one `sim_trades` row is about 390 bytes for a gross trade (heap header 24, the keys and `trade_key` about 50, 7 enum names about 45, 11 doubles 88, 4 epochs and the bar 56, the symbol and ids about 35, the small integers and flags 15, `basket_ids` about 40, the null bitmap and alignment) and about 550 bytes for a costed trade (the 24 costing columns add about 130). A run of 120,000 trades is then 47 MB gross and 66 MB costed; **a run of 217,000 trades is 85 MB gross and 120 MB costed**, plus about 20 MB of index (the primary key about 7 MB, the unique trade key about 12 MB), i.e. **about 105 to 140 MB for the largest run, about the size of its JSON, not smaller**: the gain of the normalised form is the query, not the bytes. `record_json` is the header only (the sessions, notes and counters of 214 instruments; UNVERIFIED, expected 0.5 to 5 MB before compression). Twenty such runs are about 3 GB; the owner's current database holds about 3,205 shares and their bars, which the store does not touch. **Choice: ordinary tables, no hypertable, no compression, now.** A hypertable needs a time column to partition on (`trade_date`), the unique key of a hypertable must contain it (the primary key would become `(run_id, open_at_end, ordinal, trade_date)`), every run would spread its rows over every chunk its years touch, and native compression (its `segmentby` would be `run_id`) is a 5 to 10 times gain only after the chunk is compressed, with restrictions on writing into compressed chunks that depend on the extension version (the image is `timescale/timescaledb:2.30.2-pg18`; the behaviour of inserts into a compressed chunk at that version is UNVERIFIED). That machinery pays when the table passes a few gigabytes. **Trigger to revisit**: `pg_total_relation_size('research.sim_trades')` above 2 GB, or any measured query slower than a second; then one migration makes it a hypertable on `trade_date` with a one-year chunk and compression by `run_id` (the `new-migration` skill, step 5), with the primary key extended by the time column; the row model and the port do not change. Rejected: partitioning by run (a run is the unit of deletion, but a partition per run needs DDL by `xd_owner` for each run, and `xd_app` is DML only).

**D6 — Retention.** Keep every stored run; nothing expires. A run is evidence for a comparison and for P7's ledger; the owner decides what to remove. The storage per run is bounded by D5 (about 140 MB for the largest). The runtime role cannot delete (D13); removal is an owner action as `xd_owner` (`DELETE FROM research.sim_runs WHERE run_key = …` after deleting its trades; documented in doc 17 with the order). No automatic retention is built (nothing to protect yet, and an automatic delete of evidence is the harm to avoid).

**D7 — The port.** `IRunStore` in `XDrishti.Application/Abstractions/Research` (ADR 0039: the engine and the database are reached from Infrastructure, never from Application):
- `StoreAsync(RunToStore, ct)` gives `Result<StoreOutcome>`; `StoreOutcome` is `Stored(id)` or `AlreadyStored(id)`;
- `FindAsync(runKey, ct)` gives `StoredRunHeader?` (the typed columns of D2 and the record text length, not the text);
- `ReadRecordTextAsync(runKey, ct)` gives the stored `record_json` (for regenerating `run.json`, and for the round trip);
- `ListAsync(RunListFilter, PageRequest, ct)` gives a page of headers, newest first, by cursor `(created_at, id)`; the filter is closed: strategy id, engine version, costed or gross, created from/to;
- `StreamTradesAsync(runKey, openAtEnd, ct)` gives `IAsyncEnumerable<RunTrade>` in the record's order (the whole run without holding it: the writer of `trades.csv` takes a list today, so the equality test materialises it; the stream is for the consumers that aggregate);
- `PageTradesAsync(runKey, TradePageFilter, PageRequest, ct)` gives a page of `RunTrade` by **keyset** on `(open_at_end, ordinal)`; the filter is closed: side, symbol, exit reason, trade date from/to, `open_at_end`; a page is at most 1,000 rows. No offset paging, no free-text, no sort other than the record's order (another sort is the consumer's slice).
`RunToStore(SimulationRun Run, RunRecordText Record, string Actor)`; `RunRecordText(string Json, string RecordSha256, string TradesSha256)` (a value type that checks its two figures are 64 lower-case hex characters). No type of the port names the engine, Npgsql or EF. `RunStore` is `internal sealed`, registered scoped in `Infrastructure/DependencyInjection.cs`, so that the handlers resolve under `ValidateOnBuild`.

**D8 — The write path: validation, one transaction, binary COPY, idempotency.**
- **Validation before the database** (`StoreRunHandler`, errors `store.run_invalid`, a field error each): the run is not null; `RunKey` is 64 lower-case hex; `SchemaVersion` equals `SimulationRun.CurrentSchemaVersion` and every trade's `SchemaVersion` equals `SimTrade.CurrentSchemaVersion` (a run of another schema is refused: its columns would not fit, and a new schema is a new migration and an ADR); the record text's SHA-256, recomputed from its bytes, equals `RecordSha256`; the counts: `Trades.Count`, `OpenAtEnd.Count`; the trade keys are unique across both lists; a costed trade has a `Costing` whose `SheetId` is the run's `Costs.SheetId`, and a gross run has no costed trade; **a run costed by a `test-` sheet is refused** (a synthetic sheet is never stored, ADR 0044, and a stored run of it would be mistaken for evidence).
- **One transaction** on one connection: `INSERT INTO research.sim_runs … ON CONFLICT (run_key) DO NOTHING RETURNING id`; if a row is returned, `COPY research.sim_trades (…) FROM STDIN (FORMAT BINARY)` of the trades in record order (a mapping table of about 65 columns written once and used by the copy and the reads, so that the order of the two cannot drift), then **a count check inside the transaction** (`SELECT count(*) …` equals `trade_count + open_at_end_count`, else the transaction rolls back with `store.write_failed`), then commit. The copy streams from the list the record already holds; no second copy of the trades is built.
- **Idempotent by run key**: when `ON CONFLICT` returns no row, the adapter reads the stored `record_sha256`, `trades_sha256`, `trade_count`, `open_at_end_count`, `engine_version` and the schema versions of that key. All equal: `AlreadyStored` (a no-op: no write, no error, exit 0 of the verb with the line `already stored`). **Anything different** under the same key is `store.run_conflict` (kind: conflict): the same inputs gave another record, which means a nondeterministic engine, a changed engine under an unchanged version string or a damaged row. Nothing is written, the event 4417 is logged at error with the key and the four figures that differ (never a trade), and the verb exits 3 with a line that says so. Two concurrent stores of one key are safe: the second `INSERT` waits on the unique index, then takes the same path.
- **The actor and instant**: `created_by` is `cli:simulate`; `created_at` is `TimeProvider.GetUtcNow()` read in the handler and passed down.
- **Cancellation** rolls back; nothing is left half written; the unique key never holds a header without its trades (one transaction).

**D9 — The read path.** `FindAsync`, `ListAsync` and `ReadRecordTextAsync` are EF Core projections with `AsNoTracking` over two `internal` persistence entity classes (`SimRunRow`, `SimTradeRow`) in `Infrastructure/Research` and their `Configuration` classes in `Infrastructure/Persistence/Configurations` (the entities exist for the model and the migration; the Domain's records are mapped to and from them by the same mapping table as D8, no EF type escapes). `StreamTradesAsync` and `PageTradesAsync` read with a plain Npgsql reader (a 217,000-row run through the change tracker is the cost to avoid). Reads take no lock beyond the snapshot, never write, and return the rows exactly as stored: the order is the record's, the enums are parsed back by name (an unknown name fails loudly, `store.row_unreadable`), `double` and `decimal` columns round-trip bit for bit and value for value.

**D10 — Round trip = equal to the record.** Storing a run and reading it back gives, for every trade, a `RunTrade` **equal by value** to the one in memory (`SimTrade` and `TradeCosting` are records; the lists compare element by element in the test), and the stored `record_json` equal byte for byte to `SimExportJson.WriteRecord` of the in-memory run; `TradesCsv.Write` of the read-back trades is byte-equal to the one of the in-memory trades. The test lives in `XDrishti.Api.IntegrationTests` (which can use the library through the Api project reference), not in `Infrastructure.Tests`, because the library is Api-only.

**D11 — The Lab's, P4's and P7's queries (what the shapes serve, nothing more is built).** *Lab list and compare*: `ListAsync` by strategy and engine version, newest first; `FindAsync` by key. *P4, same entries across exit policies*: two runs differ by exit policy and therefore by run key, but the same candidate has the same `trade_key`; the join `a.trade_key = b.trade_key` over two `run_id`s is served by the unique `(run_id, trade_key)`; **the join itself is P4's** and not built here. *P5, the trade list of a run*: `PageTradesAsync` with its closed filter. *P7, the evidence ledger*: per-run aggregates are computed by P7's own query over the typed columns, always with `open_at_end = false` (the rule "statistics must not count the open trades" is why the flag is a column and part of the key).

**D12 — Who writes: the `simulate` verb with `--store`; the Lab later.** `scripts/simulate.sh … --store` (an optional flag in the verb's table-driven parser, default off). Order inside the verb: run → write the export files (the atomic folder writer; the owner's audit copy) → if `--store`, build `RunRecordText` (the record text and the two digests, from the same bytes that were written) → `StoreRunHandler` → print one line (`stored: run <first 12 of the key>, <n> trades` or `already stored`). Files first, because they are the artefact the owner has asked for today and a database failure must not lose them; the store second, and its failure leaves the files in place. Exit codes extend P3-04b2's: **0** stored or already stored; **1** refused by validation (`store.run_invalid`), files kept; **3** a conflict (`store.run_conflict`) or an unexpected database failure, files kept, message names the code. The flag does not change the files, the labels or the run key. The Lab (P5) will call the same `StoreRunHandler`; there is no second writer, and no background job writes to the store.

**D13 — Privileges and ownership.** DDL is run by `xd_owner` through the one-shot `xd-migrate` service on the next `deploy/xd-up.sh` (never at API start). The migration calls `GrantSchemaUsageToAppRole(Schemas.Research)` after the `CREATE TABLE`s (which gives `xd_app` `USAGE` on the schema and DML on the tables) and then `MakeAppendOnlyForAppRole("research", "sim_runs")` and the same for `sim_trades` (`REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE`), the order the helpers document. The default privileges set at database initialisation (`deploy/db/init/10-roles.sh`) give `xd_app` DML on every table `xd_owner` creates later; the explicit `REVOKE` after each `CREATE TABLE` is what makes these two append-only, and the grants test pins it. The sequence behind `sim_runs.id` is used by `xd_app` through the default `USAGE, SELECT` on sequences. No `SECURITY DEFINER` function, no new role.

**D14 — Reversibility.** `Up` creates the schema if absent, the two tables, the constraints and indexes, and the grants; `Down` drops `sim_trades`, then `sim_runs`, then the schema `research` **only if it holds no other object** (`DROP SCHEMA research` without `CASCADE`, so a later table makes it fail loudly rather than be dropped). `Down` destroys stored runs; that is stated in the migration comment and is acceptable because every run can be re-run from the stored bars and the run key says whether the result is the same. A migration already applied or committed is never edited (the `new-migration` skill).

**D15 — Log events.** Four, ids **4415 to 4418** (the next free after 4414; `LogEventIdTests` pins the uniqueness and the range): 4415 `store.started` (run key first 12, trade count, open-at-end count), 4416 `store.completed` (the same, the milliseconds of the copy and of the whole transaction, and `stored` or `already stored`), 4417 `store.conflict` at error (the key, the names of the figures that differ), 4418 `store.failed` at error (the code, no trade, no record text). The record text, the strategy definition and the sheet are never logged.

## Acceptance criteria
Numbered, each testable. The handler is tested in `XDrishti.Application.Tests` with a fake `IRunStore`; the adapter, the migration, the grants and the round trip in `XDrishti.Api.IntegrationTests` (Testcontainers; the run comes from the seeded fixtures of `tests/Shared`, **built by the real `RunAssembler` over a 20-instrument seeded universe, not a golden file written by the store**); the verb flag in `XDrishti.Api.IntegrationTests`; the library addition in `XDrishti.SimExport.Tests`; the rules in `XDrishti.Architecture.Tests`. Nothing is read from `data/`.
1. **The migration applies on a clean database.** All migrations apply on a fresh TimescaleDB; the schema `research` and the two tables exist with the columns, types, `CHECK`s, the primary keys, the foreign key and the indexes of D2 and D3 (read back from `information_schema` and `pg_indexes`, compared with the table of D2/D3 as the test's own list); `db.Database.HasPendingModelChanges()` is false; the migration test follows `WidenAppSettingValueMigrationTests`: a scratch database built up to the migration before this one (`20261018223826_WidenAppSettingValue`), migrated, with a row of `platform.app_settings` unchanged before and after.
2. **`Down` is reversible and honest.** Migrating down to `WidenAppSettingValue` removes both tables and the schema; with an extra table in `research` the `Down` fails and removes nothing of it; up again is clean.
3. **Round trip equals the in-memory run.** A run of the 20-instrument seeded universe (both a gross run and a run costed by an ordinary invented sheet `fixture-a`, not a `test-` one) stored and read back: every trade of `Trades` and of `OpenAtEnd` is equal by value to the one in memory (all 33 fields and `BasketIds`, doubles bit for bit, decimals by value), the counts equal `trade_count` and `open_at_end_count`, the order is the record's, `record_json` equals `SimExportJson.WriteRecord(run)` byte for byte and hashes to `record_sha256`, the header columns equal the figures of the record (`RunKey`, engine `sim-5`, schemas 5 and 4, window, strategy, universe label, counters, costs sheet and rates hash, fills), and **the run key of the read-back header is the key the assembler gave** (unchanged by storing).
4. **`trades.csv` from the store equals the one from memory.** `TradesCsv.Write` of the trades read back from the store (`StreamTradesAsync`, materialised) is byte-identical to `TradesCsv.Write` of the in-memory run, and so is `open_at_end.csv`; and the pair's digest equals `trades_sha256`. This is the roadmap row's check.
5. **Idempotent by run key.** Storing the same run twice returns `Stored` then `AlreadyStored`; the row counts of both tables are unchanged by the second call; `created_at` and `created_by` of the first are kept; two concurrent stores of one key give one `Stored`, one `AlreadyStored`, never an error and never a doubled trade row.
6. **A different record under the same key is a corruption signal.** The same run key with a changed `record_json` (and so a changed hash), or a changed `trades_sha256`, or a changed trade count, or another engine-version string, gives `store.run_conflict`; nothing is written or changed (counts and hashes as before); event 4417 is logged once at error; no trade value and no record text is in the log.
7. **Grants, as `xd_app`, a standing test.** The role `xd_app` is cluster-wide and the helpers hard-code it, so creating it in the shared test container would turn the helpers from no-ops into real grants for every later migration test (order-dependent leaks). **The test therefore runs in its own dedicated container** (a separate Testcontainers fixture, disposed after the class; no drop of the role in the shared one). In it the role is created with the default privileges of `deploy/db/init/10-roles.sh` and the **whole migration chain** is applied by `xd_owner`; the assertions run over **every table of the `research` schema that exists at that time**, so a later research migration that re-grants the schema (`GrantSchemaUsageToAppRole` gives DML on all tables) and forgets `MakeAppendOnlyForAppRole` fails this test. Connected as `xd_app`: connected as `xd_app`, `SELECT` and `INSERT` on both tables succeed; `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on both fail with `42501 insufficient_privilege`; `CREATE TABLE research.x`, `DROP TABLE research.sim_trades` and `ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMN` fail with `42501`; `USAGE` on the schema and on the identity sequence is present (an insert into `sim_runs` returns an id). A text check pins that the migration calls `GrantSchemaUsageToAppRole` before `MakeAppendOnlyForAppRole` for both tables.
8. **The write is atomic.** A run whose trade row violates a constraint (on the scratch database `xd_owner` first adds a constraint such as `CHECK (ordinal < 5)` to `sim_trades`, so the copy fails mid-way; no code seam is needed) rolls back: no `sim_runs` row and no `sim_trades` row; a cancellation token cancelled during the copy leaves both tables as before; a count mismatch caught inside the transaction rolls back with `store.write_failed`; a following correct store of the same key succeeds (the key was not consumed).
9. **Validation before the database** (`StoreRunHandler`, fake store, call count 0 in each case): a null run; a run key that is not 64 lower-case hex; a run of record schema 4 or a trade of schema 3; a record text whose recomputed SHA-256 differs from `RecordSha256`; a `RunRecordText` figure that is not 64 lower-case hex; counts that do not match; two trades with one `trade_key`; a costed trade in a gross run, a gross trade in a costed run, a costing whose sheet id is not the run's; a run costed by a `test-` sheet. Each is `store.run_invalid` naming the field; all problems are listed (at most 20).
10. **The columns hold the Domain's constraints.** The `CHECK`s of D3 refuse, as `xd_owner` directly: an unknown `side`, `exit_reason`, `fills`, `costs`, `entry_fill`, `cost_segment` or timeframe name; a `costs = 'charged'` row without `cost_quantity`; a `gross` row with one of the 24 costing columns set; a run key with an upper-case letter or 63 characters.
11. **The read path.** `FindAsync` of an unknown key is null; `ListAsync` returns the stored runs newest first, filters by strategy, engine version and costed/gross, and pages by cursor with no run repeated or skipped across pages (three runs, page size 2); `PageTradesAsync` with each filter (side, symbol, exit reason, trade date range, `open_at_end`) returns exactly the rows a LINQ filter over the in-memory trades gives, in the record's order, by keyset with no row repeated or skipped (page size 7 over the 20-instrument run), at most 1,000 rows a page, and refuses a page size of 0 or 1,001 and a malformed cursor with `store.page_invalid`; an unknown enum name in a damaged row (written as `xd_owner`) is `store.row_unreadable`, not a wrong value.
12. **The same-entries shape works.** (It needs the exit policy to be in the run key: `RunAssembler.KeyText` has the `strategy` line, the SHA-256 of the definition, and the `params` line, and the exit specs are part of the typed definition of ADR 0036, so a different exit policy gives a different key; checked in the code for the definition and parameter lines, UNVERIFIED for the mechanism P4 will choose, which must stay inside one of the two.) Two runs of the 20-instrument universe that differ only in an exit-relevant parameter (so with two run keys) are stored; a join on `trade_key` over the two `run_id`s returns the entries both runs share, equal to a by-hand count from the in-memory runs, and `EXPLAIN` shows the unique index `(run_id, trade_key)` used (the test asserts a plan without a sequential scan of `sim_trades` for a single key probe; the plan check is recorded, not a speed assertion).
13. **The verb flag.** `simulate … --store` over the fixture universe: the export folder is written as without the flag (the same files, the same manifest bytes), then the run is stored and the line `stored: …` printed; a second identical invocation prints `already stored` and exits 0; without `--store` nothing is written to `research.*` and the database is not even opened for it; a store refused by validation exits 1 with the files kept; a conflict exits 3 with the files kept and the message naming `store.run_conflict`; `--store` with an unreachable database exits 3 after the files are written. The record text and both digests passed to the handler are those of the bytes written to the folder (`run.json`'s `run` object minus trades; the two file digests of `manifest.json`).
14. **The digests come from the in-memory run.** The verb computes `record_sha256` and `trades_sha256` from the `SimExportJson.WriteRecord` text and the `TradesCsv` text of the very `SimulationRun` object it hands to the store, not from files re-read from the folder; a test that changes a byte of an exported file after the write leaves the stored digests equal to the in-memory ones. The adapter recomputes nothing of the trades: on a second store it compares the two digests, so the guarantee is "the same bytes the verb wrote".
14b. **The library addition.** `SimExportJson.WriteRecord` is deterministic (two calls, equal bytes), contains no `trades` and no `open_at_end` array, differs from the `run` object of `run.json` only by those two members (the test removes them from the parsed `run.json` and compares), and leaves every existing golden file of `XDrishti.SimExport.Tests` byte-identical.
15. **Size and time are measured, not assumed.** A synthetic run of 200,000 trades (the 20-instrument trades replicated with distinct keys; costed) is stored; the test prints `pg_total_relation_size` of both tables and of the indexes, the milliseconds of the transaction, and the milliseconds of one filtered page and of the whole stream; the numbers are written into *Built* and compared with D5 (about 120 MB table and 20 MB indexes) and the loose bounds of *Non-functional*. The figures of D5 are revised if the measurement is outside a factor of 1.5.
16. **Architecture.** `IRunStore` and its types are in `XDrishti.Application/Abstractions/Research` and name no engine, EF or Npgsql type; `RunStore`, `SimRunRow`, `SimTradeRow` are `internal` in Infrastructure; `XDrishti.Application` and `XDrishti.Domain` still do not reference `XDrishti.SimExport`, **and nothing in `src` but `XDrishti.Api` references it** (the existing rule is unchanged and still green); no project or package is added; the only SQL strings that write are in `RunStore` and the migration; no module outside `Infrastructure/Research` touches the `research` schema; `LayerDependencyTests` and `LogEventIdTests` are green; each rule has a sentinel.
17. **No API surface.** `git diff` of `frontend/src/api` and of the OpenAPI document is empty, and `scripts/gen-api.sh` is not needed (checked by running it and finding no diff in the one commit).
18. **No secret, no personal data.** The record text, the trades and the strategy definition are not in any log line (a sentinel in a trade symbol and in a strategy parameter is absent from the captured logs of the success, conflict and failure paths); the migration and the tests contain no password; the verb reads `Database__Password` through the same Keychain path as `simulate.sh` and the new code never reads it.
18b. **The read path is injection-proof.** Every filter value is a bound parameter; the symbol filter is equality only (no `LIKE`, no wildcard); the cursor is opaque and validated (a malformed or foreign one is `store.page_invalid`); a symbol of `'; drop table research.sim_trades; --`, one of `%` and one of 5,000 characters return zero rows (or `store.page_invalid` for the long one) and both tables are intact.
19. **Docs.** ADR 0045 (the result store: D1 to D6, D13, D14) and Amendment 9 of ADR 0037 (the record is stored; the run key is the idempotency key; a conflict is a corruption signal) are written; doc 03 §5 states the two tables and the grants, doc 11 §6 says where a run is kept and what a `lab_runs` row will point at, doc 17 lists the flag, the append-only rule, the manual removal order and the size trigger of D5, `scripts/README.md` lists `--store`, and the roadmap row P3-10 is updated; `scripts/build-docs.sh` was run.

## Non-functional
- **Performance (measured, loose bounds, not a gate):** the transaction for 217,000 costed trades is expected in seconds (binary `COPY` of a 65-column row is on the order of 50,000 to 150,000 rows a second; UNVERIFIED) and must stay under one minute on the test container; a filtered page of 1,000 rows from a run of 200,000 under 500 ms; the stream of a whole run at least 100,000 rows a second. The write does not hold the run twice in memory: it streams from the record's list. A store of a 20-instrument run (about 2,000 to 20,000 trades) is well under a second.
- **Size:** about 105 to 140 MB for the largest run (D5, to be measured, criterion 15); the owner's disk headroom for ~20 stored runs (about 3 GB estimated; the measured figure of criterion 15 replaces it) is his to confirm (open question 3); it blocks only the storing of many large runs, not the build.
- **Security and privacy:** no personal data (a run holds instrument ids, symbols and prices, not accounts or holdings); the role `xd_app` cannot change or remove a stored result and cannot create objects (criterion 7), so a bug in the app or the future Lab cannot alter or delete a stored run (it can still insert a new run under a fresh key; the database does not check that `record_sha256` matches `record_json`, the handler does); the connection string and the password are not touched by the new code; the record text is never logged; the table is not exposed by any endpoint.
- **Integrity:** one transaction, the unique run key, the counts checked inside it, the record hash recomputed before the write and both digests compared (not recomputed) on a second store, the `CHECK`s of D2 and D3.
- **Observability:** the four events of D15 (ids, counts, milliseconds, the stored/already stored outcome); the verb prints one line.
- **Fail closed:** a run of another schema, a `test-` sheet, a hash that does not match its text and a conflict are refused, never partly stored.

## Open questions
Questions 1 to 3 change the shape; the rest are the owner's preferences. The recommended answers stand if the owner approves the spec as drafted.
1. **Opt-in or always: does the verb store by default?** **Recommendation:** opt-in `--store`, default off, for now. Storing costs a transaction of seconds and 100 MB for the largest run, and most early exploratory runs are throw-away looks at five instruments; a default-on flag would fill the database with runs nobody compares. The owner passes `--store` for a run he wants to keep or compare; the Lab (P5) will store through the same handler on its own terms. Revisit when P4 starts and every comparison needs its inputs stored.
2. **Normalised typed trade columns, or one jsonb per trade or per run?** **Recommendation:** typed columns (D1, D3). It is the larger migration (about 65 columns) but the only one that makes the Lab, P4 and P7 queries cheap, the only one whose rows are no bigger than their JSON, and it keeps the all-or-nothing costing rule in the database. The cost is a wide mapping that must change with a new `SimTrade` schema (which already needs an ADR); the round trip and a test that compares the column list with the properties of `SimTrade` (criterion 3 and 16) catch a field that is added in the Domain and forgotten here.
3. **Plain tables now, hypertable and compression later?** **Recommendation:** plain tables (D5): the 105 to 140 MB per large run is a few gigabytes only after twenty such runs, and the extension's compression and the unique-key rule for a hypertable add a migration that is easy to add when `pg_total_relation_size` passes 2 GB and costly to undo. The owner confirms the disk headroom (about 3 GB for twenty large runs; UNVERIFIED on his machine) and the 2 GB trigger.
4. **Retention and removal.** **Recommendation:** keep every stored run; `xd_app` cannot delete (the roadmap row says insert-only); the owner removes a run as `xd_owner` with the SQL of doc 17 when he wants the space. A `SECURITY DEFINER` function (`research.delete_sim_run(run_key)`, executable only by `xd_app`, the precedent of `market.undo_csv_bars`) is the later slice if removal becomes routine. The alternative, granting `DELETE`, is rejected: a stored run must not be alterable or deletable by app code.
5. **Store the labels, or only the record?** **Recommendation:** only the record (D2, D4). The labels (`exploration, not evidence of an edge`, `engine not yet verified`, the tick and opening-bar lines) are derived from the record and from the engine's verification state at the time of the export; storing them would freeze the state `engine not verified` on a run that P3-08 later verifies. A reader derives them with `RunLabels.Of` when it shows a run; the exported file keeps the labels it was written with.
6. **A reader for the whole `SimulationRun` from the stored text.** **Recommendation:** not in this slice (Out). The consumers named by the roadmap use the trades and the header columns; the text is for audit and regeneration of `run.json`. A hand-written reader of the record (sessions, notes, counters, adjustments, calendar exceptions, the strategy record) is about 1 agent-day with its tests and is built by the first slice that needs the object, so that its shape follows a real use.
7. **Should a run costed by a `test-` sheet be refused?** **Recommendation:** yes (D8): a synthetic sheet is "never stored" by ADR 0044; a stored run of it is a result with invented charges that a later comparison would take for real. The test sheet remains fine for the unit tests and the dry runs of the verb without `--store`.
8. **What if a later engine version produces other trades for the same inputs?** The run key includes the engine version string, so a new engine gives a new key and a new row, not a conflict. **Recommendation:** a conflict is therefore always a signal (same engine string, same inputs, other result), never a version difference; changing the engine without raising `SimulationEngine.Version` is the case the check exists for.
9. **Split the paging and filters (the read path beyond `Find` and `Stream`) into P3-10b?** **Recommendation:** no, keep one slice at about 3.0 agent-days, the top of the README's 1 to 3 (the cut is 0.6 d, see *Size*). If the owner wants the roadmap's 2 days, the first cut is `PageTradesAsync`, `ListAsync` with their criteria 11 and 12 and the join test (about 0.6), which would then ride with the first screen that needs them (P5) or P4.

**Decisions this draft takes (not questions; the owner may revert):** the surrogate `id` with `run_key` unique (the trades repeat an 8-byte id, not a 64-character key: 217,000 rows × 64 bytes = 14 MB saved and a smaller primary key); `text`, not `jsonb`, for the record (D4); the costing flattened into columns and the trade's sheet id taken from the run (D3); no index beyond the two keys (D3); files first, store second in the verb (D12); the actor `cli:simulate` and no audit event; the conflict is exit 3, not 1 (it is a signal, not a mistake of the owner's command).

## Risks
- **A wide mapping that drifts from `SimTrade`.** A field added to the Domain and forgotten in the 65-column mapping is a silent loss. Mitigation: one mapping table used by the copy and by the reads; the round trip compares every field by value; a test compares the mapped property names with the public properties of `SimTrade` and `TradeCosting` (the same trick as the pinned schema test), so that raising `CurrentSchemaVersion` fails here until a migration is written.
- **A run is larger than estimated.** The 105 to 140 MB figure is an estimate with a factor of error. Mitigation: criterion 15 measures it; D5 names the trigger and the later migration; the port does not change.
- **A write of 217,000 rows blocks other work.** One transaction of seconds on a single-user database; reads of other tables are unaffected. Mitigation: measured in criterion 15; the copy is binary; no index beyond two.
- **Insert-only turns a mistaken store into a permanent one.** A run stored by mistake stays until the owner deletes it as `xd_owner`. Mitigation: the opt-in flag (question 1); the removal recipe in doc 17; the size per run is bounded.
- **The key proves the inputs, not the output.** The run key is a hash of the inputs; two different results under one key cannot be told apart by the key. Mitigation: the record and the trades each have their own digest, compared on a second store (the record hash is recomputed from the text before the write; the trades digest is compared, not recomputed: the guarantee is that these are the same bytes the verb wrote); a conflict is logged at error and refused (D8).
- **`double` equality across a database.** PostgreSQL `double precision` round-trips IEEE values, and Npgsql binary copy writes them as they are; `-0` and NaN are the cases to prove. NaN cannot occur in a trade (the engine refuses it; UNVERIFIED for every field), and the test includes `-0.0`.
- **`decimal` scale.** `numeric` stores the scale given; a `decimal` read back may differ in trailing zeros from the one written (`1.50` and `1.5`), which `decimal` equality treats as equal and the byte-level csv equality must not see because `CanonicalText`/`NumberText` spell numbers canonically; criterion 4 proves it on the costed run.
- **Merge conflicts** with P3-08 (it will change `RunLabels.EngineVerification`, not the record), P3-05 and P3-06 (new walker or scanner fields would raise `SimTrade` schema and need this table's columns), and P3-02b2 (exit tables). Mitigation: any `SimTrade` or `SimulationRun` schema change after this slice carries its own additive migration for the new columns; the schema constants in the validation (D8) make a stale writer fail loudly rather than store a truncated trade.
- **The migration needs `xd_owner`; the dev flow runs it on the next `deploy/xd-up.sh`.** Until then `simulate --store` fails with exit 3 (`relation does not exist`, message names the missing migration). Mitigation: the message; the verb's summary.
- **The unique `(run_id, trade_key)` may be violated by a real run** if the "unique by construction" claim of ADR 0043 D6 has a hole (for example a swing order that rests overnight and a same-day candidate on the next decision date). Mitigation: it fails loudly in the transaction (nothing stored), the 20-instrument run and the swing cases are in the tests; if the claim is false the key is the Domain's to fix, not this table's to weaken.
