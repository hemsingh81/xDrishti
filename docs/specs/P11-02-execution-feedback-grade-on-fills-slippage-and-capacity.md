# P11-02 — Execution feedback: grade on fills, slippage per liquidity bucket and the capacity score

- **Status:** Draft (written 2026-10-24; decisions below were researched under the owner's delegation; the owner's Approved is still needed before an agent builds it)
- **Phase / roadmap row:** P11 — Small live capital, slice P11-02 of [P11-00](P11-00-small-live-plan.md). Row: "execution feedback (grade on fills, slippage per liquidity bucket, capacity score)".
- **Design refs:** docs/design/09-learning-engine.md §9 (waterfall; slippage per liquidity bucket re-estimated weekly; partial fills / rejections lower the capacity score), §12 (rule 3: judged after the expected live gap) · 08-exits-and-risk.md §8 (slippage model) · 13 §2 (R32) · 16 (`execution_feedback`: `min_turnover_cr` 50, `reestimate: weekly`) · 19 §4 · ADR 0038 (fill realism), 0042 (costs), 0064 (Beta shrinkage), 0090, 0093, 0099
- **Needs:** P11-01 (fills, orders, outcomes, journal entries), P7-13 (plan grades), P6-01 (`BetaShrinkage`, `BetaDistribution`), P8-05 (`Metric`, `MetricState`, `EvidenceBlock`). **Size:** 3.5 agent-days. **Safety:** no — it reads stored records, writes insert-only estimates and changes no trading behaviour, setting or model.

## Goal
Every live entry is measured against what the plan promised (how much worse or better, split into the delay before the order and the execution itself), every round trip is graded on the executed prices and explained by an exact waterfall, slippage is estimated per liquidity bucket with its n and intervals, and a capacity score says how often an order filled in full. Nothing is applied automatically.

## Scope
**In:** Domain `Domain/LiveTrading` (all pure): `LiquidityBucket` and `BucketEdges`, `ReferenceKind`, `SlippageDecomposer`, `SlippageStats` (mean, t interval, median, order-statistic interval), `ExecutionWaterfall`, `QueueMissClassifier`, `CapacityScore`, `SlippageProposal`; Application: `ComputeExecutionsHandler` (a step of `live-settle` after the journal), `ComputeEstimatesHandler`, queries, service `live-estimates` (Weekly, Saturday 10:00, paused); Infrastructure: stores, migration `AddLiveExecutions` (`live_executions`, `slippage_estimates`, `capacity_scores`, nullable `grades.entry_at`); Api endpoints, `xd live estimates`, the **Execution** tab of `/live`; tests; ADR.
**Out:** applying any estimate to the simulator, selection or a cell (D13 of P11-00); the live-vs-paper pairing (P11-03); halts and the ramp (P11-04); depth-based or impact models; exit "slippage" as a statistic (the exit difference is a waterfall line, not a slippage: the owner chooses the exit).

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a BUY entry with reference 1,250.00, arrival 1,250.20 and VWAP fill 1,250.475, then total slippage is +0.475 rupees = 9.5 ticks (tick 0.05) = 3.8 bp = 0.0475 R (1 R = 10.00), delay 0.20 and execution 0.275, and delay + execution equals total exactly; the SELL mirror (reference 800.00, arrival 799.90, fill 799.70) gives total +0.30, delay +0.10, execution +0.20 (positive is always worse for the owner).
2. Given no order evidence (arrival `unavailable`), then total is still given and delay and execution are `unavailable`, never zero; given no reference (no numeric ticket level and no graded plan entry) then every slippage figure is `unavailable` with the reason.
3. Given reference kinds `ticket_level` and `plan_grade` in one bucket, then they are reported as two rows, never averaged together.
4. Given 9 observations in a bucket then the row is `insufficient` with n = 9 and no mean; given the 10 observations [2.0, 3.5, 1.0, 5.0, -1.0, 4.0, 2.5, 6.0, 0.5, 3.0] bp then mean 2.65, median 2.75, sample sd 2.1220, 95 % t interval of the mean 1.13 to 4.17 (t(9) = 2.262) and 95 % median interval 0.5 to 5.0 (order statistics 2 and 9, coverage 97.9 %).
5. Given a turnover of 640 crore then L1; 500 exactly L1; 150 exactly L2; 49.99 L4; below 10 L5; unknown turnover then the entry is in no bucket and is counted under "bucket unknown".
6. Given a graded round trip then the waterfall rows (plan R, entry slippage, exit difference, charges difference, actual R) add up exactly: plan 1.4000, entry slip 0.0475, exit difference 0.2000, actual gross 1.1525, charges row 0.0960 (live charges 0.0960 minus plan charges 0), actual net 1.0565 for the fixture below; given charges unavailable then the result is labelled gross and no net R is shown; given the plan grade has no stop then no R is shown and the rupee result is.
7. Given 20 counted orders of which 18 were filled in full, then the account capacity score is the Beta(19, 3) posterior mean 0.8636 with its 95 % credible interval; a bucket with 5 counted orders (3 full) is `insufficient` (n 5) and its shrunk value 0.7757 is not shown as a score; fewer than 10 counted orders anywhere is `insufficient`.
8. Given a BUY limit at 1,251.00 that expired unfilled while the 1-minute lows of its life reached 1,250.90 then `queue_miss` (counts against capacity); given the lows never went below 1,251.00 then `price_miss` (a missed trigger, not a capacity failure); an order that is not a LIMIT is `unclassified` and counted apart.
9. Given the same stored inputs then the same rows and the same input hash; running the estimates twice writes one snapshot per week and key; `xd_app` cannot update, delete or truncate the new tables.
10. Given a lower 95 % bound of the mean total slippage above 0 with n >= 20, then the proposal sentence is stored beside the row (a text with the numbers and the knob it concerns); nothing reads it automatically.
11. Every response carries the Evidence block (basis, n, date range, reference kinds, bucket edges, limits) and the six states; no figure without n.

## Definitions (the contract of the numbers)
- **Observation unit:** one approved ticket on one account with an entry (a `journal_entries` row), not one fill and not weighted by shares.
- **Direction** d = +1 for BUY, -1 for SELL (entry side). **Reference** = the ticket's numeric entry level (P7-19) when it exists, else the plan grade's simulated entry (`TicketGrade.EntryPrice`), else none. **Arrival** = the open of the 1-minute bar that contains the entry order's `create_time`, quantity-weighted over the entry orders; absent without order evidence or bars.
- **Slippage (positive = worse for the owner):** total = d x (fill VWAP - reference); delay = d x (arrival - reference); execution = d x (fill VWAP - arrival); total = delay + execution. Units: rupees; ticks = rupees / tick (instrument master); bp = rupees / reference x 10,000; R = rupees / risk per share, risk per share = |reference - stop| (stop from the ticket level or the plan grade). For a resting limit the execution part includes the wait for the market to arrive; it is not a pure spread cost (stated on screen).
- **Round trip waterfall** (same sign convention, risk per share rho = |plan entry - plan stop| as `FillGrader`): plan R (gross, from the plan prices: d x (plan exit - plan entry)/rho) - entry slippage R (d x (entry VWAP - plan entry)/rho) - exit difference R (d x (plan exit - exit VWAP)/rho) = actual gross R (d x (exit VWAP - entry VWAP)/rho); then the charges row = live charges R (live charges / (rho x qty) from `live_fill_charges`) minus plan charges R (plan gross R - plan net R when the plan grade is costed, else 0); plan R in the first row is always gross, so plan charges are not subtracted twice; result actual net R. In the fixture the plan grade is gross-only, so plan charges are 0 and the charges row is 0.0960. "Missed triggers" and "overrides" are aggregate rows built in P11-03, not per trade.
- **Mean interval:** two-sided 95 % Student-t, t(n-1) from a constant table (df 9: 2.262, 14: 2.145, 19: 2.093, 29: 2.045, 39: 2.023, 59: 2.001, 60 to 119: 2.000, 120 and above: 1.96, nearest lower df). **Median interval:** the largest k with P(Bin(n, 1/2) <= k-1) <= 0.025, interval from the k-th to the (n+1-k)-th ordered value (n = 10: ranks 2 and 9; n = 20: ranks 6 and 15, coverage 95.9 %); defined for n >= 6, shown for n >= `MinN`. No bootstrap, no percentile above the median below n = 30.
- **Capacity:** per entry order of an approved live ticket whose replay graded or that has a fill; classes `full` (filled_qty >= qty), `partial` (0 < filled < qty), `rejected`, `queue_miss`, `price_miss` (excluded from the denominator and reported), `unclassified` (excluded, reported). Counted n = full + partial + rejected + queue_miss. Score = P(full) as the posterior mean of Beta(s x p0 + k, s x (1 - p0) + n - k), s = prior strength 10 and p0 the parent rate (cell -> bucket -> account); the account level uses the uniform prior Beta(1 + k, 1 + n - k). Interval: equal-tailed 95 % credible from `BetaDistribution`. Name on screen: "share of orders filled in full".

## Non-functional
Estimates read at most a few thousand executions (a hundred a month); the weekly job runs in seconds. Bar lookups for arrival and queue-miss read the 1-minute bars of one minute or one order life per entry (index lookups). Executions endpoint p95 < 300 ms for a page of 100. Security as P11-01; no price or quantity in logs.

## Test plan
- **Unit (Domain):** the decomposer by the two hand cases (BUY and SELL) plus a negative delay; units; the t table (every row, nearest lower df); the order-statistic ranks for n = 6, 10, 20, 30 against a brute-force binomial; the n = 10 stats case of criterion 4; bucket edges at each boundary and null; capacity Beta numbers of criterion 7 (Beta(19, 3): mean 19/22); queue-miss lows/highs; waterfall identity as a property over random prices with a negative control (a doctored exit price must break the identity); reference kinds never pooled (property: grouping key includes kind); adding an observation to a bucket never changes another bucket.
- **Unit (Application, fakes):** settle writes one execution per entry; pending grade defers; no order evidence; estimates idempotent per week; proposal only at n >= 20 with a positive lower bound.
- **Integration (Testcontainers):** migration, grants, uniqueness, `grades.entry_at` nullable, the four endpoints, an empty database.
- **Frontend (MSW):** all six states, a non-value never renders a digit, interval text, no mutation import in the tab.
- **Fixture (hand-worked):** T1 of P11-01 with plan exit 1,264.00 (target 1.4 R), live exit VWAP 1,262.00 on 40 shares, live charges 38.40: plan R 1.4000, entry slip 0.0475, exit difference 0.2000, actual gross 1.1525, charges 38.40/(10 x 40) = 0.0960, actual net 1.0565. ## Decisions (researched)
1. **Q: Against what is slippage measured?** A: The ticket's numeric level, else the plan-grade entry (priority, kinds kept apart). Why: P7-14 already measures `slippage` against the plan grade's simulated entry, which includes the modelled slippage (ADR 0038), so the figure is the model error; the numeric level (P7-19) is the owner's actual instruction. Rejected: previous close, VWAP of the minute (not what was promised).
2. **Q: Why split delay from execution?** A: The owner is the router: the largest gap at small size is the time between trigger and order, not the book. Why: it separates what discipline can fix from what liquidity decides, and it keeps the identity exact. Rejected: one number (hides the cause), seconds of delay (the plan grade has no entry instant; `entry_at` is added for a later time-based figure).
3. **Q: Is the bucket by turnover alone?** A: Yes, with participation as a stored covariate. Why: at a small capital the order is a tiny share of the day's turnover, so size effects are below noise (50,019 rupees against 640 crore is 0.0008 %); an impact model (square-root law) is UNVERIFIED here and not used. Rejected: buckets by spread or volatility (no stored source), by order size (all in one bucket).
4. **Q: Mean or median?** A: Both, labelled; gates use the mean (it is what costs money) and the median is the robust check. Why: slippage is skewed with rare large misses. Rejected: trimmed means (hide the misses).
5. **Q: What is a missed trigger versus a capacity failure?** A: A limit that was traded through (the day's low reached below it) and still did not fill is a queue miss and counts against capacity; a limit the market never reached is a price miss. Why: only the first is a liquidity property. Rejected: all unfilled as capacity (blames liquidity for price).
6. **Q: Is the capacity score used by selection?** A: No, display only in P11. Why: doc 09 §9 wants selection to prefer fillable tickets, but with n of tens the score cannot rank cells; wiring it is a hypothesis for later (P6-10), owner's act. Rejected: wiring now.
7. **Q: Does the model change from the estimates?** A: No (D13). Why: the experiment rule 3 (doc 09 §12) judges after the expected live gap; changing the gap automatically would move the goalposts. The proposal text names the knob (`slippage.ticks`) only.

## Risks and honest limits
Most buckets will be `insufficient` for weeks. Arrival from 1-minute bars is the open of a bar, not a quote; a limit order resting before the trigger has a delay near zero by construction. Charges are Dhan's recorded lines and appear only after the settle read (until then net is `unavailable`). A partial exit or an addition to a position after the first entry is not decomposed. The replay price used as reference carries the modelled slippage, so a positive live figure means "worse than the model", not "worse than the market". Sell-side swing exits are not a slippage statistic.
