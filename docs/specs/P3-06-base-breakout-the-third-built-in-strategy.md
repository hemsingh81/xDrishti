## Goal
The owner can run a swing strategy, `BASE_BREAKOUT`, from the `simulate` verb (`--strategy BASE_BREAKOUT … --gross`): a daily close that is a new closing high out of a tight base, in an uptrend, on above-average volume, is entered at the next session's open within the +0.1 % limit, held at most five sessions, and its trades come out as files and cards like those of ORB, so that the 20-trade check of P3-09 can draw a swing trade with a `MAX_HOLD` exit and a gap exit. This is a **built-in rule set as a C# builder** (like `OrbV1`, until P3-02d seeds stored text), one row of the registry, and the fixtures, the hand-calculated rows and the properties that show the strategy does what its text says. It adds no engine rule: the engine is `sim-5` and stays so.

## Definitions (numbers and rules the tests pin)
Notation: a daily bar is `O/H/L/C`; a session is written as a dense day of 375 minutes (09:15 to 15:29) built with `Day.Dense(date).Start(O).Set("10:00 O/H/L/C")`, so its daily open is `O`, its high `H`, its low `L`, its close `C` (the pattern of `DailyFixture`). Volume is 1,000 a minute, 375,000 a day, unless a day says `×k` (k times as much a minute). Tick 0.05, the default fill rules (limit +0.1 %, 1 tick + 5 % of the minute's range of slippage, 2 extra ticks on a stop exit). The calendar is the test calendar with no holiday (the dates are invented and ignore the real NSE holidays). Prices and ratios below were computed by hand and checked with a decimal script outside the repository.

### D1 — The rule set (the proposal)
Id `BASE_BREAKOUT`; style `swing`; timeframes `[1D]`; sides `[BUY]` (no `sell` block); evaluated once a day at the step that closes the 1D bar (the 15:29 minute, decision instant 15:30, ADR 0043); no filters; no overlays. Every line below is an owner decision; the last column says how it is changed.

| # | Rule | Text in the language / value | Changed by |
|---|------|------------------------------|-----------|
| R1 | Weekly trend (setup) | `tf("1W").close > tf("1W").sma(10)`: the last closed week closes above its 10-week average | a definition change (new version); the lengths are literals |
| R2 | Daily trend (setup) | `close > sma(50)` | a definition change |
| R3 | A tight base (setup) | `(highest(base_len, source=close) - lowest(base_len, source=close)) / lowest(base_len, source=close) * 100 <= base_pct`: the closes of the last `base_len` sessions, today's included, span at most `base_pct` percent | `base_len`, `base_pct` (D2) |
| R4 | Volume (setup) | `rel_volume(20) >= vol_x`: today's volume is at least `vol_x` times the mean of the previous 20 sessions | `vol_x` (D2) |
| R5 | The breakout (trigger) | `close >= highest(base_len, source=close)`: today's close is a new `base_len`-session closing high (a tie counts) | `base_len` |
| R6 | Entry | `at_close` as the model writes it: realised by the simulator as an order placed at 15:30 and filled at the next session's open within the limit of +0.1 % of the signal close rounded down to the tick (ADR 0043 E8, E9); a gap above the limit is a counted miss, a gap to or through the stop is `EntryThroughStop` | fixed by the engine |
| R7 | Stop | `swing_low`, `lookback = base_len`, no buffer, on 1D: the lowest low of the last `base_len` sessions, the signal day included | a definition change (open question 3: `bar_low`) |
| R8 | Target | one `FIXED_RR`, `rr` (default 2.0) times the risk, `risk = entry − stop` of the exact entry | `rr` |
| R9 | Longest hold | `max_hold_sessions = 5`: leaves at the close of the fifth session of the position (the fill session is the first); always on | a definition change. Not a parameter: 3 `StrategyParam`s plus the registry-level `rr` is 4; adding `max_hold` would make 5 registry parameters |
| R10 | One candidate | at most one per instrument, side and decision date (a 1D strategy has one decision a day), and at most one open position per instrument and side (the gate, ADR 0040 D11; a signal decided while a position is open is `PositionOpen`, counted) | fixed by the engine |

**Why these rules (decided on research; each is open to the owner).** The roadmap row says "setup on `tf("1W")` and a 1D trend, a tight base, `rel_volume(20) >= vol_x`, trigger `close >= highest(base_len, source=close)`, stop `lowest(base_len)`, `FIXED_RR 2.0` plus `MAX_HOLD` 5"; this draft keeps every element and makes four choices the row leaves open. (a) *The base is measured on daily closes, signal day included, not on weekly highs and lows*: `highest(n)` includes the current bar (ISS-24) and a lag cannot be written on a call, so a "previous N-day high" has no spelling; on weekly bars the last closed week may or may not include the decision day (D6) and the row mixes units (`base_len` counts days in the trigger and stop and would count weeks on `tf("1W")`). A window of closes that ends today and whose top is today's close means "the whole window moved at most `base_pct` percent and ended at its high"; its cost is that a breakout that itself lifts the close more than `base_pct` percent above the window's lowest close is not a base breakout in v1. (b) *Ties count* (`>=`): it is the only spelling that exists; a close equal to the window's high (including a flat series) therefore triggers if the other rules hold (Risks). (c) *The 1W gate is a trend gate, not a level*: `tf("1W").close` is the close of the last closed week; it is robust to the question of D6 except on the knife-edge shown by the rows W1 to W3. (d) *The stop is the base's own low* (`swing_low`, `lookback = base_len`) as the row says; with a base of up to 8 % the risk is up to about 8 %, a 2R target about 16 %, which a five-session hold will rarely reach (Risks, open question 3).

### D2 — The parameters (at most four) and their ranges
| Registry name | Default | Range (proposal) | Meaning | A `StrategyParam`? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `base_len` | 20 | whole number, 10 to 60 | sessions in the base window, also the lookback of the stop | yes |
| `base_pct` | 8.0 | 2.0 to 20.0 | the most the closes of the window may span, percent of the lowest | yes |
| `vol_x` | 1.5 | 1.0 to 5.0 | the least volume multiple of the 20-session mean | yes |
| `rr` | 2.0 | greater than 0, at most 10 (the range of `FIXED_RR`, checked by the compile) | target as a multiple of the risk | no (a literal in `FIXED_RR`, as in `OrbV1`) |

Three `StrategyParam`s and one registry parameter: "at most four" holds. The compile checks `base_len` as a whole period (`highest(20.5)` is `feature_invalid`) and `rr`; `base_pct` and `vol_x` would be unchecked (a negative `base_pct` makes R3 never true, the silent no-trade rule of ISS-24). The ranges are therefore declared with **P3-05's mechanism** (declarative `Min`/`Max` and an exclusive-minimum flag on the public Domain record `BuiltInParameterInfo`, checked in `BuiltInStrategies.Build`; code `simulation.parameter_out_of_range` at `parameters.<name>`, declared once in `SimulationErrors`; NaN and infinities stay an `ArgumentException` from `StrategyPreparation.Prepare`); that spec owns the text. This slice declares `base_len` [10, 60], `base_pct` [2, 20], `vol_x` [1, 5] and `rr` (0, 10] (exclusive minimum). The ranges are proposals; 10 as the lowest `base_len` keeps a "base" more than two weeks long.

### D3 — The spelling (ISS-24) and the lint
The conditions are the five texts of D1, as strings of a `ConditionList(All)` for `setup` (R1 to R4) and one for `trigger` (R5). The builder writes parameters by name (`base_len`, `base_pct`, `vol_x`), as `OrbV1` writes `or_min`. No condition uses a lag, `rising`, `falling` or a crossing, so: the subscript-on-a-call trap (ISS-24 (1)) is not met; the cost lint of ISS-26 stays deferred (this is not the "first strategy that uses `rising`/`falling`"; the roadmap's trigger for reopening it is not met, and the evaluation share is reported by the benchmark of P3-04, not asserted); the look-ahead lint finds nothing (a higher-timeframe read `tf("1W")` from a 1D strategy is allowed and the weekly value is the last closed week). A test compiles the built text and pins **zero errors and zero warnings** (a warning would be a signal that the text is not the one meant). The comparisons that cannot be true by construction (ISS-24 (2)) are not linted, so the tests of D11 are what shows R3 and R5 can be true.

### D4 — Entry, stop, target and exit on the daily bar
At the decision (the 15:29 step) the scanner records the candidate: trigger close `C`, stop level `L = lowest low of the last base_len daily bars`. The walker (ADR 0043) places the order for the next trading session: limit `tickDown(C × 1.001)`; the fill minute is the first minute with a trade that starts at or after 09:15 and at most 5 minutes after (09:20); open at or inside the limit: fill at the open (`AtOpen`); open beyond the limit and the minute's low touches it: fill at the limit (`AtLimit`); otherwise `LimitNotReached`; open at or below `L`: `EntryThroughStop`. `risk = entry − L` with the exact entry; target `entry + rr × risk` (touch is a fill, never better); stop `low <= L` (a gap through it exits at the open, with the 2 extra ticks of slippage in modelled mode); `MAX_HOLD` leaves at the close of the last minute of session 5 with the base slippage; a stop or target in that minute comes first. The walker rows are ADR 0038 and 0040; this strategy adds none.

### D5 — What a decision is
One decision per session, at the step that closes the 1D bar. A 1D bar closed late (the 15:29 minute is missing, so it closes at the first minute of the next date) is stale: counted in `StaleBucketSteps`, not evaluated, the signal is lost (ADR 0043 D4; the rate of a missing 15:29 is UNVERIFIED, ADR 0038 E0 measured 0.4 % empty minutes in the sampled files, not this minute). `ScanCounters.LaterTriggers` is always 0 for this strategy. The counters say why a rule did not fire: per condition true, false, unknown, not ready; `FullyReadySteps` is the number of decisions at which every feature had a value.

### D6 — The weekly bar and the week of the decision (EXPECTED from the code; W1 to W3 pin it)
`WeekMonthBuilder` documents that **with a `TradingCalendar` a period closes at the very step that closes the period's last session** (doc 11, *Weekly and monthly timeframes*), so `tf("1W").close` at the 15:29 step of the last session of a week **includes the decision day**; on the other days it is the previous week; without a calendar the bar is emitted at the first bar of the next week, so a Friday decision sees the previous week. `Simulator.Run` and `EntryScanner.Create` pass the calendar into `FrameOptions` (null accepted) and `InstrumentReplayer` requires a non-null calendar (read), so a replay and the verb supply it. This is stated as EXPECTED; W1 to W3 test it with and without a calendar. **R1 is therefore deliberately weekday-dependent**: on the last session of a week it compares today's close with a 10-week average that includes today, on the other days the previous week's close. For a trending stock the two agree; W1 to W3 show the knife-edge. This design smell is open question 4.

### D7 — The data it needs, and how many candidates it may give (UNVERIFIED)
- **Warm-up.** The replay starts at the instrument's first stored session and counts from there (P3-04): there is no pre-roll before the first stored bar. The slowest features are the weekly average (10 closed weeks = 50 sessions, plus up to 4 sessions if the stored data starts in the middle of a week) and `sma(50)` (50 sessions); `rel_volume(20)` needs 21 and the base needs `base_len`. So the first possible decision is the 50th session (a Friday, if the frame shows the week of the decision) or the 51st, and at most about the 55th: **about 11 weeks of stored daily history for any `base_len` up to 50; for `base_len` above 50 the base is the slowest**. An instrument with fewer sessions is skipped `features_not_ready` (an existing skip reason). Whether a partial first week counts as a weekly bar is UNVERIFIED (ADR 0033 open question 8, undecided).
- **How many candidates.** The owner's 214 files start between 2024-04-01 and 2024-12-18 and end 2026-04-30 (ISS-19): 1.4 to 2.1 years, about 340 to 520 sessions, so about 285 to 470 sessions after the warm-up. The rate at which R1 to R5 are all true is unknown. For scale only: if they held on 1 % of the evaluated sessions, that is 3 to 5 decisions per instrument and 600 to 1,000 over 214 instruments, before the gate, the missed fills (a gap above +0.1 % is common) and the stale days; if they hold on 0.1 %, about 60 to 100. **Neither is a forecast.** The first run's counters (D11) give the real number, and the slice does not tune to reach a count (open question 9).
- **Stored bars are minutes.** The daily bar is built by the frame from the 1-minute bars: its close is the close of the last minute that holds a trade, not the exchange's official close, and its volume is the sum of the minutes; both can differ from a vendor chart (UNVERIFIED by how much; the P3-09 protocol compares against a resampled 1-minute view, not official daily candles).

### D8 — Costs and the first runs
A swing strategy is costed in the **delivery** segment (ADR 0042 D12: `HoldsOvernight`; a swing trade that exits on its entry day is still delivery), with the DP charge once on the sell; brokerage, STT, stamp duty and the rest are the sheet's, which does not exist yet for the owner (P3-03f exit check open, ISS-25). Therefore **every real run is `--gross` and says `costs: none`** until the owner's sheet passes its check; a `--costs <id>` run with the invented `test-` sheet is refused by the store, and with `fixture-a` it is a test of arithmetic only. Two cautions for the owner's later net run (both UNVERIFIED because the delivery rates are not known to the repository): the risk of a base breakout is a few percent of the price, so fixed and percent charges are a larger share of R than for a wide intraday stop, and the default `max_cost_R` of 0.10 may skip a share of the trades (`SkippedCost`); the fates say how many.

### D10 — Fixtures and helpers
- `Day` gains `Volume(long perMinute)` (every non-empty minute of the day carries it; `MinuteVolume` 1,000 stays the default). `DailyFixture` (P3-03e) is extended, not changed.
- **`BaseDays`** (in `tests/Shared`, next to `ReplayDays`): `Ramp()` = the 60 sessions **H60** below; `Base()` = **BASE10**; `Signal()` = **S**; the continuations **T1** to **T5** of each row. All through `Day`.
- **`SeededBase`** (in `XDrishti.Simulation.Tests/Support`, next to `SeededDaily`): 80 to 160 sessions per series with **sparse** days (four minutes: 09:15 `O/O/O/O`, 10:00 `O/H/O/H`, 12:00 `H/H/L/L`, 15:29 `L/C/L/C`; the day's volume is split evenly over them) so that 200 to 500 series stay cheap; a calendar with one planted holiday week in 30 %; a planted base and breakout in 60 % (volume 1.2 to 3 times the mean, 20 % of them under 1.5); a gap above the limit after the signal in 20 %, a gap through the stop in 10 %, a signal on the last session of a week in 25 %, a missing 15:29 minute on 15 % of the sessions **in the look-ahead and counter series only** (the 50 reference series have none, see below). The test asserts that each planted case occurs in at least 5 of the 50 reference series (a generator that stops planting would otherwise pass quietly).
- **`BaseBreakoutReference`**: a plain loop over the daily arrays of a `SeededBase` series, written without the frame, the language or the walker: for each session from the 51st, R1 to R5 with `decimal` arithmetic (the weekly average built by hand from the Fridays or the last session of each calendar week, according to the behaviour that W1 pins), the stop, the limit, the fill, the stop/target/`MAX_HOLD` checks with the minute order of the sparse day (high minute before low minute, so a day that holds both target and stop is a target day: the generator's order, not a market fact), the gate. It returns decisions and trades; AC 11 compares them with the simulator's, field for field. **The reference series contain no missing minute**: the planted missing-15:29 case (stale daily bar, a weekly bar delayed by a stale last session, a `MAX_HOLD` due minute that is absent, a fill session whose first minute is missing) would need the reference to re-implement ADR 0043 D4 and ADR 0040, which would copy the engine and not check it; those cases are covered by the hand rows P9 and the counters and look-ahead tests on the 200 series that do have them.

Numbered, each testable. "The rows" are the rows of D11 through `Simulator.Create` and `Run` with `Day.Join` of the series and `TickSpec.FromRupees(0.05m)`, in both fill modes (`SlippageConfig.None` gives `EntryFilledPrice = EntryPrice`); the modelled numbers are for the default rules.

Whether the rule set is also recorded as an ADR (the next free number is 0045) or only in doc 07 §3 as ORB v1 is: recommendation **doc 07 §3 only**, like ORB v1.
2. **The base on daily closes with today included (D1, R3), or on weekly highs and lows as the row reads?** Recommendation: **daily closes**: it needs no lag on a call (ISS-24), it does not depend on the weekday (D6), its text is checkable on a daily chart, and the owner can still ask for the weekly form later (a new version of the definition, about 0.3 day). The cost is stated in D1: a breakout that itself moves the close more than `base_pct` percent is not a base breakout in v1.
3. **The stop: `swing_low` of the base (`lookback = base_len`) or `bar_low` of the signal day?** Recommendation: **`swing_low` of the base**, as the row says: it is the level at which the base has failed. Its risk is as wide as the base (up to about 8 % at the default `base_pct`), a 2R target is then about 16 % and a five-session hold will mostly end in `MAX_HOLD` (UNVERIFIED). `bar_low` gives a risk of one day's range and a reachable target, and a stop that is hit by ordinary noise. After the first run the exit reasons decide; either variant is one text in the builder and a new version (half a day with P3-05's `bar_low` already tested).
4. **Keep the weekly trend gate R1, although it is weekday-dependent (D6)?** On the last session of a week R1 compares today's close with a 10-week average that includes today; on other days the previous week's close. Recommendation: **keep it and pin the behaviour with W1 to W3**. Alternative: drop R1 and use `tf("1D")`-only trend gates (for example `sma(50)` above `sma(150)`), because a lag on a call (and so on `tf("1W")` values) is unavailable; the cost is a longer warm-up (150 sessions of 285 to 470) and a departure from the roadmap row.
5. **Declare ranges for the four parameters (D2), using P3-05's mechanism?** Recommendation: **yes**: a silent never-true rule is the trap of ISS-24. The alternative is to document the ranges and rely on the first run's counters.
6. **Lift the `--timeframe` check of the verb to any timeframe code (D9)?** Recommendation: **yes**, the compile already knows what a strategy declares; the alternative is to leave the flag intraday-only and tell the owner never to pass it for this strategy, which is a trap.
7. **The 20-trade check for this strategy (D12).** The card draws the fill day only, so the exit (up to four sessions later), the signal day and the base are not on it, and the setup cannot be recomputed from it. Recommendation: **do not change the card here**; ask the owner to decide, when he reads the P3-09 protocol, between (a) building P3-04d (the conditions at the decision bar, about 1.3 agent-days) so that the card prints R1 to R5 with their values, and checking the base against an external daily chart, and (b) checking this strategy's trades on the daily chart alone with a hand recompute of about 100 numbers per trade. (a) is recommended.
8. **Keep P3-06 in P3 or slide it to P4 with P3-03e?** Recommendation: **keep it in P3** (P3-03e is built; the exit check then covers a swing strategy, the `MAX_HOLD` exit and the gap exits that P3-09 wants to draw); sliding is the roadmap's cut line if time runs short.
9. **A minimum number of trades.** Recommendation: **no tuning to reach a count**; the first run reports `Candidates`, `Trades`, the fates and the counters; if the whole universe gives fewer than about 20 trades the strategy cannot supply a stratum of the 20-trade check, and the owner then chooses between looser parameters (a new run, in-sample) and drawing fewer of its trades.
10. **Close and volume of the daily bar come from minutes (D7).** Recommendation: **accept for v1** and let the P3-09 protocol list it among the chart differences; a daily series from the broker is a data slice (P1/P3-01d), not this one.

## Risks
- **A proposal, not a finding.** Nothing about this rule set has been tested on the owner's data; the defaults are common choices, and a number from the first run is a signal-level, in-sample result with modelled fills and no costs.
- **Few or many trades (UNVERIFIED).** D7's arithmetic spans 60 to 1,000 for the universe. Too few cannot supply the check; too many with a wide stop make the result a few trades' gaps (ISS-27 (3)).
- **A target that is rarely reached.** With a base-wide stop and `rr` 2.0 over at most five sessions, most exits may be `MAX_HOLD` and R small (open question 3). The exit reasons in the run say so; nothing here hides it.
- **Ties count.** `close >= highest(…)` is true for a close equal to the window's high: a flat close or a repeated high can trigger (P12); the strict spelling needs P3-02f. R3, R4 and R1 limit it; the counters show the rest.
- **The knife-edge of the weekly gate (D6).** On the last session of a week the weekly bar may include the decision day; the same pattern gives a candidate or not by weekday (W2).
- **Lost signals.** A missing 15:29 minute loses the day's signal (`StaleBucketSteps`; rate not measured); a gap above +0.1 % of the close is a counted miss and a breakout day is when gaps happen: `LimitNotReached` may be a large share of the candidates.
- **The 09:15 fills.** Every entry and every overnight gap exit fills in the minute whose open can be the auction price and whose range the importer may have widened (ISS-18 (a), ISS-21); the larger slippage there is in `SlippageR`.
- **Swing limits** (ISS-27, `FillLimits`): a target is never improved on a gap; a gap under the 20 % flag is a gap fill, so a handful of trades can set the mean; dividends are neither adjusted nor received; the closing auction and the circuit limit are not modelled; a position open at `To` is `OpenAtEnd` and the last four sessions of a window drop the trades that had not left (a mild selection); a position across a flagged day is excluded and counted.
- **Cost share (D8).** A base-breakout risk is a few percent of the price, so the delivery charges are a larger share of R than for ORB (UNVERIFIED); `max_cost_R` may skip trades once the sheet exists.
- **The 20-trade check is harder than ORB's (open question 7).** One-day cards, a setup that spans 50 sessions.
- **Dependence on P3-05.** It builds the range mechanism and the `VWAP_PULLBACK` row first; this slice adds its row after it, in the order of D9, and shares the `Ids` assertion plan (one test pins the ordered list; the old `["ORB"]` line becomes `ShouldContain`).
