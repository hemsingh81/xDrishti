P3 is built in slices. Each slice has a spec in docs/specs (Draft, Approved, Done) and, where it decides something, an ADR; the build history of the Built slices is in their ADRs. The Wave column says when a slice can start (the waves are described under Order and size) and the Status column where it stands: Built; Spec Approved (the owner approved it; agents may build it); Approved, in build (an agent is building it); Proposed (named by a spec as its next part, no spec file yet); Spec Draft (written, not yet approved); Spec to be drafted (on the path to the exit criterion above, no spec yet); Later (off that path, needed by P4 to P7; order below). Sizes are agent-days (see Order and size). The rules named in the rows that are not built (fills, exits, the first rule sets) are proposals until their specs are Approved (the specs of P3-02b, P3-03, P3-03b and P3-04 were approved on 2026-10-18 with plan questions Q1, Q2 and Q5 and the questions inside them, and those of P3-03a and P3-04a the same day; the seven specs of 2026-10-18 (P3-04b1, P3-04b2, P3-04c, P3-03c, P3-03f, P3-03d, P3-03e) were approved the same day with the splits and the recommended answers; P3-04d is proposed and has no spec; the others wait for their specs).

Words used here. Exit path: the slices that must be done before the 20-trade check. Critical path: the longest chain of them, which sets the earliest finish. Wave and track: slices that can be built at the same time; a track is a series of slices built one after another in a git worktree of its own. Walking skeleton: the thinnest version that runs from end to end. Seam: the one value passed between the entry scanner and the trade walker (an entry candidate), so that P4 can replay the same entries under other exits. Oracle: a second, plain implementation written without reading the first, to compare against. Negative control: a deliberately wrong version that a test must catch, to show that the test can fail. R: profit measured in units of the risk taken on the trade (doc 08 §1); MAE and MFE: the worst and the best price the trade saw before it closed. NFR-3: runs are reproducible, any plan or test can be regenerated from a data snapshot, a config version and a model version (doc 02).

What "conditional entries" means in P3. A trigger plus a window plus an invalidation. The entry is a fill at the open of the first 1-minute bar after the bar that triggered, within the +0.1 % limit of doc 07 §2; a miss is counted and never filled later. That is more pessimistic than "Entry: at trigger-candle close" in doc 07 §2 (plan question Q1). Tickets that live across bars (stop_above_high, limit_pullback), cross-session buy-stops and BASE_BREAKOUT variant B come later: the strategy model accepts them and the simulator refuses them by name. P3 implements the exits FIXED_RR, SESSION_EXIT and MAX_HOLD; the scanner knows nothing of exits, so that P4 can replay the same entries under every exit policy (doc 08 §3).

What a P3 number means. The exit criterion says the mechanics are right (entries, stops, exits and arithmetic match a chart), not that a strategy has an edge. Every run record and trade card says: fills modelled (ISS-18), costs none until the charge sheet is in (ISS-25), portfolio limits not simulated, in-sample, members as of today (ISS-22), about 2.1 years of data and no hold-out (ISS-19), and the engine line (the engine agrees with a separately written reference implementation of the rule tables, P3-08; it is not checked against charts or the charge sheet; ADR 0047). ISS-27 Swing positions carry limits that the numbers must not hide (ADR 0040). (1) A gap through a target is not improved: a target fills at the target even when the session opens beyond it, so a swing result is somewhat low on gap-up winners (OvernightTargetGaps counts how often). (2) A session in the hold on which the walker read no trade (a halt, a Muhurat or Saturday session, a missing session) is counted (SessionsUnseen) and kept: the stop and target were not tested there, the next minute is. (3) A gap under the 20 % flag threshold is filled as a gap: a 3.4 % stop and a 19.9 % unflagged gap is −5.8 R, so the mean R of a swing run can be set by a handful of trades; OvernightStopGaps, StopGapExits and the median beside the mean tell. (4) A position open across a flagged day is excluded and counted (ExcludedAcrossDiscontinuity), which can leave out a real crash (SIEMENS 2025-04-07, TMPV 2025-10-14, VEDL 2026-04-30 of ISS-20, none checked for a missing adjustment). (5) The window edge is strict: a trade still open at To is OpenAtEnd, so the last N − 1 sessions of a window drop the trades that had not hit stop or target, a mild selection. (6) The gate models the signal's position, not the account's: a trade that P3-03c skips for quantity or cost still blocks the next candidates, a blocked signal spends the day's one candidate, and a candidate that a flagged day suppresses still occupies the gate of its segment (named limit 3). (7) A due minute that is missing leaves at an earlier close (MaxHoldAtLastClose), and the instrument is closed to new orders until the end of its due session. Built with P3-03d (2026-10-18); the figures are those of invented series, nothing is measured on real data. A swing run can look better or worse than the market would have given, and a small sample (about 2.1 years of data, ISS-19) lets a few gaps decide it. Read the counters of every swing run beside its trades: OvernightStopGaps, OvernightTargetGaps, SessionsUnseen, MaxHoldAtLastClose, ExcludedAcrossDiscontinuity, PositionOpen; ask for the open at a gapped target, a longer read past To, or a lower flag threshold (a ReplayOptions value) if the counts say they matter.
