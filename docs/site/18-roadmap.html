P3 slices#

Where we are (2026-10-18). Built: the 56 features and the frame (P3-01 to P3-01c) and the rules language for one condition (P3-02), closed out on 2026-10-17. The owner approved the specs P3-02b, P3-03, P3-03b and P3-04 on 2026-10-18 ("yes to all recommendations"), which settles plan questions Q1, Q2 and Q5 and the questions inside those specs. P3-02b, the typed strategy model and the compile of its sections, was built on 2026-10-18 (ADR 0036). Next to build: P3-03. Still open: your charge sheet (Q3; best in hand by agent-day 7.5), Q4, Q6 and Q7 (none blocks the first slices), and the specs P3-03a and P3-04a, which are drafted while wave 1 runs. What you get, in order: a first run of the opening-range strategy on invented days at agent-day 5.5; its first trades on your own data, with modelled fills, at about agent-day 11.5; the 20-trade check against charts, which closes P3, at about agent-day 14.5 plus your time. Passing that check says the mechanics are right, not that any strategy has an edge.

P3 is built in slices. Each slice has a spec in docs/specs (Draft, Approved, Done) and, where it decides something, an ADR; the build history of the Built slices is in their ADRs. The Wave column says when a slice can start (the waves are described under Order and size) and the Status column where it stands: Built; Spec Approved (the owner approved it; agents may build it); Spec Draft (written, not yet approved); Spec to be drafted (on the path to the exit criterion above, no spec yet); Later (off that path, needed by P4 to P7; order below). Sizes are agent-days (see Order and size). The rules named in the rows that are not built (fills, exits, the first rule sets) are proposals until their specs are Approved (the specs of P3-02b, P3-03, P3-03b and P3-04 were approved on 2026-10-18 with plan questions Q1, Q2 and Q5 and the questions inside them; the others wait for their specs).

Words used here. Exit path: the slices that must be done before the 20-trade check. Critical path: the longest chain of them, which sets the earliest finish. Wave and track: slices that can be built at the same time; a track is a series of slices built one after another in a git worktree of its own. Walking skeleton: the thinnest version that runs from end to end. Seam: the one value passed between the entry scanner and the trade walker (an entry candidate), so that P4 can replay the same entries under other exits. Oracle: a second, plain implementation written without reading the first, to compare against. Negative control: a deliberately wrong version that a test must catch, to show that the test can fail. R: profit measured in units of the risk taken on the trade (doc 08 §1); MAE and MFE: the worst and the best price the trade saw before it closed. NFR-3: runs are reproducible, any plan or test can be regenerated from a data snapshot, a config version and a model version (doc 02).

What "conditional entries" means in P3. A trigger plus a window plus an invalidation. The entry is a fill at the open of the first 1-minute bar after the bar that triggered, within the +0.1 % limit of doc 07 §2; a miss is counted and never filled later. That is more pessimistic than "Entry: at trigger-candle close" in doc 07 §2 (plan question Q1). Tickets that live across bars (stop_above_high, limit_pullback), cross-session buy-stops and BASE_BREAKOUT variant B come later: the strategy model accepts them and the simulator refuses them by name. P3 implements the exits FIXED_RR, SESSION_EXIT and MAX_HOLD; the scanner knows nothing of exits, so that P4 can replay the same entries under every exit policy (doc 08 §3).
