08 — Exits & Risk

1. R-multiples#

  • 1R = amount risked = |entry − initial_stop| × qty. All results are in R, so trades are comparable.
  • Expectancy E[R] = P(win)·avg_win_R − P(loss)·avg_loss_R − cost_R.
  • Break-even win rate before costs: 33.3% at 1:2, 25% at 1:3.

2. Exit policy library#

Policy Description Key parameters
FIXED_RR Full exit at k×R or stop rr: a list of candidate k, each in (0, 10], e.g. {1.5, 2, 2.5, 3}
PARTIAL_BE Book x% at T1, stop → breakeven, rest at T2 book_pct in (0, 100), t1_rr in (0, 10], t2_rr: a list of candidate T2, each in (0, 10] and above t1_rr
PARTIAL_TRAIL Book x% at T1, trail remainder book_pct in (0, 100), t1_rr in (0, 10], trail_atr_mult in (0, 20], optional whole atr_period (14) — the trail is the ATR in schema 1
ATR_TRAIL Chandelier/ATR trail after activation mult: a list in (0, 20], activate_at_rr in [0, 10] (0: trailing from the entry), optional whole atr_period (14)
STRUCTURE_TRAIL Trail beyond prior swing / previous candle lookback_bars (whole, the catalog's range): the N-bar extreme (lowest for BUY, highest for SELL); "previous candle" is 1
EMA_TRAIL Exit on close beyond EMA length (whole, the catalog's range)
PYRAMID Add at progress levels, trail combined position initial_pct, add_at_rr, add_pct, max_adds, trail_atr_mult, optional atr_period: see §4

SESSION_EXIT Intraday square-off time
MAX_HOLD Swing exit at end of session N N ≤ 5
EVENT_EXIT Exit before results — an overlay, not a candidate days_before_results, whole, 1 to 10

SESSION_EXIT (intraday) and MAX_HOLD (swing) are always on: they come from the time rules of §5, not from the list of candidates.

As built (P3-02b, ADR 0036). The strategy model (XDrishti.Strategies.Model) lists the candidate policies in exits (the seven above the line, each once; the Lab compares them on the same entries, §3) and the overlays time_stop and event_exit in overlays (at most one of each, applied to every candidate, the doc 16 exits.overlays). The compile checks the parameters of every candidate and of both overlays (names, required or optional, scalar or list, whole numbers, ranges, the relations between values: t2_rr above t1_rr; add_at_rr strictly increasing; add_pct as long as add_at_rr and initial_pct plus its sum at most 100; max_adds at most the number of levels; swing_sessions below max_hold_sessions; intraday_bars at most the bars of a session). The tables of P3-02b2 (ADR 0036 Amendment 1) are accepted but provisional until P4's spec: the break-even offset, whether a trail may move down, what activation means and a pyramid trail other than the ATR are the walker's rules, not parameters, and P4 may rename or add an optional name (by Amendment 2) as long as no stored strategy uses it. The features a trail reads (the ATR, the N-bar extreme, the EMA) are plain features on the evaluation timeframe, as of the closed bar: a value computed at the close of bar k may move a stop or signal an exit only from the first minute after that close, never inside bar k (P4's spec carries this as a property: truncating the data after bar k changes no decision at or before k). FIXED_RR was the one policy the P3 simulator ran; since P4-04 it runs all seven (one candidate per strategy), and only the overlay event_exit is accepted by the model, given the warning exit_not_evaluated and refused by name by the simulator.

The seam for the others (P4-01, ADR 0049, engine sim-6). The scanner records an exit tape during the replay it already runs: one row per closed evaluation bar of an instrument and side, holding the bar's close instant and the value of each feature the candidate exits read (atr, the N-bar extreme, the ema) as of that close, NaN while a feature is warming up (a rule reads NaN as "no new level"); a strategy whose exits read no feature has the shared empty tape. The walker reads a bar k only from the first minute that starts at or after its close (one function, LastClosedAtOrBefore), calls an internal IExitRule for each new bar, and applies what it asks in order: a stop is only tightened (a looser request is ignored and counted), a level it moves is in force from that minute on and never inside the minute that closed the bar, and a share it asks to leave goes at the open of the minute that sees the request (a market fill with slippage) unless that open is already through the stop, in which case the stop takes everything. Truncating the 1-minute data after bar k changes nothing the position did at or before the first minute after k: a property test over 11,100 seeded series, with a sentinel rule that peeks and is caught. FIXED_RR is the walk with no rule, unchanged. A rule that needs an arbitrary function of the tape is an IExitRule (P4-03); the three policies below are not.

The three trails (P4-03, ADR 0051, engine sim-6). ATR_TRAIL, STRUCTURE_TRAIL and EMA_TRAIL are run by the simulator (one such exit per strategy, one mult value; no overlay yet) and leave in one fill: the trade has no legs, shows the initial stop and no target, and is charged one entry and one exit order. At each closed evaluation bar k (a bar that closes after the fill minute started), read from the first minute at or after its close: ATR_TRAIL moves the stop to H − mult × ATR (a sell: H + mult × ATR), H the highest high (a sell: lowest low) of the minutes since the entry (the chandelier, not anchored at the activation), once a bar closes at least activate_at_rr initial risks in favour (a touch inside a bar that closes back does not count; 0 is active from the first bar) and from then on for good; STRUCTURE_TRAIL moves it to the N-bar extreme including bar k; in both a level is taken only if it is not looser than the stop in force (a strictly looser one is ignored and counted), the stop is the ordinary gap-aware stop (a level that the price has already passed fills at the open), and a value that is not a number gives no level. EMA_TRAIL signals the whole position out at a bar that closes beyond its EMA (a buy: below) and leaves at the open of the next minute that holds a trade as a market fill, exit reason EmaExit: the initial stop is the only stop and wins only when that open is already through it, and the square-off comes before the signal. The leak property (truncating the data after bar k changes nothing at or before the first minute after it) holds for each rule over 11,100 seeded series, and the reference implementation of the oracle agrees with the engine on 19,000 series per rule.

The pyramid (P4-04, ADR 0052, engine sim-6). PYRAMID is run by the simulator (one exit set per strategy, with or without the time_stop) and is always a trade with legs: the first fill of initial_pct of the full size Q, one add leg for each add taken and one exit leg that takes all the open shares together. A bar that closes at or beyond the next level (close − entry₀ >= level × R0, the first fill's risk) attempts an add of add_pct of Q at the open of the next minute that holds a trade, with the entry's limit taken from that close: beyond the limit the add is missed (never filled later at the limit), and a minute that ends the position anyway (the square-off, the time stop, a reach of the stop in force) makes no add. Before the add fills the stop moves to the tighter of the stop in force and S = (Σ s·e − R0) / Σ s over the legs including the add, so the open risk of all legs at the stop is at most 1 R; this branch cannot ever skip an add (the open risk is already at most 1 R at the stop in force, and an add is made only above it), which a property holds. The combined position is trailed by the chandelier of ADR 0051 from the first counted bar (the same stop: it only tightens). The unit of R is the risk of the full size, so a pyramid stopped out before any add loses initial_pct/100 R and one stopped at the cap loses exactly 1 R; MAE and MFE are taken against the average entry of the legs open at that minute. One order per leg in the costs; the cap on capital is applied at the first entry's price, which the adds can exceed (the named limit pyramid_capital_cap).

3. How the exit is chosen#

Per cell (weekly): replay the same historical entries under every candidate policy; choose by out-of-sample risk-adjusted expectancy (mean_R − λ·downside_dev_R), requiring minimum samples and stability across folds — prefer the middle of a plateau over a sharp peak.

The comparison as built (P4-05, ADR 0053). simulate --exit POLICY:name=value,... (repeatable; a|b lists candidate values; --time-stop is an overlay on all of them) runs the exit you name in place of the strategy's own; one candidate is an ordinary run, two or more are one scan of the entries and one walk of each candidate on the same entries and the same minutes, with a run of its own for each candidate and a table that sets them side by side: trades, wins, win rate, mean, median, downside deviation, total, profit factor, average hold, the share by exit reason and the counters of the policy, on each candidate's own trades and on the common set (the entries taken under every candidate, with each candidate's mean difference from the first on them). Downside deviation is sqrt(mean(min(R, 0)²)) over all trades of the sample, target 0, in RFilled (gross) or RNet (costed); downside_dev_R above is this. A swing comparison is not gated (every candidate walks every entry, so the entries are the same; the entries a gated run would skip are counted for each candidate), and the best row is in-sample and not evidence of an edge. The walk-forward choice by mean_R − λ·downside_dev_R is P4-06.

The choice as built (P4-06, ADR 0054). simulate --exit FIXED_RR:rr=1.5|2|2.5|3 --select-exit chooses among the candidates of a comparison for one cell (strategy, timeframe, side, style; regime * until P6). Folds: expanding train from the first entry, test windows of 3 calendar months from at least 9 months in, an embargo of 5 sessions between a train trade's entry and the window, and a purge of any train trade still open at the window start. In each fold the winner is chosen on the train trades only: the score is mean_R − λ·downside_dev_R (λ 0.5, on net R when costed), a candidate needs 30 trades to take part, and a candidate on a plateau (every neighbour on its parameter line scores at least its own score minus its standard error) is preferred to a sharp peak when it scores within one standard error of the best; otherwise the best wins and is flagged a lone peak. A fold is thin when its winner has fewer than 30 test trades. The choice is stable when at least 60 % of the non-thin folds agree with the most frequent winner (or a neighbour of it) and at least 60 % of those agreeing folds scored above 0 out of sample; fewer than two usable folds, or an unqualified full sample, is "no choice" and the default (first listed) exit is used. The report prints the exact chance that random winners would pass the agreement rule, the pooled out-of-sample result of the walk-forward choice beside the default and every single candidate, and that the choice is in-sample on about 2.1 years and not evidence of an edge.

From MAE/MFE (trade "nature"), as built in the same table: the share of winners (RExact > 0) with an adverse excursion above 0.8R and above 1.2R; the median MFE and the shares reaching 1R, 2R and 3R; the median giveback of the trades that reached 1R and the share that gave back more than half. The excursions are those of the path the walk took, so each figure is censored by the walk's own exit and is printed beside its censor level (the stop at 1R, the target of a FIXED_RR or the t2_rr of a PARTIAL_BE, or none for a trail): the share reaching 3R under a 2R target is 0 by construction and is printed as censored with no reading, and a cell of fewer than 30 trades is printed n < 30 with none. The adverse excursion is the largest of the whole trade (the walker stores no order of it against the best point). The optional walk without a target to read the uncensored tail (--diagnose) is not built.

From MAE/MFE (trade "nature"):

  • Stop: if 90% of winners never went beyond 0.8R adverse, 1R is sound; if winners routinely dip 1.2R, the stop is too tight.
  • Target: if median MFE is 2.4R but only 30% reach 3R, prefer 2–2.5R or partial booking.
  • Trail activation: where MFE giveback becomes common. (ATR_TRAIL as built in P4-03 activates on the close of a bar at activate_at_rr, a value the strategy gives; deriving it from the giveback is a diagnostic for P4-05/P4-06.)

Per trade (tonight): scale by current ATR; snap to structure (swing points, previous-day levels, round numbers); obstacle check — cap target before a strong level or skip if achievable R:R < min_rr; reduce target for late intraday triggers (learned).

4. Pyramiding#

Used only where the cell shows trend persistence (fat MFE tail), mostly 30m+ and swing.

Parameter Name in the model Default
Initial position initial_pct 50% of full size
Add triggers add_at_rr (strictly increasing) +1R, +2R (on trigger-TF close)
Add sizes add_pct (as many as add_at_rr; with initial_pct at most 100) 30%, 20%
Max adds max_adds (1 to the number of levels) 2
Trail of the combined position trail_atr_mult, optional atr_period the ATR (the only trail in schema 1)
Risk rule not a parameter: always on Before each add, move stops so total open risk ≤ initial 1R (as built: §2, the pyramid paragraph; ADR 0052)

The defaults are the example the model accepts (they sum to exactly 100). The names are provisional until P4's spec (§2).

5. Time rules#

Rule Intraday Swing
Earliest and latest entry first_entry (e.g. 09:30) and last_entry (e.g. 14:30); both inclusive: the bar that closes at those times is still allowed Signal valid N sessions (default 1) — not in the model of P3-02b
Forced exit square_off (e.g. 15:15), required max_hold_sessions, 1 to 5, required: the close of session N, counting the entry session as the first and sessions by the trading calendar
Time stop e.g. < +0.5R after 6 bars (the time_stop overlay; run since P4-02, ADR 0050) e.g. < +0.5R after 2 sessions (the time_stop overlay; run since P4-02)
Overnight gap — Gap through stop ⇒ filled at the open (simulated, and counted); a target is never filled better than the target (ADR 0040)

Swing positions in the simulator (P3-03d, ADR 0040). A swing position stays open across sessions with its stop and target unchanged; the rows of ADR 0038 apply to every minute that holds a trade on every date, and there is no square-off. MAX_HOLD N leaves at the close of the last 1-minute bar of session N (the entry session is session 1; sessions are counted with the trading calendar, a special session counts and a holiday does not; with no calendar every weekday counts), with the base slippage and no extra ticks; a stop or target reached in that minute comes first. When the due minute holds no trade the position leaves at the close of the last minute that does (counted as MaxHoldAtLastClose), and when the data ends first it is reported open (EndOfData, never in a statistic). A session on which the walker read no trade is counted (SessionsUnseen), not excluded. There is at most one open position per instrument and side: a candidate decided while one is open is not walked and is counted (PositionOpen). A swing position that is open when a flagged day (an unadjusted jump above 20 %) starts a new segment of the series is excluded and counted. Dividends are neither adjusted nor received, and the closing auction and the circuit limit of a stock held overnight are not modelled (ISS-18, ISS-27).

PARTIAL_BE, PARTIAL_TRAIL and the time_stop overlay, as built (P4-02, ADR 0050). The simulator runs one exit set at a time: one candidate of FIXED_RR, PARTIAL_BE or PARTIAL_TRAIL with one value in every list, and at most the time_stop overlay; two candidates, a list of two values, ATR_TRAIL, STRUCTURE_TRAIL, EMA_TRAIL, PYRAMID and event_exit are refused by name. T1 is the target of the position (t1_rr × the initial risk R0) and fills like a target: at the level and never at a better price, no slippage; a minute that touches the stop and T1 is the stop for the whole position unless the open is already beyond T1. The share book_pct leaves there, and the minute that fills T1 is never also a break-even stop, a T2 fill or a trail level: the new levels are in force from the next minute, and the whole range of the T1 minute counts for MAE and MFE. PARTIAL_BE: from the next minute the stop is the exact entry (a stop exit at it pays slippage and fills at the open if the minute gapped through it) and the rest has a limit at T2 (t2_rr × R0). PARTIAL_TRAIL: no break-even move; after T1, at each closed bar, the stop is H − trail_atr_mult × ATR (a sell: the lowest low, plus), H the highest high from the T1 minute through the end of the bar and ATR the value as of that close, in force from the first minute after the close and only if it is tighter (a looser level is ignored and counted; a NaN ATR gives no level). time_stop: intraday, at the close of the N-th evaluation bar that closes after the fill minute starts, a progress side × (close − entry) / R0 strictly below min_progress_rr sends the open share out at the open of the next minute that holds a trade (market, slippage, reason TimeStop), unless the square-off, a stop or a target takes that minute first; swing, at the close of the last minute of session swing_sessions counted by the calendar (at the last close that held a trade if that minute held none). The three are the first production runs that make legs; the legs are exported but not stored yet (simulate --store refuses them).

A trade that leaves in several fills (P4-01, ADR 0049). A position that takes shares out (a partial exit; an add comes with the pyramid) is one trade with legs: the entry first, the exits in order, the last one last; the shares out equal the shares in. The position gate holds the instrument until the last leg; the maximum hold, the square-off and the gaps of the swing rows apply to what is still open. Its R is in the unit of the initial risk of the first fill, R0 = |entry − initial stop|, never changed by a move of the stop: R = Σ share × side × (exit − entry) / R0 over the exit legs (from the exact prices, and from the filled prices for RFilled; the entry basis becomes the share-weighted average once adds exist), and MAE and MFE are measured against the entry in units of R0 over every minute the position was open. A trade of one entry and one exit has no legs and is what it was.

An entry that rests overnight (P3-03e, ADR 0043). In a swing walk a decision taken at or after the end of its session (every decision of a 1D evaluation, and the decision of the last 30m or 1h bar) places its order for the next trading session: it fills at the first minute that holds a trade at most five minutes after that session's open, at the open when that is within the limit (the daily close plus 0.1 %), at the limit when the session opens beyond it and trades back to it, and is otherwise a counted miss; an open at or through the stop is no trade. A list that ends before the fill session is in the data is a pending order (OrderPendingAtEnd), not a miss. The trade is dated on the fill date; its key and the window cut follow the day of the decision. The fill is in the minute whose open may be the auction price (ISS-18 (a)): a daily strategy fills there by design.

How the simulator fills a stop that was gapped through, a target, a minute that holds both, the square-off minute and an entry limit order is the rule table of ADR 0038 (P3-03b): a gapped stop fills at that minute's open, a target at the target and never better, stop first unless the minute's open is already beyond the target, and a square-off waits for the next minute that holds a trade.

The four time rules of a strategy are first_entry, last_entry, square_off and max_hold_sessions (the time_rules of doc 11 §2); doc 16 spells the same four first_entry_time, last_entry_time, square_off_time and max_holding_sessions in its styles block. A stored strategy carries its own: the numbers of the configuration are not defaults for a missing time rule, so that the same stored text (and hash) always means the same behaviour.

6. Position sizing (per account)#

risk_amount = account_capital × risk_per_trade_pct
qty         = min(risk_amount / |entry − stop|, max_capital_per_trade / entry, liquidity_cap)
qty         = round_down_to_lot(qty)        # futures
skip if qty < 1 lot, or cost_R > max_cost_R

The simulator's version of this block (built in P3-03c, ADR 0042): qty = floor(min(risk_amount / |entry − stop|, max_capital_per_trade / entry)) from the exact entry, whole shares, on a fixed simulated capital with the standard profile; below 1 share, or a charge above max_cost_R of the risk taken, the trade is skipped and counted (SkippedQuantity, SkippedCost); the lot rounding and the liquidity cap are not applied (named limits).

Optional confidence scaling (capped fractional Kelly, 0.5×–1.25×) — off until calibration is proven in paper trading.

Learned multipliers on top (doc 09 §14): meta-labelling size × 0.5–1.5, equity-curve throttle × 0.5 while the system's equity is below its 20-trade average, reduced market-gate day × 0.75. The multipliers never raise risk above the profile's maximum per trade.

8. Costs & slippage#

Brokerage, exchange charges, STT/CTT, GST, stamp duty, SEBI fees — from your broker's current charge sheet in config (rates change with budgets). Slippage: ticks + fraction of bar range; extra for stop exits and gaps. Every simulation includes costs.

The simulator charges every simulated order from an effective-dated charge sheet (ten rates per segment, intraday and delivery) on the rates in force on the date of that order, on the filled price, each line rounded to the paisa once; it reports RFilled (R from the filled prices) and RNet = RFilled − CostR side by side with RExact, with the 16 lines of the charge visible per trade. A run with no sheet is gross and says costs: none; the choice of a sheet by a request, the stored sheet and the check against the owner's bills are P3-03f. The model, the rounding and what is not modelled are in ADR 0042.

Costs of a trade with legs (P4-01). Each leg that has shares is one order, charged on the schedule in force on its own date and on its filled price: an entry or add leg takes floor(Q × share) shares, an exit leg the same except the last exit leg, which takes what is left so that the quantities add up to the shares in; a leg that would have no shares is merged into the next and not charged. TotalCost is the sum of all the orders, CostR = TotalCost / (Q × R0) and a cost above max_cost_R skips the whole trade, never a leg. The favourable excursion at a close exit (engine sim-6): when a position leaves at a close (the end of the data, MAX_HOLD) the favourable extreme reaches at least that close, also when the fill minute of an entry at the limit is that minute, so −MAE ≤ R ≤ MFE has no exception.

The simulator's slippage is SlippageConfig with these defaults (costs.slippage in doc 16): market-type fills pay (ticks + extra) × tick + bar_range_frac × (high − low) against the trade, rounded to the instrument's tick, with the extra ticks on stop exits; a resting limit (a target, an entry at the limit) does not slip. The rules and what is not modelled are in ADR 0038.

The exit rules of §§1, 5 and 8 as the simulator walks them (the order inside a minute, gaps, the square-off, the maximum hold, slippage on the filled price, MAE, MFE and R) are checked against an independent second implementation written from the written rule tables only (ADR 0047, P3-08): over 18,500 seeded series and 100 hand rows the two agree on every field of every trade; the disagreement log holds three class A reading decisions of the dry read and one open class B entry for the owner (Q3-MFE: in one corner, an entry at the limit filled in the last minute of the data or of the hold, MFE can be below R). That is agreement with the rules as a second reader understands them, not a comparison with charts (P3-09) and not evidence that any strategy has an edge. The costing that follows the walk (the quantity from the exact entry, the sixteen charge lines of the two legs on the schedule of each leg's date, CostR, RNet, the two skips) is compared the same way against the same author's reference costing (P3-08c) and agrees on every field; the engine line of the exports says so, with the engine version, and says that charts were not checked.
