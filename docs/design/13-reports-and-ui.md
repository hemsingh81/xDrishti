## 1b. Clickable prototype

A working front-end prototype of these screens lives in [`prototype/index.html`](../prototype/index.html) (open it
directly in a browser — offline, demo data, no backend; services and the live feed are simulated in the
browser). It demonstrates the login (demo credentials prefilled); Accounts (list/detail tabs, role matrix, risk
profiles, wizard, data failover); plan review with approve / modify / reject, the pre-trade checklist and
**auto-approval at the cutoff**; suggestions inbox; portfolio with per-account tiles, Action center, price alerts,
XIRR and animated grouping; instruments (one scrollable grid with sticky header and symbol column) & baskets; CSV
import with aggregation; the Data quality window; the **Learning** screen (journey, lessons with loss tags, plan vs actual with
fills ledger, setups, smart rules, tweak & re-run, autonomy, weekly digest); live tickets filled with simulated
slippage and graded on fills; Reports (overview with auto-insights, basket comparison, pivot and 10 more
reports); Strategy Lab; Services with schedule configuration, dependency checks, timeline and catch-up; settings
with password re-entry; the audit log — responsive from phones to 4K monitors, light and dark.

**Visual language** (carried into the React app): Inter font and a standard, calm palette (Tailwind *slate/gray*
neutrals with one *indigo* accent; green/red/amber only for meaning), a light sidebar with a soft colour-tinted
icon per screen, tiles with tinted icons and count-up numbers, collapsible cards, animated dialogs/drawers/toasts
(respecting *reduce motion*), light and dark themes, and scrollable data grids with sticky headers instead of
pagination. In MUI this is one theme object (palette, shape, typography) shared by every screen.

**Navigation for speed:** a command palette (**⌘K / Ctrl K** or **/**) searches pages, Learning tabs, actions

### Portfolio screen (P2-01 / P2-05)

Same viewport-height layout as Instruments from `md` up (header, filters, tiles and sync bar stay; the holdings list scrolls inside its card, loads further pages of 100 as it is scrolled and keeps the "Showing N of total" footer fixed; the page scrolls normally below `md`). All view state is in the URL: `account`, `bucket`, `status` (open default, closed, all), `q`, `sort` and `holding` (the open drawer); the summary tiles follow the account and bucket only, so loading more rows never changes them.

- **Scope:** *Account* type-ahead (All accounts, or an account with the Portfolio role), *Bucket* type-ahead, a debounced search (symbol, name, ISIN) and an Open / Closed / All toggle. With "All accounts" and more than one account the list gets an *Account* column. Import, Sync now and Auto refresh act on one account: the chosen one, or the only one there is; with several accounts and "All accounts" they ask to pick one first. An account that is not connected shows "Connect the account first" with a link to its Connection tab, and its Import and Sync buttons are off (what is already stored stays visible).
- **Summary tiles** (totals of the chosen account and bucket, from `GET /api/holdings/summary`): Invested · Current value (as of the valuation date) · Unrealised P&L ₹ with % · XIRR · Realised (est.) · Holdings (open, closed, not in master list). Gains and losses always carry a sign and an arrow (▲ +₹18,400, ▼ −₹12,000), never colour alone. An XIRR that cannot be shown is "—" with its reason (*insufficient*: needs 30+ days and money in and out; *n/m*: beyond ±1,000 %; *no data*: no price) as the tile hint and a tooltip. An unpriced holding (no usable price) shows a "No price yet" chip and dashes for value, P&L, % and XIRR (never −100 %), and the totals leave it out with the note "{n} holdings without a price are left out of value, P&L and XIRR."; an XIRR beyond ±1,000 % shows "n/m"; every reason is reachable by keyboard. Under the tiles: a chip "12 lots use the default date" (it lists the oldest purchase dates first, where the default dates are) and a footnote that these gross figures are price returns (while no dividend or charge is recorded) or that the net figures count dividends and costs only from the dates on their tiles (once some are); corporate actions are not included.
- **Income tiles (P2-14, built):** a second row under the gross tiles from `income` of the summary: **Dividends** (the sum entered, "from <date>" = the first dividend entered, "none entered yet" otherwise, and the unallocated part when there is one), **Costs** (trade charges plus the ledger's account-level costs, "from <date>" = `chargesFrom`, "none read yet" otherwise), **Net total return** (with the return before costs and the date the net figures are complete from, `coverageFrom` = the later start of the sources that exist) and **Net XIRR** (with the reason when it cannot be shown, as for the gross XIRR). The gross tiles do not change; alpha, the benchmark and the value history say "price return, before dividends and charges". **While nothing is recorded** (`income` null) the row is replaced by an invitation — *Dividends and costs are not in the figures yet*, with *Add a dividend* (opens the Cash & dividends panel on the Add dividend form) and *Read the ledger* (takes the owner to the Sync trades & ledger control); zeros would claim there were none. With a bucket chosen the tiles count only the bucket's holdings' dividends and trade charges.

Two tabs: **Import files** and **History** (the batches of trades, dividends and costs with Undo). The account is the page's (the owner picks one when the page shows all accounts). The flow is the one of the trade import, for several files at once:
  - **Drop.** One or several files at once (CSV, XLS or XLSX; at most 20, 10 MB each, 40 MB together) by dropping or choosing; the account is chosen above. The browser does not refuse a file by its extension or MIME type (only by the counts and sizes above): the server detects the kind from the content, and a file of no known kind is that card's error. **Preview** reads and stores nothing.
  - **One card per file**, with the detected kind as a chip (global transaction report, DP charges, demat holding, profit and loss, tradebook, dividend list …), summary tiles (rows, valid, to add, already imported, skipped, bad), the warnings in words and the block of the kind: for a **transaction report** the trades to add, the lots that would be rebuilt (hand-dated lots that would be replaced are said first), the rows skipped **per reason** (futures and options, commodities, currencies, BSE, covered by Dhan's own trades), the charges check against the report's total and the **names** — each scrip name with what it was taken to mean and the rule that decided; for **DP charges** the lines by status (new, ₹0, buy side, already imported, already in the ledger), the rupees that would be added to the costs and the names it teaches; for **demat holding** the securities that agree, the quantity differences, the securities held at Dhan but not synced and the synced holdings missing from the statement, marked *informational — nothing is changed*; a **profit-and-loss** report says it is a statement of results and is not imported; a **dividend list** says to use Import dividends and offers the button; a file that cannot be read shows its reason on its card, and the other cards are unaffected. States of a card: detected, needs a match, warnings, nothing new, imported, error.
  - **Choices.** A name that matched nothing or more than one security has a picker (the account's holdings, the instrument list, or an ISIN typed in) and **Remember this match** (ticked); the choices apply to every file of the batch and are read again with **Preview again**. A name left alone is imported without an ISIN and does not join a holding.
  - **Another client code.** A report whose client code differs from the one remembered for the account is flagged and imported only when its box is ticked.
  - **Import and Undo.** One **Import** for all files (each file all or nothing); a file whose preview went stale gets its fresh preview in place and is confirmed again, the others are done; every stored batch has **Undo** (asks first, says how many it voided), and importing the same file again stores nothing and says so.

Then the ledger: a line saying from which day it is stored ("Ledger read from <date>. Costs before that day are not counted.", or that no ledger is stored yet, so its costs are not counted), then a **From** / **To** date filter and a **Class** filter (All classes, Funds in, Funds out, Dividends, Charges, Other; *Clear filters*; a *To* before *From* is refused in words), **tiles by class** (signed total and the number of entries of each of the five classes for the period, whatever class the list is filtered to), and the entries newest first, with *Show more*: date, description with the narration and "Voucher n · exchange" under it, a class chip, the signed amount (sign and arrow as well as colour) and the running balance. - **Import dividends (P2-15, built; [ADR 0028](../adr/0028-dividend-csv-import-with-name-matching.md), [doc 12](12-portfolio-monitoring.md) §3 "Dividend import (P2-15)"):** the Dividends part of the Cash & dividends panel has an **Import dividends** button and a drop zone for a CSV file; the dividends go to the panel's chosen account (one file per account). The flow is the one of the trade import — choose the file, **preview**, confirm, result with **Undo** — in a dialog that shows nothing stored until the owner confirms:
  - **Preview.** The detected format (a dividend payout list), summary tiles (rows read, valid rows, rows to add with their rupees, already imported, possible duplicates, names needing a match, bad rows), the rupees to add **by year**, and notices in words: payments listed twice in the file that are **counted once** (with the lines, and the hint to add the second by hand if it is real); rows whose per-share × quantity differs from the amount paid (the paid amount is used); and dividends dated **before the account's first lot**, with a button to the *Read from an earlier date* dialog, because the net XIRR is only as good as the history behind it. Tables, each with its count: **To add** (date, name as the file wrote it, the holding or instrument it was matched to with the rule that matched it as a word-chip — remembered, ISIN, symbol, same name, part of the name, instrument list — a mark asking to check matches made by part of the name, amount, warnings), **Already imported**, **Possible duplicates** (equal to a dividend typed in; unticked, tick to import anyway), **Needs a match** (names that matched nothing or fit more than one holding) and **Bad rows** (line and reason, the first 200 and how many in all). Long tables are paged, 50 rows at a time (a page control under each table): a file can have 20,000 rows. Names are shown as text, never markup.
  - **Choices.** Every name that matched nothing or more than one holding has a holding picker (type-ahead over the account's holdings and the instrument list, and "keep unallocated"); **Remember this match** is ticked by default and saves the choice for the account, so the next file with that name needs no question (nothing already imported changes). A name left alone is imported **unallocated with the file's name**: it counts in the account and total figures, not in a holding, bucket or sector.
  - **Confirm and result.** The confirm step says how many dividends and how many rupees will be stored and how many names will be remembered; the file is sent again with the preview's digest. If holdings, remembered names or dividends changed meanwhile the dialog shows the fresh preview with a notice that things changed since the owner looked, and asks again — nothing is stored. The result shows what was stored (allocated to a holding, to an instrument only, unallocated), what was skipped (already imported, possible duplicates) and the names remembered, with **Undo** (it voids the batch's dividends, asks first, and says how many); importing the same file again stores nothing and says so.
  - **History.** The imports of the account, newest first: file name, who and when, counts, rupees, status (*Undone* flagged); **Undo** on each one still in force. The **Dividends you entered** list marks a dividend read from a file with a **CSV** chip and shows its quantity and, for an unallocated one, the name the file used; voiding one by hand works as for any other.
  - **Wrong kind of file.** A dividend list dropped on the **Trades** import says it is a dividend file and shows a button that opens Import dividends for the same account; a tradebook with no buy/sell column (a sideless Dhan tradebook export) shows the explanation — it cannot be read because purchases cannot be told from sales — with the ways forward (export one that has a side, or *Sync trades & ledger*); a tradebook dropped on Import dividends points back to the Trades import; since P2-17 a Dhan report (transaction report, DP charges, demat holding) dropped on either import shows a button to **Import files**, and a profit-and-loss statement is said to be not imported. Any other unreadable file shows the API's message, which names the missing columns and the accepted header names.

(the voided-trade help, see [doc 12](12-portfolio-monitoring.md) §3); when the latest succeeded read left the holding's lots alone because one is dated by hand (the ticker is in `needsReviewSymbols`), an alert says so ("The last read from Dhan left the lots of … alone, because a lot has a date you set. Rebuild lots shows what the trades would change first, and replaces your dates only if you confirm.") with *Review the rebuild*, which opens the existing preview-then-rebuild.
- **Holdings grid** (the shared `Grid`): Symbol (monospace; "Not in master list" chip; "Closed" chip), Name, Qty, Avg cost, LTP (price time as tooltip), Invested, Value, P&L ₹, P&L %, XIRR, Dividends (P2-14: the dividends entered for the holding, "—" when none, sortable), Acquired (earliest open lot date, "Default date" chip, lot count), Bucket, and Account when several accounts are listed; sortable headers use the API's sort fields; Name, Avg cost, Invested, Dividends, Acquired, Bucket and Account are hidden on narrower screens (the drawer shows everything). At the default Open status the groups and their total leave out the dividends and trade charges of closed holdings, while the summary tiles include them; choose All to make them agree.

- **Import holdings** (dialog, 3 steps shown in its header): 1 *Fetch from Dhan* (`POST …/holdings/preview`; not connected, role off, no bucket and Dhan-unreachable are explained in words with a link or Retry); 2 *Choose dates*: a visible rule line ("Default date: 1 Jan 2025. The grid and tiles reload.

- **Live prices (P2-10a, built):** while the page is visible and the market is open the screen opens the SignalR hub `/hubs/events` once per tab (`shared/realtime`; `@microsoft/signalr`, WebSockets with `skipNegotiation`, loaded with the Portfolio chunk), calls `Subscribe({accountId, bucketId, holdingIds})` for its scope and the holdings it shows (and again when they change, `Unsubscribe` on leave) and applies each `pricesChanged` to rows, group rows and tiles with the same pure functions as the server. A changed price flashes its cell for one second with an arrow as well as colour, and not at all under `prefers-reduced-motion`; sort, selected row, scroll position and the open drawer stay. The prices line shows **Live · 10:35:42 IST** (a tick for the shown holdings at most 15 seconds old), **Pre-open** (09:00 to 09:15), **Delayed** (the feed is connected but the last tick for the shown holdings is older than 60 seconds; shows the age), **Quotes** (feed off, paused or failed: the 5-minute text) or **Closed** ("As of 15:30 · close"); it changes at most once a second and is not a live region. Holdings with no live tick keep their quote or synced price (price time and source in the drawer); while the feed streams, a note under the tiles says "N holdings are not live" (unmapped, beyond the 1,000-price cap, or not traded yet today) and is left out when the feed is off or paused. The tiles and the group rows are worked out again in the browser from every open holding of the scope (read in pages of 100, a few requests at a time) at the pushed prices; the counts, XIRR, benchmark and realised result stay those of the last read, and when the totals and those holdings do not fit each other (read at different moments) the older of the two is read again, at most once every 30 seconds. While the hub follows the screen the page reads its figures exactly every 5 minutes (a quote arriving over the hub also makes the list and totals read again); the heavy read of every holding behind the live totals follows only that timer and real changes (sync, trade, import). If the hub cannot connect (or the page is hidden, or the market is closed) the screen falls back to the once-a-minute re-read while the market is open, says so in the chip ("Quotes" with "Live updates are not available: reading every minute"), reconnects with a growing back-off (the wait starts over only once the server has answered) and shows no error dialog; when it returns it re-reads the figures once and switches back. The hub contract (methods, events, payloads, limits) is in [doc 03](03-target-architecture.md) §6 and generated into `frontend/src/shared/realtime/hubContract.generated.ts`.

### Notifications and pop-ups (P2-16, [ADR 0027](../adr/0027-self-healing-sync-and-actionable-notifications.md))

What the app can mend by itself it mends quietly ([doc 12](12-portfolio-monitoring.md) §3 "Self-healing and attention items (P2-16)"); what only the owner can do arrives once, as an **item** in the bell and as a **pop-up**, each with a button that goes straight to the fix, and goes away by itself when the cause is gone.

- **The bell and its drawer.** The bell reads `GET /api/portfolio/notices` every 60 seconds beside the data, service and account notices and shows an unread badge (max "9+") that **does not count resolved items**. The right-hand drawer (400 px, full width on phones) has two groups: **Unresolved** — the open items, most urgent first (error, warning, information), newest first within a level — and a collapsed **Resolved (n)** group of what was resolved in the last 24 hours (greyed, labelled "Resolved", with no buttons; the group header shows only when there is something resolved). Each item has a severity chip (icon and a hidden word, never colour alone), a bold title, the text, the relative time and **at most two buttons** (the first filled, the second outlined; "Retry now", "Reconnect Dhan", "Review", "Open trades", "Open"); a notice without buttons keeps its **Open** link. "Mark all read" and the empty state "You're all caught up" are as before. A button marks the item read first; a **route** button navigates (and closes the drawer), a **command** button calls the existing endpoint it names (`POST /api/accounts/{id}/sync/broker-history` …) with the session and CSRF token, keeps the drawer open and waits on that button, then reads the notices and the screens again and says the result in a toast ("Retry now: started. This notice goes away by itself once it works." or the API's own words when it refused, e.g. a run is already going). The browser follows only a route that is a path inside the app (one slash, no host) and only a command that is a `POST` to one of three whole-path patterns - `/api/accounts/{guid}/sync/broker-history`, `/api/accounts/{guid}/holdings/sync` and `/api/services/{id}/run` (`shared/api/commands.ts`) - anything else is dropped, never followed. A holdings *Retry now* cannot confirm a sync that would close all or most holdings: the refusal is shown with a pointer to *Sync now* on the account's Portfolio tab. Read state stays per browser (`localStorage`); it is keyed on the **id plus `since`** for the six server attention kinds only (`reconnect_dhan`, `sync_failing`, `holdings_need_review`, `trades_unexplained`, `dividends_pending`, `securities_unmatched`), so a condition that clears and returns is unread again; every other notice (price alerts, 'holdings need a decision', the API-key expiry) is keyed on its plain id, which already changes with the situation or the day. The server sends `Since = null` for the 'holdings need a decision' notice (its `At` still moves on each poll: cosmetic). Items of the account notices that the browser still derives itself are the API-key expiry (`key_expiring`, `key_expired`); the Dhan login (missing, expired, about to expire) is the server's `reconnect_dhan`.
- **Where the buttons lead (deep links).** `/accounts?account={id}&tab=connection` (Reconnect Dhan, Connect to Dhan) and `…&tab=portfolio` (a failing sync's *Open*), `/services` (a failing price poll's *Open*), `/portfolio?holding={id}&section=trades` (a holding open on its Trades section) with `&rebuild=preview` (the rebuild preview open too; both parameters are taken out of the URL once the section has scrolled into view, so the link can be used again), `/portfolio?cash=suggestions&account={id}` (the Cash & dividends panel with the keyboard on the suggested dividends), `/portfolio?unmatched=1&account={id}` (the holdings with no match in the instrument master, with a banner and *Show all holdings*), and the older `/portfolio?alerts=triggered`, `/portfolio?focus=actions`, `?trades=import|history`, `?cash=open|add|import` and `?import=open` (Import files).
- **One toast provider.** `app/layout/AppShell` owns the **single** `ToastProvider` of the app, so a toast raised anywhere — a page, a dialog, the bell, a background check — shows in one stack (a test covers `/system`, `/settings` and `/services`); the attention pop-ups are mounted beside it. `useToast().notify(message, severity, actionOrOptions)` keeps its form and gains the options `title`, `actions` (up to two buttons), `persistent`, `id` (notifying again with the same id replaces the toast in place and restarts its time) and `durationMs`; `dismiss(id)` closes one.
- **The stacking toast.** Up to **three** show at once, newest at the bottom: in a column at the bottom right from `md` (400 px wide) and the full width above the bottom edge and the safe area on phones; a fourth makes the oldest leave — a timed one before a persistent one. Look as in the visual system above (accent bar, tint, icon chip, title, message, buttons in the severity colour, a close button) plus a thin **countdown bar** for a timed toast; under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing slides and there is no bar, the toast simply stays its full time. Timed toasts stay **5 s** (10 s with a button); a **persistent** one — the pop-up of an `error` item — stays until it is closed, one of its buttons is pressed or its item is resolved. Hovering (a mouse, not a touch) or focusing a toast pauses every countdown, **Escape** closes the newest, and a toast is announced once, as `alert` (error, warning) or `status` (success, information). The keyboard rules of the undo toasts are kept: an action started with the keyboard moves focus to its button and gives it back when the toast closes, and Ctrl/Cmd+Z runs the newest undo while the toast is open (not while typing in a field). The stack sits in its own portal and stays announced above an open dialog.
- **Attention pop-ups.** `AttentionPopups` (mounted once in the shell, below the provider and the router) pops up an item **the first time it appears while the app is open and it is unread** — title, text and the item's buttons; an `error` one is persistent. A button marks the item read, then navigates or runs the command; a toast closes by itself when its item is resolved, gone or read in the bell. What popped up is remembered in this browser apart from "read" (`xd.notifications.popped`), so a reload does not show it again; **when the app opens only the most urgent unread item pops up** — the rest are remembered as seen and wait in the bell. The kinds that pop up are the six server items and the API-key expiry (`attentionKinds`); price-alert and decision notices wait in the bell.
